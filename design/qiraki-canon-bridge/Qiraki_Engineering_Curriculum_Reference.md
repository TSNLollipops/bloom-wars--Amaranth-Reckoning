## Year-by-year curriculum, extrapolated

**Year 1 — Simple machines.** The six classical simple machines, real, ancient, genuinely foundational engineering, lever, wheel and axle, pulley, wedge, screw, inclined plane. Cockpit-area coursework at this level is entirely mechanical: seat restraint mechanisms, manual canopy latches, basic linkages. No electronics yet, no hydraulics, pure mechanical advantage, torque, and friction. This is genuinely how real mechanical engineering education starts, not a simplification invented for the setting.

**Year 2 — Basic electrical fundamentals.** Ohm’s law, series and parallel circuits, real wiring and connector work. Cockpit-area coursework expands to basic instrumentation, simple sensor wiring, warning-light circuits. Runs in close parallel to rune-patterning’s own Year 2 material (circuits, sequence), a nice, real, unforced connection between the two curricula, both fields are teaching “sequence and connection matter” from different disciplinary angles in the same year without needing to force the parallel on the page.

**Year 3 — Hydraulics and pneumatics, and the curriculum splits by**** ****path.** Real fluid-power engineering, Pascal’s principle, pressure systems, actuator basics, exactly the technology real heavy machinery and real robotics already use for joint and limb movement. This is also the year coursework starts differing meaningfully by chosen class (mirroring rune-patterning’s own Year 3 split): a Meeps cadet studies joint and actuator systems relevant to mobility and close-range striking, a Tank cadet studies armor-mounting and structural load distribution, a Reeps cadet studies targeting-mount stabilization, a Munti cadet studies the fabricator and remote-repair delivery systems specifically. Same underlying field, genuinely different application focus per path, the way real engineering specializations diverge from a shared fundamentals base. **Third convergence on this same year, 2026-08-22:** Year 3 is also when the standing battle rig replaces the seated trainer, and a standing motion platform runs on exactly the fluid-power joint and limb technology this year teaches. Cadets learn hydraulics the same year they first stand inside a hydraulic system. Mechanism locked in Qiraki_Technobabble_Glossary.md, not duplicated here.

**Year 4 — Materials science.** Real metallurgy and composite material basics, tensile strength, fatigue, stress and strain, why a material fails and how it fails differently under sustained load versus a single sharp impact. Thematically apt for the roughest physical year already locked, cadets learning materials failure in the classroom while watching (and living, given wound-emulation) real failure happen to their own machines and bodies on the same timeline. Worth a deliberate, quiet echo if a chapter ever wants it: a materials-fatigue lesson landing the same week as a real injury.

**Year 5 — Thermodynamics.** Real heat-engine and energy-conversion fundamentals, how power generation inside a mech produces heat as an unavoidable byproduct, and how that heat has to go somewhere or the system fails. Lines up naturally with Book 5’s “combat weight increases hard,” more capable systems draw more power, more power means more heat, and cooling becomes a real engineering constraint a cadet has to actually design around rather than ignore.

**Year 6 — Control systems and servomechanisms.** This is where the two curricula, rune-patterning and engineering, meet properly for the first time rather than just running in loose parallel. Rune-patterning’s Year 5 already taught the logic of feedback loops (real control theory, a construct that checks its own output and adjusts). Engineering’s Year 6 teaches the physical half of the same real field: servomechanisms, actuators that respond to sensor feedback in real time, PID-controller- adjacent concepts, without necessarily using that exact real-world term on the page. **This is not a coincidence and should be used**** ****deliberately:** Trav’s already-locked sword-and-servo innovation this exact year isn’t just a rune-tech justification anymore, it’s the direct product of this year’s engineering coursework specifically, control theory and servomechanism design converging in his hands at precisely the year the story already needed him to have that capability. Two curricula independently supporting the same locked story beat from different disciplinary angles.

**Year 7, Devereux-Kastel — Systems integration.** Real engineering discipline in its own right, combining independently-functional subsystems (targeting, power delivery, mechanical actuation, thermal management) into one coherent, working whole rather than a collection of parts that happen to share a chassis. Matches the already-locked “by year 7, students are working weapon systems directly,” and directly supports Trav’s late-game build philosophy shift toward flexibility over raw durability, a systems-integration mindset is exactly what lets someone deliberately trade one subsystem’s strength for another’s, rather than just making everything bigger.

**Zero-g addendum, new 2026-08-20, per Qiraki_Book7_Chapter_Outline.md**** ****Ch. 3.** Devereux-Kastel’s own space/atmospheric combat curriculum adds a real mek-side task systems integration alone doesn’t cover: float-device calibration has to be tuned to each Synker’s own habitual movement patterns, not just built correctly in the abstract. The job stops being “build it right” and becomes “build it wrong in exactly the way this one pilot is wrong” — real curriculum weight, not incidental texture, and a genuinely different design problem from the mechanical/electrical/ integration sequence above, closer to a bespoke-fit discipline than a systems one.

## Mek weekend track, LOCKED 2026-08-22, per B1.CH06

Meks (cadets matched to a specific pilot at the academy, see CAST) run a second, separate curriculum track on top of the standard cadet schedule: additional coursework on Friday and Saturday, covering the coming week’s material before their assigned cadet gets to it in the regular Monday-Thursday blocks. This is why engineering scenes on the page (B1.CH04-06) show Reqa running the workshop block one-on-one with no separate instructor character present, she already has the week’s material by the time Trav does, and functionally absorbs the instructor’s role for their sessions together. Applies to non-combat curricula only, rune-patterning and engineering both (see matching note in RUNE); the combat curriculum runs on the same schedule for meks and cadets alike, no separate track.

## Where real engineering runs out, LOCKED boundary

Two places, named explicitly rather than left to blur:

**The connective-AI neural interface itself.** Genuinely defensible as near-future plausible extrapolation rather than pure invention, real brain-computer interface research already exists as an active field today, full bidirectional sensory bleed-through at symbiosis’s fidelity is beyond current real science but isn’t a clean break from it the way Heirloom effects are. Worth treating as “extrapolated,” not “invented from nothing.” **Cross-ref, do not duplicate here:** the actual division of labor between the neural channel and the hand rests, and the training-rig/battle-rig hardware split that lands the newer control gear at Year 3, are both locked in Qiraki_Technobabble_Glossary.md’s Core mechanics section as of 2026-08-22.

**Munti matter-transfer repair and the disintegrator beam.** This is the genuine break point. Mass-energy equivalence itself is real physics (the underlying principle the reversed beam trades on), but controlled, at-will conversion between matter and usable energy or matter, on demand, in the field, is not achievable with anything resembling real present-day or near-future engineering. This is where invented science has to take over, and it should be treated the same way Heirloom-grade effects are treated in the rune-tech doc, visibly, deliberately different in kind rather than a smooth continuation of everything taught before it.

## Equipment/systems catalog

Format for new entries:

### [System name]

**Real field:** [engineering discipline it’s grounded in] **Taught:** [year] **Function:** [what it does] **Notes:** [callbacks, which path uses it most, anything worth remembering]

### Manual Canopy Release

**Real field:** simple machines, lever mechanics **Taught:** Year 1 **Function:** Emergency manual override for cockpit sealing, purely mechanical, no power required, functions even if every powered system on the mech has failed. **Notes:** Good, simple, high-stakes-adjacent Year 1 lesson, the kind of thing that reads as boring in class and then matters enormously the first time it’s actually needed in the field. Strong candidate for a callback later in the series, per the standing callback discipline.

### Warning-Light Circuit

**Real field:** basic electrical, series/parallel circuits **Taught:** Year 2 **Function:** Simple fault-indication wiring, tells a pilot or mek something’s wrong without diagnosing what. **Notes:** Direct precursor to rune-patterning’s Diagnostic Ping (already catalogued in the rune-tech doc), same underlying purpose, different discipline’s version of it, good pairing if a scene ever wants to show both curricula teaching parallel material in the same year.

### Joint Actuator (class-specific variants)

**Real field:** hydraulics/pneumatics **Taught:** Year 3 **Function:** Powers limb and joint movement, mounting and priority differ meaningfully by class (Meeps prioritizes speed and range of motion, Tank prioritizes load-bearing strength). **Notes:** First genuinely class-differentiated coursework, matches rune-patterning’s own Year 3 split.
