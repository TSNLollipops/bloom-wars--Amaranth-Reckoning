## PART 4 — THE SPREAD ARITHMETIC

You asked for gradual time spans to 15, 20, 25, 30 and 40 percent, and whether 40 is the point of no return.

Short version: **your 40% instinct is defensible but it commits you to a specific and very large military, the doubling structure is more frightening than any percentage, and the universe has a hard physical ceiling that makes**** ****“****eats the universe****”**** ****impossible without a specific piece of tech your own plot is already about.**

### 4a. The exponential phase, and why percentages mislead

While the Bloom can still reach fresh territory freely, growth is exponential. Under exponential growth **the time to go from any fraction to double that fraction is always exactly one doubling time.** Which produces this, and it is not a rhetorical flourish, it is arithmetic:

- 15% to 30%: one doubling

- 30% to 60%: one doubling

- 60% to fully consumed: less than one doubling

- **15% to everything gone: 2.74 doublings**

**So there is no percentage at which anyone can meaningfully react.** By the time the Coalition can measure that fifteen percent of anything is gone, it has under three doublings left, and the last doubling takes the same time as the first. This is exactly the shape of every real epidemic curve and it is why nobody ever believes the projection.

Speed of consumption barely matters. Starting from a single galaxy with each consumed galaxy seeding four neighbours, the entire observable universe falls in **about 20 generations.** Whatever a generation is.

### 4b. The frontier phase, which is the realistic one

Exponential does not hold. Once the Bloom saturates its local region it can only grow at its edge, like a wildfire, and volume goes as the cube of time rather than exponentially. Fraction consumed is then:

**f = (v·t / R)³**, so **t = (R/v) · f^(1/3)**

Worked against **Laniakea** (our supercluster, radius roughly 260 million light years, about 100,000 galaxies) at a spread velocity of **0.1c**, which gives R/v = 2.6 billion years:

| Consumed | Time from first contact |
| --- | --- |
| 15% | 1.38 Gyr |
| 20% | 1.52 Gyr |
| 25% | 1.64 Gyr |
| 30% | 1.74 Gyr |
| **40%** | **1.92 Gyr** |
| 100% | 2.60 Gyr |

**15% to 40% is 540 million years. 40% to total is 680 million.** Scale the whole table linearly for any other velocity: at 0.5c divide by five, at 0.99c divide by roughly ten.

### 4c. The point of no return, derived rather than guessed

The frontier is a sphere surface, so the area you have to defend grows as r², while the volume already lost grows as r³. **The defence problem gets monotonically worse right up until the end**, and it is at its absolute worst at the very last moment, not the middle.

So the point of no return is not a percentage of volume at all. It is **the moment the frontier’s surface area exceeds what your total fleet can physically hold.** That gives:

**f_critical = (A_fleet / A_max)^(3/2)**

| Fleet can hold this share of the maximum frontier | Loses the geometry at |
| --- | --- |
| 5% | **1.1%** |
| 10% | **3.2%** |
| 25% | **12.5%** |
| 50% | **35.4%** |
| **54%** | **39.7%** |
| 75% | 65% |

**Your 40% works.** It requires a Coalition able to hold **roughly 54% of the maximum possible frontier**, which is an enormous, near-total-mobilisation military.

**Which is exactly the military you already built.** Hundred-year terms of service, hundreds of carriers, five per company and a hundred original companies, entire species militarised, an academy taking two thousand twelve-year-olds every month. **The 40% figure is not arbitrary. It is a measurement of how completely the COE has militarised itself, and it is the only number in this document that a reader could ever be told.** Somebody in a briefing room says forty percent and it means “we mobilised everything,” and nobody explains it.

### 4d. The ceiling, and this is the part that solves your ending

**The Bloom physically cannot eat the universe, and the reason is not a plot device.**

Dark energy is accelerating cosmic expansion. Beyond the cosmological event horizon, roughly **16.7 billion light years** in comoving distance, galaxies recede faster than light and can never be reached by anything, at any speed up to and including c, ever. The observable universe’s comoving radius is about **46.5 billion light years.**

**Reachable fraction of the observable universe: (16.7 / 46.5)³ = 4.6%**

Of an estimated two trillion galaxies, about **90 billion** are reachable at light speed. Everything else is already gone, permanently, and was gone before anyone started.

**And that is the generous case.** A sublight Bloom does far worse. At 0.1c, crossing the reachable radius takes 167 billion years, against a universe currently 13.8 billion years old with the horizon closing the whole time. **A sublight Bloom is capped at whatever is gravitationally bound to it**, because everything not bound is receding out of reach. The Local Group is bound: about 80 galaxies across roughly 10 million light years. Laniakea is **not** bound and will disperse.

**So a purely biological, sublight Bloom eats one gravitationally bound group of eighty galaxies and is then stranded forever, in a shrinking island, in a universe that is running away from it in every direction.**

### 4e. What this gives you, and it is the unifying theory you said you were missing

**The Qiraki’s weapon does not work.**

It is a perfect weapon at planetary scale, at system scale, at galaxy scale, and it fails completely at the only scale they actually care about, because dark energy is faster than biology. They can gather all the matter in their own bound group and then watch 99.99% of the universe recede beyond reach, forever, at increasing speed, carrying with it every atom they needed for the Big Bang they are trying to cause.

**Which is why they need to feed it faster. Which is why the Bloom needs FTL. Which is why, tonight, there is a compound on a preserve world researching Bloom FTL, and an order to raid it.**

That mission is currently written as the oligarchy protecting a war it profits from. It is also, without anyone in it knowing, **the Coalition accidentally doing the single most important defensive act in the history of the universe, for entirely petty reasons, and then filing it as a distraction op.**

**[CLAUDE, and this is my strongest recommendation in the whole set]** Do not have anyone say this. Not in Book 8, not in Book 12. The reader who does the arithmetic gets it. Everyone on the page thinks they were stealing a hard drive.

## PART 5 — YOUR OPINION QUESTION, ANSWERED HONESTLY

You wrote: *“**After that tho. I’m not sure the story will be fun. Its just more wars.. until the wars eat the universe. And then boom. System box. Ahahshah. Mouahahahsh. Lol. No way.. (you opinion please.)**”*

**Trust the instinct in the first half of that sentence. You already knew.**

**On**** ****“****just more wars until the war eats the universe.****”** You are right and the diagnosis is precise: the stakes go up and the *questions* stay the same. Scale is not escalation. A reader who has watched Trav lose a squad on a moon does not care more because the next thing lost is a galaxy, they care less, because a galaxy is a number and Fracrals was a person. Every book that raises the scale without raising the question spends the reader’s attachment and buys nothing.

**And you do not need it, because you already have an ending, and it is better than the one you were reaching for.** Concept’s locked climax is *a reckoning with an answered request*. Trav asks Zeteii to protect him and their dream completely, and she does it completely, because she loves him without limit and would never do the job by halves. He does not lose control of a weapon. He gets exactly what he asked for. That is an ending about the cost of being loved without limit by something powerful, and it is small, personal, and devastating, and no amount of war can improve it.

**On the system box: no. And I want to be direct about why, because it is the one move that would actually damage what you have built.** Seven books of real graph theory under the rune grammar, real materials science under the engineering, real RPD research under the command arc, real habit-formation research fixing a joke’s timing. The Style Guide’s whole argument is that the version of this story that skips the research is a weaker, more generic book. A system-box reveal retroactively converts all of that into set dressing for a joke. It is the single highest-cost sentence available to you and it buys one laugh.

**What I would keep from tonight, and it is the good part.** The three-way war is genuinely strong, not as an escalation but as a **moral trap**, and it is already load-bearing without you having arranged it:

- **The Bloom** consumes without choosing.

- **The Qiraki** will convert all matter, including themselves, to protect something they believe in.

- **Zeteii**, in your locked ending, converts everything outside the pact into fuel and machine to protect someone she loves.

**Zeteii’s ending and the Qiraki’s plan are the same act.** Same method, same totality, same willingness to spend the universe. The only difference is who was asked. Your hero’s best friend independently arrives at the enemy’s solution, out of love rather than doctrine, and the book does not have to say so because a reader who has read both will not be able to unsee it.

**That is your unifying theory.** You said the shape was building and you were not sure what it was. It is that the answer to a thing that consumes everything, arrived at by a species that hates it and by a mind that loves one person, is identical, and being right about your reasons does not change the outcome by one atom.

That does not need more wars. It needs the wars you already have, and one ending you already wrote.

## PART 6 — SOURCES AND WHERE THE REAL SCIENCE STOPS

Per the standing discipline, the break is named rather than blurred.

**Real, load-bearing, verifiable:** exponential and frontier-limited spread geometry; the surface-area-to-volume defence problem; accelerating expansion and the cosmological event horizon at roughly 16.7 Gly comoving; the observable universe at 46.5 Gly comoving; the reachable fraction at roughly 4.6%; gravitational binding of the Local Group and non-binding of Laniakea; galaxy counts between 2×10¹¹ and 2×10¹² depending on the survey.

**Where it stops.** Any spread velocity at or above c, the gate network, and anything Sovereign or Heirloom grade. Bloom FTL, if it is ever achieved on the page, is a **deliberate, named break** and should read as different in kind from everything below it, not as a smooth continuation. That is precisely what makes it worth a mission.

**One recommendation on that.** Do not let the Bloom get FTL. The most interesting version is that the Qiraki spend the entire series trying and failing to give it to them, and that the Coalition keeps accidentally stopping them while thinking about something else.
