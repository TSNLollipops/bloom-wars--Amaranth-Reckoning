## PART A — WHAT THIS ERA IS

Trav is nineteen at the start and twenty-seven at the end. He holds no command for any of it. He is a pilot, D-rank rising to C, in a Party of five aboard a carrier that is one of five in a company nobody would put a house name on.

**The era's question:** what do you do when you are very good at a job and the job keeps asking you to do things that do not add up.

**The era's answer, and it is not a good one:** you obey, you feel wrong, you cannot say why, and you get better at the job.

That is the whole shape. Era III is where responsibility starts. Era II is seven books of a young man being extremely competent inside a machine he has no ability to examine, and the reader assembling the pattern he cannot.

**Register, locked.** Gritty, gory, gorgeous, all at once, with real weight given to landscape and to threat rather than one rushed to reach the other. This is the first era where the Bestiary and the Bioterror Bank actually get used. Neither has appeared on the page at all through Books 1 to 6, and Book 7 spent one live contact. **Era II is where the descriptive banks finally do the job they were built for**, and that is worth saying plainly so a later pass does not read seven books of unused colour palettes as an oversight.

**The two-team structure is not a roster gimmick.** Team One does not survive its first arc. Team Two carries the era. That was an intentional structural choice in Concept and it reads as one on the page: the reader learns five names, learns their jokes, watches them die inside one book, and spends the next six books with people who arrived to fill the holes.

### BOOK 9 — *Book 9, "The Second Voice"*

**Working title [CLAUDE].** Alternates: "Free to Speak," "One of Several," "The Upgrade."

**Spine.** Zeteii individuates. Vrassik arrives. Trav becomes, for the first time on the page, a person other people find funny.

**Emotional target.** Delight, and it has to be real delight, not relief. Era II is grim for six of its seven books and this is the one where the reader is allowed to enjoy the company. That contrast is the whole reason Book 12 works.

**Act 1, Ch.1-12. The upgrade.**

Nine missions of accumulated points, on the military-era formula, buy the **C-grade Linktier**. Trav has been idly wanting it since the Book 7 epilogue, when he wondered about connecting with Zeteii properly right after an unrelated weapon-grade upgrade. He still does not know what it does. **He is not told, and he does not ask, and the reader who read the Concept lock is screaming.**

**The individuation itself, drafting rule.** Not a transformation scene. Not a light show. Locked physical portrait: she is a single point of light that moves as though it is looking at things, quick flares when delighted, a held narrowed glow when calculating. **What changes at individuation is not her appearance, it is her latency.** She stops waiting for a gap in conversation, because waiting for one has never once occurred to her as necessary, and that was already true and is now audible. The first sign anybody has of what happened is that she interrupts Reqa.

**Reqa notices first**, exactly as she noticed the anomalous test spikes at Devereux-Kastel. She does not say anything about it for two chapters and then says the wrong thing.

**Act 2, Ch.13-23. Team Four.**

Vrassik and four of his academy Party post in through the regular ranks. **They are not fast-tracked, they are simply good, and the difference is visible in their gear.** Vrassik is on the Warlance by now, C-tier, coil-accelerated, and he is proud of it in the way of someone who bought it himself over years.

**Krethis's payoff, [PROPOSED], closes DEC-04.** Her resentment is six academy books old and has never been stated or resolved. It resolves here by being **said once, plainly, at the worst possible moment, and then not resolving.**

The moment: a shipwide broadcast of a Synker match, the pre-war sport still running as entertainment, and Trav is on it in an old recording from Devereux-Kastel because MeltaDread's own publicity office pulled the file. Krethis watches it with everyone else. Afterwards, in a corridor, she tells him the whole sport was built out of a Hiopi survival ritual by people who wanted the feeling without the reason, and that he is the best there has ever been at a thing that was never his, and that she is going to keep working with him because the job is the job. **She is not wrong about any of it.** Concept's own lock says the pilot sport was directly inspired by witnessing a Hiopi city-fight and built to import genuine stakes into a species that had made its own combat sport consequence-free.

Trav has no answer. He is not built to have one. He says something short and mechanical and she accepts it because she was not asking for an answer.

**They never discuss it again and they work together for twenty years.** That is the resolution. **[CLAUDE] A permanent condition stated once is a resolution. A permanent condition never stated is a dropped thread. The difference is one conversation and this is it.**

**Act 3, Ch.24-36. The multi-pack purge.**

Locked at beat level in Concept as a second D-grade-era mission: a purge where the team runs into multiple separate packs of single-organism Bloom threats on top of the main objective, giving the Munti real sustained disintegrator-beam work.

**Built out, [CLAUDE]:** the objective is a gate-adjacent refinery on a matterworld already stripped to bedrock, the kind of place with as many people living in the spaceport's shadow economy as living groundside properly. The main body has been cleared. What is left is the pack problem, which is a genuinely different tactical shape from a swarm: **swarm-false units, one large specimen at a time, seven separate encounters, each of which has to be finished before the next is found.** Chitin and mineral palette, matte ambers and iron-oxide rust, dry rather than wet, against a world that is already the colour of scraped rock.

Bruvald Ashe's disintegrator does most of the actual killing and gets none of the credit, and the scoring system is why: assists run ten to fifty percent of a kill, disintegrator work on a purge earns kill-adjacent credit, and none of that reads on a leaderboard the way a Meeps's does. **Nobody complains. Somebody notices.**

**The requisition denial, political texture seed.** Team Four has earned a gear upgrade and does not get it. The stated reason is scheduling. The actual reason is that the allocation went to a better-connected battlegroup with a live Warrant and a publicity office of its own, and the mechanism is exactly the locked oligarch resentment toward sport-named formations. **Nobody in the story has the visibility to say so.** Alina says something dry about waste and does not elaborate.

**Act 4, Ch.37-47. The warming.**

Concept's locked warming-up arc pays here. Trav becomes visibly more open and animated, less shy, and it reads as years of practised humour with Reqa finally having somewhere real to go rather than a personality shift out of nowhere.

The mechanism on the page, **[CLAUDE]**: Vrassik is the only person aboard who already knows the funny version of him, because Vrassik spent seven years being the target of it. He walks into a mess hall, says something insulting and specific about Trav's power unit, and the rest of the table watches a quiet man answer instantly and well. After that they expect it. After that he does it.

**Zeteii joins the trash talk.** First time she participates in the friendship rather than being discussed inside it, and she is the only one in the room present in both conversations at once.

**Bench chapters, Book 9:** the Linktier bandwidth ladder and why individuation is hardware. Circle matrix, which Reqa learned from Zeteii over two months in Book 7 and now has to actually use. Coil acceleration on Vrassik's Warlance, which is real coilgun physics and gives Reqa and Perrin Cato a reason to argue in front of everyone.

**Propaganda, Book 9:** Function's licensing and compliance notices appear for the first time in the series, twice, attached to the Linktier upgrade paperwork, and they are helpful and bland and read as customer service.

### BOOK 10 — *Book 10, "The Station"*

**Working title [CLAUDE].** Alternates: "Two Days," "Resettlement."

**Spine.** The space station raid, locked at beat level in Concept, expanded to a book.

**Emotional target.** Exhaustion. Not horror. The book has to be tiring to read in a controlled way, because that is what changes what Trav says at the end of it.

**The locked beats.** 250 soldiers deployed. Roughly a third lost to a B-rank swarm. Two full days of purging afterward. Setting is the side of a massive space station, not a planet, core-wounded, leaking atmosphere, real corpses for anyone willing to look. The station held a large civilian population before the Bloom reached it and will be purged and resettled with displaced refugees afterward, which is a genuinely common situation across COE space. **Zeteii comments on the civilian cost. Trav's response, locked as instinct rather than stated thesis: "doesn't matter, we should have been stronger."**

**Structure, [PROPOSED].**

- **Act 1, Ch.1-12.** The approach, the briefing, the 250. This is the largest deployment Trav has been part of and the book should make the reader feel the administrative weight of it: manifests, deployment order, transponder gates, who eats when. **A visiting political officer is embedded**, per Concept's own political-texture list, and starts turning the operation into content before it happens.
- **Act 2, Ch.13-23.** The B-rank swarm. Eighty-odd people die in under two hours. **Drafting rule: the reader must not be able to keep track, and the prose must not help them.** This is the one place in the era where scale is used deliberately and it works because the reader spends the next twenty-four chapters walking through what it left.
- **Act 3, Ch.24-36. The two days.** The actual body of the book. Purging is not combat. It is slow, methodical, exhausting, and it is done in a place where people lived. Corpses for anyone willing to look for them, and the squad's own varying willingness is characterisation, not commentary. **Frida Green dies on the second day**, to something small, in a corridor that had already been cleared once. Hardass, orphan, no humour, dedicated to the job. There is no time for it. There are forty hours left.
- **Act 4, Ch.37-47.** The resettlement, and the factions competing to be seen doing it. The refugees are a political football and the actual humans involved are treated as a resource to be claimed. Trav and Zeteii both feel it and neither is positioned to do anything. **The political officer's finished piece airs, and it is good, and it is not a lie, and it is unbearable.**

**Where "we should have been stronger" goes.** Not in the corridor with Frida. Not on the station at all. **It goes in the hold on the way back**, to Zeteii, quietly, as the flattest possible answer to a real question, and it is the first time in the era the reader sees the exact shape of what is wrong with him. Consistent with his non-reflective characterisation: a felt conviction rather than a processed one.

**Reqa's jealousy runs underneath the whole book and is never named.** She has sensor returns and no audio. She spends two days watching a squad's telemetry and cannot hear any of it, while an AI in the cockpit can. **[CLAUDE] The thread does not need a scene. It needs the equipment to keep being what it is.**

**The fertility-suppression seed.** Not the realisation, which belongs to Book 19. Just a line of logistics: a quartermaster reconciling the mess-hall additive schedule against the refugee headcount, out loud, in front of Trav, who is thinking about something else.

### BOOK 11 — *Book 11, "Deep Field"*

**Working title [CLAUDE].** Alternates: "Twelve Hours," "The Honest Order."

**Spine.** A long rotation in the deep frontier. The book where the era stops being about individual missions and starts being about the accumulation.

**Emotional target.** Grind, and the specific texture of being twenty-two and good at something and tired.

**Act 1, Ch.1-12. The rotation.** MeltaDread runs deep, hundreds of light-years out, past easy gate reach. Twelve-hour deployment cycles to automatic carrier recall, transponder-gate beacons, the carrier spawning units mid-mission from its own stores. Real texture on how the deployment mechanic actually feels from inside: you are never out for long and you are never anywhere for long.

**Vrassik and Trav deploy together for the first time.** Team Four and Team Two run paired ops for the whole rotation. **This is what "survives alongside him" means and it should be four books' worth of relationship compressed into shared cockpit time, not a scene about friendship.**

**Act 2, Ch.13-23. The honest order.**

The web's fourth instance, and the important one. **An order that is exactly what it says it is.**

A distress call from a Frontier Compact settlement, a straightforward relief and purge, no hidden objective, no data, no theatre. Command gives them everything they ask for. It goes well. Nobody dies. **The civilians are grateful and the gratitude is genuine and uncomplicated.**

**[CLAUDE, and this is the single most structurally necessary chapter in the era]** Concept's own lock says the pattern needs at least one honest order so it reads as a system rather than a simple conspiracy. Without it the reader concludes there is a villain issuing everything, and there is not. The Coalition is three competent institutions optimising three different objectives while answering to a fourth body that is optimising for staying in place. **Some of what comes out of that is just help arriving on time.**

Trav has no reaction to this because he has no framework in which it is remarkable. **The reader has one.**

**Act 3, Ch.24-36. Talia Renn.**

Returning cohort, rotated in from another Guild. The grinder. Everything she has she built through repetition, and she spent five shared academy years, Books 2 through 6, watching things come easily to Trav that cost her real work, and she never stopped showing up for him regardless of how she felt about the unfairness.

She takes a hit that is entirely survivable. The extraction is clean. Restock does what restock does.

**And something does not come back.** Not the limb, the limb is fine. **The sync.** Her wound-emulation tolerance is gone, and it does not return, and there is no medical reason anybody can name. She can sit in a rig and she cannot fly.

**This is the downstream echo the Book 2 wash-out has never had.** A cadet who could not handle wound-emulation left in Book 2 and no book since has referenced it. **A soldier who handled it for a decade stops being able to, and the institution has no category for that either, and rotates her groundside inside eleven days.** Per the locked reassignment rule, a mek whose pilot is lost rotates to maintenance, teaching, or building from scratch, and meks are never long-term useless in the COE. **There is no equivalent sentence anywhere for a pilot who is still alive.**

Talia is not written as broken. She is written as somebody with a real problem and a real plan, and she leaves, and Sana Voight goes with her, and neither of them makes a speech.

**[CLAUDE] Do not have anyone say she was unlucky, and do not have anyone say the system failed her. Have somebody in the next chapter ask who is filling the slot.**

**Act 4, Ch.37-47. The Arcblade.**

Fifteen thousand points, C-tier. Laser-edge, the physical edge replaced by a sustained cutting beam, same matched-pair ambidextrous style, wrist rotation cutting an arc around himself. **The weapon he graduated on was the Pairblade and he has had it for four years.** The change is not a reward for finishing, it is a marker of having left.

**And he does not buy the power unit. Again.** Reqa runs the draw numbers, out loud, in front of Alina, who agrees with her. He buys the blade. **She keeps losing this argument for years and the reader has the tables.**

**Coherence of Process, touchpoint two.** A team loss, mid-arc. Her response is performance-framed and functional, not comforting, alien in register, a Carabil processing grief through systems rather than reaching for reassurance.

**Drafting flag, restated from Character_Sheets because it matters more here than anywhere:** "she says something true" is a placeholder, not content, and reads as an AI-tell if it survives into drafting. **This scene needs an actual specific line, concrete, performance-framed, and it does not exist yet.** Not written here either, because writing a placeholder for a placeholder makes it worse. What it should be *about*, [CLAUDE]: not the person who died. The interval. Something about how long the gap between a call and its consequence is when a carrier is the size of a country, said as a fact about the ship rather than about grief, and landing anyway.

**Bench chapters, Book 11:** thermal management on a sustained-beam weapon and why the Flareblade's vent cycle is not a design flaw. Extraction cycle time, which Ilyen will bring to a much sharper point two books later. Matter budgeting across a long rotation, which is Reqa's actual daily job and has never been on the page.

### BOOK 12 — *Book 12, "The Siege"*

**Working title [CLAUDE].** Alternates: "The Long Way Home," "Escort."

**This is the era's centrepiece and it is locked at beat level in Concept as deliberately coliseum-length**, multiple distinct beats rather than one battle chapter stretched thin. It gets the whole book.

**Emotional target.** Awe, then attrition, then silence. The book should end quieter than any other in the era.

**Scale discipline, and it is the hard part.** Gigafish are moon-sized and appear between twenty thousand and thirty-five thousand at once converging on a single life-bearing world, with countless smaller runoff creatures in the same attack. Concept's own instruction: a battle at this size **should still read as small against the Bloom's total capacity**, not as the biggest imaginable fight, one skirmish inside a war being fought at a scale no single battle can represent completely.

**[CLAUDE] The way that gets achieved is not description, it is arithmetic that nobody performs.** The carrier's own tally board is visible in three chapters. The number on it goes up. The number of gigafish does not go down enough for the number on the board to matter. **Nobody comments and the board is never explained.**

**Confirmed beat order, from Concept, with act allocation [PROPOSED]:**

- **Act 1, Ch.1-12.** Roughly half a dozen chapters of sustained monster-wave combat, escort duty against the surge. **The structural vulnerability is the point:** escort mechs are not targeted by the smaller Bloom's instincts the way ship-to-ship weapons are, which makes a pilot doing the job correctly functionally a sitting duck relative to the swarm. Then the Munti-escort mission mid-siege, protecting a Munti team performing actual hull maintenance under fire. Then saving another squad caught badly.
- **Act 2, Ch.13-23.** Three to four chapters of genuine rest and recuperation, earned downtime, not filler. **The reader needs the breath as much as the characters do and this is where Concept explicitly puts it.** Then more escort battles. Then a major swarm falls on the team, and they call for help, and they bail.
- **Act 3, Ch.24-36. The two-part organism.** They are saved, and the thing that nearly killed them has a second phase. It splits into a swarm component and a sessile component, and the sessile latches onto the carrier hull and starts eating through it. A B-grade unit handles the sessile half while Trav's team fights the swarm, **now recontextualised as the creature's own detached former body parts.** They nearly lose their Munti to it. The Munti is called away to fix the hull breach directly, on top of the maintenance job they were already out there for. Genuine overload, nothing resolved cleanly. Fighting through another smaller swarm, the team reaches its objective and completes the repair.

  **Trahsin Hyrs dies in this act.** Peregrine's unit is committed to the sessile half. The Celestial Magi's fall of ice spears kills by unbinding, the target going inert and silent rather than shattering, and **the drain is indiscriminate and does not distinguish ally mech from Bloom.** Trahsin's gunlance is planted, her shield is deployed, she is doing exactly what a Tank does, and she is inside the fall.

  **Craft rule, absolute.** No dialogue about whose fault it is. Peregrine does not apologise on the page. Trav does not absolve him on the page. The next chapter has a job to do and they do it. **The entire subject appears exactly once, four chapters later, as Peregrine asking Alina a question about spear dispersion that is not about spear dispersion, and Alina answering the question he asked.**

- **Act 4, Ch.37-47. The long way home.** A blackhole weapon fires close enough to catch them in the shockwave. The carrier's shields flare. The team loses contact and is knocked adrift into open space.

  **They fly back under their own power, thousands of kilometres, fighting and running the whole way.** Anti-grav flight units, no resupply, no recall beacon in range, twelve-hour cycles meaningless because there is nothing to recall them to.

  **Elodie Dufours dies at roughly chapter 41. Bram Solvig dies at roughly chapter 45.** Both on the flight. Both slowly. Both while Trav is close enough to talk to them and too far to reach.

  **Elodie's death takes the company's memory with it.** She was the daughter of a MeltaDread dreadnought and she always had company trivia, and nobody aboard has ever needed to write any of it down. **Nobody realises this for two books.**

  **Bram was family-oriented.** That is his entire locked characterisation and it is enough. What that means concretely on the page is decided at draft time and should be small, specific, and mentioned before the flight rather than during it.

  **Only Trav and Vrassik make it back.** Locked.

**The last chapter.** Not a debrief. Not a memorial. The hangar, the two of them, and whatever is left of two mechs. **Reqa is already at the bench when he gets there.** She does not ask him anything. This is the turn in their whole relationship and there is no dialogue in it worth quoting here, because the point is that neither of them says anything.

**Zeteii is the one who talks, later, in the cockpit, and she is not comforting him, she is changing the subject, and it works.** Same mechanism as Book 8's first bond beat, deliberately, and by now the reader knows it is the mechanism.

**Yssa's letter arrives during the siege and he opens it in this chapter.** She asks whether the weather where he is has turned.

### BOOK 13 — *Book 13, "Restock"*

**Working title [CLAUDE].** Alternates: "The Fill," "New Names."

**Spine.** Rebuild. Three arrivals, one of whom is Ilyen Pral.

**Emotional target.** Repair, in the mechanical sense and not the emotional one. The book is about a machine being made functional again, and the people are part of the machine, and that is not framed as cruelty because it is not cruelty, it is a war.

**Act 1, Ch.1-12. The fill.**

Fresh cadets arrive weekly to fill losses. That is locked and it is horrifying and nobody in the setting finds it remarkable. Team Two gets **Naomi Castell** and **Suki Arnesen** off the bench and **Ilyen Pral** with her mek Petra Lindqvist.

**Naomi and Suki are a deliberate tactical contrast and the book should field them together immediately.** Naomi holds still at extreme range and lets range do the work, and says so, unbothered by needling about speed. Suki never stops moving, in and out of most weapons' comfortable range on a rapid-evasion float, and taps a rhythm on her own cockpit rail before every mission that the squad has learned to wait for. **Cookies and milk, and a private countdown. Two rituals, two people, in a unit that has just buried four.**

**Ilyen's arrival, drafting rule.** She has not seen Trav in seven years. She is a Munti now and she is very good, and the reposition from Reeps in Book 5 reads in retrospect as the correction it was. **She is not written as arriving to be near him.** She is written as arriving because a Munti slot opened on a Guild with a survivable reputation and she applied for it.

**The five academy tells do not resume.** She is twenty-three, a little over a year younger than him exactly as locked. What is there instead is professional, and closer, and worse.

**Act 2, Ch.13-23. The underdog echo.**

Book 6's underdog trope, a background classmate outshining, has a downstream half and no in-book resolution recorded. **It pays here as Naomi Castell.**

A long-range engagement where the entire plan depends on one shot from a fusion lance at a distance nobody on the team can conceptualise. She takes it. **She is not nervous, she does not need encouragement, and nobody has to tell her she belongs.** The Bloom Eraser is a nickname she gave herself and it is accurate.

**[CLAUDE] The reason this is the right payoff for that trope: the academy version was about somebody being underestimated. The military version is about somebody who stopped caring whether she was.**

**Act 3, Ch.24-36. The farm rumour.**

The farm-world crisis proper belongs to Book 18, and Concept locks that Trav does not see an actual working farm until he is a squad commander. **What Era II gets is the rumour.**

A supply chapter. Processed food, which is what everyone eats and what everyone has always eaten. Elodie Dufours is not there to say the thing she would have said about it. Somebody in the mess mentions that a farm world went dark and that it was the third this year, and the conversation moves on inside four lines because nobody at that table has ever seen a farm and cannot picture what was lost.

**Nobody explains what a farm world is. The reader who has been paying attention to what food is in this setting gets it. The reader who has not gets it in Book 18.**

**Coherence of Process, touchpoint three, and it is the load-bearing one.**

Late pilot arc. **He witnesses her actually think through a crisis.** Not command him through it. Watches her process.

**[CLAUDE] The concrete shape:** a routing decision under a hard clock, made in the Groto, with Trav present for an unrelated reason and unable to leave. The glitter inside her moves faster and denser during a crisis and near-still during quiet, and this is the first time he has seen it move fast. **She talks the whole way through and none of it is for him.** She is not reasoning out loud for anyone's benefit, she is a mainframe running, and she happens to be audible.

He recognises something in how her mind works that his does not have. **He does not have a word for it and he does not go looking for one for another two books.** This is the direct seed of him later seeking her out deliberately once promoted, because he already knows what she does that he cannot.

**Act 4, Ch.37-47.** A clean, competent, unremarkable mission run by a team that is starting to work. **The era needs one of these and this is the only slot for it.** They come back with everyone. Nobody says anything about that either.

**Bench chapters, Book 13:** Munti schematic propagation, which is automatic and instant in the military and was manual in the academy, and Ilyen noticing out loud that the academy version was a training constraint dressed as a rule. Fusion-lance thermal cycling with Naomi. Float-unit calibration with Suki, which is the Year 7 build-it-wrong-in-exactly-the-way-this-pilot-is-wrong idea getting its second and much better outing.

### BOOK 14 — *Book 14, "Alone Yet Not"*

**Working title [CLAUDE].** Taken from the Carabil's own phrase for what a ship-pilot chooses. Alternates: "The Groto," "Asking."

**Spine.** Ilyen nearly dies. Trav asks for help for the first time since Soren Adair. The symbiosis exchange. Vrassik is promoted and Trav inherits the Party.

**Emotional target.** The first time in fourteen books that he looks backward, and it is genuinely difficult for him, and the difficulty is mechanical rather than emotional.

**Act 1, Ch.1-12.** A Party that works. Trav is C-rank and closing on B. Vrassik has been Team Two's Raid Lead since Book 12 and is visibly being groomed for something. **Trav has never once thought about command and the book does not tell you that, it just never comes up.**

**Act 2, Ch.13-23. The near-loss.**

**Locked:** at some point in this stretch Trav nearly loses Ilyen, a real close call, not a near-miss played for cheap tension.

**[CLAUDE] The shape that makes it worst:** it happens because of something Trav does correctly. A Munti's whole job is to be somewhere a downed pilot can be reached from, and protecting the Munti is effectively its own job within a team. He makes a fast, correct, RPD-perfect call that puts him where the fight is and her where the recovery is, and the fight moves. **His instinct was right and the outcome was nearly fatal, and there is no version of the decision he would make differently, and that is the problem.**

She is pulled with seconds on the clock. Quickbox cycle time is the difference and Quickbox is a purchase somebody made two books ago. She restocks. She is fine. **She is completely fine and he cannot stop thinking about it, which has never happened to him before, because he does not process backward.**

**Act 3, Ch.24-36. The Groto.**

He goes to Coherence of Process. **Not assigned, not scheduled, not a counselling session.** He walks to the Groto because he does not know what else to do with a thought that will not move forward.

**The chamber.** Part organic, part structural, her actual physical form fused into the architecture. Less a person standing in a room and more the room being, unmistakably, someone. **Full sensory description of this chamber is still open in Concept and this is the scene that needs it, so it needs building at draft time rather than gesturing at.**

**What he actually asks is not for help.** He is not capable of framing it that way. He asks her a question about her ship. How she senses it. Whether it is like anything.

**And she answers with real curiosity, which is what defines her**, and asks him back, and he explains symbiosis plainly, badly, in his own vocabulary, which is mechanical and short and has no abstraction in it at all.

**The exchange, locked in Concept:** the micro and the macro turning out to describe the same underlying feeling at two different scales. A single blade and a single nervous system against a whole carrier and a Longsight-adjacent collective cognition. **Not stated as a thesis by either of them. Discovered together, in a quiet, curious conversation.**

**Two things happen in this scene that neither of them notices.** He has, for the first time in his life, talked about something that already happened. And she has learned enough to make the later teaching possible, which is the mechanism behind him eventually receiving his own carrier, and which strains the oligarchy's logistics in a real causal way years from now.

**She also does not comfort him and does not mention Ilyen.** She does not know that is what this was about. **She is not wrong not to know.**

**Act 4, Ch.37-47. The vacancy.**

Vrassik is promoted off MeltaDread's Team Two and into the Guildmaster track.

**[CLAUDE, smoothing a jump the rank ladder makes abruptly]** RANK's table has him going Raid Lead to Guildmaster directly, which is a large step from five pilots to a battlegroup of five carriers plus escorts. The smoothing that costs nothing: he goes as **Hand-designate on a sister carrier in the MeltaDread clan**, deputy to a sitting Guildmaster, which is a real posting, is still a Hand-side title, and takes him off the Party roster immediately. He reaches Guildmaster proper in Era III. **Flagged as a Claude-lane addition to a locked ladder rather than a change to it.**

**Trav inherits the vacancy at B-rank.** Not a transfer. Not an institutional fast-track. The same self-made mechanism that made Vrassik a CO in the first place, repeating.

**Vrassik advocating for him, or simply not standing in the way, is the natural beat**, and the version that fits both characters is that he does it in the loudest possible manner and pretends it was a joke.

**The scoring change is the first thing Trav actually feels.** Per-mission points roughly triple against the current formula value, and a new bonus is added for every team member who returns alive. **He feels the reward structure change before anybody explains it to him**, and the era ends on him looking at a number he does not yet understand the implications of.

**Era II closes here.** He is twenty-seven, B-rank, on an Arcblade and a power unit two tiers below what anyone in his band carries, holding a Party of five that includes the woman who has been his hands since the day they were matched and the woman who spent the academy years reading him more accurately than anybody alive and was never once noticed doing it, and he has never been responsible for anybody.
