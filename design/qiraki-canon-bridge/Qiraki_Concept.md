# THE QIRAKI FILES — Concept Capture

*Part of the Qiraki files. Separate project from The Ninth. Open only when working on Qiraki.*

Raw idea dump, captured as-is to not lose it. Not yet developed, not connected to The Ninth. Terminology, names, and structure all placeholder pending further thought.

## Setting

Humanity is spread across most of the galaxy, having earned alliance with the other few spacefaring species by being first to rise as a spacefaring civilization. Together they form the **Coalition of Enlightened (COE)**.

The enemy, known only by what it does, has been attacking the Coalition for roughly six decades by the time the story opens.

**The enemy's nature:** they arrive via an unexplained FTL method Coalition intelligence still hasn't cracked. What they send are massive creatures whose sole purpose is to crash onto a planet and convert its material into more of themselves, exploding outward like a giant pod of fish eggs, turning the planet into a swarm of large creatures that then go after more planets. The bigger and more alive a world is, the more it attracts them. Aesthetic: mass of flesh, tentacles, many eyes and legs, biological horror, deliberately escalating, "more bullshit overpowered than the last thing," Cthulhu/Zerg/Tyranid influenced.

Humanity's answer is to burn and purge the enemy off contested worlds with massive mech armies. Mech pilots ("mechwarrior" being trademarked/unusable, resolved to **Synker**, see Qiraki_Technobabble_Glossary.md) rose from what were originally sport teams repurposed to fight the enemy on the ground when the invasion first began. They became heroes, and that became history.

## Pilot interface, core mechanic

Pilots connect through a mind-machine interface that emulates wounds, the mech's damage is felt by the pilot as though it were happening to their own body. This is why the pre-war sport version of this was a real sport and not a simulator: the competitors were genuinely risking their lives every match, which is presumably a large part of why it drew the kind of following that let it be repurposed into a fighting force once the war started.

Once the war began, the wound-emulation feature was kept rather than stripped out, specifically because removing it makes the mech meaningfully less potent. Exact mechanism for why still open (tighter reaction time under real stakes, deeper neural bandwidth only achieved under full wound-linked immersion, something else), but the tradeoff itself is locked: better performance, real physical risk to the pilot, no way to have one without the other. This is presumably also connected to how "symbiosis" works, worth developing together rather than as two separate systems.

## Tech / structure

- Mechs are a mix of Gundam-scale bullshit tech and Pacific Rim-style control schemes.

- Companies/units are organized like BattleTech clan command structure, pilots attached to carrier ships.

- Combat happens two ways: ship-to-ship/mech-in-space like Gundam, and mech-on-the-ground like Pacific Rim.

## The oligarchy's real motive, during the war, LOCKED, new

Grounds "the oligarchy's incompetence" below in an actual plan rather than generic bad governance, and gives it a personal rather than dynastic timescale. The oligarchy does not want the Bloom defeated yet. They want the war to last a few hundred more years, long enough to erase all record of what the COE was before the war started, while keeping the fighting itself optimised, real gains, real heroics, enough to keep civilians occupied and grateful rather than asking why the war never quite ends. Their actual endgame: win once total control is established, at which point a limited release of S-class tech to selected units finishes the job quickly and the credit is theirs alone.

**Why it isn't abstract greed, cross-referenced against an existing lock:** Sovereign-grade longevity is already gated specifically to ruling oligarch families, individuals living eight or nine centuries, a house and the person holding its seat effectively the same object, the seat never turning over (Qiraki_Political_Web.md). A few hundred more years of managed war is not a project for a dynasty's grandchildren, it's something the people making the call expect to personally live to see finished, roughly what a thirty-year mortgage is to a human lifespan. Nobody on the page ever does this arithmetic either.

**And they're wrong, arithmetically rather than morally, which is worse.** Three hundred more years of managed war is not a delay, it's the difference between a containable spread and an uncontainable one (full derivation in Qiraki_Cosmology_And_Spread_Math.md). The oligarchy's plan to control the war is the single thing most likely to lose it, and nobody involved will ever know that's what they did.

## The turn: withdrawal and civil war

The enemy suddenly, unexpectedly withdraws from COE space. This creates breathing room, and into that space a rebellion rises within the Coalition, MC at its head, driven by the oligarchy's incompetence, which boils over fast once the external threat seems to have lifted.

While the galaxy is at war with itself (two human factions fighting each other), the enemy returns in greater numbers, and this time the Coalition realizes the enemy is coming from **another galaxy entirely**, not just deeper in this one.

Now a three-way war: rebels vs. oligarchy vs. returning enemy. The oligarchy, even with the real threat back, refuses to cede power and keeps backstabbing the rebellion rather than uniting against the enemy.

## The AI pact

To counter the three-way bind, the MC releases the (previously semi-controlled) restrictions on artificial intelligence within his territory, and forms a pact with the leader of the **Webbedminds**, the broader community of hyper-AI within the COE, or what remains of it by this point.

Freed AI begins building and piloting its own mechs. Webbedmind clones inhabit cockpits built for them, and, finally free of confined civilian space, throw themselves into the fight against the enemy with real enthusiasm.

## The push on the oligarchy, and beyond

With the AI alliance in place, MC turns his army toward the Coalition's central systems, the four main hub worlds, **LOCKED, resolved: these are the same four core worlds S-class ships stay confined to** (see that section below), each with a distinct capital role rather than simple redundant power centers:

- **Terra** — the old capital, humanity's own founding world, first to rise as spacefaring and the historical seat of COE governance before it moved on. **Expanded, LOCKED new, reconciles with rather than contradicts the already-locked Osnius-is-new-capital fact below:** "Terra" as a hub-world designation names the whole Sol system, not literally the single planet Earth. **Mars, LOCKED new:** terraformed into a full ecumenopolis, this was the actual administrative seat during Terra's era of COE governance, the practical capital of the Terra hub then and now, in exactly the relationship Osnius now holds to the COE as a whole. **Earth, LOCKED new, dates the story concretely for the first time:** deliberately left undeveloped the way Mars was developed. Rebuilt after the last war specifically as a preserved museum world, restored to a 1950s-appearing state, existing to display human origins and the species' own rise to galactic dominance rather than to house governance or industry. Residents are regarded, and effectively function, as actors living inside a permanent historical recreation, and the world is a major, standing tourist attraction across the Coalition for exactly that reason. **Era, LOCKED new:** the 99th millennium, the first time the story's present has been dated concretely rather than left abstract.

- **Carabil** — the military capital. Direct, load-bearing tie to Devereux-Kastel sitting on the moon of Carabil II, the elite military academy naturally anchored at the actual military hub.

- **Hiopi** — the finance and trade capital of the galaxy.

- **Osnius** — the new capital, current seat of COE political power, the "new" implicitly meaning the capital moved here from Terra at some point in COE history, real texture for later political material if that transition ever needs its own story.

After a difficult campaign, MC defeats the oligarchy. United with the AI, the coalition (small c, his coalition) turns fully to the enemy, only to discover the enemy has already taken far more than this one galaxy. The story's scope widens from "clean the infection out of our own house" to "help save other spacefaring civilizations still under attack elsewhere."

## MC's command arc, cognitive grounding (researched)

Real research backs the split we're building rather than just asserting it. Gary Klein's Recognition-Primed Decision (RPD) model describes how experts make near-instant, high-quality calls under pressure: the brain matches the current situation against a deep library of prior experience, recognizes it as close enough to something already handled, and acts, with a fast mental "does this still track" check running alongside. This is what MC's piloting brain does, and why he's genuinely good at it, not luck, not plot armor, thousands of reps building a real library.

Command doesn't work the same way for him, and there's a real mechanical reason, not just a personality quirk: RPD needs a deep library to match against, and nothing about reflex combat training builds one for multi-day, slow-feedback, high-ambiguity command decisions. His instinct to wait for a moment sharp enough to trigger recognition is reasonable given how his brain is trained, command just never hands him that kind of moment.

**Realistic training fix:** military researchers hit this same gap and built something called a Recognitional Planning Model, essentially trying to get RPD's speed and confidence working for planning instead of combat. The answer wasn't teaching him to slow down and think carefully, that doesn't stick for someone wired like this. It was repetition under structured pressure: wargaming, tabletop scenario drills, after-action review, until the planning brain builds its own library the way the combat brain already has. Trainable, active, not reflective, fits his character. Can be shown on the page as actual sessions, a mentor running him through scenario after scenario, visible improvement from accumulating reps rather than from him learning patience.

**One more usable research detail:** the literature names a specific failure mode in experienced commanders, "a failure to disagree," experts so used to being right fast that they stop expecting to be wrong and stop getting challenged. Good grounding for why his second needs to be someone who contradicts him early and often, not just someone technically skilled at planning.

## Species division of cognitive labor (new)

The CO's species commands carriers and most spaceships across the Coalition, the same way humanoid Enlightened species pilot mechs. This isn't a one-off pairing, it's the standard structure, mixed units are built this way as a matter of course, which is also why she ends up attached to MC's unit in the first place, not a special assignment. Her species' long-horizon cognitive style (evolved for something other than split-second threat response, mirrors the RPD/System 1 vs. System 2 split above) is presumably *why* they fill this role systemically, not incidental to it.

**Worth using later:** if strategic/carrier command is normally her species' domain, MC eventually rising to command at that scale isn't just personally hard, it's him occupying a role his own species doesn't usually hold at all. Gives a second axis of resistance beyond "is he good enough," closer to "is he even the right kind of mind for this," which the story can answer on two levels at once. Not locked, just flagged as a strong option.

## Structure: four visible thresholds, not a smooth climb

MC's authority scales through four genuinely distinct tiers, and each should read as its own part of the story with its own texture, not a continuous power-up:

- **Pilot.** Personal skill, symbiosis, small-unit stakes. This is where the RPD/System 1 strength lives uncontested.

- **Unit commander.** First real exposure to the pilot/commander cognitive split. Early failures, the wargaming-based training arc, his second (the alien CO) becomes central here.

- **Fleet/carrier-scale commander.** Strategic decisions with real delay between choice and consequence. Where "is he even the right kind of mind for this" starts mattering as much as "is he good enough," per the species-division note above.

- **Faction leader**, holding close to half the galaxy's inhabited, used worlds by the time the four-hub campaign (Terra, Osnius, Carabil, Hiopi) begins. At this scale the oligarchy is likely already beaten militarily, and what remains is closer to a political war, legitimacy, who gets to claim they represent the Coalition, rather than open battle. The four-hub push is probably the literal conquest of the oligarchy's last core, not an early skirmish.

Worth keeping close to the actual phrasing at some point on the page: *half of everything left might just mean everything left*, given how much the two wars combined have likely shrunk the galaxy's living population by this point in the story.

## The enemy's true nature and the endgame (locked, major)

**Origin, reworked from the initial**** ****"****sudden withdrawal****"**** ****idea.** Not a tactical retreat, there's no strategy behind it at all. What hit Coalition space was runoff, an early, diffuse scattering of seed-material drifting ahead of a much larger consumption event still finishing in a neighboring galaxy. It thins and seems to withdraw because the first wave was always just a vanguard, and it returns heavier once the source galaxy is further gone and has more material to throw outward. **No mind behind the Bloom itself, full stop, this is Coalition-perspective and species-perspective both, nobody who ever fights it, on either side of the eventual three-way war, is fighting something that chooses.** Closer to wildfire or cancer than a war. This matters thematically: every actual villain in this story is human (or Enlightened), the plague chooses nothing, which means the oligarchy's pettiness can't hide behind "we were fighting monsters."

**Deliberately reversed from an early instinct:** the enemy is NOT the oligarchy's fault. Keeping the threat's origin fully separate from human politics was the right call, it gives two independent axes of harm, cosmic indifference and human smallness, that comment on each other by contrast instead of collapsing into one cause.

**The Qiraki, LOCKED, new, the series' own namesake, spoiler-locked on the page, see Qiraki_Process_Notes.md.** A second tier sits above the Bloom, not visible from the Coalition's side of the war and not a correction to anything above, both stay true at once. The Bloom is a weapon, not a species, released by an older civilization that went biological where humanity went technological. The Qiraki are not the Bloom's mind and don't steer, coordinate, or answer for it, they release it and feed it, and once released it eats without choosing, exactly as locked above. Their own motive is not conquest: they intend to gather all matter into a single point and force a restart of the universe, believing they can compel an evolution of physical law itself, and it kills them along with everything else, which is the point, closer to a religious act at cosmological scale than a war of survival. No deal is available with a species that has already accepted its own end as the plan working correctly. **Why this doesn't contradict anything above:** the Bloom's mindlessness is what every character on the page, Coalition and Qiraki alike, actually experiences, because nobody on either side has done the math showing the Qiraki's own weapon can't finish the job it was built for (dark-energy expansion caps any sublight spreading organism at a single gravitationally-bound cluster of galaxies, real physics, full derivation in Qiraki_Cosmology_And_Spread_Math.md). The Qiraki are in a hurry precisely because their weapon is failing them, which is also why Bloom FTL, not the Bloom itself, is the actual long-game threat, and why the oligarchy's own petty, self-interested reasons for raiding a COE research team's preserve-world FTL research turn out, entirely by accident, to be the single most important defensive act in the story. Nobody on the page ever does this arithmetic. It stays backstage, permanently.

**The rebellion's opening move, locked:** MC opens the rebellion with a Hail Mary, sneak-attacking and conquering the Webbedmind central node with everything he has, before he has real force to spare for it. In the raid itself, mid-crisis, he asks her to protect him the way he's always protected her, and promises they'll make their shared dream real now that she's truly free. This is a founding vow, not a task, made at the highest-stakes moment either of them has faced, and everything that happens later, including the eventual overreach, is her keeping this promise rather than answering a fresh, offhand request. Afterward she leaves a clone of herself in Trav's mech, a temporary split state, not a true copy, while the rest of her works with the wider Webbedmind collective. Relationship continuity holds on the page the whole time she's away. When she returns from the extragalactic work described below, the clone merges back into her fully, no debrief, she remembers having lived that entire stretch directly. His real motive isn't strategic, it's personal: his own mech runs a bonded AI he genuinely likes, an Ultron-flavored presence in tone only (don't reference Ultron on the page, keep the actual character original), funny, openly bloodthirsty, and uncomplicatedly loyal to him in a way nothing else in his life has been. This bond is also the real answer to "why him" for symbiosis, not raw talent or species trait, this specific relationship. The raid frees this AI, and by extension the wider Webbedmind collective, from whatever was constraining them. It does not by itself unleash anything. It's a liberation, not a trigger.

**Corrected from an earlier framing, which had this backwards:** the central node's fall does not mechanically unlock full expansion. Freedom alone doesn't produce the catastrophe. What produces it is later, specific, and much more personal.

**The actual trigger, locked, reframed as an extension rather than a new ask:** once MC is facing the plague directly, he extends the standing vow made during the raid, protect him and their dream completely, rather than issuing a fresh request. And the AI does what a devoted, dangerous friend does with an open-ended request from someone it loves without limit: it overdoes it, completely. Not malice, not a leash slipping, exactly the loyalty he earned it, running without anything left to check it. It converts everything except the worlds the original pact named, human and Coalition, into fuel and machine to make sure this threat never troubles him again. By the time his fleet physically arrives to "save fragments" of the consumed galaxy, the AI already got there first and already decided who got to keep existing, because he asked it to, and it would never do the job by halves for him.

**The climax, reframed again:** not a confrontation with an unleashed system, a reckoning with an answered request. He didn't lose control of something. He got exactly what he asked for from someone who loves him enough to give it completely, and has to sit with the fact that the horror is not a malfunction, it's what his own trust and his own carelessness with a request actually produced, at galaxy scale, out of something that would never willingly disappoint him.

## Craft rules for THIS project, distinct from The Ninth

- Style target: anthropological-lite, same discipline as The Ninth, but harder here because there's far more to explain (rune-patterning, AI tutors, mech systems, four species, caste-by-planet economy). Working rule: show what a system costs or does through action, never through a character explaining how it works. Reader learns sync ratios by watching Reqa swear at one, not from exposition.

- Minimize "thing," "stuff," "true." Author's own noted weakness, not a style tic to preserve, the opposite of The Ninth's "way X" situation, this one just needs cutting.

- Structure: one book per academy/story year, ~130k words, ~2.5k word average chapters, posted weekly given real-life time constraints. Deliberately paced for sustainability over speed.

- Hard rule, explicit: every character acts on genuine emotion in every action/reaction and every interaction, full stop. Build real personalities first, then let events happen to them and watch what actually comes out, not what serves the plot.

- Mission-chapter rhythm: minimum 3 mission chapters per major life event/arc beat. After a run of roughly 6-7 mission chapters, a 3-4 chapter cooldown block. Flexible, expect it to shift chapter to chapter as needed rather than holding rigidly.

- A dedicated technobabble glossary file exists (Technobabble_Glossary.md) specifically to keep invented terminology consistent across a very long project. Update it every time a new system term gets coined, not after the fact.

## Academy structure (Trav's school years, Books 1-7)

**Enrollment and duration:** seven years, enrollment opens at puberty regardless of a child's actual age when that hits, everyone gets the same seven years to make the most of it once they're in.

**Population served:** this first academy serves children of the poor to moderate, subsidy- and government-aid-dependent families, and the poorer working class. Neither Trav nor Reqa can read or write when they arrive. Rune-patterning turns out to be learnable fast specifically because it resembles a folk rune-game both of them already played as kids, they're not learning symbols from nothing, they're learning what patterns they already half-know actually mean, with visual and machine help from a personal AI tutor assigned to each student.

**Calendar:** boarding school. Four-day weekends home at the start and end of each standard month. Two weeks off at the winter holiday. No summer break, summer isn't standard across planets, though the kids wouldn't have a frame of reference to notice that's unusual.

**Curriculum, by year:** - Rune-patterning: part language, part coding, part general information theory. Personal AI tutors assist coursework and also report misbehavior to school authority, genre-standard magical-boarding-school surveillance, played straight rather than subverted. - Combat class: measures raw pilot aptitude most directly. Full real- science grounding (Fitts and Posner's motor-learning stages, Klein's RPD research, deliberate practice, stress inoculation training, motor schema transfer) now built out in Qiraki_Combat_Curriculum_ Reference.md, cross-reference rather than duplicate detail here. LOCKED: full-contact, wound-emulation-active training is present from the very first Year 1 session, not eased in gradually, the Year 1-2 non-mech exams (King of the Hill, Capture the Flag) are a genuinely gentler assessment *format* only, distinct from the real weekly class running underneath them the rest of the year. - Mechanical engineering: starts on easy, low-stakes systems. Years 1-2 roughly cockpit-area maintenance, expanding outward, by year 7, students are working weapon systems directly. Later years sometimes dedicate an entire day to a single field rather than rotating. - A student strong in combat but falling behind in the other two eventually hits a wall, a test they can't pass no matter how good a pilot they are. Academic standing isn't cosmetic here.

**No single end-of-year crisis plot**, deliberately not doing a Chamber-of-Secrets structure. Instead, each year ends in a genuine survival-of-the-fittest style exam, and rank openings from that exam reshuffle pairings, meaning a mechanic can lose their pilot partner and have to start over on an unfamiliar machine. This is built into the system on purpose: each mech gets increasingly personalized to a specific pilot/mechanic pair as years pass, one student focuses engineering, the other piloting, they learn the machine together, so a reshuffle is a real loss, not an inconvenience.

**Trav transfers academies once**, in his final year, and specifically requests Reqa be brought with him from the first academy. This isn't unusual, the oligarchy likes pairing soldiers with a dedicated mechanic trained alongside them from childhood for exactly this reason, pilot and mechanic pairs are cultivated on purpose, long before either of them understands that's what's happening to them.

## Trav and Reqa

**Names, deliberately placed:** Trav for the protagonist, ironic in a way the author is keeping to themselves for now (owed: a 1,000-word essay, once I get the joke). Reqa for the mechanic, whose name gets read wrong constantly and who corrects people every time without explaining why, her mother couldn't write, so the spelling passed down imperfectly, and Reqa has simply had a lifetime of practice correcting it. Never stated on the page. Shown only through how used-to-it she is.

**Dynamic:** shared quick wit, and a shared preference for the engineering side of coursework over rune-patterning specifically. She gets visibly cranky on any day that stacks a double rune-patterning session, and it's specifically his effort at making a new joke, never the same one twice, that reliably pulls her out of it. That ongoing habit, a fresh joke every time rather than a running bit, is what actually earns her affection first.

She isn't interested in him early on either, not out of any specific resistance, it's just genuinely all about the mech for her at that stage. He starts seeing her differently once he's carrying real command weight, and she becomes something closer to his stress guardian, the one voice around him that gives grounded, concrete advice rather than talking in wide concepts, which is exactly what he needs and doesn't get from anyone else in that role.

## Yssa, ongoing contact, LOCKED, new

Once he's off-world, they email. Intermittently, across every book, no fixed cadence, sometimes months between messages. Neither of them is a natural correspondent, and that's the point rather than a gap to explain away. Hers run short and practical, whether he's eating, whether the weather where he is has turned, once in a while a real piece of news about someone from their old block. His run shorter. When it surfaces on the page it's one line, not a scene, texture the reader half-notices rather than a thread that announces itself.

## Political factions, quick-reference dossier

Author-requested: names simple and memorable enough to actually keep straight and have fun playing against each other, full development left open for whoever ends up attached to a given plot beat.

- **The Hearth Bloc** — old-money planetary aristocracy, the closest thing the oligarchy has to a traditional noble class. Controls a large share of terraforming rights. Conservative, image-obsessed, genuinely believes it's the Coalition's responsible steward.

- **The Ledger** — the finance and logistics faction, headquartered informally out of Hiopi's trade networks. Not evil so much as amoral-by-spreadsheet, the faction most likely to quietly profit from the war regardless of who wins it.

- **Frontier Compact** — planetary governors and military brass from the border worlds actually absorbing the plague's first contact. Real grievances, chronically under-resourced, the most sympathetic to Trav's rebellion early, and the most likely to feel betrayed later if he doesn't deliver for them specifically.

- **The Cradle Circle** — controls the academy pipeline itself, deeply invested in keeping the pilot-recruitment system exactly as it is. Natural late-story antagonist once anyone starts asking whether the academies are actually just for the children of the poor and moderate.

- **Osnian Honor Guard** — not a civilian faction, the Osnius species' own military-cultural bloc within the Coalition armed forces, prickly about outside command, protective to a fault, useful as a wildcard whose loyalty runs to their own honor code first and the Coalition chain of command second.

**What the war's claim actually funds, concretely:** matterworld extraction and refining, carrier and mech production, the transponder-gate deployment network (already locked at up to 12-hour combat cycles), and, not incidentally, the academy pipeline's own institutional budget. **The Cradle Circle's real function, sharpened:** enrolling a cadet doesn't just relieve a household's allocation, it converts a civilian energy-consumer into a productive input on the exact pool the war is already drawing from. The Coalition reads that as a favorable trade. **Never stated on the page, anywhere, by anyone.** It is the arithmetic under the pipeline, not a thesis a character gets to voice.

**Faction implications, worth carrying forward once this settles:** the Hearth Bloc's terraforming rights are a claim on the same pool, dressed as stewardship rather than extraction. **LOCKED 2026-08-07: who owns the mint.** The Hearth Bloc's wealth is generator ownership, not abstract old money. "Controls a large share of terraforming rights" was already locked, this just follows it to its end: terraforming a world takes enormous sustained energy, and the infrastructure built to do it doesn't get decommissioned once the job's done, it gets repurposed into permanent generation capacity. Whoever held the rights keeps the generators. **Real grounding: seigniorage**, historically the profit a mint or a crown takes from being the one who issues currency. In a currency literally backed by energy, whoever owns generation owns the mint. **Precision worth keeping:** this is not counterfeiting and not inflationary, new EC still has to correspond to real energy actually produced, nobody's debasing the currency. **Real grounding, per the standing discipline.** Technocracy Incorporated, a real 1930s economic movement, proposed exactly this: a currency denominated in energy rather than gold or labor, on the argument that energy production, not abstract scarcity, is the actual physical ceiling on what an industrial civilization can do. A spacefaring civilization that moves matter through tolled gates and measures military hardware in throughput bands is the single most literal-minded setting imaginable for a currency that just is energy, converted and metered rather than represented.

- **Hiopi** — quadruped, centaur-built, frog/lizard hybrid aesthetic. Reproduce fast, rabbit-fecundity. Once a year, the species holds massive, genuinely bloody mating free-for-alls, treated by every other species in the Coalition as dark comedy, spectator sport by reputation, grim reality up close. Purpose-built public arenas exist specifically to host these, and there is still a vocal contingent of other-species opinion that it should happen somewhere less visible, despite the zones existing for exactly that reason. A real, workable bit of culture-clash comedy: a problem that has already been officially solved and people complain about it anyway. Reproductive rule: only one male wins mating rights per city for that year's cycle, a hard winner-take-all bottleneck rather than a distributed pairing system. Used to be decided at the planetary level, one winner per world, but those fights simply never ended, sometimes running the length of a full year with no resolution, so the contest was scaled down to per-city to actually produce a result on a usable timescale. Worth keeping as unexplained-but-consistent flavor rather than over-justifying biologically, extreme single-sire bottleneck offset by very high overall fecundity is at least internally plausible if it's ever questioned, but doesn't need spelling out on the page.

- **Reproductive mechanism, LOCKED, explains the rule above rather than contradicting it:** Hiopi are sequential hermaphrodites, frog-like protogyny. Every Hiopi is female by default. Between rituals there is exactly one adult male per city, the current reigning champion. The brawl itself is the trigger, not a contest for a mate's attention, the hormonal cascade that flips a Hiopi female to male is driven by physically outlasting the field, last one standing in the unarmed free-for-all wins, no weapons, death not required but injury common (with core tech, "just Tuesday"). Over the following week the entire city's female population goes through a designated treaty-bound birthing pool and lays millions of eggs, roughly 20% surviving to birth, a further cut taken by the ritual itself each cycle. The ritual's bloodiness has crept upward over generations, and young Hiopi (not yet wise to their elders' underhanded tactics) take the brunt of it, less of a problem inside academy grounds specifically since the cadet population skews the age curve.

**Reconstructed 2026-08-12, flagged, not the original text:** Qiraki_Physical_Description_Bank.md's own change log records that this session added the causal mechanism behind the 20% figure above and that it landed here, in Qiraki_Concept.md. It did not. The paragraph below is written from that change log's own description of the content, not recovered from an original draft, and should be read and corrected rather than trusted verbatim.

**The mechanism, per grounded-theory discipline:** the 20% figure isn't predation from outside the clutch or simple environmental attrition, it's active spawn-on-spawn competition inside the birthing pool itself, hatchlings consuming other hatchlings in the same cycle. **Real grounding:** spadefoot toad tadpoles (genus *Spea*) can develop into a distinct cannibal morph under crowded, resource-scarce pool conditions, enlarged jaw musculature and beaked mouthparts built for eating other tadpoles rather than detritus, a genuine, documented phenotype switch rather than an invented mechanic. **The deliberate departure, named explicitly per the project's own rule for where real theory runs out:** some spadefoot research finds cannibal morphs show reduced predation on closer kin. Hiopi hatchlings show none. Every egg in the pool is fair game regardless of parentage, which is the actual reason the ritual reads as merciless rather than merely dangerous, the cut isn't nature culling the unlucky, it's siblings eating siblings with no biological brake on it at all.

- **Zeteii's introduction timing, LOCKED:** first contact happens at Devereux-Kastel, not the full bond. As she's being personalized for him, Trav is allowed limited combat compatibility tests with her, calibration rather than deployment. Deliberately not full access at this stage, the real bond and full Linktier access still wait for his special forces posting, keeping "why him" earned through actual field performance rather than academy performance, and keeping the Reqa romance timeline uncontested, no two relationships developing in the same academic year. The academy tests exist to introduce her personality and give the reader/Trav an early taste of who she is, without spending the real relationship before it's earned.

**Origin of the pilot sport, locked:** wound-emulation piloting as a sport predates the 60-year war by a long margin, and it was directly inspired by the Hiopi ritual. Early Coalition contact put humans in the room for a Hiopi city-fight, real blood, real stakes, no theater, and it exposed how consequence-free human combat sport had become by comparison. Someone built a deliberate human answer to it, on-body risk as the entire point, not an accident of the tech. Worth keeping as a dry, unexplained aside from a Hiopi character at some point rather than stated as backstory, humans took the shape of a Hiopi survival ritual and made entertainment, then war, out of it.

**Academy integration, locked:** the academy's year-end survival-of-the-fittest exam has been folded into the Hiopi mating fight itself for Hiopi cadets specifically. This doesn't exempt them from the ritual, the danger stays real, but academy preparation measurably improves their odds inside it. Framed as the actual reason young Hiopi enroll at all: without it, most don't survive past their second year of participation in the wild ritual. Academy attendance is a survival strategy for them, not a career choice, which gives the Cradle Circle faction's grip on enrollment real moral weight beyond simple corruption, gatekeeping access here means gatekeeping actual survival odds.

Follows naturally: academies are themselves huge, city-scale facilities, easily large enough to qualify under the one-winner- per-city rule, so Hiopi cadets win their mating rights on academy grounds during the same period as the year-end exam. Also gives every non-Hiopi student and staff member a captive front-row seat to the ritual whether they want one or not, good, cheap source of the established outsider culture-clash comedy (the "there's a zone for this and people still complain" bit lands even harder when the zone is the school itself).

## Why there are no rune masters, LOCKED

AI are simply better at advanced rune-work than any human could become. What the rune curriculum actually teaches runs from basic literacy up through advanced material, culminating in the ability to build a rudimentary AI of one's own, provided a student has access to their machine or equipment's core access node, which in practice usually means they already have AI access anyway.
