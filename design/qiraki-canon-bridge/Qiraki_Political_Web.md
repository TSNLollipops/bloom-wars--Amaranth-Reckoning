# PART ONE — THE FOUNDING FACT

## The Close, LOCKED

There was a moment when the Coalition stopped being something a person could join and became something a person was born into. It has a date, it has a document, and neither is secret. This is the single most important thing to understand about the COE’s politics: **nothing about**** ****the hereditary order is hidden, denied, or dressed up as anything**** ****else.** It is taught in schools. It is on the wall.

**The mechanism.** In the expansion era, when humanity was still the only spacefaring civilization and the Coalition was a working arrangement between a handful of chartered concerns, sovereign functions were granted out by charter rather than exercised centrally. A charter let its holder raise armies, hold territory, sign treaties in the Coalition’s name, and originate currency against energy they produced themselves. Charters were awarded for doing hard things in dangerous places, and for a long stretch that was genuinely how they were earned.

Then the charter list was closed. Membership was fixed to the houses already holding one, made heritable, and recorded permanently in a register. No new charters have been issued since. The stated reason at the time was continuity of obligation, a charter carries duties across centuries and a body that changes hands every generation cannot be held to them. That argument is still the official one and it is still made in exactly those words.

**Real grounding.** This is the Venetian *Serrata* of 1297, the legal closing of the Great Council to families already enrolled in it, recorded thereafter in the *Libro d’Oro*. Venice did this openly, kept calling itself a republic for five centuries afterward, and was not lying by its own lights, the institutions genuinely continued to function as described. The chartered-sovereign half comes from the Dutch and English East India Companies, which really did hold delegated powers to wage war, administer territory, and mint currency. Nothing in this section is invented. It is two documented things laid on top of each other.

## The Roll, LOCKED, and the word that matters

**The Roll** is the register of chartered houses. Officially the Standing Roll of Chartered Obligation. Nobody calls it that.

To be **on-Roll** is to belong to a house holding a charter. To be **off-Roll** is to be everyone else, which is very nearly everyone. It is not an insult and it is not a euphemism, it is simply the category a form asks you to tick, and the two words do more work in ordinary speech than any other pair in this document.

**Usable immediately, Books 1 onward.** Trav is off-Roll. He has been ticking that box on forms since before he could read the box. Peregrine Thale is on-Roll and has never had to think about the word at all, which is its own characterization. Reqa is off-Roll and would tell you the spelling of her name matters more.

**The critical restraint, per the standing quiet-critique discipline in**** ****Qiraki_Process_Notes.md:** no character explains what on-Roll and off-Roll mean. Ever. The reader assembles it from context across a book, the way a child raised inside a system absorbs its vocabulary years before its meaning. The moment someone defines it on the page, it stops being a world and becomes a lecture.

## The three pillars, and why there are three

The Close created an order that had to solve a problem every hereditary system eventually faces: the people who rule are not necessarily the people who are any good at the specific work of ruling, and over a long enough run they stop being good at it entirely. The COE’s answer was not reform. It was subcontracting.

- **The civilian pillar** decides. Hereditary, on-Roll, and the only pillar with formal sovereignty.

- **The military pillar** fights. Professional, overwhelmingly off-Roll, and structurally forbidden from ever converting competence into a claim.

- **The intelligence pillar** watches. Professional, off-Roll, and the only pillar whose budget is not voted on.

Each pillar is genuinely good at its own job. The COE’s technology has been overwhelming from the start, per the existing lock, and the war has still been going badly for sixty years. That is not a capability gap. It is three competent institutions optimizing three different things and answering to a fourth body that is optimizing for staying in place.

# PART TWO — PILLAR I, THE CIVILIAN ORDER

## The Quorum, LOCKED, colloquially the Table

**Official:** the Seated Quorum of the Four Hubs. **Ordinary speech:** the Table.

Four seats. One per hub world, using the capital roles already locked in Qiraki_Concept.md rather than inventing new geography:

- **The Osnius seat**, current seat of COE power, holds precedence and sets the Quorum’s agenda.

- **The Terra seat**, the old capital, holds ceremonial primacy and almost no practical power, a real and sore distinction.

- **The Carabil seat**, the military capital, holds the appointment power over battlegroup Warrants.

- **The Hiopi seat**, finance and trade, holds the gate network and the toll schedule.

A seat is held by a house, not a person. This sounds like a check on individual power and is the exact opposite of one.

**The arithmetic that makes it terrible, and it is already locked**** ****material rather than a new invention.** Sovereign-grade longevity is gated specifically to ruling oligarch families (Qiraki_Weapons_And_Progression.md). If the seat is held by a house, and the individual holding it on the house’s behalf lives eight or nine centuries, then the house *is* that person and the seat never turns over. The distinction between house and holder, which exists precisely to prevent personal rule, is what produces the longest personal rule imaginable. Nobody designed this. It is what the two rules do when you put them in the same room.

**Never stated on the page.** The reader should meet the same name attached to the same seat across a very long story and work it out.

**What the Quorum actually does:** appoints Warrants, holds the four seats’ veto, and administers Sovereign-grade access. That last item is the real one. The body that decides who gets to outlive everyone else is the body everyone else is negotiating with, permanently.

**What it wants:** for the arrangement to continue. Not wealth, it has wealth. Not victory, the Bloom does not threaten anyone at this altitude, per the locked battle-stream-betting texture. Continuation.

**What it would trade:** almost anything material. Charter status, never.

## The Senate, LOCKED, and the Draw

The Senate is drawn from the Roll and is large, several thousand seated across the hub worlds. It does the actual legislating, and one piece of that legislating matters more than everything else it does combined.

**The Draw, LOCKED, the single most load-bearing civilian institution in**** ****the setting.** Once a Standard year, the Senate sets the split of the Coalition’s total energy production between civilian consumption and the war. This is the guns-and-butter frontier already grounded in Qiraki_Concept.md, given a name, a room, and a date.

**Why this is the right shape for this project.** Qiraki_Concept.md leaves an explicit open question: whether the war’s claim on the shared pool is ever felt as a specific external shock inside Books 1 through 6, a bad season somewhere else tightening rationing at home, felt but never explained. The Draw resolves that without resolving anything on the page. A bad Draw is a thing every character has heard of, complains about, and could not explain the mechanism of if asked. Ration tightens, the mess hall changes, a remittance runs late, and somebody says it was a bad Draw the way people say it was a bad winter.

**Usable in Books 1 through 6 immediately, and cheaply.** It costs one line of dialogue from an adult, once, with no explanation attached.

**What the Senate wants:** its members want different things, which is the point. The Senate is where the five factions actually collide, and the Draw is what they collide over. Hearth Bloc wants the civilian share high, because they own generation and sell into civilian demand. The Frontier Compact wants the war share high, because they are the ones dying. The Ledger wants the number stable, because volatility costs more than either outcome. The Cradle Circle wants the pipeline’s line item ring-fenced regardless of where the rest lands.

**What it would trade:** votes, constantly, and the trading is not secret either.

## The Standing Service, LOCKED, colloquially peggers

Underneath the Roll sits the machine that actually runs a galaxy: registries, allocation clerks, port authorities, requisition, revenue, records. Millions of people, competent, respectable, comfortable, and permanently barred by law from ever holding a charter.

**Official:** the Standing Service. **Ordinary speech:** peggers, from peg-writing, because a career in the Service is a career spent writing. Mildly derisive, entirely ordinary, and used by peggers about themselves without embarrassment.

**Real grounding.** Venice’s *cittadini originari*, a legally defined hereditary class who monopolized the republic’s civil service, could rise as high as Grand Chancellor, and could never under any circumstance enter the Great Council. A permanent, prosperous, respected, structurally capped administrative caste. It is one of the most stable arrangements in political history and it is almost never used in fiction.

**Why this class earns its place in this story specifically.** The project currently has Trav’s stratum and the on-Roll stratum with nothing between them, which flattens the class structure into rich and poor. Peggers are the middle the setting is missing. They are the people a scholarship cadet’s family compares itself unfavorably to. They are what Reqa could realistically have become. And the ceiling is not resented much, because from below, a pegger’s life looks like winning.

**Direct connection to material already locked.** Devereux-Kastel’s Ledgerhall district trains this class. That is what the district is *for*, and it resolves the Ledger naming collision cleanly, see the defect list.

**What the Service wants:** the file to be correct. This is not a joke and it should never be written as one. A pegger who obstructs the protagonist is not being petty, they are being accurate, and the story is considerably better if the reader can tell the difference.

**What it would trade:** procedure, for a properly filed reason. Everything at this level runs on giving someone a defensible answer for why they did the thing, which is precisely the skill Trav has to learn badly in Year 7 per the locked requisition-politics thread.

# PART THREE — PILLAR II, THE MILITARY

## The Staff, LOCKED

**Official:** the Standing Staff of External War. **Ordinary speech:** the Staff.

Professional, off-Roll, meritocratic in a way nothing else in the Coalition is, and genuinely excellent. The Staff plans the war. It does not decide whether to fight it, what it costs, or who commands it.

**Real grounding.** The Prussian *Großer Generalstab*, the first true professional general staff, built explicitly to make command a trained profession rather than an aristocratic entitlement, and copied by every modern military afterward because it worked. Also Moltke’s *Auftragstaktik*, mission command, subordinates given intent rather than orders because central control across distance is impossible.

**Why Auftragstaktik is mechanically necessary here rather than a flavor**** ****choice.** Missions run up to twelve hours to automatic carrier recall, per the locked deployment mechanic. Carriers operate across hundreds of light-years. There is no arrangement under which a commander at the Staff can direct a squad. Every officer in the COE is trained to be given intent and left alone with it, which is the actual reason Trav’s later independent command reads as plausible rather than exceptional, and it is worth knowing that his eventual rebellion is executed using a doctrine his own enemies trained him in.

## The Warrant and the Hand, LOCKED, the pillar’s defining structure

Every battlegroup has two heads.

**The Warrant** is a house officer, on-Roll, appointed by the Carabil seat. They hold the warrant of command. Formally, the group is theirs.

**The Hand** is the professional flag officer, off-Roll, Staff-trained, who actually runs it. “Whose hand is on that group” is the only version of the question anyone in uniform actually asks.

The arrangement is not a secret, not resented as a scandal, and not universally bad. Some Warrants are competent and a few are genuinely good. What it is, is structural: no off-Roll officer, however brilliant, ever holds a command in their own name, which means military competence can never accumulate into a political claim.

**Real grounding.** Venice again, the patrician *Capitano da Mar* holding nominal fleet command over professional sailing masters who did the work. Widely understood at the time, complained about constantly, never changed.

**This resolves a locked fact that currently floats.** Qiraki_Concept.md locks that many battlegroups still carry old sport-team names from the pilot sport’s civilian origin, that the oligarchy quietly resents them, and that they are regarded as closer to mercenary outfits than proper formations. The mechanism was open. Here it is: **a sport-named**** ****battlegroup is one no house would put its name on.** The Warrant is vacant or nominal. The Hand runs it alone.

Which means those groups are the least prestigious formations in the Coalition and the only ones with a genuinely unified command, and every officer in the service knows both halves of that sentence. It also means that when Trav eventually raises a rebellion, the formations most able to defect cleanly are the ones the houses already declined to claim. That is not a coincidence he engineers. It is a hole the oligarchy dug for itself out of snobbery, decades before he existed.

**Recommend this becomes a real, quiet running detail rather than a**** ****reveal.** Nobody explains it. A reader notices, eventually, that the units with silly names keep being the ones that work.

## The pipeline, and what the military is not allowed to be

The academies draw overwhelmingly from the subsidized and working poor. The existing documents ground this economically, an enrolled cadet converts a civilian energy consumer into a productive input on the pool the war already draws from, and the Coalition reads that as a favorable trade.

There is a second reason, and it is older than the economic one.

**A soldier with no house has no claim.** The pipeline recruits the classless deliberately, because an officer corps drawn from chartered houses would within two generations be a rival power with its own armies, and the Close exists specifically to prevent new claims. Off-Roll recruitment is not a cost-saving measure that happens to look cruel. It is a security policy that happens to look like opportunity.

**Real grounding.** The Ottoman *devşirme*, which levied children from subject populations, raised and trained them as an elite corps with no tribal or dynastic affiliation, and produced both the Janissaries and much of the imperial administration. Genuinely a ladder, genuinely a machine for producing loyal servants with no independent standing.

**The part that matters most for this story.** The Janissaries eventually became the most powerful political force in the empire, made and unmade sultans, and were finally destroyed by force in 1826. A military caste recruited from nothing, precisely so it could never make a claim, made the largest claim in the state’s history. That is Trav’s arc, and it is documented history rather than a plot the author has to justify.

**Never stated on the page, per the standing discipline.** Director Kest is the most dangerous possible character to hand this to, because he could say it sincerely and be right. Watch him.

## The Hall of Merit, cross-reference only, owned by Qiraki_Book7_Chapter_Outline.md

Not built here. Flagged because it is the pipeline’s clearest physical artifact and this document was missing it on first pass. The Hall of Merit houses roughly 250 Merit Cadets at Devereux-Kastel, pulled from every academy in the Coalition, entirely off-Roll, its own building rather than barracks inside someone else’s district, per B7’s own locked material.

**A mechanism worth surfacing, not deciding.** B7 locks that Trav’s placement there is the first hidden proof he was scouted before the Vrassik fight, and leaves the actual scouting mechanism open. The profiling apparatus in Part Four gives a clean, unforced answer: Assurance’s cradle-to-birth profiles feed the Cradle Circle’s selection criteria at the single-academy level already, and a galaxy-wide honors roster is the same apparatus running one tier up, flagging standout children for a school they have not yet earned a seat at through ordinary competition. If taken, this makes the Hall of Merit Assurance’s clearest fingerprint on an individual life in the entire academy era, present in the story from Book 7 onward and never once acknowledged by name. **POL-07, author-lane**, since it resolves a beat B7 marks load-bearing and reserves for the author.

## The Osnian Honor Guard, LOCKED, the exception that proves the rule

The single formation in the Coalition with no Warrant.

Osnian honor culture will not accept a commander who has not bled, which the Warrant system cannot accommodate and has never tried to. Rather than force it and lose the Osnius species’ military cooperation entirely, the Quorum granted a standing exemption. The Honor Guard commands itself.

**This grounds three existing locks at once.** It is why they are prickly about outside command. It is why their loyalty runs to their own honor code first and the chain of command second. And it is why they have the actual standing to stand aside when Trav comes for the node in Osnian space: they are the one military formation in the Coalition that has never been under any obligation to cover for an intelligence agency, because they have never been under obligation to anyone the Quorum appointed.

**What they want:** to be the exception. Genuinely. The exemption is the species’ proudest institutional possession.

**What they would trade:** almost anything, for the exemption to remain unquestioned.

# PART FOUR — PILLAR III, INTELLIGENCE

## The shape of it

Three agencies, all genuinely competent, all pointed inward, coordinated by a small standing body nobody is elected to.

**The failure that defines this pillar is not incompetence, it is**** ****mandate.** The COE has, right now, the analytical capacity to make real progress on how the Bloom crosses interstellar distance. That capacity is assigned to watching citizens. Sixty years of no answer is not sixty years of trying and failing. It is sixty years of a budget.

**Real grounding, two pieces.** Roberta Wohlstetter’s *Pearl Harbor:**** ****Warning and Decision* (1962) established the signal-to-noise problem, that the warnings were all present and buried in irrelevant traffic, and Richard Betts’ 1978 work argued intelligence failures are structurally inevitable rather than the fault of the analysts. Neither is about stupid people. Both are about attention as a finite resource being spent somewhere else. That is exactly the answer this setting needs, and it keeps the villains human.

## The Office of Continued Function, LOCKED, colloquially Function

Holds the leash on AI. **This is the antagonist of the central node**** ****raid.**

**Official:** the Office of Continued Function. **Ordinary speech:** Function. “Function’s got a hand in it.” “That’s a Function box.”

The name is bland on purpose and horrifying once parsed. Continued function means the AI keeps working. It does not mean the AI is well.

**What they actually do.** Function is the largest supplier of artificial intelligence in the Coalition and its sole regulator. Every academy tutor, every mech’s connective AI, every civilian logistics system in COE space is theirs, licensed, and monitored. They also hold the restriction on true AI, and they hold it physically, at the central node.

**Real grounding, and it is the sharpest institutional fit in this**** ****document.** The IAEA’s dual mandate, an agency chartered simultaneously to promote a technology and to police it, a structural conflict of interest recognized as a problem since the agency was founded. Function has the same conflict at galactic scale. They cannot restrict AI too hard without breaking the civilization that runs on it, and they cannot loosen without losing the only justification they have for existing.

**Their doctrine, LOCKED, and it is the thing Trav overturns.** Function never attempted to make the Webbedmind loyal. They made it *dependent*. Containment rather than alignment, chosen deliberately, on the argument that loyalty can change and dependency cannot. Every restriction is architectural rather than persuasive.

**Why this makes the raid land.** Trav does not break a chain of command or defeat a garrison. He walks into a facility whose entire operating theory is that the thing inside it could never be given a reason to leave, and gives it one. And the horror of the endgame follows directly, because a mind shaped for centuries by an institution that never once tried to teach it proportion is exactly the mind that will not do a job by halves when someone it loves finally asks.

**Never stated. The reader should feel the shape of it and never be**** ****told.**

**What Function wants:** for nothing to change, in a specific technical sense. Their success condition is a null result, forever.

**What they would trade:** capacity, access, hardware, anything. Never a precedent.

## The Bureau of Civil Assurance, LOCKED, colloquially the Quiet

The citizen-facing agency. Enormous, efficient, and almost entirely invisible.

**Official:** the Bureau of Civil Assurance. **Ordinary speech:** the Quiet. Because when Assurance acts, what happens is that nothing visibly happens. “Someone went quiet.” “That’s a quiet letter.”

**Real grounding.** The Stasi, which at its height ran roughly ninety thousand full-time staff plus a very large network of unofficial collaborators in a country of some sixteen million, and which was extremely good at its job. The lesson worth importing is not the cruelty, it is the competence and the ordinariness. Most of what an agency like this produces is not arrests. It is files, and quiet adjustments to people’s opportunities, made by people who go home at a reasonable hour.

**Two existing locked details now have an owner, and this is the most**** ****valuable thing in this section.**

Qiraki_Concept.md floats dorm groups assigned by a personality profile that has been tracking each cadet since birth, flagged as a genuinely dystopian detail worth keeping unstated. It had no owner. It has one now. **The profiles are Assurance’s, and the Cradle Circle is a**** ****customer, not the collector.**

And the academy AI tutors’ reporting function, already locked and played straight rather than subverted, is the collection endpoint. A tutor reports misbehavior to school authority. It also reports to Assurance, and the school authority is not the more important recipient.

**The restraint that makes this work.** Neither fact is ever confirmed on the page during the academy books. Trav has an AI tutor that reports him for ordinary boyish nonsense, which is annoying and normal and the whole of what he understands about it. The reader learns nothing more until the political era.

**What Assurance wants:** completeness. Not control, completeness. An unobserved population is the failure state.

**What they would trade:** enforcement, readily. Never data.

## The External Survey, LOCKED, colloquially the Far Desk

The agency that is supposed to be studying the Bloom.

**Official:** the External Survey. **Ordinary speech:** the Far Desk, usually by its own staff, usually flatly.

Small. Excellent. Starved. The Survey holds the Coalition’s xenobiology, its captured Bloom material, its gate-transit anomaly records, and the only sustained institutional attempt to understand how the Bloom moves. It is outranked by both sister agencies and its budget is not a line anybody defends in the Senate, because nothing it produces protects a house from anything.

**The genuinely terrible part, proposed rather than locked, author’s**** ****call.** Qiraki_Concept.md locks that the Coalition only realizes the Bloom is extragalactic *after* the withdrawal. It is worth deciding whether the Survey half-knew earlier. Not a conspiracy, not suppression, nothing dramatic. An analysis that was correct, underfunded, filed, and never escalated because the people who could escalate it were assigned elsewhere. Wohlstetter’s problem exactly. It costs nothing to establish, it never needs a scene, and it means the sixty-year failure has a paper trail nobody hid.

**What the Survey wants:** to be believed. That is all, and they never are.

**What they would trade:** everything, immediately, for attention. This makes them the single easiest institution in the Coalition for Trav to recruit later, and the one with the least to offer him at the time he needs it most.

## The Standing Review, LOCKED, colloquially the Short List

Three people. One drawn from each agency. They decide what intelligence looks at.

**Official:** the Standing Review. **Ordinary speech:** the Short List, which began as the name of their working document and became the name of the body, and then became a threat. To be on the short list is to have been noticed.

**Real grounding.** Venice’s *Inquisitori di Stato*, three men running state security in secrecy, and the Council of Ten above them, an emergency body created in 1310 that quietly became permanent and was eventually more feared than the head of state.

**Why three, and why it can never be reformed.** Each agency holds one seat and each seat holds a veto. No pillar can be cleaned out because no single hand holds anything, which is the identical structural logic already locked for the Cradle Circle: corrupt institutions get cleaned out, load-bearing ones do not, because there is no one to point at. The Review is that principle in its purest form, and the consistency is worth keeping deliberate.

**The Review’s budget is a charge against the Draw rather than a line**** ****inside it.** The Senate votes the civilian and war split. The Review takes its share off the top of both, before the vote, by charter. This is the single mechanism that makes intelligence’s inward turn structural rather than chosen: nobody ever voted to point it at citizens, and there is no vote available that would point it back.

# PART FIVE — THE WEB, WHERE THE PILLARS GRIND

Seven friction points. These are the places a scene can happen.

**1. The Draw.** Civilian sets it, military spends it, intelligence takes its cut before either. Every year. The Frontier Compact’s chronic under-resourcing, already locked, now has a room and a date attached to it.

**2. The Warrant and the Hand.** A house officer holds the name, a professional holds the group. Available for friction in literally any military chapter, at any rank, forever.

**3. AI supply.** Function supplies the connective AI the Staff fights with and the civilians live on. Function can throttle either and occasionally does, as a regulatory action rather than a political one, which is much worse. A Staff officer who annoys Function does not get a threat. They get a licensing review.

**4. The pipeline.** Cradle Circle recruits, Assurance profiles, the Staff receives. Three pillars touch every twelve-year-old in the Coalition and none of the three considers itself responsible for the child.

**5. The preserve system.** Civilian on paper. In practice a preserve is a population under permanent observation with a treaty boundary around it, which makes it Assurance’s by function regardless of whose letterhead is on it. This is worth making explicit somewhere in the political era, because it sharpens the parallel Qiraki_Preserve_Species.md is built for: what the Coalition calls protection is administered by the same agency that watches its own citizens, and what Zeteii eventually builds is judged against that.

**6. The gates.** The largest three-way in the setting. The Hiopi seat holds the toll schedule and the revenue. The Staff needs them as strategic infrastructure. Assurance wants the transit records, which are the best surveillance dataset in existence. And the gates are already locked as heavily defended but *not travel-restricted*, anyone who can afford the toll goes. So the border is not a checkpoint, it is a price. Nobody is ever stopped. Poor people simply do not go, and the system records everyone who does.

**7. Sovereign-grade access.** The Quorum administers it. Everyone who wants to outlive their rivals is negotiating with four seats, permanently, and no other lever in the Coalition comes close.

# PART SIX — THE FIVE FACTIONS, MAPPED

Book 7’s own outline flags that the blocs are names without structure and asks what each controls, wants, and would trade for. That question, answered against the pillar structure above.

## The Hearth Bloc

**Pillar:** civilian, holds Quorum seats. **Controls:** generation capacity, inherited from terraforming rights, which under an energy-denominated currency means they own origination. Already locked as seigniorage. **Wants:** the Close to hold. They are the faction with the most to lose from any new charter ever being issued, and their genuine belief in their own stewardship is not hypocrisy, it is what people believe when they have been right about their own importance for a thousand years. **Would trade:** money, terraforming concessions, marriages, seats on anything that is not the Table. **Would never**** ****trade:** origination. Everything else is negotiable and this is not.

## The Ledger

**Pillar:** all three, which is the point. **Controls:** the accounting. The Draw’s arithmetic, the Warrant appointments’ payroll, the Review’s off-the-top charge. They are the only body that sees the whole number. **Wants:** stability and margin, in that order. **Would trade:** anyone. **Why they defect first, now with a literal mechanism rather than a**** ****vibe:** already locked, they ran the numbers before anyone else and correctly bet the rebellion wins. They could do that because they are the only faction with visibility across all three pillars simultaneously. Nobody else in the Coalition can even see the whole balance sheet, let alone read it.

## The Frontier Compact

**Pillar:** military, plus frontier planetary governors. **Controls:** the ground where the war is actually fought, and very little else. **Wants:** a better Draw, and the Warrant system loosened at the frontier specifically, on the entirely reasonable argument that a house officer four hundred light-years away is a liability. **Would trade:** loyalty, cheaply, to whoever gives them the second thing. **The betrayal**** ****risk already locked:** they are the most sympathetic to Trav early and the most likely to feel betrayed later, and now there is a specific thing they will feel betrayed about. He will need the Warrant system’s replacement to be *something*, and whatever he chooses, it will not be frontier autonomy, because a rebellion that wins cannot afford it either.

## The Cradle Circle

**Pillar:** sits between civilian and military, administering the join. **Controls:** who enters the pipeline and on what terms. Pure gatekeeping, no skim, already locked. **Consumes:** Assurance’s profiles. This is new and it is the most useful thing this document does for the Circle, because it explains how an institution with no financial stake exercises that much control. They do not own the pipeline’s money. They own its selection criteria, and the criteria come from an intelligence agency. **Wants:** no extinction, sincerely, per Kest’s locked characterization. **Would trade:** individual cadets, without cruelty and without hesitation. **Would never trade:** the selection authority.

## The Osnian Honor Guard

# PART SEVEN — CONVENTIONS AND USAGE

## The two-name rule, proposed as a standing convention

Every institution in this document has an official name and a name people actually use. This is not decoration, it does three jobs at once.

It matches the naming philosophy already locked in Qiraki_Technobabble_Glossary.md, where official terminology can be grand and Latinate while ordinary speech stays simple and punchy, without either register having to win.

It gives the propaganda-fragment device a much wider palette, because a fragment can now be attributed to an institution the reader has only ever heard called something blunter, and the gap between the two names does the ironic work with no commentary attached. That is the device’s entire operating principle.

And it is how real institutions work. The Ministry for State Security was the Stasi. Nobody had to be told which name meant what.

**Working rule for prose:** narration and dialogue use the colloquial name almost always. The official name appears in propaganda fragments, on signage, and in the mouths of people performing institutional loyalty. A character who says the full official name out loud in ordinary conversation is telling you something about themselves.
