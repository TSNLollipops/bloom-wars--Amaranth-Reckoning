# THE QIRAKI FILES — Points Shop Catalog (G through A; S reserved)

*Part of the Qiraki files. Companion to Qiraki_Weapons_And_Progression.md and Qiraki_Technobabble_Glossary.md, cross-reference rather than duplicate. Now covers G through A, spanning academy through military service. DRAFT, flagged for review, not LOCKED.*

## Scope and cost logic

Availability matches the existing lock: first upgrades unlock years 1-3. Reading against the Year 1 point-generation table in the progression doc (combat success climbs 20/success in month 1 to 240/success by month 12, class activity 10 to 120), a genuinely active cadet accumulates low thousands of points across a full year. Costs below are set so G-tier is reachable inside month 1-2 for anyone actually training, F-tier by mid-year-1, E-tier stretches into year 2, D-tier is a real year-2/3 commitment, consistent with Trav’s D-tier special forces starting chassis reading as solid-but-unremarkable rather than elite.

C-through-A costs are set against the military-era point formula (Qiraki_Weapons_And_Progression.md), not the academy formula, since that’s genuinely when they become reachable. C-tier lines up with the late-academy/Devereux-Kastel window where a cadet’s accumulated years of points finally clear the threshold. B and A both assume real military-era point accumulation, survival-mission and kill points climbing fast per the locked formula, plus the higher-rank tier’s tripled per-mission value once someone hits unit-commander threshold.

| **Grade** | **Rough cost band** | **Reachable by** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| G | 150 – 300 | Month 1-2, Year 1 |
| F | 400 – 800 | Mid Year 1 |
| E | 1,200 – 2,500 | Year 1-2 |
| D | 3,000 – 6,000 | Year 2-3 |
| C | 12,000 – 20,000 | Late Year 6 / Devereux-Kastel |
| B | 40,000 – 70,000 | Early-to-mid military service |
| A | 150,000 – 250,000 | Sustained deployment, genuinely elite, few pilots ever reach it |

**S-tier, LOCKED, deliberately not in this catalog:** per the existing lock, the Zeteii-tier jump is the one and only S-tier leap in the story, a single narrative event, not a purchase. No cost band exists for S and none should be added, buying one’s way to S would undercut the exact thing that jump is supposed to mean.

Genetic/stimulant upgrades run slightly cheaper than path weapons at the same grade, consistent with weapons being the more visible, more status-bearing purchase (per the progression doc’s “real status to be an early recipient” note). Per the standing world-logic rule (Qiraki_Process_Notes.md): gear expands what’s possible, it doesn’t replace skill, every tier below should read as “a better tool in the hands that can use it,” never a substitute for the pilot actually being good.

## Meeps (Melee DPS) — weapons

Anchors Trav’s own already-locked progression (lance -> two lances -> two swords -> two laser-blades by year 6/7), but the table below is a menu, not a rail. Each grade typically offers more than one weapon shape at a comparable stat cost, a cadet picks based on what actually suits how they fight. Trav’s specific path through it (lance, into twin lances, into swords) reads as him genuinely feeling out his own style match by match, not the economy dictating his choice for him. The swap to blades at D-tier is a real in-fiction decision he makes, discovering he trusts an edge and his own movement more than raw committed impact, not a purchase that happens to unlock a sword. Worth letting a chapter actually show that discovery rather than just narrating the gear change.

| **Grade** | **Name** | **Cost** | **What it is** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| G | Stocklance | 200 | Standard-issue single lance, no personalization, what every cadet starts with regardless of purchase. Baseline reference point, not really “bought” so much as issued, but priced here for comparison. |
| F | Heavylance | 550 | Same frame, reinforced core and a denser tip. Hits harder, costs a little more reach on the swing, cadets feel the weight difference immediately. |
| E | Twinlance | 1,800 | Second lance issued, the actual “two lances” stage. Real coordination cost, most cadets fumble the offhand for weeks before it stops feeling like carrying a second stick for no reason. |
| D | Pairblade | 4,500 | Both lances swapped for a matched set of short swords, available to any cadet at D-grade cost, but only worth choosing over a heavier D-grade lance variant if a cadet’s actually found they fight better with an edge than an impact weapon. First real taste of the cutting-weapon philosophy that eventually defines Trav’s laser-blade endgame, edge over reach, control over raw impact. |
| C | Arcblade | 15,000 | The laser-edge upgrade, Trav’s own already-locked Year 6/7 endgame weapon. Same matched-pair, ambidextrous, wrist-rotation fighting style the swords already taught, the physical edge replaced with a sustained cutting beam, a real jump in cutting power that doesn’t change the philosophy that earned it, only sharpens it. |
| B | Flareblade | 55,000 | Military-era upgrade, adds a short-range heat-burst discharge along the blade’s edge for finishing strikes, real tactical risk attached, the discharge draws heat that has to vent before the next one, no spamming it. |
| A | Stormblade | 190,000 | Genuinely elite, integrates a short-range kinetic thruster burst directly into the weapon system, a real, grounded repositioning tool (not a teleport, no Heirloom-adjacent physics involved), letting a Meeps close distance or escape a bad angle mid-strike. Very few pilots ever carry this. |

**The lance branch, new.** Same Meeps catalog, same tiers, same costs, a genuine alternate path rather than a separate track, per the standing rule that this table is a menu, not a rail. G and F are identical to the branch above, every cadet starts on the same Stocklance and Heavylance. The fork is E-tier, where a cadet who’s found they fight better with one committed weapon than two coordinated ones stays on a single lance instead of taking the Twinlance. **Vrassik’s own progression, LOCKED to this branch:** he never picks up a second weapon, the naming staying “-lance” all the way to A-tier is itself the on-page signal, where Trav’s own branch changes its name at D to mark a philosophy shift, Vrassik’s branch keeping the same suffix the whole way is the same device pointed the opposite direction, loyalty to one weapon rather than a discovered new one.

| **Grade** | **Name** | **Cost** | **What it is** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| E | Piercelance | 1,800 | Narrowed tip geometry, real physics: the same kinetic energy Heavylance delivers, concentrated into a much smaller contact area, pressure is force over area, a narrower point goes through what a blunter one only bruises. The actual fork point, a cadet choosing this over Twinlance is choosing one committed weapon over two coordinated ones. |
| D | Burstlance | 4,500 | Adds a contained secondary charge in the tip, impact-triggered rather than remotely detonated, penetrate first, charge fires a beat later. Grounded in real shaped-charge ordnance logic, a penetrator opening the path for a payload behind it, not exotic tech. The actual mechanism behind “gore his opponent and then make them explode from the inside.” |
| C | Warlance | 15,000 | The philosophy-crystallizing tier, matching Trav’s own Pairblade/Arcblade beat on Vrassik’s own divergent path. Adds a magnetic coil along the shaft, real coilgun-adjacent physics already inside this setting’s tech level, accelerating the strike well past what charge-momentum and muscle alone produce, dramatically deeper penetration and a bigger payload the Burstlance tip can carry. |
| B | Ruinlance | 55,000 | Military-era upgrade, a multi-charge magazine, three to four payloads carried and triggered in sequence without a full reload between strikes. Real tactical cost to match: successive charges in the same engagement window draw down the coil’s capacitor, the first strike of a fight hits hardest and follow-ups taper until it recharges, no free lunch. |
| A | Havoclance | 190,000 | Genuinely elite. A regenerative capacitor system harvests energy from the strike’s own deceleration on impact, real energy-recovery-system logic, the same principle behind regenerative braking, and removes the B-tier taper, sustained full-power strikes rather than a diminishing string of them. The practical ceiling before Heirloom-grade, same standing as Stormblade on the other branch. |

## Munti — equipment

The Munti’s full feature set (fabricator, remote repair, cockpit evacuation, disintegrator weapon) is described in the progression doc as a complete package. Reading that against the tiered economy: academy Muntis unlock that feature set in stages as cadets earn points, full functionality isn’t standard-issue until D-grade. Military Muntis, per the existing lock, run the complete package from day one, this staged unlock is specifically an academy-era training constraint.

| **Grade** | **Name** | **Cost** | **What it is** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| G | Quickfix kit | 200 | Basic onboard fabricator, small parts only, no remote function, has to physically dock to repair anything. |
| F | Longarm | 550 | Adds short-range remote repair, no dock required inside a tight radius, still can’t reach a teammate mid-fight across real distance. |
| E | Farfix | 1,900 | True matter-transfer remote repair at real combat range, the actual “no physical dock required” capability the writeup describes. |
| D | Lifebox | 4,800 | Cockpit evacuation system unlocked, the box on the back that can pull a downed pilot’s cockpit clear. Highest-stakes, most sought-after Munti purchase at this tier band for exactly the reason the casualty-rate notes already establish. |
| C | Quickbox | 15,000 | Faster cockpit-evacuation cycle time, the same Lifebox system, meaningfully quicker, which given the existing lock that extraction speed is the actual life-or-death variable, is a genuinely bigger deal than the price tag alone suggests. |
| B | Widefix | 55,000 | Remote repair radius extended enough to cover multiple teammates at once rather than one at a time, the actual mechanism behind a veteran Munti keeping an entire squad patched through a running fight instead of triaging one pilot at a time. |
| A | Overcharge | 190,000 | The disintegrator weapon’s reverse-mode output significantly increased, the point where a Munti stops being purely support and becomes a real combat threat in its own right, without losing anything on the repair side that earned the role its respect in the first place. |

## Power unit — G through A

Confirmed gap, new category. Every mech needs one, most cadets treat it as an afterthought next to weapons and armor, which is exactly the gap Trav’s own build philosophy exploits: he underinvests here on purpose, prioritizing weapon and mobility spend over raw power and protection, per his locked “weapon and the ability to move and react before all else” priority. A cheap power unit means less energy on tap for shields or heavy weapon draw, real, felt tradeoff, not a stat dump.

| Grade | Name | Cost | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| G | Coreline | 200 | Standard-issue reactor, no personalization, baseline energy budget every cadet starts with. |
| F | Coreline-B | 550 | Marginal output increase, most cadets’ first upgrade here, cheap enough not to compete with weapon spend. |
| E | Twinfeed | 1,800 | Dual-output line, meaningfully more headroom for a Tank’s shield draw specifically, less relevant to a Meeps build. |
| D | Steadyburn | 4,500 | Real output jump, the point where skipping this purchase becomes a visible, felt choice rather than just cheap thrift. |
| C | Hotcore | 15,000 | Late-academy tier, sustained high-output draw, standard for Tank and Munti builds by now, still skippable for a Meeps prioritizing elsewhere. |
| B | Deepwell | 55,000 | Military-era output, the tier most B-rank pilots actually carry. Trav notably does not, running a D-or-C-tier unit well into his B-rank service, the real mechanical signature of his build philosophy. |
| A | Fullburn | 190,000 | Elite tier, sustained output rivaling a small Tank’s dedicated shield generator on its own. Genuinely rare outside Tank and Munti specialists. |

Not path-specific, available to any cadet regardless of track. Priced below path weapons at the same grade per the note above.

| **Grade** | **Name** | **Cost** | **What it does** |

| F | Ironvein | 400 | Muscle density and stamina increase, more staying power across a long class or a long match, not a combat-damage upgrade on its own. |

| C | Overclock | 15,000 | Deeper AI-sync throughput upgrade, information moves between pilot and connective AI meaningfully faster, the practical ceiling of what a standard human nervous system can process before the AI itself becomes the bottleneck instead of the pilot. |
| B | Ironmind | 55,000 | Stress-tolerance and pain-management overhaul, genuinely relevant given the already-locked symbiosis pain-spike, though it arrives well after the roughest years rather than fixing them retroactively, worth keeping that timing honest rather than letting it feel like a missed opportunity. |
| A | Apex | 190,000 | Full-body overhaul at the real edge of what the human-performance-ceiling boundary allows, per the existing lock that this is exactly where genuine science stops and the genetic/stimulant line takes over.
