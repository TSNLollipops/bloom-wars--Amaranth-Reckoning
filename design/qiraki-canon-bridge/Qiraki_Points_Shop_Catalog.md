## Availability, the ruling

Two rules, not one.

**Six boards on the combat-corridor wall.** Four wide ones and two narrow ones sitting together in the middle. **Corrected 2026-09-06:** v3 put the power unit on the path side, which contradicts Qiraki_Outline_Update_Log’s own ruling that universal items are the genetic/stimulant line *and* the power unit, since neither needs a path to exist.

- **The four path boards are gated behind year-3 specialisation.** Meeps, Reeps, Tank, Munti. Nothing on those can be bought by anyone who has not chosen a path. Years 1 and 2 fight on issued gear, which is why a first-year cohort looks identical on the field regardless of what any individual has banked.

- **The genetic and stimulant board is ungated.** Open from the first week of Year 1 to anybody. Price alone rations it.

- **The power unit board is also ungated,** open on the same terms, and priced on the path band anyway. **That combination is deliberate.** It is available to a first-year and its cheapest item is 400,000 against a Year 1 income of 111,960, so it is open and irrelevant for two years running. Two boards nobody talks about, for opposite reasons: one because it costs nothing, one because it costs everything. It also has to stay expensive for Trav’s locked underinvestment to read as a choice rather than as thrift.

**Grade is not gated.** Once a cadet has a path, every grade on that board is open and money is the only constraint. That is deliberate and it is where the locked snowball lives: two cadets who specialise in the same week walk out with different kit and the gap widens from there.

**Confirmed on the page, B1.CH22.** Six boards, four paths and two narrow open ones in the middle. Seven items on the genetic board, 130 at the top and 76,000,000 at the bottom, and Corw dismisses the reactor board in one line at 400,000.

## Cost logic

Every price below is set so that a grade costs more than the entire cumulative income of every year before the one it belongs to. Derivation and the full income model live in Qiraki_Points_Economy_Math_v2, not here. G-tier is the exception and comes out unchanged from v1, those prices were always right.

| **Grade** | **Path boards** | **Open board** | **Reachable by** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| G | 180 – 220 | 130 | Open board week 1 Year 1. Path G is issued gear |
| F | 360,000 – 430,000 | 1,700 | Path F is the first purchase after specialising in Year 3 |
| E | 1,400,000 – 1,700,000 | 74,000 | Path E late Year 3 for a strong earner |
| D | 3,700,000 – 4,400,000 | 340,000 | Path D affordable Year 5 |

| B | 60,000,000 | 17,000,000 | Mid service |

**The gap between the two columns at E and D is deliberate and it is large.** The open board’s figures are fixed by delivered prose and cannot move. The path boards are priced for a third-year’s income, the open board for a first-year’s pocket. **The in-world reason is never stated on the page:** the institution prices what it wants cadets to want. A weapon is visible and creates rank. The thing that goes in you doesn’t show.

## Meeps (Melee DPS) — weapons

Anchors Trav’s own already-locked progression (lance -> two lances -> two swords -> two laser-blades by year 6/7), but the table below is a menu, not a rail. Each grade typically offers more than one weapon shape at a comparable stat cost, a cadet picks based on what actually suits how they fight. Trav’s specific path through it (lance, into twin lances, into swords) reads as him genuinely feeling out his own style match by match, not the economy dictating his choice for him. The swap to blades at D-tier is a real in-fiction decision he makes, discovering he trusts an edge and his own movement more than raw committed impact, not a purchase that happens to unlock a sword. Worth letting a chapter actually show that discovery rather than just narrating the gear change.

| **Grade** | **Name** | **Cost** | **What it is** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| G | Stocklance | 200 | Standard-issue single lance, no personalization, what every cadet starts with regardless of purchase. Baseline reference point, not really “bought” so much as issued, but priced here for comparison. |
| F | Heavylance | 400,000 | Same frame, reinforced core and a denser tip. Hits harder, costs a little more reach on the swing, cadets feel the weight difference immediately. |
| E | Twinlance | 1,600,000 | Second lance issued, the actual “two lances” stage. Real coordination cost, most cadets fumble the offhand for weeks before it stops feeling like carrying a second stick for no reason. |
| D | Pairblade | 4,000,000 | Both lances swapped for a matched set of short swords, available to any cadet at D-grade cost, but only worth choosing over a heavier D-grade lance variant if a cadet’s actually found they fight better with an edge than an impact weapon. First real taste of the cutting-weapon philosophy that eventually defines Trav’s laser-blade endgame, edge over reach, control over raw impact. |
| C | Arcblade | 15,000,000 | The laser-edge upgrade, Trav’s own already-locked Year 6/7 endgame weapon. Same matched-pair, ambidextrous, wrist-rotation fighting style the swords already taught, the physical edge replaced with a sustained cutting beam, a real jump in cutting power that doesn’t change the philosophy that earned it, only sharpens it. |
| B | Flareblade | 60,000,000 | Military-era upgrade, adds a short-range heat-burst discharge along the blade’s edge for finishing strikes, real tactical risk attached, the discharge draws heat that has to vent before the next one, no spamming it. |
| A | Stormblade | 250,000,000 | Genuinely elite, integrates a short-range kinetic thruster burst directly into the weapon system, a real, grounded repositioning tool (not a teleport, no Heirloom-adjacent physics involved), letting a Meeps close distance or escape a bad angle mid-strike. Very few pilots ever carry this. |

**The lance branch, new.** Same Meeps catalog, same tiers, same costs, a genuine alternate path rather than a separate track, per the standing rule that this table is a menu, not a rail. G and F are identical to the branch above, every cadet starts on the same Stocklance and Heavylance. The fork is E-tier, where a cadet who’s found they fight better with one committed weapon than two coordinated ones stays on a single lance instead of taking the Twinlance. **Vrassik’s own progression, LOCKED to this branch:** he never picks up a second weapon, the naming staying “-lance” all the way to A-tier is itself the on-page signal, where Trav’s own branch changes its name at D to mark a philosophy shift, Vrassik’s branch keeping the same suffix the whole way is the same device pointed the opposite direction, loyalty to one weapon rather than a discovered new one.

| **Grade** | **Name** | **Cost** | **What it is** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| E | Piercelance | 1,600,000 | Narrowed tip geometry, real physics: the same kinetic energy Heavylance delivers, concentrated into a much smaller contact area, pressure is force over area, a narrower point goes through what a blunter one only bruises. The actual fork point, a cadet choosing this over Twinlance is choosing one committed weapon over two coordinated ones. |
| D | Burstlance | 4,000,000 | Adds a contained secondary charge in the tip, impact-triggered rather than remotely detonated, penetrate first, charge fires a beat later. Grounded in real shaped-charge ordnance logic, a penetrator opening the path for a payload behind it, not exotic tech. The actual mechanism behind “gore his opponent and then make them explode from the inside.” |
| C | Warlance | 15,000,000 | The philosophy-crystallizing tier, matching Trav’s own Pairblade/Arcblade beat on Vrassik’s own divergent path. Adds a magnetic coil along the shaft, real coilgun-adjacent physics already inside this setting’s tech level, accelerating the strike well past what charge-momentum and muscle alone produce, dramatically deeper penetration and a bigger payload the Burstlance tip can carry. |
| B | Ruinlance | 60,000,000 | Military-era upgrade, a multi-charge magazine, three to four payloads carried and triggered in sequence without a full reload between strikes. Real tactical cost to match: successive charges in the same engagement window draw down the coil’s capacitor, the first strike of a fight hits hardest and follow-ups taper until it recharges, no free lunch. |
| A | Havoclance | 250,000,000 | Genuinely elite. A regenerative capacitor system harvests energy from the strike’s own deceleration on impact, real energy-recovery-system logic, the same principle behind regenerative braking, and removes the B-tier taper, sustained full-power strikes rather than a diminishing string of them. The practical ceiling before Heirloom-grade, same standing as Stormblade on the other branch. |

## Munti — equipment

The Munti’s full feature set (fabricator, remote repair, cockpit evacuation, disintegrator weapon) is described in the progression doc as a complete package. Reading that against the tiered economy: academy Muntis unlock that feature set in stages as cadets earn points, full functionality isn’t standard-issue until D-grade. Military Muntis, per the existing lock, run the complete package from day one, this staged unlock is specifically an academy-era training constraint.

| **Grade** | **Name** | **Cost** | **What it is** |

| G | Quickfix kit | 200 | Basic onboard fabricator, small parts only, no remote function, has to physically dock to repair anything. |

| E | Farfix | 1,700,000 | True matter-transfer remote repair at real combat range, the actual “no physical dock required” capability the writeup describes. |

| C | Quickbox | 15,000,000 | Faster cockpit-evacuation cycle time, the same Lifebox system, meaningfully quicker, which given the existing lock that extraction speed is the actual life-or-death variable, is a genuinely bigger deal than the price tag alone suggests. |

## Power unit — G through A

Confirmed gap, new category. Every mech needs one, and this board is open from the first week of Year 1 alongside the genetic board rather than waiting for specialisation. Most cadets treat it as an afterthought next to weapons and armor, which is exactly the gap Trav’s own build philosophy exploits: he underinvests here on purpose, prioritizing weapon and mobility spend over raw power and protection, per his locked “weapon and the ability to move and react before all else” priority. A cheap power unit means less energy on tap for shields or heavy weapon draw, real, felt tradeoff, not a stat dump.

| Grade | Name | Cost | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| G | Coreline | 200 | Standard-issue reactor, no personalization, baseline energy budget every cadet starts with. |
| F | Coreline-B | 400,000 | Marginal output increase, most cadets’ first upgrade here, cheap enough not to compete with weapon spend. |
| E | Twinfeed | 1,600,000 | Dual-output line, meaningfully more headroom for a Tank’s shield draw specifically, less relevant to a Meeps build. |
| D | Steadyburn | 4,000,000 | Real output jump, the point where skipping this purchase becomes a visible, felt choice rather than just cheap thrift. |
| C | Hotcore | 15,000,000 | Late-academy tier, sustained high-output draw, standard for Tank and Munti builds by now, still skippable for a Meeps prioritizing elsewhere. |
| B | Deepwell | 60,000,000 | Military-era output, the tier most B-rank pilots actually carry. Trav notably does not, running a D-or-C-tier unit well into his B-rank service, the real mechanical signature of his build philosophy. |
| A | Fullburn | 250,000,000 | Elite tier, sustained output rivaling a small Tank’s dedicated shield generator on its own. Genuinely rare outside Tank and Munti specialists. |

## Universal genetic/stimulant upgrades

Not path-specific and not gated. Available to any cadet regardless of track, from the first week of Year 1, price alone rationing access. This is the open board on the combat-corridor wall, the narrow one in the middle of the five, and for the whole of Years 1 and 2 it is the only thing a cadet can actually spend on. Priced below path weapons at the same grade per the note above.

| **Grade** | **Name** | **Cost** | **What it does** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| G | Quickstep | 130 | Small reaction-time patch, shaves real but modest milliseconds off reflexive response. First upgrade most cadets buy simply because it’s cheap and universally useful. |
| F | Ironvein | 1,700 | Muscle density and stamina increase, more staying power across a long class or a long match, not a combat-damage upgrade on its own. |
| E | Syncline | 74,000 | Neural throughput patch, faster HUD reading and faster AI-sync response, the first upgrade that actually changes how fast a cadet’s connective AI can hand them information. |
| D | Hotwire | 340,000 | Full adrenal reflex overhaul, the real reflex jump rather than the shaved-milliseconds version G-tier offers. Genuinely sought after, real status attached to being an early Hotwire recipient specifically, per the progression doc’s “real status” note. |
| C | Overclock | 7,200,000 | Deeper AI-sync throughput upgrade, information moves between pilot and connective AI meaningfully faster, the practical ceiling of what a standard human nervous system can process before the AI itself becomes the bottleneck instead of the pilot. |
| B | Ironmind | 17,000,000 | Stress-tolerance and pain-management overhaul, genuinely relevant given the already-locked symbiosis pain-spike, though it arrives well after the roughest years rather than fixing them retroactively, worth keeping that timing honest rather than letting it feel like a missed opportunity. |
| A | Apex | 76,000,000 | Full-body overhaul at the real edge of what the human-performance-ceiling boundary allows, per the existing lock that this is exactly where genuine science stops and the genetic/stimulant line takes over. The practical top of what’s earnable before S-tier’s single narrative jump. |
