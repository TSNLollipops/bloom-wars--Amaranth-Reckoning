## PART A — ARRIVAL

### A1. The MeltaDread

The primary carrier. **One of five in the company.** Trav and Peregrine are two of hundreds joining **one of the oldest carriers still in service since the start of the war.**

**Physical description, new tonight, first hard visual on record.** Grey and grey and grey. Patched hull. A **slightly flattened egg shape.** The hangar they enter is on the **front left side.**

**[CLAUDE]** Three greys is the whole institution in one image and it should not be improved. Concept locks a ship-shape convention; this reads as compatible but wants a cross-check before it hardens.

Immediately on arrival, **Trav and Peregrine are separated to their respective units.**

### A2. Company history, from Peregrine

During the second week, Peregrine shows up with a box of food, Alina with him, and the four of them eat on the material crates in the team workshop.

MeltaDread is one of the **original hundred companies** that ran gladiator fights in the pre-war era. They are essentially **saboteurs and tricksters**, using new unhinged tactics every cycle. Sometimes it worked, sometimes it did not. Great spectacle. They will see soon enough.

**[CLAUDE, cross-reference, strong]** The Political Web locks that battlegroups carrying old sport-team names are regarded as closer to mercenary outfits than proper formations, that the oligarchy quietly resents them, and that a sport-named formation is one no house would put its name on. Which makes MeltaDread structurally one of the least prestigious and most genuinely unified formations in the Coalition, and therefore, per that same document, exactly the population able to defect cleanly later. His entire rebellion's recruiting pool is the company he was posted to at nineteen because it was the harder direction. Nobody should ever say this.

### A3. The carrier commander

**CoP**, the troop's nickname for the carrier's pilot commander, **Coherence of Process**. Carabil. Consistent with the locked naming convention (abstract process phrases, identity as function within a system) and the locked Carabil ship-piloting culture.

**The Groto** is where CoP resides aboard the ship. Trav does not go there until the fifth mission block.

### A4. The two weeks

Trav and Reqa spend the first two weeks getting his mech up to military spec and learning shipwide codes, politics, where things are and who people are, why and how. Standard acclimatisation.

**RULED, DEC-27, timing.** Somewhere in this same window, ordinary contact while the new team is still getting used to each other, Hiro infects Trav. Not combat, not near a Bloom, nothing either of them or anyone watching would ever think to flag. Dormant and sub-clinical at this dose, undetectable by current COE instruments, not the full drone-conversion Mission 8's station reveal describes later. Kept separate from why Hiro's right twice, two facts sharing a character, not one explaining the other. Still open: whether Hiro knew.

**Peregrine's single visit.** He hugs Trav once, hugs Reqa once, then talks their ears off about the requisition forms he has to fill to get material for his unit. Lunch, the three of them. He brings a box.

**Other than the hug he keeps his hands and comments to himself.** He is there because he misses them and does not want to be thrown out of the workshop for being handsy. A cool guy, just usually without a filter.

**[CLAUDE]** Restraint that costs him something, shown by absence rather than stated. This is the character's best scene so far and it is four sentences long.

## PART C — THE MISSIONS

### C0. The era's spine

What the era is built out of, besides the missions, is **the development of a massive web of weird orders.** Trav is spec ops. He does not do clean work. **RULED, corrected: the web resolves, but Trav never consciously assembles it.** He isn't built to, felt first, articulated never, per his own voice lock. What he has instead is a wrongness he can feel and can't source. The reader is allowed to get there before he does, and does.

**Reqa's line, placed right after the bunker.** Firm, not comforting. That is the path he chose. *"We gotta stick to it even if it feels dirty. One day we will be free."*

**[CLAUDE, and this is doing a lot]** She is holding him to his own decision, which costs her something to say. Three freedoms are stacked in that sentence and she means only the smallest: the literal end of a term of service. Zeteii's, whose freedom and singularity are the same object to her, is in the room hearing a human use the word. And the one that actually happens, which is rebellion. Never repeat it as a refrain. A refrain announces itself.

**Surveillance is the era's Rule 4 enforcement.** They are watched. The anger at the bunker is felt, shared through the bond, and not spoken, because speaking it is not available. That is the discipline enforced by the setting rather than by the author.

**The bond runs both directions.** Reqa gets sensor returns only. Zeteii gets feeling, both ways, always. Never thought.

### C0a. RESTOCK, AND WHY IT CHANGES THE REGISTER

**RULED, DEC-18, ratified explicitly [R-39].**

Hiro is killed in Mission 1's opening clash. Later in the same block he is back, and the squad **ribs him for getting offed by falling.** Fracrals's line about him, delivered flat while he is currently dead: *he's just waiting on restock, Derek.*

Cross-referenced against the locked battlefield-medicine rule: restock catches nearly everything, "just alive" at retrieval is enough, and death happens specifically when extraction fails. So **"getting offed" is squad slang for losing your mech and being pulled, not for dying.**

**[CLAUDE, and this is the most valuable thing tonight produced]** A squad that jokes about death because death is usually reversible teaches the reader the rule without one line of explanation. Which means the mission where it is *not* reversible needs no narration at all. The reader has already been taught, by watching people laugh.

**Consequence for drafting:** Team One's wipe needs a stated cause and **extraction failure is the strongest available**, because it is the one condition the locked rule says kills people. It also means the squad's own running joke is what fails them.

### C1. Mission 1 — the city sweep and the bunker

**Deployment.** Trav deployed groundside with roughly twenty others to clear a **mid-size city that looks ruined, and old-ruined**, different from what is normal for COE far-area worlds. Its building layout is different from what he believes standard. **No comment from his unit except Hiro**, who goes on about rebellious houses left to fight by themselves.

**RULED: Hiro is right.** The world is what he says it is. This is not a theory left hanging.

**The sweep.** They encounter about a dozen different Bloom units and kill them. Minor wounds all around. Fracrals comments on the low number. Derek asks whether pinging the boss for more data would get them a demerit. Hiro comments on the absurdity of what more data would even mean here. Trav keeps his focus on his senses, because something feels off.

**The ambush.** Trav stops moving as his team walks on. Then the floor collapses under them and there is no time to think. A squad of four enemy mechs. Tunnels.

- **Hiro dies in the opening clash**, because the fall spoils Derek's aim. (See C0a: he restocks.)

- Trav jumps down and kills the enemy Meeps who was charging Fracrals.

- Fracrals takes the opening and puts a small tachyon lance through the enemy Muntis, **easily recognised by the squarish box on their backs.**

- The Munti is erased and it is an easy mop-up.

Derek: *"So boss, is now a good time to ping the boss for more data?"*

**RULED, and this is the load-bearing fact of the era.** The ambushing mechs were **the house's own surviving defenders.** Not Bloom, not Bloom-adjacent. The people who had been holding that ground and that door.

**[CLAUDE, drafting rule]** Write the ambush clean and fast the first time. Tactically flawless, no hesitation, no wasted motion. The square box that lets the squad kill efficiently is a support unit's marker, not a monster's, and it is the single most damning image in the mission. Do not dress it or slow it down. Let it read clean. That is what makes the truth land harder on the second pass.

**Reinforcement.** They hold the zone. Reinforcement arrives with a replacement Reeps, **Yren Tourignie.** Derek says he will miss the scenery commentary Hiro used to give. Fracrals: *he's just waiting on restock, Derek.*

**Underground.** Another team holds the entrance. Trav's team goes into the tunnels.

**The bunker.** Not a sessile tomb. A **true fallen-house survivor population.** The door has been **grown over.** Mech corpses and Bloom corpses around it. Derek cuts it clean. Inside: civilians, starved, dirty, alive, seemingly untouched.

**The order.** The team is told to clean the place. Command says they are infected. The evidence is cloudy. *"But orders are orders,"* Fracrals says. *"Kid. Stay at the door. Make sure none run out."*

**Fracrals actually did most of the cleaning, with Derek. Trav and Yren were told to guard the door because they were both new.**

**Trav does not save them.** He is held at the door and does nothing. **His outrage at the COE starts here.** He will fight to protect the citizens, but one day they will be free. **Kept entirely in his head, because everything is watched.** Zeteii, connected, feels it. Neither says anything.

**[CLAUDE]** *"Kid. Stay at the door. Make sure none run out."* is the line. Protection and complicity in the same order, and it does not need one word of comment. Everyone who died at that door died for people the squad then killed. Nobody in the scene ever knows that. This is Rule 4 at maximum.

**Content flag, author's own.** This chapter needs a trigger warning. Civilian executions, fade to black.

**Aftermath.** The rest of the mission is a blur to Trav. Once home, he has a talk and a good cry with Reqa. Then he is called for another mission and gets back in the cockpit, and **it is Zeteii who talks him out of his funk.** The genuine start of their bond. She reads his emotional cues from the internal sensors and talks to him about how satisfying it is to fight the Bloom, ignoring entirely that they just fought humanoid remnants and cleaned a civilian bunker.

**[CLAUDE]** She is not comforting him. She is changing the subject, badly, on purpose or not, and it works. That is a much stranger and better beat than comfort, and it is the first thing she ever does *for* him.

**[GAP]** Mission 1's debrief. Not recovered.

### C3. Mission 3 — the sessile tomb, and the end of Team One

**RULED, DEC-17 and DEC-19. ### C4. Mission 4 — the seam scan

Team Two's first. **Escorting scientists on another world**, there to take readings of the Bloom. Some combat, few casualties, nobody dies.

**The exposition mission, and it is doing real work.** The scientists explain **the seams**: the Bloom's spreading form is weak where the various organisms composing it are joined. Normally that junction is very difficult to find, which is why they are here, studying Synkers fighting the spreading form in order to learn to see it.

**Terminology, locked here:** the **spreading form** is the Bloom's mobile combat body. A **sessile** is a spreading form that has stopped and taken root.

The scientists ride a **VTOL** with good sensors, shadowing the Synkers as they traipse through Bloom country. Twenty strong, good odds of contact, and the VTOL can direct the group along.

Fights happen. **Synkers get better at killing Bloom as the scientists finally manage to sense the seams.** Test completed. Mission ends. Soldiers are happy, because it means they will soon have a **critical hit module**.

**[CLAUDE, flag]** The seams have no anatomy, appearance, or vocabulary anywhere in the Bestiary, Bioterror Bank, or Technobabble Glossary. The Open Items Register already rates this High risk. This mission is where they become real and it cannot be drafted without them existing first.

**Aftermath, one day of R&R.** Trav eats with Reqa and spends most of it trying to joke. It falls kind of flat. Reqa just starts talking rune and gets him to follow her onto her project idea. **Zeteii comments: deadly beams soon? Reqa: maybe one day. Soon, yeah.**

**[Recovery beat, no conflict, and worth protecting]** Trav helps Reqa with a divergent point in a knot calculus that seems to depend on chance to find a solution. He tells her the path taken is probably just the easiest one, and to make it follow what they want they have to make it *want* what is next on the menu. Give it a path. To make the severance cut, just give it something to aim for.

**[CLAUDE]** This is the founding insight of **The Divider**, Reqa's Heirloom weapon, already locked in Weapons and Progression as the thing that severs the bond of reality at the edge. He does not know he has done it. She does. And "give it something to aim for" reads entirely differently the second time.

### C7. Mission 8 — the research facility

Sent to clean a research facility because **the agents there went rogue. Human opponents.** After many battles and some horror, they are told to **destroy the data.** And they do.

**The research was on the Bloom. The Bloom can infest Enlightened too, at the sessile state, turning them into brainless drones, even through the station shielding.**

Once the data is destroyed they return home for debrief and **Trav is told not to say a word of what he saw.**

**The station is destroyed by a dreadnought shot. Obliterated.** The shockwave makes even the carrier vibrate with the inertial dampeners on. (Or not. Bullshit tech, hand-wave, author's own note.)

**What Trav does not know: the data was already beamed aboard. The destruction was cinema.**

**[CLAUDE, and this is the mission that turns the web from texture into plot]** Mission 1's order was an atrocity. Mission 3's was disposal. This one is the first order Trav *obeys correctly and completely* and it still turns out to have been theatre. That is a different and worse feeling than being made to do something monstrous, and it is the one that would actually make a person start counting backwards through the other orders.

**RULED, DEC-20. Yes, it complicates once**, but not inside Book 8's own roster. ### C9. First contact with Coherence of Process

Trav and his team are called to **the Groto**, where the Carabil resides in the ship.

**The order:** they are being sent with one of the escorts to **retrieve the contents of a computer in a compound on a safe world.** They are to **act with haste and impunity.** The target is holding important data hostage. They will be the **distraction for an infiltration unit.** They are not to worry about collateral.

**What Trav is not told.** The compound is on a **preserve world.** The group there was **researching Bloom FTL**, and the raid is ultimately to slow that research down, because the oligarchy does not want the Bloom defeated yet.

**[CLAUDE, cross-reference, and this closes a real hole]** Preserve worlds are already locked as deliberately untouched by COE terraforming by treaty, visually distinct because nothing has been smoothed or optimised. Trav walking through a world that looks *wrong* in the opposite direction from the fallen-house city, unsmoothed rather than ruined, and not having the vocabulary for either, is the same device twice pointed opposite ways. He noticed the building layout in Mission 1 and said nothing. He will notice this and say nothing. Neither observation ever gets explained to him.

**The oligarchy's motive, full statement, first time on record.** See the cosmology document. In brief: they want the war to last a few hundred more years, because that is how long it takes to erase all record of what the COE was before the war. They also want the fighting optimised, to keep civilians occupied while the history is rewritten. They believe they can defeat the Bloom once total control is established, with a limited release of S-grade tech to selected units. **They are wrong, and the reason they are wrong is arithmetic.**
