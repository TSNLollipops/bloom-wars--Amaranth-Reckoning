## Trav Calder

**Role:** Protagonist. **Species:** Human. Surname Calder, decided here for the first time so his mother’s entry has something to share with him.

**Physical portrait, LOCKED:** unremarkable at rest, which is exactly the point, nothing about him reads as a protagonist until he’s moving. Wiry rather than broad, weight already shifted forward onto the balls of his feet even standing still in a mess-hall line, a body that learned proximity-triggered reflex before it learned to relax. Hands calloused specifically along the first two fingers and the heel of the palm, the lance-then-blade grip pattern, visible proof of what he actually does with his time rather than what he says about it. A thin, pale line along his left forearm from Book 3’s real injury, cosmetically correctable and never corrected, not out of vanity or stubbornness, just genuinely never occurred to him that it mattered enough to bother with, exactly the kind of detail his non-reflective processing produces without meaning to. Eyes track motion before faces, a habit that reads as inattentive in conversation and precise in a fight, the same trait wearing two different names depending on the room. Leaner year over year as his build philosophy shifts toward flexibility (see the Visual Reference Bank), the chassis change and the physical change tracking each other, armor traded for speed on both the mech and the man piloting it.

**Coloring, LOCKED, new 2026-08-15:** browned/tan skin, visibly mixed at a glance rather than blended past recognition, matching the already-locked African/Chinese ancestry (Qiraki_Physical_Description_Bank.md). Wavy black hair. Almond-shaped eyes, reading brown-black. On the shorter side for his age.

**On the page, LOCKED, new 2026-08-15:** deliberately never self-described. Ch.1-2’s existing pattern (tight first-person present tense, no mirror, no self-inventory) holds going forward rather than getting broken for this. The coloring above is bible-canon for reference and adaptation purposes, not something the prose needs to state directly.

**Future beat, flagged, not yet placed, author’s estimate Book 8+:** the first time anyone actually remarks on his coloring or build on the page, it’s Zeteii, post-liberation once she has her own avatar and voice (see her own sheet), needling him that his build reads like his mother’s side more than his father’s. Exact placement open, no outline exists yet to hold it, flagging here so it doesn’t get lost.

**Family, LOCKED:** father died in the war before the story starts, background fact Trav wouldn’t naturally dwell on or bring up given his established non-reflective, forward-only processing, present in his history rather than something the narration returns to. Mother, Yssa Calder, works as a mechanic, hands-on mech repair and maintenance, no connective-AI/pilot-interface involvement at all. Meaning Trav grew up around the wrench-and-repair side of mech work specifically, not the piloting side, long before enrolling, and grew up around that kind of labor as unglamorous and ordinary. Worth noting as a quiet thread: his comfort around mechanics generally, Reqa included, may trace back to this well before any romantic feeling develops. They stay in intermittent email contact across the years once he’s off-world, see Yssa Calder’s own entry for the pattern.

**Warming-up arc, LOCKED:** genuinely reserved and quiet at arrival (consistent with Ch.1-2’s established voice, “I don’t tell him I’m curious, it feels like the wrong thing to admit out loud”). Humor and ease with people isn’t innate, it’s a practiced skill, built specifically through years of trying to make Reqa laugh, a new joke every time, never repeating one. This is training, not talent, the same discipline logic already governing everything else in this world. The payoff surfaces later, during the difficult, unglamorous early months of special forces deployment, once Vrassik joins him and survives alongside him: Trav becomes visibly more open and animated, less shy, and it should read as years of quiet practice with Reqa finally having somewhere real to go, not a sudden personality shift out of nowhere.

**The reversal, LOCKED, the actual mechanism behind the warming-up**** ****payoff above:** the ritual was built one-directional, Trav cheering Reqa up, for years. It turns for the first time in Book 4, after Soren’s death or a bad Vrassik clash (exact trigger open at draft time), when Reqa uses the same “new joke, never repeated” ritual on him instead, the first time it’s ever run in reverse. This is the actual origin of what becomes his whole social armor by Books 5-6, not a personality shift, humor stops being purely a gift he gives and starts also being something he needs, and Reqa is the one who notices that shift first, already established as the person who reads him closest. Escalates gradually across Books 4-6 against real incidents, never assigned as a blanket trait from one chapter onward, no single scene where he “becomes the funny one,” a pattern that deepens, not a switch that flips. **Hard constraint:** stays purely behavioral, never internal. Trav’s non-reflective, forward-only cognition means he can never think “I joke because it helps me cope,” that would be a stated thesis about himself, exactly the kind of thing the project’s own discipline forbids a sympathetic character from saying on the page. The pattern is shown, never explained, including in his own head.

**Combat path, LOCKED:** Melee DPS, close-range striker, see Qiraki_Weapons_And_Progression.md for the full path system. The only role consistent with his established RPD-style reflexive combat, which needs proximity to trigger, and with wound-emulation’s stakes, which are maximal at close range. Deliberately not called “Lancer” in-universe despite the genre reference that prompted the discussion, needs its own coined term consistent with the kid-game naming philosophy.

**Symbiosis teaching, LOCKED:** he can teach symbiosis, but only to Carabil specifically, because their collective/Longsight cognition is already built on the kind of deep connectivity symbiosis requires. This is the actual mechanism behind him eventually receiving his own carrier, not raw specialness alone, freed Carabil no longer bound to mandatory ship-isolation can be reassigned, straining the oligarchy’s logistics in a real, causal way. **Age at story start:** 12y 207d 4h 4m, puberty onset and academy enrollment acceptance timestamp. Stating this precisely to a new roommate or acquaintance is normal small talk in this culture, the in-universe “what’s your sign.”

**Cognition:** Split-second reactive genius, grounded in real Recognition-Primed Decision research, not raw talent alone. Poor deliberate planner for a mechanical, not personality-based reason, no built “library” for slow, high-ambiguity command decisions the way he has one for combat. Doesn’t process backward, no reflective interiority, moves forward through the next problem rather than examining the last one. Trained via repetition/wargaming, not “learning patience.”

**Arc:** Pilot -> unit commander -> fleet commander -> faction leader, four distinct thresholds, not a smooth climb. Falls for his mech before he falls for anyone. Real command failures with real, named costs required at each threshold, not private self-doubt.

**Relationships:** Reqa (romance begins only once he’s in his main combat unit, not before). His bonded AI (romance develops later, central to the endgame). His Carabil CO (planning mentor, deliberately not a romantic interest, keeps that role separate).

**Core belief, LOCKED, captured for the first time tonight in the**** ****author’s own half-asleep words:** a galaxy free of its own war-cycle, and AI explicitly included as Enlightened, argued through direct personal analogy, he was made too, by his mother, with help, but she made him, and that’s never been grounds to question his right to be free, so whoever made an AI has no right to dictate its rights either. This is the actual foundation of his trust in Zeteii and the wider Webbedminds, not naivety, a considered ethical position, likely spoken to her directly around the central node raid alongside the founding vow. See Qiraki_Concept.md for the fuller capture.

**Real origin point, LOCKED new, 2026-08-12:** this belief has an actual seed now rather than arriving pre-formed at the Node raid, see Qiraki_Character_Sheets.md’s Alina Firemoss entry for the full scene. Year 7, on learning what was actually done to her, he first reaches for something naive and sweeping, everyone should just get to be free, and she takes it apart in front of him, on the grounds that nothing resembling that has ever existed for anyone either of them knows, himself included. He doesn’t win that conversation and isn’t meant to. What survives it, refined over years without him ever consciously working the refinement, is the much narrower, more defensible claim he actually states at the Node: not freedom from circumstance, which he now half-understands nobody gets, but freedom from having your personhood dictated by whoever made you. The gap between the sweeping thing he said at nineteen and the precise thing he says years later is the actual shape of the growth, and it should never be narrated as growth, just left for a reader who remembers the earlier scene to notice on their own.

**Name note:** “Trav” chosen for an author joke not yet revealed to Claude. A 1,000-word essay is owed once it’s understood.

Same discipline as the Lally (2010) and Barrett (2017) research already grounding other locked details, real math under an invented timescale. **Ritual origin and timing, LOCKED, corrected against real research.** Original instinct had this ritual solidifying in Year 3. Checked against actual habit-formation research (Lally et al., 2010, European Journal of Social Psychology, the real study behind the popular “21 days” claim, which is itself a misreading of an unrelated 1960 book): average time to full automaticity was 66 days, but behaviors with strong contextual cues and immediate reward form much faster, sometimes in as little as 2-3 weeks. The joke-ritual has both, a specific recurring cue (double-rune days) and immediate reward (her mood visibly lifting). By that research, this is very plausibly automatic within the first couple months of Year 1, not something waiting for Year 3 to exist at all.

**Corrected mechanism:** the ritual is fully established early, Year 1, as “cheer her up after a bad rune day.” Year 3 doesn’t invent it, it gives an already-automatic habit a new context to generalize into. Once combat class expands to three days and the shared coursework block shrinks (Qiraki_Concept.md’s weekly-structure section), real multi-day separation exists between them for the first time, and the existing ritual naturally extends to that new gap, landing after combat class specifically, rather than being built fresh under pressure. More realistic than manufacturing a new habit at Year 3, and better supported by the research.

**Motivation, LOCKED:** her fascination with mechs is childhood-rooted. She couldn’t connect to the interface herself, so she redirected that same pull into wanting to build them from scratch instead, to make real the designs she sees in her own dreams. A genuine calling, not a consolation prize for being unable to pilot.

**Dream, sharpens at Year 6, LOCKED, new:** “build a mech” narrows into “build a mech carrying a Heirloom-grade weapon,” the same year Trav’s own equipment innovation is already locked, real, matched Year 6 beat for both of them, not just his. Full mechanism and timing in Qiraki_Weapons_And_Progression.md’s “Reqa’s Heirloom weapon” section, completed before Trav reaches Legion Commander rank.

**Rune-patterning arc, real growth tracked against the curriculum, not just standing crankiness, LOCKED, new:** the established dislike (cranky on double-rune days, prefers engineering) stays exactly as locked, this doesn’t soften it, it gives it a payoff. She gets good at it anyway, because the Heirloom weapon’s actual mechanism (reversal taken to its extreme, see Qiraki_Weapons_And_Progression.md) runs on the exact Year 1 backward-trace lesson she’d have found tedious at the time. Real arc shape, track against Qiraki_Rune_Tech_Reference.md’s year-by-year curriculum rather than leaving it vague: Year 1-2, actively resents it, purely a subject she tolerates for Trav’s sake and the joke-ritual’s sake. ## Coherence of Process

**Role:** Commands MC’s carrier group, trains him in command. **Species:** Carabil.

**Introduction timing, LOCKED:** first contact happens during the pilot arc, the rough early specops months, as the distant carrier commander overseeing his unit, not yet an active mentor. The relationship shifts into real mentorship later, once Trav is actually promoted to unit commander and needs what she teaches. Two distinct roles at two distinct points, not the same dynamic carried unchanged from first meeting.

**Three touchpoints across the pilot arc, LOCKED, so reaching out to**** ****her later is a conscious choice, not institutional default:**

- **Early mission debrief**, before he knows her as anything but a voice giving orders. One sharp, specific observation about his performance that nobody else caught. Plants that she’s watching him specifically.

- **A team loss, mid-arc.** She responds through performance and function, not comfort, this is deliberately her register, not a human one, a Carabil processing grief through systems and cognition rather than reaching for reassurance. **Craft flag, important:** “she says something true” is a placeholder, not content, and reads as an AI-tell if it survives unchanged into actual drafting. Whoever drafts this scene needs an actual specific line of dialogue here, concrete, performance-framed, alien in register, not a narrated summary standing in for one.

- **Late pilot arc, he witnesses her actually think through a crisis**, not command him through it, just watches her process. He recognizes something in how her mind works that his doesn’t have. This is the load-bearing beat, the direct seed of him later seeking her out deliberately once promoted, because he already knows what she does that he can’t, not because she’s assigned to him.

**Name, LOCKED:** Carabil naming convention established here, abstract process/concept phrases rather than personal or family names, “Coherence of Process” being the first example. Reflects the species’ whole cognitive orientation, identity expressed as a function within a system rather than a self set apart from one, visible in the most basic fact about a person before anything else is known about them. Other Carabil characters should follow the same convention going forward.

**Physical description, LOCKED:** a pillar of semi-transparent, starry-glittering crystal, her carrier grown organically around her rather than built and occupied. No two Carabil look alike, so this description is specific to her, other Carabil Trav meets should look visibly different. **Expanded, LOCKED:** the glitter inside her isn’t decorative, it moves, slow constellations shifting position with whatever she’s actually processing, faster and denser during a crisis, near-still during the quiet moments that eventually become Trav’s real mentorship sessions. No face, nothing that reads as an expression in any human sense, and the story shouldn’t manufacture one, her “tone” lives entirely in that internal movement and in the exact cadence of her voice, which carries a faint resonance, like it’s arriving from slightly inside a large empty room even in open air.

**Chose this role, LOCKED:** ship-piloting is voluntary among Carabil, not assigned, most often chosen by young “sprouts” (the species’ term for their juveniles) from the homeworld specifically seeking a bounded, tethered solitude, “alone yet not,” rather than duty or sacrifice as the primary motive. Worth remembering this when writing her, she wanted this, even knowing the cost, which is a different emotional register than a dutiful sacrifice would be.

**Personal history, LOCKED (see Bible Skeleton’s expanded Carabil**** ****entry for the species-wide mechanics):** she is a lithoid, and like every Carabil ship pilot, she was deliberately separated from her species’ collective cognition and trained for years to function as an isolated individual, serving now as her carrier’s actual mainframe rather than merely commanding it. Not lesser for this, explicitly, just less connected, relying on comparatively crude technology to reach her own kin. This should inform how she reads as a mentor: she already knows firsthand what it costs to hold coherence alone, which is presumably part of why she’s suited to teaching Trav something adjacent, how to function individually at a scale his own cognition wasn’t built for either. Worth letting this surface in how she teaches rather than stating it as backstory exposition.

Standard COE structure, not a special assignment, Carabil command carriers and most ships the way humanoid Enlightened pilot mechs. Deliberately kept separate from the romance role. Trains Trav through Recognitional-Planning-style repetition (wargaming, scenario drills, after-action review), should contradict him early and often, avoiding the “failure to disagree” failure mode common to experienced commanders who stop being challenged.

**Needs:** a specific early command failure of Trav’s that she calls correctly in front of witnesses, still open. (The “needs a name” note here was stale, she’s been named “Coherence of Process” since this entry’s own naming-convention lock above.)

When it surfaces on the page, one line is enough. It’s texture the reader half-notices, not a thread that gets its own scene.
