# THE QIRAKI FILES — Physical Description Bank

*Part of the Qiraki files. Answers one question: when a character speaks or** **acts on the page, how do I describe them with enough variance that six** **appearances across six chapters don’t read as the same paragraph copy-pasted,** **without contradicting what’s already LOCKED on their own Qiraki_Character_Sheets.md** **entry or drifting from their species’ own baseline. Companion to** **Qiraki_Emotion_Craft_Reference.md, same discipline, different target: EMO** **governs how a feeling gets put on the page without repeating a tic, this** **governs how a body does. Species facts themselves stay owned by** **Qiraki_Concept.md and Qiraki_Character_Sheets.md, this doc points to them** **rather than repeating them, per Qiraki_Master_Index.md’s own anti-duplication** **rule. Where this doc corrects or extends a locked fact, the correction was** **made at the owning document directly (see the change log at the bottom),** **this file carries only the craft layer on top of it.*

## Purpose and how to use this

Two failure modes this exists to prevent. First, the checklist failure: a character gets introduced with a paragraph of skin/hair/eyes in a fixed order, every time, for every character, and it reads like a character sheet was pasted into the prose. Second, the blank failure: description gets skipped entirely because nothing’s been decided, and six books in the cast has no visual reality at all beyond what’s in CAST’s own portrait notes, which were written for behavior and tells, not for the reader’s eye.

The fix already exists in miniature, twice, in this project: Trav and Zeteii’s joke ritual (never repeated, generated fresh in context every time) and EMO’s whole approach to emotion (somatic and specific, not a named-noun label reused sentence to sentence). This doc applies the same instinct to physical description. Never write the same sentence shape for the same character twice. Rotate the angle. Let the species baseline and the individual’s own CAST portrait supply the specific material so nothing here has to be invented cold at draft time.

## 1. The core discipline: five angles, rotate rather than stack

Don’t reach for all five at once. Pick one, sometimes two, per appearance, and let which one gets picked vary scene to scene. A character doesn’t need re-introducing in full every time they speak, most of the time a single detail is the whole job.

- **Kinesthetic/posture** — already the house style for the whole cast’s CAST entries (Trav’s forward-set weight, Reqa’s attentive stillness, Yssa’s exit-checking habit). Cheapest, most reusable angle, and the one already doing most of the work across the existing portraits. Draw straight from the character’s own CAST entry rather than inventing a new tell.

- **Hands** — second-most reused angle in CAST already (Reqa’s ink stains, Denic’s restless-except-in-a-cockpit hands, Ilyen’s precise small motions). Good for dialogue-heavy scenes where posture isn’t in view.

- **Voice/register** — how something gets said rather than what’s said, underused so far in CAST relative to the other two, real room to work here without colliding with anything locked.

- **One species-specific marker** — see Section 2. The single detail that couldn’t belong to any other species in the room. Use sparingly, once it’s been used for a character early on the reader doesn’t need it repeated every scene, a single callback later is stronger than constant restatement.

- **A situational/environmental detail** — light catching something, effort or fatigue changing how a baseline trait reads in the moment (Trav’s ancestry note on CAST is written specifically to be surfaced this way, sun on a forearm, a strap sitting differently on his frame, never as a standalone paragraph).

**Never:** a fixed-order recitation (skin, then hair, then eyes, every character, every time). If two consecutive character introductions in the same chapter use the same angle, swap one before it goes to the page.

## 2. Species baselines

Full facts live at the owning document, cited per entry. This section is the individual-variance layer on top: given the baseline, how does any one member of the species differ from the last one the reader met.

### Hiopi

**Facts owned by:** Qiraki_Concept.md (quadruped/centaur build, reproductive mechanism, mating-rights ritual) and Qiraki_Character_Sheets.md, Vrassik’s entry (the referenced physical baseline every other Hiopi character points to rather than repeats, now made explicit on the body-plan question, see this doc’s change log).

**Individual variance — coloring:** real frogs vary population to population by locality far more than most people expect, strawberry poison dart frogs on different islands off Panama’s Bocas del Toro archipelago range from solid red to blue-jeaned to green-and-black on populations a few kilometers apart, same species, wildly different color, driven by local selection pressure rather than anything else about the animal. Hiopi coloring works the same way, in-world driven by two things at once rather than one: the birth planet’s own environment (a jungle world’s Hiopi run differently than a Hiopi born to an arid one), and which spawning pool the reigning champion selects for that cycle’s ritual, since pool site (silt content, mineral runoff, light exposure) measurably shades that generation’s coloring, a real, in-world causal reason for an entire cohort of same-year Hiopi to share a family resemblance in tone even across unrelated bloodlines. Worth a Hiopi character remarking on it once, dryly, since it’s the kind of thing a species takes as obvious and an outsider finds strange.

**Individual variance — the hide-tone/texture tell:** already established per-character in CAST (Vrassik’s reads loud, Jifsook’s runs hot and fast, Lissrak’s reads calm and slow, Krethis’s flattens hard around Trav specifically). New Hiopi characters should get their own specific version of this tell rather than reusing one already spent, this is effectively this species’ equivalent of a human’s expression, worth as much individual variance as a human face gets.

**Individual variance — scarring:** worn openly and with pride as a rule, but how it’s worn is still individual, Vrassik’s is a medal, another Hiopi’s might be a private-professional fact stated flatly, worth deciding per character rather than defaulting to Vrassik’s specific register every time.

### Carabil

**Facts owned by:** Qiraki_Character_Sheets.md (Coherence of Process’s entry, the species’ physical baseline: lithoid, no two individuals look alike, grown into a ship rather than piloting one) and Qiraki_Concept.md (the corrected “uplift” history, added this session, see change log).

**Individual variance — shape and color:** already locked that no two Carabil look alike, this doc adds the mechanism. Color is personal preference, not biology-fixed, and ranges the full visible spectrum, worth treating like a chosen aesthetic (closer to how a human picks clothing than to eye color) rather than a fixed trait to describe once and never revisit, a Carabil could plausibly shift their own coloring over a long enough timescale the way a person redecorates. **Cross-species**** ****perception, new:** Osnius genuinely see more color than humans do, a real, grounded distinction, more photoreceptor types means finer discrimination between wavelengths a trichromatic human eye reads as the same shade (the real-world version: human trichromacy vs. a tetrachromatic visual system, well-documented across birds and some other animals). Practical consequence for prose: a scene with both a human and an Osnius POV-adjacent character present can legitimately have them describe the same Carabil’s coloring differently without either being wrong, worth using at least once somewhere for the texture rather than leaving it a stated fact nobody notices on the page.

**Individual variance — the internal glitter/constellation movement:** already locked for Coherence of Process specifically (slow constellations, denser and faster under load, near-still in quiet moments). Treat her specific rhythm as hers, a new Carabil character needs their own version of what that internal movement looks like and means, not a copy of her pattern.

### Osnius

**Facts owned by:** Qiraki_Concept.md, corrected this session, see change log below for exactly what changed and why. Bear-adjacent, not human-passing, the earlier “near-indistinguishable from human” line is gone, corrected against Bruvald and Peregrine Thale’s own CAST entries (both already written bear-adjacent before this pass caught up to them) and the author’s direct steer.

**Individual variance — coloring by lineage:** black through brown and grey to white, tied historically to homeworld region, no social weight left in it. Practical use: this is a clean way to give a new Osnius character a specific, grounded visual identity in one word (a black-coated Osnius reads differently on the page than a white-coated one) without needing to invent anything, and it’s a detail that can be dropped once, early, and not need repeating, same principle as the hide-tone tell above.

**Individual variance — the eye-luminosity marker:** locked, universal, unchanged, the one truly fixed Osnius trait. Individual variance lives in how a given character relates to it, not in the trait itself. Bruvald’s reads faintly warmer than baseline, unexplained, just true. Peregrine’s gets deliberately lit and shown off, real vanity. A third Osnius character should get a third relationship to the same fixed trait, ignoring it entirely is itself a valid choice.

## 4. Patterns to avoid

- The fixed-order recitation, see Section 1.

- Leading a character’s very first physical beat with their species marker before anything individual, it flattens them into “a Hiopi” or “an Osnius” before they’re a person, save the species marker for the second or third appearance once the individual’s already established.

- Reusing another character’s already-spent specific detail on a new character of the same species (a second Hiopi getting “faintly ridged hide” described in the same words as Vrassik’s entry rather than that character’s own version of the trait).

- Describing a character’s ancestry or species baseline as though it’s the reason for a personality trait. Ancestry and species biology are physical facts, not character explanations, keep them where CAST’s existing behavioral portraits already live, adjacent, not load-bearing.
