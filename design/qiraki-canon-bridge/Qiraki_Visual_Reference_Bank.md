# THE QIRAKI FILES — Visual Reference Bank

*Part of the Qiraki files. Shared descriptive material for consistent** **imagery across the whole project, vistas, spaceships, mechs, and** **bioterror. Built from image research plus original synthesis, not meant** **to be copied directly into prose, meant to keep description consistent** **scene to scene without reinventing the visual language every time.*

## Transport ships

Building on Chapter 1’s cargo-first personnel transport, imagined in more detail:

- **Silhouette:** boxy, flat-paneled, no aerodynamic curve anywhere on it, built for atmosphere-to-orbit hops and short system transit, not elegance. Engines mounted low and rear, visibly bolted rather than integrated, maintenance access panels outnumbering windows by a wide margin.

- **Boarding:** single rear ramp, wide enough for cargo pallets, which is the actual design priority, personnel boarding through it is a secondary use the ramp tolerates rather than was built for.

- **Hull:** matte grey-green, service scuffs and re-paint patches visible up close, a working vehicle rather than a showcase one, no academy or Coalition branding beyond a small stenciled registry number near the ramp.

- **Interior, already established in Ch.1:** bench seating both walls, cargo webbing overhead, standard-issue grey uniforms folded on laps, boots that fit almost nobody exactly right.

- **Scale reference for later, larger vessels:** this transport is the smallest class in the setting. Escort ships, cruisers, and carriers (see Qiraki_Concept.md’s ship-scale section) should read as dramatically larger and more ornate by comparison, ornate meaning angular and capital-ship-grand rather than transport-utilitarian, the visual gap between “the thing that gets you there” and “the thing you eventually command” should be enormous and deliberate.

## Mechs

General design language, split by established class:

- **Meeps (Trav’s class):** built for silhouette efficiency, narrow profile, visible joint articulation at shoulders and hips rather than smoothed-over housing, since a Meeps unit needs the widest possible range of motion for close-range cutting work. As his build shifts toward flexibility over durability (per the locked weapon progression, lance to twin laser-blades by y6/7), the chassis itself should read as progressively leaner and less armored across the books, a visible, physical record of the build philosophy shift.

- **Tank class:** the visual opposite, broad, slab-sided, weapon hardpoints prioritized over mobility, the kind of silhouette that reads as “built to still be standing” rather than “built to move.”

- **Reeps class:** long-limbed relative to torso size, sensor/targeting housing visibly oversized compared to other classes, the silhouette itself should communicate “this unit sees before it shoots.”

- **Munti:** utility-first, visibly asymmetric, the fabricator/repair housing bulking out one side of the frame, the cockpit-evacuation system’s launch mechanism visible as a distinct back-mounted unit, should never be mistaken for a combat-first silhouette even at a glance, its whole shape says “support,” which is part of why an enemy targeting one specifically reads as especially cruel.

- **Heirloom-grade units:** deliberately ornate compared to earned-tier gear, visible non-functional decoration, filigree, house colors, crests, the opposite instinct from every earned-tier chassis above, which are built to hide unnecessary detail rather than display it.

- **Hiopi centauroid chassis, new, cross-cutting the class list above**** ****rather than replacing it.** Body plan is a separate axis from combat path. Every class above (Meeps, Tank, Reeps, Munti) still applies to a Hiopi pilot, layered onto a quadrupedal base instead of a bipedal one, Vrassik’s own mech reads as a centauroid Meeps build, narrow-profile torso and arms for close-range work, mounted on four digitigrade legs rather than two, same silhouette logic as human Meeps at the shoulders and up, completely different logic from the waist down. **Grounded, per the project’s own discipline, in two real, separate**** ****bodies of research rather than invented from nothing:**

- **Stability.** A bipedal chassis is never actually standing still, it is running a continuous active-balance correction loop, the same real control problem (the Zero Moment Point criterion) that makes bipedal robots hard to build and easy to knock over mid-correction. A four-legged stance is statically stable by default, it can brace, absorb an impact, or stand dead still without spending processing or power to not fall over. That freed budget is the actual in-fiction reason centauroid units read as such committed melee platforms, the balance problem a bipedal Meeps chassis is always quietly solving simply doesn’t exist here, and the power a bipedal unit spends on it goes into the charge instead.

- **Charge momentum.** The real, historical reason cavalry favoured the couched lance over a swung weapon: a mounted charge transfers the mount’s own momentum through a rigid weapon rather than an arm’s, and kinetic energy scales with mass times velocity squared, not just muscle. A four-legged chassis at a dead run carries dramatically more of both than any bipedal unit generates through torso rotation alone. This is the actual mechanical reason the lance philosophy survives among Hiopi pilots specifically, rather than every Meeps eventually converging on Trav’s own blade endpoint, a charging quadrupedal chassis is a fundamentally better lance platform than a bipedal one will ever be, not a cultural preference dressed up as physics.

- **Pilot fit, a smaller, quieter third point.** A Hiopi pilot’s own proprioception, four legs, two arms, maps onto this chassis with zero body-plan mismatch, the same principle real prosthetics and teleoperation research already documents, control latency and error both rise with body-schema mismatch between operator and machine. A human Meeps pilot and a bipedal chassis are already a matched pair for this same reason. A Hiopi pilot in a bipedal chassis historically wasn’t, which is the real, unstated institutional reason the centauroid chassis exists at all rather than every Hiopi cadet simply learning to fight bipedal.

- **Bespoke cost, texture only, never stated on the page:** a body-plan-specific chassis is a real institutional accommodation, worth remaining aware of as background economic texture (who gets one issued standard versus who has to fight for the requisition) without ever being the thing a chapter is about.

- **Osnius mech customization, whiskers, LOCKED, promoted from Qiraki_Concept.md, this is now that entry’s authoritative home.** Osnius pilot the standard bipedal chassis, the same default body-plan match every earned-tier class already assumes, no bespoke chassis the way Hiopi now have. Their own visible personalization sits at the faceplate, whisker-like antennae rather than a structural change to the frame underneath.

- **Function, confirmed, not decoration.** Grounded in real, active robotics research on biomimetic whisker arrays, modeled on rodent whisker sensing, genuinely used for close-range tactile and proximity detection exactly where cameras struggle, low visibility, cluttered close range, contact registering before sightline does.

- **Integration.** Feeds directly into the same symbiotic sensory stream every other mech sensor already feeds an Osnius pilot through the connective interface, not a separate readout or HUD element sitting apart from the rest of what the pilot feels. An object brushing the mech’s own face registers the way any other proximity data already does, immediate and felt, not observed on a screen.

- **Visual note:** length and density can vary by individual customization the way any other personal mech detail does, worth treating as a real character-specific choice when a given Osnius pilot’s mech gets described, rather than a uniform, standard-issue look across the whole species.

## Vistas

Reference pull for planetary/alien landscape variety, useful for the established “gritty, gory, and gorgeous” ambition and the “spend real words on the land, not just the threat” instruction from earlier sessions:

- **Terraformed-in-progress worlds:** useful recurring image, visible seams between terraformed and native terrain, an unnaturally green valley running straight up against untouched rust-colored native rock, the exact line where human atmosphere processing simply stops.

- **Bloom-touched worlds, pre-purge:** worth developing further once the bestiary’s swarm-type mechanics get used in an actual scene, landscape visibly reshaped by biomass growth, structures overtaken rather than destroyed outright, a wrongness in color and texture more than obvious ruin.

- **Preserve worlds:** deliberately untouched by COE terraforming by treaty, should read as visually distinct from Coalition-standard worlds specifically because nothing’s been smoothed over or optimized, useful contrast device whenever a preserve appears on the page.

- **Academy/core-world environments:** functional and engineered, weather likely controlled or heavily managed, worth deciding at some point whether “real” uncontrolled weather itself becomes an exotic experience for a core-world-raised character the way an actual farm already is for Trav specifically.
