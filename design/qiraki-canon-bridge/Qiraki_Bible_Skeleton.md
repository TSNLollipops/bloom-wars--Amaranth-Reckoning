## 1. Premise, one line

A talented but reflectively-empty pilot rises through a militarized academy pipeline, learns to command at real cost, and ends up confronting what his own trusted AI became after he asked it, once, to protect him completely.

**Working title, LOCKED:** *Enlightened*, possibly *Enlightened: The Synker Wars* as a full title, or “The Synker Wars” reserved as an arc-name for the later military-campaign books. “Qiraki” stays the internal project name only, spoiler-locked, never on a cover.

## 2. Setting

Humanity spread across most of the galaxy, allied with a handful of other spacefaring species as the **Coalition of Enlightened (COE)**, having earned that alliance by being first to reach spacefaring status. An external, mindless (at least at first) threat, colloquially **The Bloom**, has been attacking COE space for roughly sixty years by story start.

**Population scale, LOCKED, explicit calibration:** this is a planetary-scale, pan-galactic civilization, not a small, intimate population. Academies exist at planetary scale, number varies by planet, sometimes a planet’s academy sits on a different nearby world entirely. Explicit anti-comparison on file: this is not Hogwarts and its tiny sorcerer population, resist defaulting to small numbers just because the named cast is small. A tight cast inside an enormous, faceless population is the intended contrast.

**Naming philosophy, LOCKED:** most in-universe slang traces to a culture raised on kinetic-pad and tablet games, simple, punchy, often two common words smashed together. Exceptions: species’ own self-descriptions (Longsight, Sprout) and Sovereign/Heirloom-grade naming, which deliberately breaks the convention since inherited privilege should sound different from earned gear.

## 3. Timeline (loose, book-scale)

An official year framework can be established once the story reaches Devereux-Kastel, a wealthier institution with the kind of bureaucracy that would actually stamp dates on things. Playfully acknowledged: this setting borrows freely across many franchises already, one more borrowed flourish (a distant, unspecified millennium) fits right in rather than standing out.

## 4. Structure discipline

Four visible thresholds of authority, each its own part of the story: pilot -> unit commander -> fleet/carrier commander -> faction leader.

**Rank vs. command position, LOCKED, two separate axes, not a conflict:** “rank” (D through S, same letter scale as gear and connective-AI grade) is an individual soldier’s general standing, climbed alone regardless of whether they lead anyone, gated by connective-AI level per the points economy. All soldiers start D-rank out of the academy. “Command position” is the four-tier authority structure above, a separate merit-based track layered on top of rank. A soldier can sit at high rank and never hold a command slot, or be pulled into a command role without being the highest-ranked soldier around, per Trav and Vrassik’s mechanism (each inherits a CO slot via the other’s promotion, not a transfer, not strictly rank-ordered).

**Academy years, tightened seven-book outline, LOCKED:**

- *The King and the Climb* — arrival, illiteracy to literacy, King of the Hill exam.

- *Flags and Foundations* — Capture the Flag exam, first institutional friction, class-choice debate chapter (Tank/Meeps/Reeps/Munti).

- *The Choosing* — class specialization locks, path-system access opens, first real injury, symbiosis’s pain-spike begins.

- *Blood Between Rivals* — the roughest year, Trav/Vrassik injure each other repeatedly, first loss, Vrassik’s mating-rights win.

- *The Weight of It* — combat weight increases, Cradle Circle interference turns visibly unjust, Ilyen repositions Reeps to Munti.

- *What It Costs to Leave* — the definitive Trav/Vrassik rivalry year, single Devereux-Kastel transfer slot, Trav wins, explains why Vrassik never gets the elite pipeline. Equipment innovation year (lance to improved sword/servos), doubly explained by both curricula converging (see Section 10).

- *Devereux-Kastel* — transfer academy, requisition-politics thread (running all book, seeds his later carrier-command skill), graduation exam, first contact with Zeteii.

**Chapter-level devices, LOCKED:**

- Daily-rhythm template: breakfast/social, class 1, class 2, class 3, optional evening beat, for ordinary days needing full length.

- Study-session chapters: Trav and Reqa learning together, subjects pulled from the rune-tech/engineering catalogs, tracked in Qiraki_Book_Expansion_Plan.md’s subject tracker.

- Trope discipline: every deployed trope needs to actually resolve within the book that uses it, not just get planted and left open, and separately still needs a planted downstream echo later in the series. Two distinct obligations, see Qiraki_Book_Expansion_Plan.md.

- **Relationship trope loop, LOCKED, extends trope discipline specifically to bonds:** naming a pilot’s mek, or seating a cadet permanently on Trav’s team, opens a trope the same way any other deployed trope does, and creates the same two-part obligation. In-book: the relationship needs to visibly warm and develop across the academy years on the page, not just exist as a name on a roster. Downstream: that closeness needs to actually cost or pay off something later in the series, a joke that lands harder in year 6 because it started awkward in year 1, a name that hits differently on a casualty list specifically because the reader watched the bond form first. A mek who barely gets screen time still needs to be missed if lost, which only works if the bond read as real, however briefly, before that happens.

- **Party assembly mechanism, LOCKED, new 2026-08-23, author’s own ruling:** five-pilot Parties assemble the same way Matchset pairs do (pilot/mek, Section 10), an unstated institutional process, not a classroom accident and not a pure coincidence of dorm or arrival order. Two things happen at once, neither named on the page: cadets who click fast on their own inside the first weeks, and an unseen psych-profile compatibility pass running underneath that self-selection, nudging compatible strangers into proximity before any of them could explain why. **Consequence for the outline:** Year 1 cadets already share a Party-sized workshop bay (five stations) from the first day of the workshop chapter, well before Book 2’s team formally assembles (Qiraki_Book_Expansion_Plan.md). Book 2’s beat is the point the grouping becomes official and permanent, not the point these five first end up in a room together, which already happened in Book 1. No character ever articulates the mechanism, consistent with the project’s own discipline against stated thesis, the bay assignment simply is, and stays unexplained on the page indefinitely.

- **Academy-year tone, LOCKED:** every academy-year chapter runs two tracks at once, real curriculum/systems content (study-session chapters, the points economy, the combat and engineering curricula) and genuine character interaction, bromance, friendship, chosen-family texture building among Trav’s cohort and their meks. Neither track waits for the other to finish, the same chapter teaching the reader a real mechanic should be doing relationship work too. All of it plays out against the explicitly cold backdrop already locked elsewhere, no participation bonus, real casualty rates even in early-grade missions, the Cradle Circle pipeline running twelve-year-olds through a mandatory survival curriculum. The warmth between characters is the load-bearing contrast against a system that doesn’t care whether any of them live, not an escape from that system, a direct answer to it.

- Propaganda-fragment chapter openers: short in-universe official text contradicted by the chapter’s own content, no connecting commentary. Bank in Qiraki_Propaganda_Bank.md, which now includes a dedicated seven-year Bloom curriculum sub-bank (three fragments per academy year, escalating from sanitized taxonomy toward the near-truth, “Qiraki” never named).

- Combat class schedule: Years 1-2, Friday/Saturday only. Year 3+, full Thursday-Saturday. Uneven weekly split creates double-rune and double-engineering days, the real mechanism behind Reqa’s crankiness (and, on the mirror day, her favorite day of the week). Also the concrete reason meks end up more technically skilled than pilots by graduation.

Mission-chapter rhythm (military-era): minimum 3 mission chapters per major arc beat, cooldown block of 3-4 chapters after roughly 6-7 running mission chapters.

## 6. Species

- **Human** — pipeline species, split between piloting culture and a heavily subsidized underclass the military draws from disproportionately.

- **Osnius** — bear-adjacent humanoid, closest species to human. Fiercely protective of their females, males duty-restrained testosterone. Overrepresented in frontier forces. Honor Guard stands aside at the central node raid, likely tied to the node’s location and their protectiveness extending to the collective itself.

- **Carabil** — lithoid, mineral/crystal physiology, no two individuals look alike, ships grown organically around a chosen pilot rather than built and occupied. Longsight (long-horizon strategic cognition) is the species’ own term for itself, likely the actual substrate of their normal collective/networked cognition. Ship-piloting requires deliberate separation from that collective, trained over years, voluntary, chosen most often by young “sprouts” seeking a bounded “alone yet not” solitude. Naming convention: abstract process/concept phrases (Coherence of Process) rather than personal names, identity as function within a system.

- **Hiopi** — quadruped, centaur-built, frog/lizard hybrid. Rabbit-fast reproduction, annual bloody winner-take-all mating fights, one male per “city” (a rule that extends to carriers and to Devereux-Kastel, both qualifying as their own city by scale). Was per-planet originally, scaled down because planet-wide fights never ended. The pilot sport’s true origin: predates the 60-year war, directly inspired by witnessing a Hiopi city-fight, wound-emulation built to import genuine stakes into a species that had made its own combat sport consequence-free. Academy year-end exams are folded into the ritual for Hiopi cadets specifically, a genuine survival mechanism (without it, most don’t survive past their second year of participation), not exempting them, measurably improving their odds. Long-timeline: the sport eventually expands pan-species inside the ritual’s framework, Hiopi competitors get a relationship bonus for placing well. **Mech chassis, new:** pilot a bespoke centauroid chassis rather than the standard bipedal frame, body plan is a separate axis from combat path, a Hiopi Meeps pilot still fights Meeps-style, just mounted on four legs instead of two. Grounded in real quadrupedal stability and cavalry-charge physics, the actual mechanical reason the lance survives as a live doctrine choice for Hiopi pilots specifically rather than every Meeps converging on a blade. Full writeup, not duplicated here, in Qiraki_Visual_Reference_Bank.md’s Mechs section.

- **Jhubi** — scaly, small humanoid, work as caretakers for children of busy parents. Conquered by the Hiopi before humans arrived, then the Hiopi were conquered by humans in turn, buried, unremembered history.

- **Gytb** — mollusk-adjacent, small population, played bluntly as unattractive. Trav learns about them (and the preserve system generally) only after freeing Zeteii.

- **Doorax** — common pet, has replaced cats culturally, fennec/arctic- fox-adjacent, docile, makes a nose whistle instead of purring.

- **26 preserve species** — pre-spacefaring civilizations kept deliberately undeveloped under COE “protection,” most getting a line or two, existing specifically to draw a parallel between what Zeteii later builds and how the COE actually treats peoples like these. Full roster in Qiraki_Preserve_Species.md.

## 7. The Bloom / the true enemy

No real mind, at least not as it presents to the Coalition, closer to wildfire or cancer. What hit Coalition space was runoff, an early diffuse scattering of seed-material drifting ahead of a much larger consumption event finishing in a neighboring galaxy. Deliberately NOT the oligarchy’s fault, kept fully separate from human politics on purpose.

**True name is**** ****“********Qiraki,********”**** locked as a major late-story reveal. See the spoiler lock in Qiraki_Process_Notes.md before writing this word anywhere in prose.** Colloquial name in general use, “The Bloom.”

**War state, LOCKED, grounds the oligarchy’s complacency in an actual number:** the war is not going badly for the oligarchs. Over 66 years they’ve lost an average of 35% of Coalition holdings, absorbable, not existential, and the army grows roughly 10% a year, outpacing losses. Even an indefinite Bloom presence is something the Home Fleet can hold against.
