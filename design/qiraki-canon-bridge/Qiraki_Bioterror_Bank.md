# THE QIRAKI FILES — Bioterror Descriptive Bank

*Part of the Qiraki files. Fills the open slot flagged in** **Qiraki_Visual_Reference_Bank.md (**“**Bioterror / the Bloom — Left open,** **author’s own material to build here**”**) and gives Qiraki_Bestiary.md’s** **modular stat categories an actual descriptive/sensory layer to roll** **against. Bestiary answers** **“**what does this creature do.**”** **This doc** **answers** **“**what does that look, sound, and smell like on the page.**”** **Companion, not a replacement, cross-reference rather than duplicate the** **mechanical categories.*

## Purpose and how to use this

The Bestiary generates a creature as a bundle of independently-rolled categories (Weapons, Movement, Perception, Intelligence, Endurance, Vitality, Swarm type). This doc takes each of those same categories and gives them a bank of sensory, textural language to pull from once a category’s been rolled or hand-picked, so a “spines + burrowing + seismic perception + pack-coordinated” creature reads as something specific and alien on the page rather than a stat block wearing a description. Pull, mix, and mutate freely. Nothing here is a fixed creature, it’s raw material for building one, same spirit as the Bestiary itself.

**Anchor points, already locked, govern everything below:** - Fish-egg/pod-spreading visual logic (Qiraki_Concept.md). - Mass of flesh and tentacles, many eyes and legs, biological horror. - “More bullshit overpowered than the last thing,” deliberate, escalating power creep across the series, not a flaw to correct. - No symmetry, ever. Biology reads as improvised first, engineered second (Qiraki_Bestiary.md’s design principle). A Bloom creature should never look like it was drafted on a sheet of graph paper. - Landscape-level wrongness over obvious ruin, per Visual Reference Bank’s vistas section, structures overtaken rather than destroyed, color and texture wrong before shape is.

## Weapons — descriptive bank

Same categories as the Bestiary. Each entry below is a set of textures and behaviors to draw from, not a fixed image, mutate freely and combine two or three on one creature for the “improvised, not engineered” read.

**Claws** — never a clean matched pair. Different lengths on the same limb, one hooked and one straight, joints that bend a segment too many times, or too few. Chitin that looks grown around an old injury rather than cast from a mold.

**Spines** — irregular spacing, never a tidy ridge-line. Some fused into clusters, some solitary, lengths varying enough that a few read as vestigial next to others clearly built to do real damage. Worth letting a few look accidental, overgrowth rather than weapon, until one moves.

**Acid/enzyme spray** — texture over color as the primary descriptor, viscous, threading, closer to drool or sap than a clean liquid weapon. Delivery point should look wrong for its function, a gland bulging somewhere anatomically inconvenient, a duct that clearly used to do something else.

**Sonic disruption** — described through effect on the environment and the pilot’s own body (rattling cockpit glass, a felt pressure behind the eyes through wound-emulation) more than through the organ producing it. The source organ, when shown, should look soft and internal, a throat-sac or resonating cavity, not an obvious “weapon.”

**Concussive limbs** — mass and impact over speed, described through what they do to terrain and armor plating on contact rather than through the swing itself. Good candidate for visibly mismatched limb pairs, one built for this, the others clearly repurposed from locomotion.

**Projectile biomass** — living ammunition, not inert rounds. Should read as something shed or expelled rather than fired, a wet, organic launch rather than a mechanical one. Good tie-in to the pod-spreading anchor image if the projectiles themselves are capable of independent growth on landing.

**Energy discharge (rare, high tier only)** — the one weapon type that should feel genuinely alien rather than just biological-horror-scaled- up, reserve the “more bullshit than last time” escalation specifically here. Visual cue: should still read as coming from something wet and organic (a bioluminescent chamber, a vein-threaded organ) even though the output itself isn’t. Never let it look like it belongs on a mech.

## Movement — descriptive bank

**Burrowing** — surface disturbance as the actual descriptive unit, what the ground does, not the creature itself, until it breaches. Displaced soil that’s the wrong color for the local terrain is a good, cheap tell that something’s been feeding underneath.

**Swarm-crawling** — described at the level of the mass moving, not individual units, a texture and a sound (wet clicking, shifting carapace) more than a shape. Individual units only resolve into distinct silhouettes once close enough to be a real threat.

**Flight, membrane** — never symmetrical wing pairs, tears and irregular vein-patterning read as more alien than a clean bat/insect silhouette. Membrane should look thin enough to see light through in places, wrong texture for something that size.

**Flight, spore-jet** — propulsion by expulsion rather than aerodynamics, described through the visible cloud/residue it leaves rather than clean thrust. Good, cheap way to seed environmental contamination texture in the same beat as the creature itself.

**Limbless propulsion (peristaltic/muscular)** — visible ripple moving the wrong direction relative to travel is an easy, effective wrongness cue, a body that seems to be moving away from where it’s actually going for a half-second before the reader’s eye catches up.

**Sessile** — doesn’t move, spreads instead. Described through what’s changed around it over time rather than through the organism itself in a single moment, the landscape-level wrongness anchor point applies most directly here of any movement type. **Expanded 2026-08-20, per**** ****Qiraki_Military_Era_Outline_v2.md’s locked terminology:** a sessile is a *spreading form that has stopped and taken root* — not a separate species, the same composite organism in a different phase, which matters for how it should read. Movement stops, growth doesn’t. Describe it through what a scene finds already true when the team arrives (root-mass breach in a cavern floor, ambient temperature wrong for the depth, a heartbeat where nothing should have one) rather than through anything the creature does on-page. It shouldn’t act. It should already have happened.

## Seams — the spreading form’s joined structure, new 2026-08-20

**Grounding, real biology.** The spreading form isn’t one organism, it’s a colony — the same body plan as a siphonophore (a Portuguese man o’ war is genetically several distinct animals functioning as one) or a slime mold’s plasmodial stage, separate founding organisms fused into a single working body. Real biology already names the failure mode this creates: a graft line. Two tissues joined after the fact never integrate as completely as tissue that grew continuous from one origin, the same reason a scar is measurably weaker than the skin around it and a welded joint is the first place a stressed frame cracks. A seam is that line, at Bloom scale, wherever two of the composite’s founding organisms meet.

**Why it’s hard to find.** The seams don’t look different. The spreading form’s whole survival strategy depends on the join reading as continuous tissue to both eye and sensor, texture and color matched as close as the biology allows. What doesn’t match is underneath — density, fiber orientation, how force travels through it. Tap two points on the same wall: one rings hollow, one doesn’t, and nothing about the paint told you which was which. Same asymmetry, biological instead of structural.

**Why Synkers can learn to sense it.** A synk read already tracks how a target’s mass will move under impact — a seam changes that math locally, because a joined structure transmits force differently than continuous tissue does, the same way a trained fighter can feel a weak point in an opponent’s stance before consciously seeing it. The seam-scan scientists (Qiraki_Military_Era_Outline_v2.md, Mission 4) aren’t teaching pilots something new. They’re calibrating an existing sense onto a signal that was always reaching it, just below where anyone had reason to pay attention. **Cross-reference:** same Fitts- Posner grounding already used for the combat curriculum generally, applied to a narrower, higher-stakes discrimination task.

**Combat consequence, the critical-hit module.** A targeting overlay that surfaces wherever the seam-signature reads strongest, translating a trained pilot’s sense into something a less experienced Synker can use immediately. It doesn’t create the weak point. It finds it faster than an untrained pilot would on their own.

**Visual/craft note for drafting.** No symmetry, ever, same standing rule as everywhere else in this document — a seam should never read as a clean line. Mismatched grain where two different tissues meet. A faint ridge where growth direction changes without warning. Sometimes still visibly trying to knit further on contact, ongoing biological negotiation rather than a settled scar. It should look less like a wound and more like two different things that were never supposed to touch, touching anyway, for a long time now.

## Perception — descriptive bank

**Compound-eye visual** — never a clean, uniform cluster. Some facets clouded, differently sized, angled in ways that don’t obviously cover a full field of view, unsettling specifically because it looks almost functional rather than clearly functional.

**Heat/thermal** — no visible eyes at all is the strongest version of this, described entirely through behavior, the creature tracking a pilot’s cockpit heat signature rather than the pilot’s actual movement, a specific, usable horror beat (it knows where the warm part is).

**Vibration/seismic** — stillness as the actual tell, a creature that reads as inert until footfall or engine vibration reaches it, then reacts with no visible sensory organ having done anything. Good pairing with burrowing movement.

**Chemical/pheromone** — described through what draws it rather than what it looks like sensing, blood, coolant leaks, restock residue, smell described as a hunting logic rather than an organ.

**None (blind, purely reactive)** — the most purely disturbing option on this list, precisely because there’s nothing to read intent from at all, reaction is instant and total the moment contact happens, no warning beat available to a pilot at all.

## Intelligence — how it should read on the page

Not a separate description bank, a behavioral filter applied on top of everything above.

**Reflexive only** — described through single-beat cause and effect, no hesitation, no adjustment, a creature that keeps executing the same attack even after it’s clearly stopped working.

**Pack-coordinated** — described through the *group* adjusting, not any single unit, a formation tightening, flanks reforming, without any one creature obviously “in command.” Never show a leader unless the Bestiary category specifically calls for one.

**Emergent** — the hardest and most valuable to land. No individual creature should ever look like it’s thinking. The coordination should only become visible at a zoomed-out, whole-swarm scale, something a pilot notices in hindsight rather than in the moment (see Qiraki_Concept.md Section 7 for the underlying logic, no true “mind” even here).

## Endurance / Vitality — descriptive-only for now

No numeric ranges exist yet (open item, both here and in the Bestiary). Until they do, these read on the page through pacing and damage description rather than numbers:

- **High endurance, low vitality** — wears down slowly, dies fast once actually breached. Describe a long attrition beat followed by a single, sudden collapse, not a gradual weakening.

- **Low endurance, high vitality** — describe the opposite shape, visibly damaged and struggling early, but still lethal and still moving long after it should reasonably have stopped.

- Whichever combination, avoid describing either stat directly (no “it can take more damage than most”), show it through how the fight actually goes.

## Swarm type — descriptive approach

**True (10-20 units):** describe the mass first, individuals second. Prose should mirror the swarm-crawling movement bank above regardless of actual movement type, a real threat only resolves into individually readable creatures once the encounter is already underway.

**False (single large specimen):** the inverse, full sensory attention on one body, worth spending real, specific words per the standing “gritty, gory, and gorgeous” instruction, since there’s no mass to hide behind, every visible asymmetry and texture choice actually needs to land.

## Color palette system

Color is rolled/randomized the same way the mechanical categories are, not hand-matched to a creature’s role. A concussive-limb brute doesn’t automatically get muted “durable” colors, an acid-spitter doesn’t automatically get green. Mismatch between color and function is a feature, it reinforces “improvised biology” over “engineered creature,” the same discipline the shape-language above already runs on. Roll color independently, let it land wherever it lands.

### Base palette families (roll or pick 1-2 per creature)

- **Necrotic bloom** — bruised purples, greyed reds, the color of tissue that shouldn’t still be moving. Strongest pairing with sessile/spreading types.

- **Wet viscera** — deep wet reds, glistening pinks, exposed-muscle tones. Strongest with acid/enzyme or projectile-biomass weapon types, reinforces the “wet, organic” delivery note above.

- **Chitin/mineral** — matte ambers, bone-white, iron-oxide rust tones, dry rather than wet. Strongest with claws/spines and burrowing types, reads as armor grown rather than shed.

- **Bioluminescent wrongness** — sick, saturated colors that don’t occur in the local flora, acid green, radioactive blue, warning-sign yellow, glowing from within rather than reflecting light. Strongest with energy discharge, sonic organs, or anything that needs an obvious “don’t touch this part” visual cue.

- **Ashen/bleached** — colors that look drained rather than vivid, greys and dead-whites, unsettling specifically for looking closer to dead than alive. Good option for blind/reactive perception types, reinforces the “nothing to read intent from” horror beat.

- **Deep-void iridescence** — oil-slick shifting color across a dark base, shifts hue depending on angle/light. Reserve for high-tier or named threats specifically, this is the palette family that should feel expensive and rare on the page, not a default roll.

### Application rules, LOCKED

- **2-3 colors per creature, never more.** More reads as decorated, fewer as flat. Two from the same family plus one clashing outlier (an amber-and-bone chitin brute with one sick bioluminescent-green gland) is usually the strongest combination, function-mismatch logic applies to color choice the same way it applies to weapon placement.

- **No symmetry in patterning**, same absolute rule as body shape. Color shouldn’t run in a clean, mirrored pattern down two sides of a creature, blotches, patches, and asymmetric bleed between colors read as biological, stripes and clean gradients read as designed.

- **Swarm-type true:** individual units within a swarm should NOT be color-uniform. Roll minor variation per unit or per small cluster (a handful running paler, one or two running an outlier color entirely), reinforces mass-first description, a uniform-colored swarm reads as a texture asset, not a living thing.

- **Swarm-type false (single specimen):** the opposite, this is where a genuinely unusual, specific color combination earns its keep, spend real descriptive words on it the same way the Endurance/Vitality note above asks for real words on a solo specimen’s damage.

- **Bloom-touched terrain should echo the creature’s palette**, not match it exactly. Per Visual Reference Bank’s vistas section, a landscape reshaped by a necrotic-bloom-palette infestation should shift the local color grammar toward that family without literally repainting the ground the same color as the creature standing on it.
