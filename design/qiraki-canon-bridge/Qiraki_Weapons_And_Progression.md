**THE QIRAKI FILES —**** Weapons, Tiers, and Progression**

*Part of the Qiraki files. Companion to Qiraki_Technobabble_Glossary.md, cross-reference rather than duplicate definitions.*

**Tier scale, LOCKED**

G through S, standard ascending grade convention (this genre’s most legible option, deliberately not reinvented, fits the “idiot-proof readable stats” design philosophy below). **No god-tier, deliberately.** This is science fiction, not fantasy, S is the ceiling, full stop, undecided whether that ever gets revisited, currently no.

Applies to: mechs, weapons, connective-AI individuation level, and genetic/stimulant upgrades, same scale across all systems so a player (or reader) never has to learn a second scale.

**Weapon variety, LOCKED**

No restriction on weapon type within the earned tier system, missiles, lasers, swords, kinetic maces, piledriver lances, anything, all gradeable G-S like everything else in the progression system.

**Sovereign-grade, LOCKED, a separate category, more exclusive than Heirloom-grade:** ruler-only technology, not available even to the broader ultra-wealthy the way Heirloom-grade weapons are, gated specifically to actual ruling oligarch families. Flagship example: extreme longevity tech, an S-grade upgrade layered on top of the existing 500-year baseline everyone else has access to, extending lifespan dramatically further. Not immortality, not unkillable, still mortal, still vulnerable to injury and violence, just outliving everyone else around them by sheer duration rather than any invincibility. Real narrative payoff: the same individuals never age out of power, which is a colder, more specific explanation for entrenched oligarchic corruption than institutional inertia alone, the exact people who’d need to change the system are the same people, alive and benefiting from it, centuries on. Open question for the endgame, not yet decided: does defeating the oligarchy mean removing them from power while they remain alive somewhere, or something more final, worth a deliberate choice rather than a default.

**Heirloom-grade, LOCKED, a separate category:** exotic energy weapons that read as functionally indistinguishable from magic, deliberately outside the earned points-for-gear economy entirely. Not tiered G-S, not earnable through performance, accessible only through extreme wealth. Naming convention deliberately breaks from the kid-slang philosophy governing everything else in this glossary, inherited privilege should sound different from earned gear, poor cadets get weapons named like game stats, rich ones get weapons named like bloodlines. Direct reinforcement of the class tension already running through the Hearth Bloc and Devereux-Kastel’s legacy-vs-scholarship split, gear itself becomes a visible marker of which system a given soldier answers to.

**Why stats are shown this way, in-universe justification**

The mech system displays everything as tiered stats specifically because it’s legible and idiot-proof, a design choice by whoever built the interface, not an authorial shortcut. Worth keeping consistent: any in-universe HUD or status readout a character glances at should look like a stat block, not prose description, if it’s meant to be genuinely diegetic rather than just narration dressed up as a screen.

**RESOLVED, both questions.**

**Baseline tier:** per-correct-unit reward within rune class specifically, every individual pattern traced correctly, every question answered correctly, at Month 1 value, each is worth 1 point on its own. Distinct from “class activity” (10/success at Month 1), which is the larger graded exercise or assignment tier, baseline is the smallest, most granular unit of success. **No participation or attendance bonus exists at any tier.** Showing up and training isn’t separately rewarded, it’s simply mandatory and unrewarded, since it’s what guarantees survival on its own terms, not something the points economy needs to additionally incentivize. Real, harder-edged thematic texture for a school built around twelve-year-olds: the baseline expectation here is stated plainly rather than softened.

**No reset, deliberate:** the formula climbs continuously and uninterrupted across all seven years with no annual reset. This is the actual mechanism behind the intentional snowball effect already governing the wider setting, good students accumulate points faster, earn better gear sooner, and that gear compounds their advantage further. By Year 7 (month ~84), combat success is worth roughly 1,680 points per win, a deliberately enormous late-game scale, not an oversight.

**Military-era point formula, LOCKED, second tier**

Distinct scale jump from the academy formula above, roughly 6x the academy’s Year 7 peak combat value at the very start of military service, reflecting the much higher stakes of actual deployment.

| Month in service | Per survival mission | Per kill |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 10,000 | 2,000 |
| 2 | 20,000 | 12,000 |
| 3 | 30,000 | 22,000 |
| 4 | 40,000 | 32,000 |
| 6 | 60,000 | 52,000 |
| 12 | 120,000 | 112,000 |

Both values climb by the same flat 10,000/month, keeping the gap between them fixed at 8,000 points permanently. Surviving a mission is always worth meaningfully more than any single kill, consistent with the established “one or two a mission, standard rate” reality and the “mad respect for munties” theme, staying alive and keeping the team alive matters more than combat output on its own.

**Scoring system, LOCKED, what a mission-point award is built from:** an individual’s score inside a mission is kills plus assists combined. An assist is worth a fraction of a full kill, roughly 10% to 50% depending on the actual weight of the action, not a flat half-credit. This is specifically why Munti pilots tend to score well despite rarely landing a kill themselves: healing/repair actions count as assists, and their disintegrator weapon (the repair beam run in reverse, see the full Munti writeup below) earns kill-adjacent credit on purge missions. A purge mission is typically an escort-style deployment, and it’s what Trav runs almost exclusively in his early months in the field, before his combat rating climbs him into active-zone rotation.

**Higher-rank tier, LOCKED, third scale jump:** once promoted to higher rank, likely tied to the unit-commander threshold in the already-locked four-tier authority structure (pilot -> unit commander -> fleet/carrier commander -> faction leader), per-mission points triple against whatever the current formula value is at that point in service. A new bonus is added on top: extra points per team member who returns alive. This isn’t just a bigger number, it’s the reward structure itself reshaping to match the actual job at that rank, personal survival stops being the whole incentive and team survival becomes explicitly, mechanically rewarded, directly reinforcing the command arc’s real lesson (a pilot thinks in terms of what he alone can survive, a commander has to think in terms of what an entire team can). At carrier-commander scale specifically, the score inputs widen further: enemy soldiers killed, own soldiers who survive the mission, the enemy vessel’s own destruction, its escorts destroyed, and how many of the commander’s own escorts survive all factor in, the same personal-to-team incentive shift as above, just with a battle’s worth of moving pieces instead of a squad’s.

**Starting gear and progression arc**

- **Academy training mechs:** low tier, standardized, no personalization.

- **Special forces entry (LOCKED):** Trav is issued a D-tier machine with a C-tier connective AI. The AI is C-tier specifically because it syncs easily with him, not because the hardware itself is advanced. **Only the AI evolves at this stage**, the machine itself is static until upgraded through the points-for-gear economy. The AI gains experience from use, leveling up the way a trained skill would, becoming faster and more efficient at its job over time. Trav’s symbiosis accelerates this specifically, his AI levels faster under him than the same AI would under anyone else.

- **The Zeteii-tier jump:** when Zeteii is freed at the central node raid, that’s the S-tier jump, not a gradual climb. Worth keeping the gap between “everyone else’s progression” and this moment visually and narratively enormous.

**The path system (LOCKED)**

Genetic/stimulant upgrades tied to the points economy come in role- specific tracks, sought after early (first ones become available years 1-3, real status to be an early recipient):

- **Tank** — endurance, durability, and utility equipment, deployable overshields for allied units, big-damage weapons (massive maser, tachyon beam) at higher grades.

- **Range DPS,**** ****“****Reeps****”** — varies between ultra-long-range specialist platforms and medium-range rapid-fire units. All long-range specialists start as medium-range rapid-fire and specialize later. True long-range specialist rigs are either Heirloom-grade or earned “true specialist” status.

- **Melee DPS,**** ****“****Meeps****”** — Trav’s track, LOCKED, in-universe term now chosen, resolves the earlier open item. Either endurance+movement or dps+movement builds, mostly melee weapons. Matches his RPD-style reflexive combat and makes wound-emulation’s stakes maximal, proximity is required for his strengths to trigger at all.

- **Recovery/Repair mobile unit,**** ****“****Munti****”** (colloquial, from Mobile Unit) — see full writeup below.

Early selection “sets you down a path,” genuine build commitment, not easily reversed, real RPG-adjacent weight to the choice.

**The Munti, full writeup**

Mobile repair/recovery unit, colloquial name from “Mobile Unit.” Essentially a mobile parts factory and field hospital in one chassis.

- **Fabricator:** onboard, produces parts and materiel in the field.

- **Remote repair:** uses matter-transfer tech to repair mechs at a distance, no physical dock required.

- **Cockpit evacuation system:** carries a box on its back that teleports a downed pilot’s cockpit directly into a dedicated hold on the command carrier, real emergency extraction, not just battlefield presence.

- **Weapon, elegant dual-use design:** fights via matter disintegrator, which is the repair beam run in reverse, forward mode reconstructs and repairs, reversed it strips mass and converts it into usable matter and energy. One piece of tech, two functions, no separate weapon system needed.

- **Schematic sharing, LOCKED:** a Munti needs a mech’s schematic on file to actually repair it. In the academy, this is done manually, part of the learning curriculum, cadets handle the transfer themselves. In the military, it’s automatic, updates propagate instantly across every deployed Munti the moment a schematic changes. Good, concrete texture for the academy-to-military contrast, learning the fundamentals by hand versus a system too fast-moving to afford that anymore.

**The Tank, full writeup, LOCKED, resolves the earlier asymmetry**

Identity: the wall the fight breaks against, not the wall that breaks. Endurance, durability, and utility-first, built around soaking damage and creating space for the rest of the team rather than personal kill count, which is exactly why a Tank’s own scoring skews toward assists (see the scoring system note above) and why the role is a genuine ego trap for a cadet who enrolled expecting to be the obvious standout.

- **Personal defense line:** starts physical and passive (a riot shield that stops only what it physically covers), climbs into active-tech personal overshields by E-grade, the first real “modern tech” moment in the Tank’s own progression.

- **Group utility line, the actual identity shift:** D-grade is where the role stops being “the guy with the biggest shield” and starts being “the guy protecting a squad instead of one body,” overshield generators wide enough to cover teammates standing nearby. Everything above this tier deepens that same shift rather than reversing it.

- **Higher-grade weapons:** big, committed, high-damage platforms at the top of the tier scale (massive maser, tachyon beam), deliberately not a Tank’s early identity, the role earns its own offense late, after the defensive/utility identity is already established, so it never reads as “DPS in heavier armor.”

- **Fighting philosophy:** plant, hold, absorb, let the rest of the team work inside the space that buys. The opposite temperament from Meeps’ proximity-triggered reflexive combat, patience and positioning over reaction speed.

- **Craft note, Corw’s arc specifically:** a top-ranked cadet landing on the one path whose entire success metric is “how well did the team do,” not “how well did I do,” is the actual mechanism for his flagged ego-test arc, not a separate plot he needs, the path itself is the test.

**The Reeps, full writeup, LOCKED, resolves the earlier asymmetry**

Identity: aim discipline and patience, not raw damage, the path’s real skill is knowing when to fire and when to wait, not how hard the shot hits. Per the already-locked rule, every Reeps starts as a medium-range rapid-fire unit, none of the early gear is a true sniper platform, “true long-range specialist” is either Heirloom-grade or a status earned later, never handed out at the start.

- **Weapon line:** single-shot carbine (the “Popgun” name is earned and cadets are glad to outgrow it) into three-round burst (first gear that rewards aim discipline over spray) into dual burst-rifles at E-grade, real ammo-management stakes, staggering reloads instead of both hands going empty at once, into a medium-range platform with an actual optic mount at D-grade, the first visible hint of where true specialization eventually goes.

- **Core tension, LOCKED:** real piloting is still demanded even at range, positioning, tracking, movement under fire, none of that goes away just because the engagement distance is longer. This is specifically why Ilyen struggles here despite being the cohort’s rune-patterning prodigy, precision on a rune-pattern and precision inside a moving cockpit are not the same skill, and why her Book 5 reposition to Munti reads as a genuine correction rather than a demotion.

- **Fighting philosophy:** information and setup as much as damage, a good Reeps is often the reason a Tank’s overshield timing or a Meeps’ opening actually lands, spotting and softening rather than closing a fight alone.

- **Craft note, team texture:** Trav’s team runs two Reeps at once (Ilyen and Talia), same path, opposite relationship to it, one a prodigy everywhere except here, one who’s never been a prodigy at anything and grinds this one the hardest of the whole team. Worth keeping that contrast visible rather than treating “same path” as “same character.”

**Workshop fabrication economy, LOCKED**

Fixed to the shared pilot/mek workshop itself, not portable, not personal-scale, the same paired workshop space already established (Qiraki_Concept.md, daily structure section, “kept paired specifically so they keep working on the same machine together”). Installed equipment scales up starting Year 3 alongside the fluid-power/actuator coursework and the path-system split (see Qiraki_Engineering_ Curriculum_Reference.md’s Year 3 entry): matter fabricator, armor compressor, and comparable print/forming equipment, expanding in capability through Year 7. A meaningfully downscaled version of the Munti’s fabricator/matter-transfer tech, same underlying lineage, fixed-installation and workshop-scale rather than mobile-unit-scale, a cadet who eventually chooses Munti arrives at that path with real hands-on history on the smaller version already.

**Cost split, LOCKED:** two separate, genuinely distinct spends, not one shared pool.

- **Pilot** buys the schematic/blueprint, the design, what gets made. Draws from the existing points-shop catalog logic (see Qiraki_ Points_Shop_Catalog.md).

- **Mek** buys the raw matter to actually print it, priced per-unit of matter used, cheap per-unit but real in aggregate across a month.

**Purpose, LOCKED:** teaches economy of scale directly through the mechanic rather than stating it. Priced low enough that it’s never a hard bottleneck on its own, deliberately, but real enough that batching several small jobs into one combined print run genuinely saves matter cost over printing them separately across the month. A mek who plans well stretches further on the same points than one who prints reactively, piece by piece, whenever something’s needed. Good, earned mechanical grounding for Reqa’s already-established technical edge over Trav by graduation, she isn’t just more skilled by narrative assertion, she’s had years of real budgeting practice he never had to develop, same discipline as the existing skill-gap mechanism (see Qiraki_Concept.md’s weekly-structure section).

**Team texture:** since matter cost is real but small per-unit, informal team-level batching (a mek timing print runs to combine jobs across teammates, or trading favors with another team’s mek to fill a print slot) is a plausible, unforced source of texture, not required, but available whenever a chapter wants to show meks managing something pilots never see or think about.

**Threat-scale taxonomy for deployed Synkers, LOCKED**

- **C-grade** — smaller threats (mech-scale).

- **B-grade** — bigger threats (ship-scale).

- **A-grade** — handles both, plus occasional cleanup sweeps around B- and C-grade operations.

- **Biggest fish / megafish** — handled mostly by spaceships directly rather than individual Synkers.

- **Gigafish** — need special weaponry costly enough to use that it costs lives every time, the extreme end of the scale.

Deployment order during an incursion: C-grade goes in last as cleanup crew, still extremely dangerous, some Bloom threats hide well enough to ambush a cleanup team specifically. Survive until a B-grade arrives.

**Escort ship classes and the gigafish blackhole weapon, LOCKED, expands the taxonomy above:** Coalition escort ships range from cruiser to dreadnought, each fitted for a different threat scale. Dreadnoughts are the ones that answer both megafish and gigafish. A gigafish engagement can require deploying a whole wing of cruisers just to physically hold the creature in place long enough for the dreadnought’s blackhole-generating weapon to fire, the extreme-end special weaponry the taxonomy above already flags as costly enough to cost lives every time. That cost is literal: the weapon carries no friendly-fire protection, so any escort still in range when it fires is at risk, and blackhole size scales with the gigafish’s own size (exact math still open, flagged as a fun problem to work out when a scene actually needs it). This is also the mechanism behind the draft purge-mission beat where a squad gets an emergency gravity-reversion recall, pulled out of a collapsing engagement zone specifically because a commander chose to eat the tactical cost of an early blackhole deployment rather than lose the team outright.

**Casualty rates and mission reality, LOCKED, important for tone**

Early-grade missions are genuinely hard and worth very little individually, survival is measured minutes at a time. Standard rate: losing one or two team members per mission is considered normal, even with restock available. Without restock, casualty rates would run around 80%, most or whole teams dying once the Munti support unit is lost. Protecting the Munti is effectively its own job within a team. Munti crews are the unit’s unsung heroes, genuine, earned respect from everyone who’s actually been in the field. **This entire note describes the military era specifically, once cadets are actually deployed. The academy years run on a very different rate, see below.**

**Academy-era casualty rate, LOCKED, distinct from the military-era rate above:** actual death during the seven academy years is rare, roughly 1% or less. Wound-emulation training is genuinely dangerous and genuinely hurts, Trav loses limbs during years 3-4 and that’s normal, not exceptional, but restock handles the overwhelming majority of it, something like 99% of training incidents. A cadet gets hurt, gets pulled, gets healed, gets pumped back into training, same as everyone around them. This is a controlled environment with immediate medical response on-site, not real combat against an enemy actively trying to kill anyone, which is the entire reason the rate is so much lower than the military-era numbers above. Keeps the gritty/gory tone intact, injury is frequent and real and described at full weight, while making an actual academy-era death genuinely rare and worth the weight the story gives it when it happens (see Book 4’s locked loss in Qiraki_Book_Expansion_Plan.md), a true outlier against a backdrop where almost everyone survives, not a statistically expected event.

**Cumulative military-era attrition, working average, not fully locked:** roughly 40% of a given academy cohort has died by the time a survivor from that cohort reaches carrier-command rank, a rough average to build the casualty/survivor roster against rather than a precise locked figure, somewhere in that range. This is cumulative across years of actual deployment, consistent with the much harsher military-era rate above, and stands in deliberate contrast to the academy’s own near-total survival rate, the war only starts actually killing people at the scale the setting’s grimness implies once graduation happens.

# Reqa’s Heirloom weapon, LOCKED, effect

**What it does:** it severs the bond of reality at the edge. Not a bigger weapon, a weapon that makes the cut itself stop obeying the material it passes through.

**Battlefield consequences, LOCKED:**

- **Cuts as though armour and shields are not there.** Carrier shielding, capital-ship plate, Bloom chitin, the edge does not negotiate with any of them. Directly answers the already-locked shield rule (shields are real and powerful but not absolute) rather than contradicting it, this is the specific exception the rule always implied existed.

- **Extends almost indefinitely, gated only by available energy.** This is where Trav’s locked power-unit underinvestment finally bites him in a way that costs, rather than reading as a quirk. **The weapon is the argument against his own build philosophy**, and it arrives from the one person who has been quietly funding the counter-argument for a decade.

- **It can cut a gigafish in two.** Which produces two megafish. **So he has to keep cutting.** That is the joke and it is also the correct physics for an organism that is a continent-scale amoeba rather than an animal with a body plan. A cut is not a kill, it is a division, and the work scales with how well the cut went.

**Why the mechanism follows from the build, and this is the part that makes it feel inevitable rather than granted.** The design’s whole principle is reversal, reading a live construct and re-emitting it turned. **Reversal taken to its extreme is severance.** The Year 1 lesson (a trace run backward is a different instruction, not a mirror) is the same idea at the smallest possible scale. The reader met this in Book 1 Study Session I and will not have been told they were looking at the endgame.

**It is a sword, and Trav carries it, which reconciles the deadline.** She builds it for reasons that are hers (proof, and a clan), and it still requires a wielder, because a weapon becomes an Heirloom by having been carried by someone who mattered rather than by being well made. **All three motives stack rather than competing.** Nobody says any of this out loud.

**Timing, LOCKED, new:** the dream sharpens at Year 6, from “build a mech” in general to “build a mech carrying a Heirloom-grade weapon” specifically. Same year Trav’s own equipment innovation is already locked (the sword and servo work he rigs himself, winning the Devereux-Kastel slot), same year Qiraki_Rune_Tech_Reference.md’s curriculum reaches weighted, trainable circuits, the edge of real machine-learning-adjacent material, and the same year the engineering curriculum reaches servomechanisms and control systems. Three separate already-locked Year 6 beats converging on the same two cadets at once, not a coincidence worth hiding, worth actually showing them clock it. The weapon itself is completed later, still “late military era” as already locked, but now with a sharper marker: finished before Trav reaches Legion Commander rank (see Qiraki_Bible_Skeleton.md’s rank vocabulary section), which also means finished before, or right around, the timing already locked for their first child.

**Why this requires rune-tech, not just engineering, LOCKED, new:** the mechanism note above already says the weapon’s whole principle, reversal taken to its extreme is severance, traces straight back to “the Year 1 lesson (a trace run backward is a different instruction, not a mirror).” That lesson now has an exact on-page specification, Qiraki_Rune_Tech_Reference.md’s Third and Fourth Patterns, the letters T and D, same two traces, opposite order, taught in Ch.2 as literacy before either cadet has any idea what it’s actually for. Reqa visibly prefers engineering over rune-patterning and gets cranky on double-rune days, already locked on her own sheet, and none of that gets contradicted here, she doesn’t need to enjoy the subject to eventually need it cold. The weapon she ends up building is proof she never actually got to skip it, the thing she complained about for six years turns out to be exactly what her actual dream was quietly asking of her the whole time. Real, earned reason for her to keep pushing at a subject she doesn’t love, tracked against Qiraki_Rune_Tech_Reference.md’s year-by-year curriculum rather than staying vague, see her own sheet for the arc-level version of this.

# Power unit category, bullshit-tech note

The G-A power unit line is grounded, incrementally better energy on tap. **The top of the line should not be**, consistent with the standing rule that Sovereign- and Heirloom-grade effects are the flagged, deliberate break from real science and should read as visibly different in kind rather than as a smooth continuation.

**Concretely:** everything G through A is engineering, better throughput, better thermal handling, real tradeoffs against mass. **S-grade and any Heirloom-adjacent power source is not**, and should read as an entirely different category of thing rather than a bigger battery. This matters specifically because the Heirloom sword’s reach is energy-gated, which means the power question is the actual ceiling on the endgame weapon, and a grounded ceiling would quietly cap something that is supposed to be uncapped. **Needs a real pass**, currently only flagged.
