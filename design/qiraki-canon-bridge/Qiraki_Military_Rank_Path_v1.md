## PART ONE — the two axes, restated

Already LOCKED in Bible_Skeleton §4: **rank** and **command position** are separate tracks, not one ladder.

**Rank** is an individual soldier's personal standing, climbed alone, gated by connective-AI level under the points economy, regardless of whether that soldier ever leads anyone.

**Command position** is a merit-based authority track layered on top of rank. A soldier can sit at high rank and never hold a command slot. A soldier can be pulled into a command role without being the highest-ranked person nearby. Trav and Vrassik each inherit a command by walking into the other's old vacancy, not by seniority order.

This document's job is to lay both tracks out as one usable reference, and then show where that reference bends once the oligarchy stops being the thing the ladder answers to.

## PART TWO — the rank scale, individual standing

**Tier scale, LOCKED (Weapons_And_Progression.md):** G through S. G–F–E–D–C–B–A–S, the genre's standard ascending grade convention, deliberately not reinvented. No tier past S. Applies uniformly to mechs, weapons, connective-AI individuation, and genetic/stimulant upgrades.

**Where a soldier's own rank sits on that scale:** G through E are academy cadet territory, ungated, standardized training gear with no personalization. **All soldiers start D-rank out of the academy** (Bible_Skeleton §4), which makes D the actual floor of a *soldier's* rank rather than a cadet's. From there, C, B, and A mark a real career, most soldiers spend it entirely inside that band. S is the ceiling, and functionally exceptional rather than a normal endpoint. Trav is issued a D-tier machine with a C-tier connective AI at special forces entry, only the AI evolving at that stage. When Zeteii is freed, that is described explicitly as an S-tier jump, not a gradual climb, and it happens to a connective AI, not to a soldier's personal rank title. Whether a human soldier's own rank can nominally reach S is unaddressed anywhere in canon and this document does not assert an answer either way.

**Real grounding, worth adding formally since it was already sitting in the naming choice.** The project's naming rule bars real-world *military* tradition from this ladder, but says nothing about civilian craft tradition, and the command-ladder vocabulary is already guild-native. Historical craft guilds ran exactly this kind of objectively-gated advancement: apprentice, journeyman, master, with promotion earned by submitting an actual work-sample (a "masterpiece") to the guild hall for judgment, not by time served. That is the same discipline already locked for the points economy ("no participation or attendance bonus exists at any tier... showing up and training isn't separately rewarded"). The guild-apprenticeship model is a clean, already-fitting real anchor for why this world's soldiers are ranked by demonstrated tier rather than by years in service, and it costs nothing to name on the page as flavor, an instructor comparing a cadet's gear tier to a journeyman's marks.

## PART THREE — the command ladder

Five thresholds, current and reconciled. Bible_Skeleton §4 originally locked four ("pilot -> unit commander -> fleet/carrier commander -> faction leader"); the later Legion Commander lock inserts a fifth rung between Guildmaster and Faction Leader. **Flagging for the Defect Queue:** Bible_Skeleton §4's own "four visible thresholds" line now undercounts by one and is a candidate for a one-word fix next time that file gets a pass. Not resolved here, just surfaced.

| Command position | Guild-native title | Unit led | Scale | Locked example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pilot | — (no command title) | Self, one seat in a Party | 1 pilot | Trav and Vrassik both start here |
| Unit Commander | **Raid Lead** | Party | 5 pilots | Vrassik reaches this at C-rank. Trav reaches it a full rank later, at B-rank, "team who sticks together rises together" as the locked reason for the gap |
| Fleet/Carrier Commander | **Guildmaster** | Guild (battlegroup) | 1 carrier clan, 5 carriers plus dozens of escorts | Vrassik is promoted here first; his old Raid Lead vacancy is what Trav inherits, not a transfer |
| Legion Commander | **Legion Commander** (title kept as-is, first rung not run through kid-slang) | Legion, a raid-alliance of several Guilds united for a campaign | Roughly half a dozen billion soldiers at full COE scale, deliberately keep the number off the page | Trav's rank by the time he and Reqa have one or two children together |
| Faction Leader | Faction Leader, **deliberately un-gamified** | A Faction, a political-military bloc | Up to roughly half the galaxy's inhabited worlds by the four-hub campaign | Where military command bleeds into civilian politics outright |

**Real grounding for why the ladder can even work this way.** Every rung above Raid Lead depends on Auftragstaktik, Moltke's mission-command doctrine, already locked in Political_Web as mechanically necessary rather than flavor: missions run up to twelve hours to automatic carrier recall, carriers operate across hundreds of light-years, and there is no arrangement under which a distant commander can direct a squad in real time. Every COE officer is trained to be handed intent and left alone with it. That is also, explicitly, the doctrine Trav's eventual independent command runs on, and it is worth restating here because it is the hinge the whole rebel-era section below turns on: **his officers already know how to run their own war. The Coalition trained them to.**

**Real grounding for the incentive shift between rungs.** Weapons_And_Progression locks that reaching the unit-commander threshold roughly triples per-mission points and adds a bonus for every team member who returns alive, and that carrier-commander scale widens the score inputs to a whole battle's worth of moving pieces. That is not decoration, it is the reward structure itself being re-pointed from personal survival to collective survival exactly at the moment the job starts requiring it, a diegetic version of the same RPD/System 1 vs System 2 command-cognition research already grounding Trav's arc elsewhere in the project. Worth keeping visible on the page whenever a promotion happens: the character should feel the scoring change before anyone explains it.

## PART FOUR — the Warrant and the Hand, and why Guildmaster is a Hand-only title

Every rank in Part Three above is an **off-Roll, Staff-trained, earned** title. None of it is available to a house officer by birth, and none of it is gated by anything but demonstrated tier and a vacancy to fill.

The Warrant sits entirely outside this ladder. It is an on-Roll appointment made by the Carabil seat, no tier requirement, no merit climb, a house's claim on a Guild rather than a soldier's earned command of one. "Whose hand is on that group" is already locked as the only version of the question anyone in uniform actually asks, because the Warrant's name on the paperwork and the Guildmaster's hand on the actual formation are frequently two different people.

**The one detail worth stating plainly for drafting purposes, since it is the load-bearing fact for everything in Part Eight:** a sport-named Guild, per the already-locked oligarch-resentment mechanic, is one no house will put its name on. Its Warrant is vacant or nominal. Its Hand runs it alone. MeltaDread, Trav's own posting, is exactly this. The units with silly names are the ones with a genuinely unified chain of command, and every professional officer in the Coalition already knows it.

## PART FIVE — cost of the climb, for pacing

Texture already locked, useful for judging how heavy a given promotion should read on the page. Pilot-tier missions run one or two losses as the accepted normal rate, roughly 80% casualties without restock support. Cumulative across actual deployment, roughly 40% of a given academy cohort has died by the time a survivor from it reaches carrier-command rank, a working average rather than a precise figure. The ladder gets lonelier at the top by simple attrition math, and that fact does not need a character to say it out loud.

## PART SIX — the real grounding for the whole rank-to-rebellion throughline

Already locked in Political_Web and worth keeping attached to this document specifically, since it is the actual argument for why a rank path built entirely by the oligarchy is the thing that ends up overthrowing it.

**The pipeline recruits the classless deliberately**, because an officer corps drawn from chartered houses would become a rival power within two generations, and the Close exists specifically to prevent new claims. Off-Roll recruitment is a security policy wearing the face of opportunity.

**The real grounding is the Ottoman *devşirme***, which levied children from subject populations and raised them as an elite corps with no tribal or dynastic affiliation, producing both the Janissaries and much of the imperial administration. The Janissaries eventually became the most powerful political force in the empire, made and unmade sultans, and were finally destroyed by force in 1826. A corps built from nothing, specifically so it could never make a claim, made the largest claim in the state's history. That is Trav's arc, already locked as documented history rather than a plot the author has to justify.

Combined with Auftragstaktik from Part Three, the setup is complete before the rebellion ever starts: the Coalition trained a classless corps to operate with total independence, gave that corps its most unified formations by declining to claim them, and never armed it with anything but the exact autonomy it now uses against the Quorum.

## PART SEVEN — how the ladder bends into the rebel era

**What carries over unchanged.** Raid Lead, Guildmaster, Legion Commander, Party, Guild, Legion. Every one of these titles was already off-Roll and earned, never tied to the Roll in the first place, so there is nothing about them the rebellion needs to invent or rename. A rebel Guildmaster and a loyalist Guildmaster hold the identical rank, earned the identical way. This is a genuine strength for drafting: no new vocabulary has to enter the reader's ear at the exact moment the story most needs clarity.

**The one structural amputation.** The rebellion's actual mechanical act, at the level of a single Guild, is the Warrant going away, killed, fled, refused, or, for sport-named formations, simply never having been real to begin with, and its Hand becoming sole and total commander, now answering upward through Legion Commander to Faction Leader instead of through the Carabil seat to the Quorum. Nothing new is invented here either. It is the sport-named Guild's existing structure, universalized. The rebellion does not out-organize the Coalition's military. It completes a gap the oligarchy already dug for itself out of snobbery.

**Where the ladder stops being military.** Faction Leader is already locked as deliberately un-gamified, the one rung with no Guild-native slang, because the job at that altitude stops being command and starts being legitimacy, who gets to claim they represent the Coalition, which is a Senate-and-Quorum question wearing a general's uniform. That is the correct place for the vocabulary itself to visibly change register on the page.

**Two open calls this document surfaces and does not decide, both already flagged Author-lane in Qiraki_Defect_Queue.md:**

**POL-02 / DEC-10, does the Staff fracture, defect wholesale, or hold to the last?** Everything above assumes a *spectrum*, Guild by Guild, Warrant-vacant formations defecting first and cleanly, Warrant-held formations lagging or fighting for the Quorum, which reads as gradual absorption and determines whether the story calls this a rebellion. The other available shape, a Staff that holds together and Trav's forces are a genuine breakaway fighting the rest of the professional officer corps as a hostile army, reads as mutiny, and needs two Guildmasters and two chains of command actually fighting each other rather than one side quietly running out of Warrants. Both are fully compatible with everything locked in this document. Neither is chosen here.

**POL-05 / DEC-13, does the Warrant system survive Trav's victory, renamed?** If the devşirme parallel is carried all the way through, since it is the project's own chosen grounding rather than an import, the historical shape on offer is uncomfortable and specific: the corps that overthrows the old order eventually gets formally recognized as the new one's most powerful faction, and generations later gets forcibly abolished once its own power outgrows whatever throne it just built. That would mean Trav's rebellion, structurally, risks producing a future version of exactly the problem it solved. Worth naming as an available shape for the endgame precisely because the project has already told the reader, once, in a document nobody on the page has read, that this is what happens to armies built this way. Not a decision. A shape sitting on the table if the author wants it.
