## PART ZERO — WHAT THIS DOCUMENT IS FOR

The author's instruction: take the Military Era Outline as the working example and build out the rest of the story on the same plan, at a minimum of seven books per era.

Everything below that instruction was already decided. The academy era is seven books. The rank ladder has five military rungs. The endgame is locked down to its final emotional beat. The cosmology has an arithmetic under it that nobody on the page is allowed to state. What did not exist was a map showing how those pieces sit next to each other across the whole run, which book carries which locked beat, and what each era is actually about besides being the next one.

This document is that map. The six companion documents (ERA2 through ERA7) expand each block at the fidelity of Qiraki_Military_Era_Outline_v3.docx.

**One correction absorbed on the way in.** The project-instruction copy and Qiraki_Concept_v5.docx still carry "~2,500 word average chapters, ~130,000 word books." That was corrected on 2026-08-14 to **~4,500 average, 3,500 to 5,500 range, 47 chapters, roughly 210,000 words per book**, acts running 12 / 11 / 13 / 11, and Qiraki_Master_Style_Guide_v2.docx already carries the corrected figures including Book 7 as the single series exception at 54 chapters with acts 12 / 11 / 13 / **18**. Every allocation in this document and its companions uses the corrected numbers. **The stale side is the project-instruction copy and Concept, not the Style Guide**, and both want a one-line fix next time they get a pass.

## PART ONE — THE SEVEN-ERA MAP

Seven eras of seven books. Forty-nine total.

The number is not decoration and it is not a target the material was bent to fit. It falls out of two things already locked. Qiraki_Military_Rank_Path_v1.md establishes **five** command thresholds, not the four Bible_Skeleton §4 still says (that undercount is already flagged in RANK's own open items). Add the academy that precedes all five, add the post-victory era the locked endgame requires and no rank covers, and the story has seven natural blocks before anyone counts anything. Seven books each is the author's floor applied evenly.

| Era | Arc name | Books | Trav's rung | What the era is actually about |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| I | **The Academy Era** | 1-7 | Cadet, G through E | Learning to read, learning to fight, being sorted |
| II | **The Synker Wars** | 8-14 | Pilot, D through C | Being given orders that do not add up, and obeying them |
| III | **The Ones Who Come Back** | 15-21 | Raid Lead, B | Being responsible for other people and being bad at it first |
| IV | **The Hand** | 22-28 | Guildmaster | Running the machine, and finding out the machine has an owner |
| V | **The Quiet Years** | 29-35 | Legion Commander | The war stops and nothing gets better |
| VI | **The Broken Table** | 36-42 | Faction Leader | Winning, and what winning costs the people who asked for it |
| VII | **The Answered Request** | 43-49 | No rung, this is the point | Being loved without limit by something powerful |

**Arc-name provenance.** I and II are the author's own (Concept locks "The Academy Era" as the arc-name and reserves "The Synker Wars" for the military-campaign books). **III through VII are [CLAUDE], swappable**, each built from vocabulary already inside the setting rather than imported:

- **The Ones Who Come Back** names the reputation Trav's Party actually earns, already locked in Concept's team-structure note ("Trav's team should become known for unusually few deaths despite taking increasingly harder missions").
- **The Hand** is the in-universe half of the Warrant/Hand pair, and "whose hand is on that group" is already locked as the only version of the question anyone in uniform asks.
- **The Quiet Years** carries the Bureau of Civil Assurance's colloquial name inside a phrase that reads, on a cover, as peace. Nobody in the story ever points at the double meaning.
- **The Broken Table** uses the Quorum's own colloquial name. The four-hub campaign is the literal breaking of a four-seat table.
- **The Answered Request** is the author's own phrasing for the climax, lifted directly from the Cosmology document.

**Per-book title convention, unchanged.** *Enlightened: The Ones Who Come Back, Book 16, "The Mantle."* Same shape as *Enlightened: The Academy Era, Book 1, "The King and the Climb."*

## PART TWO — THE FORWARD TIMELINE

**[CLAUDE, first pass, all figures swappable, none of them stated on the page.]** The story has never been dated concretely except by Concept's 99th-millennium lock and the war's own duration. This is a working spine so that a book in Era V knows how old everyone is.

Baseline: Trav enrolls at 12, graduates at 19. Human lifespans run to roughly 500, averaging 479. Terms of service in the frontier forces are long enough that Reqa can call the end of one "free" and mean it literally.

| Era | Trav's age | Elapsed | War year at era start |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| I | 12 to 19 | 7 years | ~60 |
| II | 19 to 27 | ~8 years | ~67 |
| III | 27 to 40 | ~13 years | ~75 |
| IV | 40 to 62 | ~22 years | ~88 |
| V | 62 to 85 | ~23 years | ~110, the withdrawal begins here |
| VI | 85 to 105 | ~20 years | the civil war, then the return |
| VII | 105 to ~215 | ~110 years, 101 of them a single gap | after |

**Three consequences worth holding onto.**

The Bible's war-state lock (66 years, 35% of holdings lost, army growing 10% a year) is a snapshot taken during the academy era, not a permanent condition. By Era IV the same arithmetic has run another two decades and the oligarchy is still comfortable, which is the point.

**The oligarchy's plan fails on schedule and nobody notices it failing.** They wanted a few hundred more years of managed war to finish erasing the pre-war record. They get roughly fifty. The Bloom thins at war year 110 for reasons that have nothing to do with anyone's plan, which means the Quorum's entire strategy is defeated by an accident before Trav ever raises a hand against it. **Nobody on the page ever knows this, including Trav.** It stays in the same drawer as the spread arithmetic.

**Reqa is older than Trav and this eventually matters.** The locked enrolment timestamps give the real figure, **1 year 261 days**, not the round two years it is easy to quote. At 479 average against a 500 ceiling, a head start that size is nothing for four hundred years and then it is not nothing. **[OPEN, author-lane, and it belongs to Era VII rather than to any earlier book.]** Whether the story ever touches this, and what Trav does about Sovereign-grade longevity once he controls who gets it, is the single largest unasked question sitting under the ending. See Part Seven.

## PART THREE — THE SIX STANDING DEVICES

Era I ran on the propaganda fragment, the study session, the joke-ritual, the daily-rhythm template, the points economy, and the socket. Six devices, deployed every book, none of them ever explained. The forward series needs its own set or the seven military-era books will read as seven mission collections.

These are the six. Each one already exists in locked material. What is new is the instruction to run them the whole way.

### D1. The web of weird orders

**Locked, Concept and Military_Era_Outline_v3.** Spec ops work is not clean. Trav accumulates a pattern of orders that do not add up: an atrocity dressed as procedure, a disposal dressed as bad luck, a destroy-the-evidence order that turns out to be theatre, and, at least once, an order that is honestly what it says it is, so the pattern reads as a system rather than a conspiracy.

**He never consciously assembles it.** Felt first, articulated never.

**Extension across the eras, [CLAUDE]:** the web does not stop when he starts giving the orders. Era III is the first time he passes one down that he does not understand. Era IV is the first time he writes one. Era V is the era the reader finally has enough of them to see the shape, and it resolves not as a revelation but as a decision. Era VI is the era Trav's own faction begins issuing orders of the same species, and the book does not remark on it.

### D2. The accidental defense of the universe

**Locked, Cosmology PART 4e.** The Qiraki's weapon fails at the only scale they care about because dark energy is faster than biology. That is why the Bloom needs FTL, why there is a compound on a preserve world researching it, and why the order to raid that compound is, without anyone in it knowing, the most important defensive act in the history of the universe, filed as a distraction op.

**Standing recommendation from that document: do not have anyone say this. Ever.**

**Extension, [CLAUDE], and this is the strongest series-long device available:** it happens **once per era**, always for petty reasons, always filed as something else. The Coalition, and later Trav's faction, keeps blindly cutting the same throat for forty books. No character ever connects two instances. The reader who is counting gets the whole cosmology for free and is never told they were right.

| Era | The operation | The stated reason | What it actually was |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| II | The preserve-world compound raid, Book 8 | Data held hostage, act with haste and impunity | Bloom FTL research set back, ordered because the oligarchy wants the war to continue |
| III | The mantle bomb on the sessile-tomb world, Book 16 | Deny the Bloom a staging world | The tomb held the largest continuous seam-network yet recorded, and seam-continuity is the mechanism the FTL work depends on |
| IV | A gate-network transit audit, Book 24 | Assurance wants better records | Closes the one route Bloom material had been drifting along inside COE shipping |
| V | A budget line cancelled in a bad Draw, Book 30 | The Ledger wants the number stable | Defunds the only lab that had reproduced the effect |
| VI | A hub-world orbital cleared for the campaign, Book 41 | Trav needs the orbital for the assault | Destroys three centuries of Qiraki-adjacent seed material nobody had catalogued |
| VII | Zeteii's own conversion of the neighbouring galaxy, Book 47 | She was asked to protect him completely | Ends the FTL problem permanently, by removing everything that could have solved it |

**[CLAUDE, and this is the part that makes the device land]** the last row is the one that hurts. The device spends six eras teaching the reader that the universe keeps getting saved by accident and by pettiness, and then the seventh saves it on purpose, completely, at a price nobody would have paid deliberately.

### D3. The propaganda fragment, second register

**Locked device, Propaganda_Bank.** Every chapter opens with a short in-universe official text, under three sentences, no connecting commentary, contradicted by the chapter's own content. Book 7 dropped the device entirely and handed its job to legacy cadets explaining the COE to each other.

**[PROPOSED] The device comes back in Book 8 and runs to Book 49, on a completely new source list.** Nine institutional voices were unlocked by Political_Web and have never been used: Quorum ceremonial texts, Senate allocation notices, Standing Service procedural boilerplate, Staff doctrine publications, Warrant investiture language, Function licensing and compliance notices, Assurance civil-guidance material, External Survey research abstracts, and Standing Review notices. Add the military registers already banked: condolence templates, after-action language, debrief transcripts, field manuals, private journals of unconfirmed provenance, and cadet-origin graffiti grown up into soldier-origin graffiti.

**Two escalations built into it:**

The Standing Review's fragments are always one sentence and never explain themselves. Their frequency should climb quietly from Era III to Era V and then stop entirely in Era VI, because by then the Review has other problems. Nobody remarks on the absence.

**And in Era VI, Trav's own faction starts producing fragments, and they are not better.** Same register, same reassurance, same gap between the text and the chapter under it. A rebel condolence template reads exactly like a Coalition one because there is only one way to write that letter. **No character notices. No paragraph ends on it.**

### D4. The bench chapter

**[PROPOSED, direct successor to the study-session chapter.]** Era I ran study sessions where Trav and Reqa learned real curriculum content together, doubling as reader education. The military era's version is the workshop bench: real engineering and rune-tech content delivered through people who now need it to survive rather than to pass.

The cast expands the device rather than replacing it. Reqa and Trav in Era II. Reqa and Alina from Era II onward, curator against builder, each proven right once. Ilyen from Era III, who brings a Munti's specific arithmetic. Zeteii from her individuation onward, and she is the one who makes it funny.

**Same rules as the study session.** No two sessions cover the same ground twice. The subject is drawn from Rune_Tech_Reference and Engineering_Curriculum_Reference rather than invented. The reader learns a mechanism by watching somebody swear at it.

**Tracker obligation:** ERA2 through ERA7 each carry a bench-chapter allocation, three per book, same as the academy's three study sessions. The academy tracker in Qiraki_Book_Expansion_Plan_v3.docx is currently empty and eighteen already-named subjects need entering into it. That is a separate cleanup, flagged not done here.

### D5. Yssa's letters

**Locked, Concept and Character_Sheets.** Intermittent, no fixed cadence, hers short and practical, his shorter. Long silences on his end are not distance. When it surfaces it is one line, not a scene.

**Extension, [CLAUDE]:** it runs to the end. One line per book, forty-two more books. She asks whether he is eating while he commands a Legion. She asks whether the weather where he is has turned while he is standing on a hub world he has just taken. **She does not die during the run and nothing is made of that either.** The final appearance is in Era VII and it is the same question.

**[CLAUDE, and the reason this is worth protecting]** it is the only thread in the story that does not escalate. Everything else in forty-nine books gets bigger. This one is identical in Book 49 and Book 8, and that is what makes it readable at all.

### D6. The socket

**Locked with a live discrepancy.** Qiraki_Concept_v5.docx says the third appearance is Peregrine's AI box arriving at the Thale compound during the academy-years epilogue. Book 7's v40 outline spends the third appearance on Zeteii's AI box during lockdown. **One of the two is stale and it is not resolved here.** (Qiraki_Session_Locks_Addendum.docx carries only the Year 7 workshop placement, status PENDING.)

**What is unambiguously unpaid, and it belongs to the forward series:** the tool is illegal, it came down his mother's line, and he does not know either fact. **[CLAUDE] Beyond that, the reading that Yssa was her husband's Matchset and performed this exact act on his father's restricted gear is inference, not lock.** Character_Sheets says only that she was his war-mechanic and that this *presumably* implies he was a pilot she was matched to, and Concept says she kept obsolete hardware rather than that she fitted it. **Useful, unconfirmed, and nobody on the page may say it either way.**

**[PROPOSED] The payoff is a pegger doing their job correctly.** Era III, Book 20. A Standing Service allocation clerk reconciling a Guild manifest flags an unregistered tool on a soldier's personal inventory. Not a raid, not a threat, a correctly filed observation with a form number on it. That is how Trav finds out, and the clerk is not wrong, and there is no villain in the scene. **The father is never mentioned.** The reader who remembers Book 1 assembles it. Trav does not, because Trav does not process backward.

## PART FIVE — CHAPTER ARCHITECTURE FOR THE FORWARD ERAS

**Unchanged from Era I:** 47 chapters, acts 12 / 11 / 13 / 11, roughly 210,000 words, weekly posting.

**What changes is what fills them.** The academy had a timetable. The military has a deployment cycle, and the cycle is the template.

### The deployment-rhythm template, [PROPOSED], the military successor to the daily-rhythm template

For ordinary chapters that need full length and are not carrying a mission:

1. **The bay.** Reqa or the Guild's mek floor. Actual work on actual hardware. This is where the bench chapter lives when it is a whole chapter rather than a beat.
2. **The board.** Orders, rosters, requisition, whatever the institution has decided about them this week without asking.
3. **The mess or the crate.** The squad, eating. Concept and the Military Era Outline both put the best scenes in this project on food crates in a workshop, and that is not an accident, it is the one place in a carrier where rank stops mattering for twenty minutes.
4. **Cockpit or hold.** Trav alone with Zeteii. Short. The only place he is ever unobserved, and even that is not fully true.
5. **Optional evening beat.** Peregrine, Vrassik, Alina, Ilyen. The friendship track, which has to keep running or the losses stop landing.

### Mission rhythm, unchanged

Minimum three mission chapters per major arc beat. Cooldown block of three to four chapters after roughly six or seven running mission chapters. Expected to drift with the material rather than held rigidly.

### The attrition clock

**Locked:** Trav loses a squadmate roughly every 20 chapters as the baseline rate, and gets measurably better at covering for losses as his skill grows. Pilot-tier missions run one or two losses as the accepted normal rate. Cumulative attrition runs to roughly 40% of an academy cohort dead by the time a survivor reaches carrier command.

**[CLAUDE] The distinction that keeps this survivable to read:** most of those twenty-chapter losses are **getting offed**, not dying. Restock catches nearly everything. The squad jokes about it, which is exactly how the reader was taught the rule in Book 8 without one line of explanation.

| II | 2 to 3 | ~1 | Team One entire, plus two named |

| IV | 1 to 2 | rare, the reputation holds | one, and it is not his fault, which is worse |

## PART SIX — THE FIVE THREADS THAT RUN THE WHOLE WAY

Everything else is era-local. These five cross every block and are the reason forty-two books read as one life.

### 6.1 Trav and Reqa

**Locked:** the romance starts once he is in his main combat unit, well past graduation, and she is genuinely all-mech at the academy stage. He falls for his mech before he falls for her. She becomes his stress guardian and grounded advisor once he is carrying real command weight, which is later than the romance's actual start. Book 7's handoff note says plainly: **the actual warming-up happens in Book 8.**

Shape across the eras, **[CLAUDE]**:

- **Era II.** Warming. Her jealousy from the ball is live and unexamined and she does not resolve it, she works next to it. The turn is Book 12, after the siege, and it is not a scene of anyone saying anything. He comes back and she is already at the bench and neither of them mentions the two weeks.
- **Era III.** An ordinary, load-bearing fact. The one register nobody writes: a good relationship happening quietly next to a bad job. She loses the power-unit argument again, and again, and the reader has the tables.
- **Era IV.** Children, the crèche, the Matchset question, and the Divider. **The Divider is the argument she has been having with him for a decade, finally built into an object, and it is a gift.** Nobody says that either.
- **Era V.** She is the only person who tells him the rebellion is a bad idea and then packs for it.
- **Era VI.** Her Heirloom weapon in his hands during a civil war. Her own line about sticking to it even if it feels dirty, said in Book 8, is now describing something else, and **it is never repeated as a refrain.** A refrain announces itself.
- **Era VII.** Four hundred years. Several children, a clan of a few dozen descendants. And a hundred and one years of him living with a copy of somebody else.

### 6.2 Trav and Zeteii

**Locked:** flirty and crude-joke-forward from the earliest establishment straight through to Faction Leader era, unchanged in register even as everything around them escalates. Jokes generated fresh in the moment during drafting, never reused verbatim. Her core fear is plurality. Her freedom and her singularity are the same object. She is a legitimate love interest and is never played for novelty.

**The structural fact that has to keep being true and is never stated:** she can talk to him on mission and Reqa cannot. That is not anybody's fault, it is the shape of the equipment, and it decides the whole thing.

Shape across the eras:

- **Era II.** Individuation. She becomes a person in the room rather than a voice in the cockpit. Book 8's line already does the damage: Reqa smiles, glad to see him back to himself, not knowing she has been overtaken.
- **Era III to IV.** The daily one. The one who is there.
- **Era V.** He goes and gets her freed, and the reason is personal, not strategic, and the vow he makes is a vow.
- **Era VI.** She leaves a clone and goes to the collective, which is the thing she is most afraid of, and she goes anyway, for him.
- **Era VII.** The answered request.

### 6.3 Trav and Vrassik

**Locked:** rival, then CO by vacancy, then genuine friend. His whole rise runs on the confidence surge from winning mating rights, and his path is the harder self-made one because he lost the Devereux-Kastel slot.

**[PROPOSED, author-lane, strong]** The civil war costs Vrassik the one thing his entire forty-book arc was built on. Hiopi mating rights are a civic institution, held at city scale, and a carrier or an academy counts as a city. Civic institutions sit inside the Quorum's reach. **A Hiopi who defects loses the city.** He does it anyway, and he does not make a speech about it, and Trav is the only person in the galaxy who understands exactly what was just spent.

That is the payoff for a motive locked since Book 4 and never cashed. It also gives POL-02 a face: Vrassik's Guild has a real Warrant, unlike MeltaDread's, so his defection is the hard one, and watching one Guildmaster do it slowly and at cost is what turns a mutiny into a rebellion.

**Alternative shapes, both workable:** he holds for the Quorum and they fight, which is colder and possibly better, or he dies before the choice, which is cheaper and I would not take it.

### 6.4 Ilyen Pral

**Locked:** rejoins as Trav's Munti three-quarters through the pilot era, stays his Munti once he commands, is nearly lost in a real close call that finally cracks his avoidance, and carries feelings across five academy books that he never once notices. Whether and when she says it is her choice and her timing, deliberately not decided.

**[PROPOSED] She is his second, and this is the load-bearing structural use of her.** Concept's own command research names a specific failure mode in experienced commanders, "a failure to disagree," and says plainly that Trav's second needs to be someone who contradicts him early and often. The cast already contains a person who notices everything precisely and has spent six books being invisible in rooms. A Munti as second is also the correct thematic choice for a story that keeps saying meks and Munti crews are unsung and then not doing anything about it.

**[PROPOSED] On the confession, Era IV, Book 26.** She says it once, plainly, in the least romantic available moment, as a factual correction to a wrong assumption he has just made about why she did something in the field. He hears it correctly, which is the first time in the entire series that he hears anything of this kind correctly. Nothing changes. They finish the after-action review. **She is not waiting for anything and the scene must not let the reader think she is.**

### 6.5 The joke-ritual

**Locked:** a new joke every time, never the same one twice. Established Book 1, reversed by Reqa in Book 4, escalating through Books 5 and 6, still running in Book 7. **The standing series obligation is that it must still be a joke forty books later, changed by what has happened.**

**[CLAUDE] The instruction that makes it survivable:** it stops being aimed at her specifically somewhere in Era III. It becomes how he runs a room. By Era IV it is a command tool and he does not know that either. In Era VI it stops working on soldiers, because a Faction Leader making a joke is not the same transaction, and the first time it lands wrong is worth a chapter. **In Era VII it works again, on one person, because there is nobody left to outrank.**

Never narrate any of that. He never thinks about why he does it. He is not built to.

## PART SEVEN — OPEN AUTHOR-LANE DECISIONS THIS ARCHITECTURE SURFACES

Nine. Six are already in the Defect Queue and are restated here only because they now have a book number attached, which changes the cost of leaving them open. Three are new.

**A1. DEC-10 / POL-02, which pillar breaks first.** Now dated: it has to be answered on the page in Book 38. The choice determines whether the whole of Era VI is a rebellion or a mutiny, and Part 6.3 above proposes a shape.

**A2. DEC-13 / POL-05, does the Warrant system survive.** Now dated: Book 43. The Frontier Compact's betrayal made concrete. The uncomfortable historical shape is already on file in Political_Web, and it says a corps built this way eventually gets abolished by force by the state it built.

**A3. DEC-12 / POL-04, Sovereign-grade longevity and the seats.** Now dated: Book 42, and then again, differently, in Book 49. Defeating people who can live nine hundred years is not the same as removing them.

**A4. DEC-11 / POL-03, is there a fifth chair above the Quorum.** Four seats with no head of state makes the four-hub campaign a literal necessity. Needed before Era VI is outlined at chapter level.

**A5. DEC-06, the Coalition's name for the expansion era.** Author explicitly asked for help. **[PROPOSED] "The Open."** The founding fact of the old order is the Close, the legal closing of the charter list, taught in schools and written on the wall. A civilisation that has just broken that would not reach for something grand. It would reach for the plainest available inversion, and it would mean it. "On-Roll" and "off-Roll" die as categories the same year. People say they are Open now, and within a generation nobody remembers it was ever an argument. **Alternates: The Wide Table, The Unclosed, The Long Roll.**

**A6. DEC-27, whether Hiro knew he was infected.** Small, and it decides how Book 8's Team One reads on a second pass.

**A7. NEW. Does Trav take Sovereign-grade longevity.** Once he controls who gets it, he controls whether he ages out. Reqa is two years older. The 500-year vow the battle clans swear has a return date past a normal human lifespan, so either the vow is made by people who know they will not see it kept, or the winners of the civil war quietly become the same kind of person the civil war was fought against. **Both versions are good and they are not the same book.** This is the largest new question this architecture creates and it belongs to Era VII.

**A8. NEW. What happens to Director Aurelian Kest.** Character_Sheets calls this the one genuine unresolved question left for him. He is not corrupt, he is convinced, and the pipeline he defends is the one that produced Trav. **[PROPOSED] He is not tried and he is not killed. He is asked to keep running it, under Trav, with the selection criteria changed, and he says yes, sincerely, within the same conversation, because in his model the alternative is worse.** Era VII, one scene, nobody comments.

**A9. NEW. Does the fifth kind mean the energy-discharge Bloom or the sessile.** Book 2's curriculum bank plants an unscripted instructor aside, "You'll meet the fifth kind eventually. Everybody does," against four textbook families of claw, spine, spray and swarm. Book 4 gives it a chapter title. It has never been defined. **[PROPOSED] Primary reading: the fifth kind is the energy-discharge family, the Bestiary's own reserved high-tier rung, and it debuts as a genuine escalation in Era III.** Secondary reading, and the two are not exclusive: the instructor meant one and the world means the other, and the other is the sessile, because every soldier eventually fights on ground that is already the enemy. **A veteran says the second version flatly, once, in Era II, and is not corrected.**

## PART EIGHT — WHAT THIS ARCHITECTURE DELIBERATELY REFUSES

Three things, all of them tempting, all of them named here so a later pass does not quietly adopt one.

**No scale escalation as a substitute for escalation.** The Cosmology document's warning is exact and it is the single most useful craft note in the project: a reader who watched Trav lose a squad on a moon does not care more when the next thing lost is a galaxy, they care less, because a galaxy is a number and Fracrals was a person. Every era in this map raises the question, not the tonnage. Era V has the fewest battles in the series and is the era where the most changes.

**No war against the Qiraki.** They are learned about in Book 48 and never fought. There is no deal available, no negotiation, and no boss. They are a cosmological fact with a motive, closer to a second physics than a second antagonist, and turning them into an enemy fleet would undo the reason the ending works. The story ends on a conversation between two people who love each other.

**No system box, and no reveal that reframes the research.** Seven books of real graph theory, real materials science, real motor-learning research and real habit-formation research are the argument the Style Guide makes for why this project is worth the length it takes. Nothing in Era VII may retroactively convert that into set dressing.
