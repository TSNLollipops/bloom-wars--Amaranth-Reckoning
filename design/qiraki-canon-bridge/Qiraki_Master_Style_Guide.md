# MASTER STYLE GUIDE & MANDATORY RULES — THE QIRAKI FILES

Modeled on The Ninth’s own Master Style Guide, adapted for this project’s different genre and different discipline. Where the two conflict, they’re not supposed to agree, these are separate projects with separate rules, see Qiraki_Process_Notes.md’s standing reminder not to cross-apply.

**Precedence, added 2026-08-05.** This guide governs prose mechanics, voice, and craft discipline. Qiraki_Bible_Skeleton governs canon: timeline, character detail, world mechanics (once the architecture additions in Qiraki_Bible_Architecture_Additions.md are merged in). The two shouldn’t conflict, they cover different domains, but if a specific claim ever does, the Bible wins on what happened and this guide wins on how it’s written. Same division of authority The Ninth’s own Style Guide holds against its Bible.

## 1. CORE NARRATIVE VOICE

The reference point is Jean M. Auel, not as pastiche, as method: immersive, researched, sensory-dense narrative where real material (here, real science and systems theory instead of real prehistoric anthropology) is carried at genuine length inside the story rather than gestured at from a distance. The environment, the technology, and the species are not backdrop, they are load-bearing, the same way climate and craft were load-bearing to Auel’s Ice Age. Alien landscape stands in for prehistoric Earth. Real theoretical grounding (already established for rune-tech and engineering, see their reference docs) stands in for real anthropological/botanical research. Prioritize earned, specific detail over cinematic gesture, the same discipline as The Ninth, applied to a different subject matter.

Target register, already locked: gritty, gory, and gorgeous simultaneously, sensory weight given to both landscape and threat, not one rushed to reach the other.

## 2. TENSE, LOCKED

First-person, continuous present tense throughout. Same discipline as The Ninth. Trav’s POV primarily, other POVs only when genuinely needed for a wider view of simultaneous events, per the already-locked POV discipline.

## 3. GRAMMAR, PUNCTUATION & READ-ALOUD FLOW

Natural contractions throughout, no artificial stiffness.

Zero em dashes, zero semicolons. Same absolute rule as The Ninth.

Minimize “thing,” “stuff,” “true,” per the author’s own flagged weakness, this is a personal habit to cut, not a stylistic choice worth preserving. See Qiraki_Emotion_Craft_Reference.md’s theoretical grounding section for the actual mechanism behind why “true” specifically misfires as a character’s private verdict on their own thought.

No mid-sentence train shifts, complete one thought before starting the next.

Flows read-aloud clean, no forced or archaic syntax, this is a kinetic-pad-game generation’s story, the prose should never sound like it’s straining for gravitas it hasn’t earned.

## 4. BAN PARAGRAPH-ENDING MORALS AND STATED THESIS

Same rule as The Ninth, absolute. Never close a paragraph or scene by explaining what it meant. This applies with extra force to this project’s quiet structural critiques (the Cradle Circle/war-widow pipeline parallel, the preserve system, the propaganda-fragment device), which only work if nobody on the page states the point. Let facts sit next to each other and stop. See Qiraki_Process_Notes.md’s dedicated section on this discipline.

**Worked examples, added 2026-08-05:**

WRONG: *“************…and something in him settled, the way it always did once the plan was moving instead of just spoken.************”* (states what the moment meant to him)

WRONG: *“************…he’d never tell Reqa what it cost him to say it, and some part of him already knew he never would.************”* (names the emotional mechanism directly)

RIGHT: End on the concrete image or the choice itself, cut the sentence that would have explained it. *“************He racks the last magazine and doesn’t look at her while he does it.************”* Stop there. Let the reader supply the rest, the same discipline as Emotion_Craft_Reference’s “tell, not the feeling” entry, applied at the scene level instead of the sentence level.

## 5. NO SYMMETRICAL SENTENCES

Avoid structural dualism and perfectly balanced sentence construction. Same rule as The Ninth, carried over as a universal craft discipline rather than something specific to that project.

**Worked example, added 2026-08-05:**

WRONG: *“************He trusted the mech with his life, and the mech answered with everything it had.************”* (mirrored halves, matched clause lengths, the sentence balancing on itself instead of moving)

RIGHT: Break the pair apart, give each half its own weight rather than a matched set. *“************He trusts the mech with his life. It’s never once given him a reason not to.************”* Two sentences doing different work instead of one sentence doing the same work twice.

## 6. GROUNDED THEORY & RESEARCHED REALISM (the project’s own version of “anthropological realism”)

This is this project’s central discipline, worth stating as its own rule rather than folding into general craft notes. Before inventing any system, technology, or cultural mechanic, ground it in real, existing science or research as far as it will legitimately carry, and verify rather than guess. Already proven out repeatedly this session: graph theory and boolean logic under the rune-tech grammar, real materials science and control theory under the engineering curriculum, real RPD/System 1-System 2 cognitive research behind Trav’s command arc, real habit-formation research (Lally et al., 2010) correcting the timing of the joke-ritual, real constructed-emotion research (Barrett, 2017) underneath Qiraki_Emotion_Craft_Reference.md. Each time this discipline was applied, the story got measurably stronger and more specific, not just more “realistic” in a vague sense.

Where real theory runs out, the break is named explicitly, not blurred. Sovereign- and Heirloom-grade effects are the flagged exception, deliberately “bullshit tech,” and should read as visibly, deliberately different in kind from everything grounded below them, not a smooth continuation of the same logic. The vast majority of the curriculum, technology, and world mechanics should sit on the real side of that line.

No caricatures. Every species gets the same anthropological seriousness Auel gave Ice Age human cultures, sophisticated internal logic, no species exists just to be quirky or comic relief, even the ones that are funny (Hiopi, the propaganda-fragment device) are funny because the underlying logic is real and specific, not because they’re simplified for a laugh.

## 7. PACING AND STRUCTURE

~4,500 word average chapters, ~210,000 word books (47 chapters, unchanged chapter count), one book per academy year for the first seven, weekly posting. **Revised 2026-08-14**, up from the original 2,500/130,000 target. Chapter count and the Act split (12/11/13/11) are unaffected, only the per-chapter and per-book length changed, so Qiraki_Chapter_Index.md does not need regenerating. B1.CH01 (4,672 words, delivered) was already running at this weight before the target caught up to it. **Book 7 exception, new 2026-08-20:** the one series-wide break from 47 chapters — 54 chapters, 12/11/13/18, per Qiraki_Book7_Chapter_Outline.md. Act Four’s own content (naming arc, Heirloom exercises, the exam, the epilogue) didn’t fit 11 chapters even at the low end of every range; rather than split into two books, it runs longer instead. Chapter-length target is unaffected, same 4,500 average applies.

**Clarification added, discussion of chapter length vs. realism, 2026-08-05, figures updated 2026-08-14.** “~4,500 average” means average across the book, not a per-chapter floor or ceiling. A chapter’s length is decided by where it actually finishes doing its job, not by a target number. Some chapters will run shorter, some longer, that’s the average working as intended. The failure mode to actually watch for isn’t a chapter landing at 4,000, it’s a *pattern* of chapters consistently landing under ~3,500 across a stretch, that’s usually a sign the grounding work itself is being cut short (resistance beats skipped, sensory weight thin, researched detail undersold), a discipline problem wearing a word-count shape. Diagnose it as the latter before reaching for word count as the fix, adding words back rarely repairs what thin grounding actually costs a chapter.

Daily-rhythm chapter template available for ordinary days needing full length: breakfast/social, class 1, class 2, class 3, optional evening beat.

Mission-chapter rhythm (military era): minimum 3 mission chapters per major arc beat, cooldown block of 3-4 chapters after roughly 6-7 running mission chapters.

Study-session chapters as a standing device, real curriculum content delivered through Trav and Reqa’s own learning, doubling as reader education.

No trope used once and abandoned, every deployed trope needs a planted downstream callback, tracked in Qiraki_Book_Expansion_Plan.md.

## 8. LANGUAGE & NAMING CONSTRAINTS

Naming philosophy: simple, punchy, kinetic-pad-game-derived slang for most in-universe terms. Exceptions, deliberately breaking the convention: species’ own self-descriptions (Longsight, Sprout), and Sovereign/Heirloom-grade tech, which should sound like inherited privilege rather than earned gear.

“Qiraki” is spoiler-locked, the enemy’s true name, never appears in drafted prose before the reveal point. See Qiraki_Process_Notes.md.

Mek (the mechanic role/person) and mech (the machine) are never interchanged.

## 9. CONTENT CONSTRAINTS, HARD RULES

No romantic or sexual content involving any character while they are a minor, full stop, no exceptions. This story tracks its cast from early adolescence into adulthood. The Trav/Reqa romance is already locked to begin only once he’s in his main combat unit, years past graduation, well into adulthood by any reasonable reading of the timeline, and that sequencing is a hard boundary, not a preference. Any content involving cadets during the academy years stays entirely in the register of friendship, rivalry, and found family, nothing romantic or sexual, regardless of what any individual scene might seem to invite.

Violence, injury, and body horror (Bloom biology, combat, restock) can be graphic and specific, consistent with the gritty/gory target, but this sits in a different category entirely from the rule above, which has no exceptions and no sliding scale.

Any potential sex scenes involving adult characters, once that’s actually reached in the timeline, fade to black, same standard as The Ninth, for the same publishing-platform reasons.

### 10. Ordinary resistance under duress

*(Promoted from Qiraki_Emotion_Craft_Reference.md, 2026-08-05.)*

Ordinary actions meet ordinary resistance from mundane sources under stress. A physical action doesn’t complete cleanly (that reads as unearned calm) and doesn’t fail from added drama either (that reads as manufactured tension). The resistance comes from the mundane thing itself, a stuck zipper, a numb hand fumbling a strap, a door that sticks the way it always sticks, not from the scene’s emotional stakes inflating the physical difficulty. Originally surfaced on The Ninth (Ch.33 pass, a fix to one grief gesture), generalized and adopted directly for Qiraki.

### 11. The tell, not the feeling

*(Promoted from Qiraki_Emotion_Craft_Reference.md, 2026-08-05. Named here as a corollary of Rule 4, not a freestanding discovery, Rule 4 bans stating what a scene meant, this is the same discipline scoped down to a single character beat rather than a new principle in its own right.)*

Every locked character’s emotional register in the Character Sheets already routes through one specific, repeatable physical or behavioral tell rather than a named internal state, Trav’s forward-set weight, Reqa’s mouth-watching, Denic’s cockpit stillness. State the tell, trust the reader, stop there, the same restraint Rule 4 already demands at the scene level. See Qiraki_Emotion_Craft_Reference.md’s per-character notes and replacement-pattern list for the worked examples across the cast.

### 12. Isolated single-line paragraphs are a tool, not a habit

*(Added, Cross-Project Writer’s Note update — Lesson 11, 2026-08-05.)*

Holding a line alone as its own paragraph earns its keep only when the line marks a genuine narrative hinge, the exact moment suspicion turns into fact, a search becomes a discovery. Used to dress up an ordinary beat, a hit landing, a mundane detail, an aside, it reads as posturing the material hasn’t earned.

**Test:** does the isolation come from what the line is doing in the story, or from wanting the line to feel dramatic? If the second, fold it back into the paragraph around it.

**Grounding check, added 2026-08-05:** checked both drafted chapters (Book1 Ch.1 v2, Ch.2) directly rather than assuming, per the writer’s note’s own Lesson 2, verify against delivered text, not memory. Neither chapter currently uses an isolated single-line paragraph anywhere, so there’s no existing instance to audit yet, correct or incorrect, and no worked example from Qiraki’s own prose to cite until a chapter actually calls for a real hinge moment. One plausible future test case is already sitting in the draft: the socket Trav’s mother presses into his hand at the end of Ch.1 (“I don’t know what it’s for either”) is a planted open thread, and whichever future chapter reveals what it’s actually for is a legitimate candidate for this technique when that scene gets drafted, worth checking against this rule specifically when it does.

### 13. Warmth lives in the dialogue, the dark lives in the background

*(Added, Ch.1 tally session, 2026-08-12.)*

The setting is genuinely bleak, war-widow economics, an illiterate twelve-year-old shipped off to a militarized academy, and that bleakness should stay real and stay in the background, in objects, facts, and things left unsaid, the same discipline as Rule 4. But the people talking over that backdrop don’t have to carry the bleakness in their voices too. Light, warm, funny dialogue between characters who clearly like each other is what makes a dystopia feel lived-in instead of oppressive, and it’s what this project needs to actually land with the teen and young-adult readers it’s written for. A coming-of-age story lives or dies on whether the reader wants to spend the next six books next to these people.

**Grounding check, added same date:** checked against the current Ch.1 draft rather than assumed. Denic already does this correctly, funny, warm, immediately likable, against a setup that’s just as bleak as everyone else’s. Every other relationship in the same draft, Trav and his mother, Trav and the shop owner, currently isn’t, rationed down to bare-minimum lines or silent gesture instead of real exchange. The gap between Denic’s scene and the rest of the chapter is the actual proof this rule is right, not a hypothetical.

## A NOTE ON WHAT THIS GUIDE PROTECTS

The Auel comparison is a genuine, earned one, not just a flattering reference. It gives this project real permission to be dense with researched material, real science under the systems, real anthropology under the species, without apologizing for the length that takes. The version of this story that skips the research and just gestures at “sci-fi stuff happening” is a weaker, more generic book than the one already being built tonight. Protect the discipline that’s already working.
