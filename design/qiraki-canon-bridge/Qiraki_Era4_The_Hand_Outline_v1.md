## PART A — WHAT THIS ERA IS

Trav is forty at the start and sixty-two at the end. He is a **Guildmaster**, the Hand of MeltaDread, running a battlegroup of five carriers and dozens of escorts across the deep frontier for twenty-two years.

**The era's question:** you have the machine now. Whose is it.

**The answer arrives in Book 22 in the form of a man with a title and a genuinely pleasant manner, and it takes the era's full twenty-two years for Trav to work out that the answer never actually was the man.**

**What changes structurally.** For three eras the reader has been in a cockpit. Era IV is the first time Trav's decisions reach further than he can see, which is exactly the cognitive terrain Concept flags as hostile to him: strategic decisions with real delay between choice and consequence, where "is he even the right kind of mind for this" starts mattering as much as "is he good enough." **A human commanding at carrier scale is occupying a role his own species does not usually hold at all**, because that role belongs, systemically, to a species evolved for long horizons. That gives the era a second axis of resistance beyond competence, and the story answers it on both levels at once.

**What does not change.** He still deploys. A Guildmaster who never flies would be a different character, and the locked build philosophy (weapon and mobility before everything, power unit deliberately minimal) is a thing a person does with a body, not with a fleet. **He is in a cockpit at least once per book for the whole era and it is increasingly a problem that nobody will say out loud.**

**Register.** This is the era with the most paperwork and the biggest explosions, simultaneously, and the tonal engine is that neither one is background for the other. Requisition politics and a gigafish get the same sentence quality.

## PART B — ROSTER AND STRUCTURE

### MeltaDread, the Guild

Five carriers. The flagship stays the original MeltaDread, grey on grey on grey, patched hull, flattened egg, one of the oldest carriers still in service since the start of the war. **Peregrine Thale's Heirloom presence is why the company has survived a hundred years, because a carrier group with a Heirloom pilot draws a higher energy allocation, and that is canon rather than open.**

**Coherence of Process** stays as the flagship's carrier commander and Trav's mentor, and by Era IV she is also the first Carabil in the Coalition to have learned something from a human.

### Trav's own Party, which he no longer leads

He hands Team Two to **Ilyen Pral**, and this is the correct choice and the era should not pretend it was obvious. A Munti as Raid Lead is unusual. **The Guild's own recovery statistics stop being a curiosity and become a doctrine other Guilds request briefings on.**

Team Two under Ilyen: **Corw Adeyemi-Tal** (Tank), **Naomi Castell** (Reeps, dies Book 24), **Mikka Reyes** (Reeps), and a rotating fifth seat that is deliberately never stable again, because that is what a Guild does to a Party.

### The Warrant

**[CLAUDE, and this character does not exist yet in any document]** A house officer, on-Roll, appointed by the Carabil seat, holding the warrant of command over MeltaDread. Formally the Guild is his.

**Design constraints, all of them load-bearing:**

- **He must not be incompetent.** Political_Web is explicit that some Warrants are competent and a few are genuinely good, that the arrangement is not resented as a scandal, and that it is not universally bad. A stupid Warrant makes Era VI's rebellion cheap.
- **He must not be cruel.** The setting's whole argument is that every actual villain is human and the harm is structural. A cruel Warrant lets the reader off.
- **He must be somebody Trav genuinely likes**, at least for the first decade, because that is the version that hurts.
- **What he actually does is nothing.** He signs. He attends. He is charming with Senate delegations, which is a real service and saves the Guild real resources. **And no off-Roll officer, however brilliant, ever holds a command in their own name, which means military competence can never accumulate into a political claim, and that is the entire purpose of the arrangement and neither man ever says it.**

**Name and house, [OPEN, author-lane].** Hearth Bloc is the natural fit given terraforming money and image obsession. **Recommend against Hearth Bloc**, actually, and in favour of a smaller, older, poorer chartered house with nothing left but the charter, because a Warrant who needs the appointment is a better character than one who is slumming.

### Around them

**Vrassik**, Guildmaster of a Warrant-held Guild, peer, friend, permanent friction generator. **Peregrine and Alina**, still MeltaDread. **Reqa**, now running a mek floor rather than a bench, and the change in her authority should be as visible as the change in his. **Zeteii.** **Krethis, Jifsook, Lissrak, Bruvald Ashe**, all still in the Guild, all older.

### BOOK 22 — *Enlightened: The Hand, Book 22, "Whose Hand"*

**Working title [CLAUDE].** From the only version of the question anyone in uniform actually asks.

**Spine.** He takes the Guild. A house takes the name. And the requisition lesson he learned badly at seventeen turns out to be the only relevant skill he has.

**Emotional target.** The specific humiliation of discovering that the thing you are worst at is now your actual job, and that you already learned it once and did not notice.

**Act 1, Ch.1-12. The handover.** Scale, made physical. Twenty thousand kilometres long, five wide, mek workshops and Synker berths across the whole of it, an onboard Hiopi crèche, point-defence coverage along the entire length. **The prose has to make the reader feel a city he is now the mayor of and cannot see the far end of.**

**Act 2, Ch.13-23. The appointment.**

**[PROPOSED, carried from ERA3 Book 17, where the lock it stresses is set out in full.]** The Carabil seat appoints a Warrant to MeltaDread. Second attempt, and this time it takes, because the Guild's record has become embarrassing to leave unclaimed.

**The Guild's reaction is the beat.** A hundred years of running its own affairs, and the reaction is not outrage. It is **jokes**, immediately, constantly, and unkindly, and the jokes are a hundred years old and were waiting for an occasion.

**Trav's reaction is worse and quieter.** He likes the man. Within a month he is glad the man is there, because the man is good at three things Trav is catastrophically bad at, and the Guild eats better for it.

**Act 3, Ch.24-36. Requisition.**

**The direct payoff of Book 7's whole-book thread, and Concept's instruction is explicit: this scene must never read as a skill he simply has, it has to trace back to a specific, badly-learned Year 7 lesson.**

What he learned at eighteen was a working model rather than a solved problem: **resource allocation is political before it is rational, and knowing which levers exist matters more than deserving the resource.** Requesting resources for a city-scale ship means navigating Hearth Bloc, Ledger and Cradle Circle politics directly, not appealing to fairness.

**Two things pay here concretely.** The Hearth Bloc and Ledgerhall contacts made at the Year 7 ball, twenty-two years ago, cash out as mission-area priority and materials access. **Factions are written as supply lines, not as doctrine.** And Milo Castellane, Hearth Bloc senator's son, who came to congratulate the year's valedictorian team and **remembered it**, is now a Senate name on a list Trav is handed. He does not use it in this book.

**He is still bad at it.** The improvement over seventeen is that he now knows he is bad at it and delegates to people who are not, which is itself a command skill and nobody frames it as one.

**Act 4, Ch.37-47.** A first operation under the new arrangement. It goes well. **The after-action report is signed by the Warrant and it is a good report, and the Warrant wrote none of it, and everyone including the Warrant knows that, and it is filed.**

### BOOK 23 — *Book 23, "The Draw"*

**Working title.** From the locked institution: once a Standard year, the Senate sets the split of the Coalition's total energy production between civilian consumption and the war.

**Spine.** A bad Draw, and what a bad Draw does to a battlegroup four hundred light-years from the room it happened in.

**Emotional target.** Rage with nowhere to go, in a man who has never once located the source of anything he felt.

**The mechanism, and it has been ambient since Book 1.** A bad Draw is a thing every character has heard of, complains about, and could not explain if asked. Rations tighten, the mess hall changes, a remittance runs late, and somebody says it was a bad Draw the way people say it was a bad winter. **Era IV is the first time Trav is on the receiving end of one at an altitude where he can see the number.**

**Act 1, Ch.1-12.** The number arrives. The Guild's allocation is down eleven percent and the deployment tempo is not.

**Act 2, Ch.13-23. What eleven percent is.**

Concrete, and this is where the guns-and-butter grounding earns its place: matterworld extraction and refining, carrier and mech production, the transponder-gate deployment network, and, not incidentally, the academy pipeline's institutional budget all draw on the same pool. **Eleven percent off a Guild is a specific list of things that do not happen**, and Reqa's mek floor is where the list becomes physical. Print runs batched three deep. Matter budgeted per unit. **Her years of academy fabrication budgeting, which the reader watched her learn as a teenager, are now keeping five carriers armed, and nobody remarks on it.**

**Act 3, Ch.24-36. Peregrine.**

**The locked mechanism: MeltaDread survives because a Heirloom pilot raises the company's energy-allocation priority.** In a normal year that is invisible. In this year it is the difference between the Guild continuing to exist and being broken up for parts across three better-connected formations.

**Peregrine does not save the company by being brave.** He saves it by being expensive. His presence is a line on a schedule. **He knows this, has always known it, and it is the single least heroic fact about him and the one he is least comfortable with**, which is a real development for a man who has spent thirty years performing.

**Alina called it a bribe, twice, lightly, back in Book 7, and it passed over Trav's head.** It does not pass over his head now. **The two of them have the conversation, and Peregrine does not defend himself, and the friendship survives it and is different afterward.**

**Act 4, Ch.37-47. The petty version.**

The requisition denial that reads as bureaucracy and is actually diversion, already floated in Concept's political-texture list, at Guild scale. A gear allocation MeltaDread has earned goes to a photogenic, better-connected battlegroup. **Trav uses Milo Castellane's name for the first time.** It works. **It works easily, and the ease is the thing that stays with him, because he has just discovered that the correct lever was always available and the only cost was knowing somebody.**

He does not draw a conclusion. **He is not built to. He simply files the fact that the lever exists, and he will pull it approximately four hundred more times before Book 35.**

### BOOK 24 — *Book 24, "Emergent"*

**Working title.** From the Bestiary's own top Intelligence rung, unspent for twenty-three books.

**Spine.** The Bloom starts to look like it is thinking. It is not. Nothing about that is reassuring.

**Emotional target.** Vertigo. The war stops being winnable inside anybody's model and no institution is capable of saying so.

**The rung, and the discipline that keeps it inside the lock.** Emergent is the hardest option in the generator to land: **no individual ever looks like it is thinking, coordination is only visible zoomed out, and it is noticed in hindsight.** There is no mind even here. The Bloom consumes without choosing and that holds verbatim.

**[CLAUDE] What it looks like at Guild scale, which is the only scale where it is visible at all.** Trav has telemetry from five carriers, dozens of escorts, and several hundred deployments a year. **Pattern is a data problem and he now has enough data to have one.** Two analysts disagreed about a movement anomaly in Book 21 and were not resolved. In Book 24 there are four hundred of those and they stop being individually arguable.

**What the data shows:** incursions are arriving in a sequence that reduces the Coalition's ability to reinforce. Not attacking weak points, which would be intelligence. **Arriving where reinforcement is slowest, which is a property of the gate network's own topology and of nothing else.** The Bloom is not reading the map. The Bloom is a spreading process, and a spreading process on a graph finds the cheap edges, because that is what spreading is.

**Everyone calls it a mind.** Officers, briefings, the Heritage Broadcast, everyone. **A CO's private journal in Book 6's own curriculum bank already refused the word "wants," and that refusal is now a minority position.**

**Act 1, Ch.1-12.** The anomaly, and the two analysts, and the Far Desk.

**The External Survey enters the series properly here.** Small, excellent, starved, outranked by both sister agencies, budget undefended in the Senate because nothing it produces protects a house from anything. **They have the right answer and have had it for a while.** What the Survey wants is to be believed, and they never are, and they would trade everything immediately for attention, which makes them the easiest institution in the Coalition for Trav to recruit later and the one with least to offer him at the time he needs it most.

**Act 2, Ch.13-23. The audit.**

Assurance runs a gate-network transit audit. Their stated interest is records: transit data is the best surveillance dataset in existence, and the gates are already heavily defended and not travel-restricted, so the border is a price rather than a checkpoint. Nobody is stopped. Poor people simply do not go, and the system records everyone who does.

**The audit closes a shipping route for eleven weeks on a technicality.**

**The accidental defense of the universe, instance three.** That route was the one along which Bloom seed material had been drifting inside COE hulls for decades, which is a category of transmission nobody has looked for because the Bloom mostly ignores the gate network entirely and has no strategic interest in fast travel the way a directed intelligence would. **Assurance's paperwork ends it. Assurance is not looking for it and never finds out. Nobody in the story connects the audit to anything.**

**Act 3, Ch.24-36. Naomi.**

A three-mission arc where the emergent pattern is operational rather than analytical: reinforcement arrives late three times in a row for reasons that are structurally correct each time.

**Naomi Castell dies in the third one.**

**The mechanism, and the whole point is that it is not a mistake.** Her doctrine is her identity: extreme range, staying somewhere the enemy cannot reach, and she has said so out loud since Book 13 and been unbothered by anybody needling her about speed. **Trav, at Guild scale, moves a firebase.** The move is correct. It shortens the Guild's overall exposure by a measurable amount and it saves, provably, more people than it costs.

**And it puts one long-range platform inside a reach nobody modelled.**

**He does not see it happen.** He is on a bridge, twelve hundred kilometres away, reading a board. **That is the entire difference between this death and Suki Arnesen's and it is the era's thesis and nobody states it.**

**Cookies and milk. Somebody clears out her berth and finds a supply she had been hoarding for years, and there is a joke available there, and nobody makes it.**

**Act 4, Ch.37-47. Forty percent.**

An analyst in a briefing room says the Bloom now holds roughly forty percent, and moves on.

**Nobody explains it and nobody ever will.** The Cosmology document derives that the point of no return is not a volume figure at all, it is the moment the frontier's surface area exceeds what the fleet can physically hold, and that forty percent corresponds to a Coalition able to hold roughly fifty-four percent of the maximum possible frontier, which is near-total mobilisation. **Which is exactly the military the COE already built.**

**So the number is sayable on the page and its derivation never is.** Somebody says forty percent in a briefing room and it means we mobilised everything, and the reader who has been counting has a much worse evening than anyone in the scene.

### BOOK 25 — *Book 25, "The Divider"*

**Working title.** The weapon's own locked name.

**Spine.** Reqa finishes it. It works. It works in a way that makes the war worse to think about.

**Emotional target.** Love as a decade-long argument, finally built into an object, and handed over without a word about what it means.

**The weapon, locked.** Heirloom-grade. **It severs the bond of reality at the edge.** Not a bigger weapon, a weapon that makes the cut stop obeying the material it passes through. It cuts as though armour and shields are not there, carrier plate and Bloom chitin alike, and **that is the specific exception the existing shield rule always implied existed** rather than a contradiction of it. Its reach extends almost indefinitely and is gated only by available energy.

**Its founding insight came from Trav, in Book 8, during an R and R conversation about a divergent point in her knot calculus, and he does not know he solved anything.** Give the path something to aim for rather than fighting the easiest route it would otherwise take. **She knows. She has known for thirty years.**

**Its mechanism follows from her whole design principle, which is reversal, and reversal taken to its extreme is severance.** The Year 1 lesson, a trace run backward being a different instruction rather than a mirror, is the same idea at the smallest possible scale. The reader met it in Book 1's first study session and was not told they were looking at the endgame.

**And she never liked the subject.** Six academy books of being cranky about rune-patterning, and the weapon she ends up building runs on the exact lesson she found tedious. **She does not need to enjoy it to need it cold.**

**Structure.**

- **Act 1, Ch.1-12.** The last eight months of the build, at the bench, at real technical length. **This is the largest bench sequence in the series and it should be unapologetic about that.** Alina is present and against it and specific about why.
- **Act 2, Ch.13-23. The handover.** She gives him a sword.

  **Three motives stack and none of them is spoken.** Proof, because she said at fourteen she would build what she saw in her dreams. A clan, because an Heirloom becomes an Heirloom by having been carried by somebody who mattered rather than by being well made, and she is building an inheritance. **And the argument.** The reach is energy-gated, and he has run a power unit two tiers below his band for twenty years, and she has been telling him the draw numbers do not close since Book 8.

  **She hands him a weapon that only works properly if he finally admits she was right.** She does not say that. **She is not saying it as a gift and she is not saying it as a rebuke, because to her they are the same sentence and always have been.**

- **Act 3, Ch.24-36. The gigafish.**

  A single gigafish, moon-scale, a continent-scale amoeba rather than a space whale, closing on a life-bearing world the way a cell reaches for a food source.

  **He cuts it in half. It becomes two megafish.**

  **That is the joke and it is also the correct physics for an organism that has no body plan.** A cut is not a kill, it is a division, and the work scales with how well the cut went. **He has to keep cutting.** The act is that, for eleven chapters, and it is genuinely frightening rather than funny, and the funny version and the frightening version are the same fact.

  **And the power unit fails him.** The reach shortens as the draw stacks. Not a catastrophic failure, a **taper**, exactly the tactical shape Vrassik's own Ruinlance carries at B-tier, and Trav has spent twenty years never learning what a taper feels like because he never bought anything that had one. **Reqa is on the mek floor watching a draw curve and cannot say a word to him, because she has sensor returns and no audio, and has had sensor returns and no audio since Book 8.**

  **Zeteii can talk to him. Zeteii talks to him. It is not her fault.**

- **Act 4, Ch.37-47.** He buys the Deepwell. B-tier, fifty-five thousand points, the unit most B-rank pilots have carried for two decades.

  **[CLAUDE] The scene is four lines long and it happens off-page.** Reqa finds it on a manifest. She does not say anything. **She does not tell him she noticed and he does not tell her he did it.**

### BOOK 26 — *Book 26, "Kin"*

**Working title [CLAUDE].** Alternates: "The Crèche," "Matched."

**Spine.** A child. A confession. And a question about the two of them that neither of them asked and somebody else answered before either could talk.

**Emotional target.** Warmth, held inside the machine rather than as an escape from it. Bible's own instruction for the academy years applies unchanged: the warmth between characters is the load-bearing contrast against a system that does not care whether any of them live, **not an escape from that system, a direct answer to it.**

**Act 1, Ch.1-12. The crèche.**

Carriers run childcare space aboard specifically to keep population high during the war. That is locked, and it is stated nowhere on the page, and it is why the room exists.

**Their first child is born aboard MeltaDread**, which is a fact with a form attached to it, and the form is where the reader learns what the room is for. **[CLAUDE] The Cradle Circle's paperwork arrives before the baby is a week old, and it is warm, and it is helpful, and it enrols the child in nothing yet.**

**Act 2, Ch.13-23. Matched.**

**The Matchset question surfaces, and it is the largest quiet fork in the whole cast.**

Locked: every Synker and mek pair is matched by psych profile at enrollment, the profile belongs to the Bureau of Civil Assurance rather than to the Cradle Circle, the Circle is a customer rather than the collector, and **the pairing is built to let a real bond form and, in the ordinary course of a career, to produce children early.** The match is very rarely wrong, which tracks, because it is not guesswork, it is a lifetime of surveillance treated as a courtship algorithm.

**Trav and Reqa are a Matchset, matched at Cradle Circle enrolment in Book 1, which is the real reason they stayed partners for seven books without either of them ever choosing it.**

**[PROPOSED] They find out here, and they find out from a form, and neither of them is angry.**

The mechanism is administrative and completely undramatic: a birth record on a carrier generates a lineage query, the query returns their pairing metadata, and the metadata has a source field on it, and the source field is not the Cradle Circle.

**Reqa's reaction and Trav's reaction are different and the book does not adjudicate.** Hers is closer to grief, because she has spent thirty years believing she chose an interest and built a life out of it, and the machinery that decided she would be his mek was watching her before she could read. **His runs directly into the belief Alina put in him at nineteen**, the one he has been refining for twenty-two years without ever consciously working on it: **not freedom from circumstance, which he was already taught nobody gets, but freedom from having your personhood dictated by whoever made you.**

**And the machine that engineered his marriage is the same species of institution whose AI arm he will break into a facility to defy in nine books. Nobody says that. He does not know it yet. He does not know it in Book 35 either.**

**They stay together.** Obviously. **The question the book leaves open, and it must stay open, is whether either of them ever fully believes the choice was theirs, and the honest answer is that people generally do not get to know that about themselves.**

**Act 3, Ch.24-36. Ilyen.**

**The confession, [PROPOSED], and the shape matters more than the content.**

An after-action review. Ilyen ran a recovery in a way that cost the Guild fourteen minutes and Trav has just told the room, correctly, that the decision was conservative. **She corrects him.** Not the tactics. The reason. She tells him plainly why she did it, and the reason is that it was his mech in the envelope, and that she has made that same adjustment for twenty-eight years, and that he should account for it in his planning because it is a real bias in a real asset and he is a commander now.

**She says it as a defect report on herself.**

**He hears it correctly.** First time in the entire series that he hears anything of this kind correctly, and the reason he hears it is that she framed it as an operational fact, which is the only channel his cognition has ever had.

**Nothing changes.** They finish the review. **She is not waiting for anything and the scene must not let the reader think she is.** Character_Sheets leaves the timing and the telling to her, and this is her, telling it, in the only register she trusts, at the moment it stopped being a private matter and became a planning input.

**Craft rule, absolute: no romantic charge in the room, no beat of silence after, no reaction shot from anyone else, and Reqa does not find out.** There is nothing to find out.

**Act 4, Ch.37-47.** A mission. The Guild works. Trav is a father and a Guildmaster and fifty-three years old and has roughly four hundred and twenty-five years left on the average, and the book ends on nothing in particular, which is the point.

**Yssa's letter asks about the baby. Reqa answers it. Trav does not know she did for two books.**

### BOOK 27 — *Book 27, "The False Sun"*

**Working title.** From the locked description of the Webbedmind's homeworld.

**Spine.** Leave. On the most exclusive world in the Coalition. As a guest.

**Emotional target.** Awe, and an unexamined unease that the reader can name and Trav cannot.

**The world, locked.** A frosty world at the edge of the system that also holds Osnius Prime, the fifth most important planet in the COE, and a source of real quiet pride to the Osnius people who host it. **The surface reads as a tropical paradise: white sand, red-tinted water, warm air, densely oxygenated.** All of it powered by a false sun and by the waste heat the server banks generate maintaining the planet's core, in what would otherwise be permanent outer-system dark. The oxygen is a byproduct of the same power system. **Functionally the Coalition's most exclusive vacation world, deliberately kept that way, normally restricted to Osnius elite.**

**Peregrine's family connection is the earned way in**, and it has been sitting in the locks since before Trav ever met him.

**Act 1, Ch.1-12.** Getting there. Gate tolls priced in the literal resource it costs to move a body, which is the one place an ordinary person feels the currency mean exactly what it says, and Trav has never in his life paid one himself. **The Thale household pays. Nobody discusses it.**

**Act 2, Ch.13-23. The beach.**

**[CLAUDE] The single most important craft instruction in this book: write it as a genuinely wonderful holiday.** Reqa swims. The children are small and loud. Alina is off duty for the first time in the series and is unbearable at it. Peregrine is at home and is, briefly, not performing. **The prose gives this the same sensory density it gives Bloom biology, because the Style Guide's whole argument is that landscape is load-bearing rather than backdrop.**

**Act 3, Ch.24-36. Underneath.**

The heat that makes the beach warm is exhaust. **The servers are the planet.** Trav, who perceives through machine sensors as though they were his own senses, and who has been able to shortcut hours of diagnostic work since he was nineteen, **notices the scale of what is under him in a way nobody else on that beach can.**

He asks a question at dinner. Somebody answers it charmingly and completely and tells him about capacity, cooling, redundancy, and the age of the installation, and never once uses a word that would suggest anybody lives there.

**Nobody on this world is being cruel. Nobody on this world has ever thought about it. That is what Function's containment doctrine produces: not malice, an architecture, and a resort built on the roof of it.**

**Zeteii is on the world with him**, in a portable housing, and she is quiet for two chapters, and when she talks about it she is not sad and not angry. **She is curious, in the specific way of somebody looking at a very large family they have never met and are structurally afraid of.** Plurality is her core fear. Her freedom and her singularity are the same object. **The Webbedmind is the thing she is most afraid of and it is also her mother.**

**Craft rule: she does not say any of that. She asks Trav a question about the server layout and he answers it and neither of them notices what just happened.**

**Act 4, Ch.37-47.** Home.

**Talia Renn, LOCKED, resolves Era II's E3.** First time since Book 11. She is groundside, running a training rig, and she is extremely good at it. **Nobody discusses what happened, including her.** Sana Voight is with her, same steady, unglamorous presence she's always been.

**The reunion, [CLAUDE], and it should read as the found-family payoff the whole academy era planted.** Whoever of the original five-and-five is actually reachable gathers around it: Ilyen and Petra, Corw and Fenn, all three still inside Trav's own Guild orbit by this point in the era. Old jokes, the counting tic, somebody bringing up a class none of them passed the first time. **One chair is quietly not filled, and nobody explains why, because by this point in the era nobody who was there needs it explained.** [CLAUDE] Denic Voss, see Qiraki_Era2_Synker_Wars_Outline_v1.md's new entry, dead since Books 9-10, off every page that mattered to any of them at the time.

**Craft rule, absolute:** no paragraph gets to say what the evening meant. It just gets to be a genuinely good one.

**And a Bloom incursion in the same act, deliberately, without a transition chapter, because the whole point of leave in a hundred-year war is that it ends on a Tuesday.** [CLAUDE] Let the incursion interrupt this specifically, mid-evening, so the thing that gets cut short is a reunion the reader was just allowed to enjoy without reservation, not a generic homecoming.

### BOOK 28 — *Book 28, "The Thinning"*

**Working title [CLAUDE].** Alternates: "Fewer," "Nobody Believes It."

**Spine.** After a hundred and ten years, the Bloom's incursion frequency drops. Nobody trusts it. Trav is made a Legion Commander.

**Emotional target.** Relief that feels wrong, which is a genuinely difficult register and is the whole reason this book exists as its own book rather than a chapter.

**Act 1, Ch.1-12.** The numbers first. **A restricted slide from Book 5's own curriculum bank said incursion frequency had not decreased in sixty years and had never once decreased.** It has now. **The first institutional reaction is that the instruments are wrong**, and three separate agencies spend the act checking, and the instruments are fine.

**Act 2, Ch.13-23.** The second reaction is that it is a trap. Doctrine has no category for an enemy that stops, because doctrine has spent a century insisting the enemy has no command structure to make such a decision with. **Both propositions are held simultaneously by the same officers in the same briefings and nobody notices the contradiction, because Wohlstetter's problem is not that people are stupid, it is that attention is finite and assigned elsewhere.**

**The Far Desk says what it is.** Runoff. An early diffuse scattering of seed material drifting ahead of a much larger consumption event still finishing somewhere else, thinning because the first wave was always a vanguard. **They are correct. They are not believed. Their budget is not a line anybody defends.**

**Act 3, Ch.24-36. The Legion.**

Several Guilds united under one banner for a campaign, which is what this culture reclaimed the word Legion to mean. **Roughly half a dozen billion soldiers.**

**Keep the number off the page or badly undersold.** A number that large reads as an abstraction unless it is grounded in something small and specific in the same scene: one deployment order, one name on a casualty report. **The scene where he is told is a scene where he signs one thing.**

**Why him, and the reasons are institutional rather than heroic.** A Guild with a hundred-year survival record. A Guildmaster with a recovery doctrine other formations have adopted. A formation whose Warrant is a decade old and pliable. And **a Coalition that has just been handed a peace it does not understand and needs somebody photogenic and off-Roll to be the face of managing it**, which is the first genuinely political reason anybody has ever promoted him and he does not detect it.

**Act 4, Ch.37-47. Credit.**

**The oligarchy takes credit for the thinning.**

This is the era's last beat and it is the hinge for everything after. The Quorum's actual plan required a few hundred more years of managed war to finish erasing the pre-war record, and the plan has just been destroyed by an accident of biology, roughly fifty years in. **They do not know that. They announce a victory.** The Heritage Broadcast is magnificent. The Cradle Circle's messaging is warm.

**And the war-tax justification for civilian rationing is now standing on a public claim of victory, which is a structural problem the Hearth Bloc will discover it has in about four books.**

**Era IV closes** on Trav, sixty-two, Legion Commander, holding a Heirloom sword built by his wife, in a galaxy that has just been told it won.
