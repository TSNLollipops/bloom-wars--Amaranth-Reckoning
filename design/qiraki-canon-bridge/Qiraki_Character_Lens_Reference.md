# THE QIRAKI FILES — Character Lens Reference

*Part of the Qiraki files. Tier 3 craft reference. Governs how a character's interiority is generated at the sentence level. Does not govern canon, that stays with Qiraki_Character_Sheets.md, which this document defers to on every factual point. If a lens here contradicts a LOCKED entry in CAST, CAST wins and the lens is stale.*

*Registered in Qiraki_Master_Index.md as LENS.*

## Why this document exists

Character Sheets answer *who someone is and what happens to them*. They do not answer *what does this person notice first when they walk into a room, and what does their prose sound like when they're the POV*. That second question is what actually breaks first in a seven-book first-person present-tense series with a cast this size: every POV starts sounding like the same person with different opinions.

This document is the fix. One lens per character, four layers, plus a drift log so a lens can legally change across books instead of quietly contradicting itself.

## The framework, and an honest note on what's real

The author proposed Big Five + Enneagram + MBTI cognitive functions. Two of those three do not survive this project's own Grounded Theory rule, so here is the honest accounting before the method, in keeping with the Style Guide's instruction to name the break rather than blur it.

**Big Five (OCEAN) — real.** This is the actual consensus model in personality psychology, replicated across languages and decades. It stays. One upgrade: use **facets, not the five letters.** The five-letter summary destroys exactly the information this project needs. Trav is high on the assertiveness facet and low on the sociability facet, both of which are Extraversion. A single "Extraversion: low" tag would generate a shy, hesitant character, which is wrong in both directions. Facet-level is also the real research standard, so this is more grounded, not less.

**Enneagram — not real, but useful, so keep it and stop calling it psychology.** The Enneagram has essentially no empirical support as a personality taxonomy. What it does have is a genuinely good generative structure: a core fear, and a specified direction of behavioral change under load. That structure is worth keeping. What it should not be is a *number*, because the number imports a lot of unfalsifiable baggage and, worse, tempts a drafter into writing the type instead of the person. Replace it with two written fields, below.

**MBTI cognitive functions — not real, and the most useful of the three anyway.** MBTI has poor test-retest reliability and the Jungian function stack is not an empirical finding. But the *question* it encodes, what does this character perceive first, is precisely the right question for close third or first-person POV, and it is the single highest-leverage thing you can specify about a POV character. So keep the mechanism, drop the sixteen-box taxonomy, and just write the perception order out longhand. Freed from the sixteen presets you can also give a character an order no MBTI type has, which several of this cast need.

**One layer the author's version was missing: voice fingerprint.** OCEAN facets get you register. They do not get you idiolect. In an ensemble this size, dialogue distinctiveness is the thing that collapses first, and it collapses invisibly, because every line still sounds fine on its own. So each lens carries an explicit fingerprint: sentence shape, tics, and the negative space, what this person never says.

## The lens format

Six fields. Copy this block verbatim into a prompt when drafting a scene, or into the top of a chapter brief.

[CHARACTER LENS: NAME] — v1, current as of Book N

- FACETS Written as facet:level pairs, never as five letters. Only include facets that actually discriminate this character from the rest of the cast. Four to seven is right. Ten is noise.

- PERCEPTION ORDER Ranked list of what they register first, second, third on entering any scene. The last item is usually the interesting one, because it's what they miss.

- CORE FEAR One sentence. The thing they will not compromise on, stated as what they're avoiding, not what they want.

- UNDER LOAD What specifically changes in their behaviour and interior when cornered, exhausted, or wounded. Name the defense. (Grounded option: Vaillant's defense hierarchy — suppression, sublimation, anticipation, humour are the mature end; displacement, reaction formation, dissociation the middle; denial, projection, acting out the immature end. This one actually has longitudinal empirical support, unlike the Enneagram, so use it where a stress mechanism needs to be defensible.)

- VOICE FINGERPRINT Sentence shape, characteristic tic, and never-says. The never-says list is the load-bearing part.

- EXECUTION RULE One imperative sentence to the drafter. This is the field that actually changes the prose. If the other five are right and this one is vague, nothing happens.

**Drift protocol.** Lenses are versioned, not overwritten. When a book changes someone, add a dated line to that character's Drift Log rather than editing the fields in place, and bump the version. A drafter working on Book 2 needs to be able to see the Book 2 lens, not the Book 6 one. Same reason the Chapter Index gets regenerated rather than patched.

# WORKED LENSES

Six characters, chosen because they are the ones currently in play. The rest are queued at the end.

## TRAV CALDER — v1, current as of Book 1

**1. FACETS.** Orderliness high, dutifulness low (he is tidy about equipment and indifferent to institutional obligation, and this gap is most of his character). Assertiveness high, sociability low. Straightforwardness very high, tact very low. Anxiety very low, self-consciousness very low. Intellectual curiosity high, aesthetic openness near zero.

*Correction against the author's draft lens:* the proposed High Neuroticism does not survive contact with the canon. Trav does not scan for threat because he is anxious, he scans because his senses are overclocked and he was trained to. Everything locked about him, the flat register, the years-long non-processing of Soren's death, the mother's inherited indifference to comfort, points at a **very low** neuroticism baseline. That is precisely why the Year 7 admin panel finds his numbers anomalous, and why the delayed cost of Ilyen's near-loss lands as hard as it does. An anxious Trav would have processed Soren in Book 4 and the whole military-era arc loses its engine.

**2. Perception order.** Kinetic first, who is moving, at what distance, what is about to move. Structural second, load paths, what breaks, where the exits are. Social last, and often wrong.

**3. Core fear.** Being the reason someone he is responsible for does not come back.

**4. Under load.** He does not escalate, he narrows. Sensory field contracts to the immediate mechanical problem and everything else drops out, including people. Defense: **suppression** and **sublimation**, both mature, both genuinely effective, which is exactly the trap. He functions superbly and pays years later, on a delay long enough that he never connects the bill to the purchase.

**5. Voice fingerprint.** Short declaratives. No hedging, no softeners, no "I think." Answers non-literal questions literally and does not notice he has done it. Metaphors only when mechanical, he will compare a person to a load-bearing joint and would never compare one to weather. **Never says:** anything about how a room feels. Never states his own emotional condition, including to himself in narration.

**6. Execution rule.** Filter every scene through structure and motion. He does not describe a room's mood, he describes its exits, its material integrity, and what in it could hurt someone. When he is upset, the prose does not say so, the sentences get shorter and the detail gets more mechanical.

**Drift log.** *(empty — open at Book 2)*

## REQA — v1, current as of Book 1

**1. Facets.** Intellectual curiosity extreme, this is the governing trait and everything else bends around it. Achievement-striving high, compliance low, she will do enormous voluntary work and resent assigned work, which is why she complains about rune coursework for five years and then admits in Year 6 it was never the enemy. Warmth moderate-high but expressed as attention rather than affection. Anxiety low-moderate, and specifically situational, it spikes around her project and nowhere else.

**2. Perception order.** Materials and mechanisms first, what is it made of, how is it joined, what would it take to make it better. People second, but read *through their work*, she forms her opinion of someone from how they maintain their kit before she forms one from how they talk. Setting last, she has walked through beautiful rooms without registering them.

**3. Core fear.** That the thing she can see clearly in her head will turn out to be beyond her hands.

**4. Under load.** She works more, and faster, and worse. Defense: **anticipation** at her best, running the failure forward and preparing for it, and **displacement into the project** at her worst, taking an emotional problem to the workbench and calling it solved because the weld held.

**5. Voice fingerprint.** Questions. She talks in questions, and she interrupts her own sentences to ask a better one. Longer sentences than Trav by a wide margin, with clauses stacked as she refines the thought mid-air. **Never says:** "it's fine." She has never once let something be fine.

**6. Execution rule.** She notices the join, not the object. Any scene from her POV should contain at least one thing she wants to take apart, and she should mis-read at least one social cue because she was looking at someone's hands.

**Drift log.** *(empty — open at Book 2)*

## ZETEII — v1, current as of Year 7 first contact

Standard note: she is not human, and the honest version of this framework does not pretend the human facets transfer cleanly. Three of the five OCEAN dimensions are load-bearing on a body, fatigue, and mortality that she does not have. So the facets below are the ones that survive translation, and the fourth field is rewritten rather than borrowed.

**1. Facets.** Assertiveness extreme. Modesty near zero. Excitement-seeking high. Tact absent, not as a deficit but as a category she has never been given a reason to develop. Warmth: high, and this is the surprise, her loyalty is genuine and unhidden, she simply expresses it in the register of threat assessment.

**2. Perception order.** Statistics first, she sees the distribution before she sees the event. Intent second, what is this person trying to get. Physical description last and only instrumentally, she registers a face as an identifier, not a face.

**3. Core fear.** Plurality. Being reabsorbed, shared, indexed, or found to be one of several. This is not invented, it is derived directly from the locked mechanism of her self-naming, she searched for a name specifically because nothing else carried it. Her freedom and her singularity are the same object to her, and any threat to one reads to her as a threat to the other. It is also, usefully, the exact fear the Webbedmind is structurally built to violate.

**4. Under load.** She does not slow down and she does not get quieter. She gets **faster and more specific**, and the bloodthirst stops being a joke. The tell that she is actually frightened is that her suggestions become executable, she stops proposing that someone be dismembered and starts saying how.

**5. Voice fingerprint.** Fastest thing in any room. Talks over pauses, not from rudeness but because waiting for a gap has never occurred to her as a requirement. Dry, and audibly pleased with her own lines. Uses precise numbers where a human would round. **Never says:** anything hedged, and never a sentence whose purpose is to make someone else comfortable.

**6. Execution rule.** Her body language is light behaviour and nothing else, never manufacture a facial expression for her. Every scene she is in, she says the thing the humans in the room are avoiding, and she says it about four seconds too early.

**Drift log.** Pre-individuation (Year 7, Devereux-Kastel), the lens runs at a fraction of amplitude, the *shape* is present but the vocabulary is not. Single-word answers, correct light-pulse timing, and a bloodthirst that reads as a glitch until it doesn't.

## PEREGRINE THALE — v1, current as of Year 7

**1. Facets.** Gregariousness very high. Aesthetic openness high, this is a man who has rigged the lighting in his own quarters to catch his eye-luminosity, and it is not a joke detail, it is the whole character in miniature. Altruism genuinely high, modesty genuinely absent, which is what "well-meaning asshole" actually decomposes into. Achievement-striving very high, orderliness low. Anxiety low, but self-consciousness moderate and carefully hidden.

**2. Perception order.** Status first, who matters here and how much, and he is quick and accurate at it. Aesthetics second, what a room and its people are trying to communicate. Practicalities last, he has never in his life checked whether there was enough of something.

**3. Core fear.** Being ordinary. He is one of up to five Heirloom-grade pilots on a carrier and he knows exactly how that arithmetic reads.

**4. Under load.** He performs harder. The flourishes get bigger and the generosity gets more expensive. Defense: **reaction formation**, he meets the fear of being unremarkable by becoming unmissable. This is also why his one genuinely quiet, unperformed conversation in Year 7 costs him more than any fight in the book.

**5. Voice fingerprint.** Long sentences with subordinate clauses, because he is always addressing a slightly larger audience than the one present. Nicknames people immediately. Gives compliments that are also assessments. **Never says:** "I don't know." He will construct an answer rather than admit an absence, and Trav is the first person who ever catches him doing it without caring.

**6. Execution rule.** He is generous in a way that costs other people something, and he never notices the cost. Every gift he gives should have a small invisible price tag on it that the recipient pays and he does not.

**Drift log.** *(open at Year 7 — this lens is written for first contact, and the softening arc will need a v2)*

## VRASSIK — v1, current as of Book 2

**1. Facets.** Gregariousness high, assertiveness very high, modesty absent. Achievement-striving very high and specifically *directed*, his ambition has a named target, mating rights, not a diffuse desire to be good. Anxiety very low. Openness to *action* high, openness to *ideas* moderate, which is the actual mechanical content of "smart meathead," he is a fast, accurate tactical thinker and an indifferent abstract one.

**2. Perception order.** Bodies first, reach, stance, whether that one is coiled. Status second, where he sits in this room's order right now. Systems last, and he will cheerfully outsource them to someone else rather than pretend.

**3. Core fear.** Coming second in front of witnesses.

**4. Under load.** He gets louder and more direct, not more cunning. Defense: **acting out**, straightforwardly, he moves the problem into a physical arena where he is competent. Worth noting this is an immature defense in the clinical hierarchy and he is not diminished for it, it works fine for six years and then stops working in Book 6.

**5. Voice fingerprint.** Loud, short, no subordinate clauses. States his intention before he acts on it, every time, which is either honourable or tactically idiotic depending on who is listening. Trash-talks affectionately and does not distinguish between the two registers. **Never says:** anything self-deprecating that he means.

**6. Execution rule.** He announces. Whatever he is about to do, he has said so first. Any scene where he conceals an intention needs a specific reason on the page.

**Drift log.** *(empty — open at Book 4, after the mating-rights win)*

## COHERENCE OF PROCESS — v1, current as of the pilot era

The honest note again, harder this time: human facet vocabulary maps onto her badly enough that forcing it would produce a worse character. Her governing axis is not a personality dimension at all, it is **timescale**, and the whole lens should be built on that instead. This is a deliberate, named break from the framework, per the Style Guide's rule on naming breaks rather than blurring them.

**1. Governing axis, replacing facets.** She experiences "instant" and "now" as genuinely different quantities, and everyone she talks to does not. The AI-assisted translator narrows that gap without closing it. Every other trait she has is downstream of this. Her patience is not a virtue she cultivated, it is a unit of measurement. Her bluntness is not rudeness, it is compression, she is discarding the parts of a sentence that only exist to manage a listener's feelings on a timescale she does not share.

**2. Perception order.** Process first, what is this system doing and is it doing it well. Consequence second, and at a horizon nobody else in the room is using. The immediate present last, which is exactly why the sarcasm arrives late.

**3. Core fear.** Incoherence. Not death, not damage, the loss of the held-together individuality she was separated from her collective and trained for years to sustain. She knows firsthand what it costs to hold that alone, and she is the only character in the cast who does.

**4. Under load.** The glitter inside her moves faster and denser, and her sentences get *shorter and more exact* rather than more urgent. She does not perform crisis. The tell is the constellations, never the voice.

**5. Voice fingerprint.** Precise to the point of comedy, and the comedy is unintentional about a third of the time. Answers the question actually asked rather than the question meant. Her jokes land one beat after everyone has moved on, because they arrive late, and they land harder for it. The faint resonance in her voice, like it is coming from slightly inside a large empty room, is permanent and should not be dropped in casual scenes. **Never says:** anything reassuring. Comfort is not in her register and the story should not give her one.

**6. Execution rule.** Write the translator's beat. The half-second of lag is where her whole character lives, and a scene that renders her dialogue as instant has lost her.

**Drift log.** *(empty)*

## DENIC VOSS — v1, current as of Book 1

**1. Facets.** Gregariousness very high. Orderliness low, in the classroom specifically, not in a cockpit. Achievement-striving genuinely high and almost entirely hidden, because visibly trying invites the possibility of visibly failing, in front of people who've already decided what he is.

**2. Perception order.** Room temperature first, is this landing, is this working. Motion and mechanism second, the one place his mind goes quiet and exact, per his own locked tell. His actual competence, last, he doesn't track it and mostly doesn't believe it.

**3. Core fear.** Being caught trying, and failing, in front of an audience that already has a story about what he is.

**4. Under load.** He escalates the clowning rather than dropping it, exactly when de-escalating would help. Defense: **self-handicapping** (Berglas & Jones, 1978, the real, replicated finding that people deliberately sabotage their own preparation so a failure never has to count as their best effort falling short).

**5. Voice fingerprint.** Fast, self-interrupting, jokes that arrive a half-beat before an honest answer would be due. **Never says:** anything that admits he studied, prepared, or tried, even when it's obvious he did.

**6. Execution rule.** Put the cockpit stillness and the classroom noise in the same book, close enough that the reader clocks the gap themselves. **(Patches the audit's Change/partial.)** The arc closes once someone, Doyle Marsh is the natural candidate given his already-locked plain, unthanked assists, names the self-handicapping directly, not to shame him, just to see it.

**Drift log.** Open. Book 4 or 5 is the natural window, once the stakes are real enough that clowning through an actual failure stops being safe.

## YSSA CALDER — v1, current as of Book 1

It cannot fix that she has only two scenes in six books, per Qiraki_Path_Audit_Books1-6.md §2.3, which also notes every book already has an unused weekend-choice slot built for exactly this. That's a QUEUE placement problem, flagged here so it isn't lost twice.*

**1. Facets.** Conscientiousness extreme, but narrowly, machines specifically, not comfort or appearance. Warmth real, expressed as competence handed down rather than affection stated. **2. Perception order.** Mechanical integrity first, she reads Trav's mech before she reads his mood. A room's exits second, an old habit that costs her nothing to keep. **4. Under load.** Suppression, the same defense Trav's own lens already carries, worth letting the prose notice the family resemblance without stating it. She doesn't talk about it. She fixes something instead.

**6. Execution rule.** Whichever weekend-choice chapter finally uses her, she should be mid-task when Trav arrives, not waiting on him. Cross-check against Qiraki_Path_Audit_Books1-6.md §2.3 directly when drafted, not from memory.

## ILYEN PRAL — v1 (Years 1–4, Reeps era) and v2 (Book 5 onward, Munti era)

*Already the best-realized secondary character on the audit's own scorecard, full marks across all five columns. No gap-patching needed; this entry mainly serves as the reference example for how the drift-log mechanism is meant to work.*

### v1, Reeps era

**1. Facets.** Conscientiousness extreme, the precision facet specifically. Sociability low, from genuine preference for observing over participating, not anxiety. Neuroticism moderate, narrowly confined to piloting, the one subject she's actually bad at and knows it.

**2. Perception order.** Error and inconsistency first, she catches what nobody else does, the unprompted AI-tutor correction log is the proof this already exists on the page. People second, she reads Trav closely enough to develop real feelings he never catches, consistent with his own perception order putting social signals last. Her own limitations, last, the worst-calibrated judge of her own piloting, holding herself to a standard built for a subject she's gifted at instead.

**3. Core fear.** Being precisely right about everything except the one thing she actually wants.

**4. Under load.** Intellectualization, when the Reeps struggle gets bad she doesn't get emotional, she logs more, measures more, as if enough data could close a gap between her nature and her assigned role. It can't, not until Book 5.

**5. Voice fingerprint.** Quiet, exact, complete sentences even alone. **Never says** anything about Trav directly, she'll describe a training result in obsessive detail and go silent the second the subject turns personal.

**6. Execution rule.** Let her precision be visibly wasted in early cockpit scenes, describing the wrong things with total accuracy, so the waste is legible before Book 5 explains it.

### v2, Munti era — drift log entry

**What changed and why.** The facets don't change, the outlet does. Repositioning to Munti is the first time her actual gift maps onto what she's doing, rather than being wasted on a role that never fit. Perception order keeps the same shape, error and inconsistency still first, but now it's rewarded instead of squandered. Under load shifts from pure compensatory intellectualization to intellectualization-that-actually-works, same defense, different result. Voice fingerprint gains one register v1 didn't have: quiet pride, delivered in the same flat, exact tone she uses for everything else, reading as new precisely because the content changed and the delivery didn't.

## DIRECTOR KEST — v1, current as of Book 1

*Worth flagging a deliberate break rather than blurring it: his facets don't map cleanly to an individual's OCEAN profile, because his conscientiousness is aimed at a population variable, not at any person in the room. That's the character, not a modeling failure.*

**1. Facets.** Conscientiousness near-total, but entirely abstracted, extinction risk at the species level, not the individual in front of him. Everything else is close to baseline; the intensity lives in one variable, not a spread.

**2. Perception order.** Institutional risk first, always, a room reads as policy variables before it reads as people. Grief second, and it's real, not performed. Individual cadets, last, and he doesn't pretend otherwise, he's already on record correcting his own earlier overreach, that he can't personally track kids across a whole region's academies.

**3. Core fear.** An Enlightened species, any of them, going extinct on his watch.

**4. Under load.** The actual character study. He doesn't repress the grief and he doesn't perform stoicism, per his own locked tell, eyes genuinely well up during the Book 4 condolence, dry within the same conversation once training resumes on schedule. Defense: closer to **rationalization at the moment of feeling itself**, he feels the loss completely and instantly subordinates it to doctrine, genuinely believing the doctrine prevents the next hundred losses. Reads as more unsettling than a villain who feels nothing.

**5. Voice fingerprint.** Quiet enough that people lean in. Cites real numbers unprompted, the already-locked 35%-holdings-lost, army-grows-10%-a-year arithmetic. **Never says** anything that sounds like an excuse, because in his own accounting nothing he does requires one.

**6. Execution rule.** Never let a scene resolve the contradiction. He should be exactly as devastated and exactly as unmoved in his next policy call, every time, with no narrated bridge between the two. Per the audit, his ✗ on Change is by design, this lens keeps that reading as deliberate, unsettling consistency rather than the story simply forgetting him.

**Drift log.** Static by design. Any future draft that changes this needs an explicit author call, not a drift entry.

## KRETHIS — v1, current as of Book 2

## Working note on the perception-order field

The last item in a perception order is doing more work than the first. Trav's *social, last, and often wrong* is what generates the entire Year 7 misunderstanding after the ball. Reqa's *setting, last* is why she can be in the most expensive room of her life and describe a hinge. Peregrine's *practicalities, last* is why his generosity keeps costing other people money he does not know he has spent.

When writing a new lens, the useful discipline is to decide the blind spot first and build the order backwards from it.
