# THE QIRAKI FILES — Book 4, “Blood Between Rivals,” Chapter-by-Chapter Outline

*Part of the Qiraki files. Same template and standing rules as Book 1’s outline. Cadets are 15-16 this book. This is the series’ first genuinely devastating year and should be allowed to be that without rushing to comfort the reader afterward.*

**Locked spine:** the roughest year, Trav and Vrassik injure each other repeatedly, Soren Adair dies, Vrassik wins his mating-rights bid. The joke-ritual reverses for the first time.

**Curriculum, Year 4:** rune-patterning reaches branching under pressure, conditional logic, circuits that behave differently depending on what they read. Repair and diagnostic coursework gets notably more sophisticated this year, the curriculum quietly answering to how often this cohort now actually needs it. Combat class runs Live Contact Sparring, longer duration, harder scenarios, more skilled opponents, the direct mechanism behind both the Trav/Vrassik damage and Soren’s death.

**Emotional target:** this is the cry book. It also needs real warmth to earn the grief, and gets it from an unlikely direction, Vrassik’s ritual win landing in the same season as the loss, Hiopi culture-clash comedy at its most festive colliding directly with mourning. Deliberate juxtaposition, not tonal whiplash.

## Act 1 — The year announces itself (ch. 1-12, January-March)

**Governing psychology:** still the associative stage, still the least reliable bodies in the program, now being asked to do meaningfully more. The danger was never new. What’s new is the scenario difficulty meeting kids who are objectively clumsier than they’ll be next year.

- **Live Contact.** Year 4’s combat coursework opens hard, wound-emulation sparring at longer duration against better opponents. The year announcing its own difficulty rather than easing in. Good slot for the Year 4 Bloom fragment on condolence-letter templates, dropped cold, no commentary.

- **The Pain, Worse.** The symbiosis spike carries over from Book 3 and deepens. Still unexamined. Still shown only through what Trav does differently, never through him considering it.

- **Branching.** Year 4 rune-patterning, conditional logic, real reader-facing content. The repair-and-diagnostic emphasis noted in-world as practical rather than academic.

- **Soren.** Soren Adair gets a real chapter this year, not a background presence, deliberately deepening the reader’s investment months before Act 3. The old literacy-group bond from Book 1 given weight. **Critical setup, per the casualty roster.**

- **First Blood, Year Four.** Trav and Vrassik, worse than Book 3, real damage exchanged, both of them accepting it as simply what this year is.

- **Study Session I.** Trav and Reqa, Year 4 diagnostic circuits, the curriculum’s practical turn visible.

- **Hotwire Money.** D-tier upgrades within reach for the strongest performers, real status attached, the snowball economy visibly compounding advantage.

- **Vrassik’s Year.** Vrassik-focused chapter, his mating-rights ambition sharpening into something specific and immediate. Jifsook needling him through it, Lissrak quietly supporting him, Perrin uninvested and therefore the only one he actually hears.

- **The Ensemble.** Ordinary daily-rhythm chapter, deliberately warm, the found family at its best. Placed here on purpose, the reader needs this before Act 3.

- **Talia and Trav.** Their long friction reaches its most honest moment yet, still unresolved, still real.

- **Second Blood.** Vrassik again, and this time one of them is genuinely, seriously hurt.

- **The Choice.** **No propaganda-fragment opener** (Qiraki_Process_Notes.md’s weekend-chapter exception). Weekend chapter, home now genuinely strange to kids who’ve bled this much, ahead of Act 3, worth letting this one stay light, the book gets heavy soon enough on its own.

## Act 2 — Attrition (ch. 13-23, April-July)

**Governing psychology:** normalization of injury, which is its own quiet horror, and the specific adolescent capacity to absorb something adults couldn’t.

- **Routine Damage.** The cohort treating serious injury as ordinary. A severed limb is a Tuesday. Nobody comments. *Psych: normalization at this age is real and adaptive, and the reader should find it more disturbing than the characters do.*

- **Ilyen, Still Struggling.** Her Reeps problem compounding, three years in, real cost, real frustration, no softening.

- **Study Session II.** Engineering-side.

- **Soren, Again.** Second Soren chapter, the friendship deepened specifically, his own path (Meeps, same as Trav) established as the thing that puts him in the same danger. **Do not signal what’s coming.**

- **The Fifth Kind.** Year 4 Bloom fragment lands, the veteran’s letter about the ones that don’t move, followed by ordinary training, no connecting commentary.

- **Denic, Load-Bearing.** Denic’s Munti work now genuinely holding the team together, the “unsung hero” respect earned on the page rather than asserted.

- **Third Blood.** Trav and Vrassik, the exchange now written into both their bodies permanently, the rivalry legible as scar tissue.

- **June.** Ensemble day, deliberately light, the last genuinely easy chapter of the book.

- **Corw and Fenn.** Corw’s ego arc progressing, Fenn’s unbothered calm still doing quiet work.

- **The Near Miss.** Someone almost doesn’t get pulled in time. The arena safety system works, barely. Direct, deliberate setup for what fails in Act 3.

- **Midpoint.** Ensemble chapter, everyone still alive, worth letting the reader feel that in retrospect.

## Act 3 — The loss (ch. 24-36, August-October)

**Governing psychology:** first real death in a cohort that had been taught, implicitly and correctly, that this almost never happens. Grief at fifteen is not adult grief, it’s more physical, less articulate, and far more socially contagious.

- **Ambition.** August, rivalry sharpest, the ordinary year continuing right up to the edge.

- **The Session.** Live Contact Sparring, an ordinary Thursday. Soren takes a survivable hit. The extraction window fails, chaos and escalated scenario difficulty meaning his Munti reaches him late. **The near-save must read as genuinely close, not a clean miss.**

- **After.** Immediate aftermath, the cohort’s reaction, no institutional comment yet.

- **Kest, In Person.** **Kest’s second appearance, LOCKED, a major one, reframed for scale.** He’s at the academy for a scheduled regional audit, the kind of visit that happens on a fixed cycle regardless of what’s occurred recently, not a dedicated trip for one cadet. Soren’s death happens to fall within that visit’s timing, and the cohort-wide address he gives, part condolence, part standard regional messaging, includes a genuinely sincere, specific acknowledgment of it. He means every word of that part. He is genuinely, visibly moved, for the few minutes it’s in front of him. The same address confirms training resumes on schedule tomorrow, standard audit-cycle language, and he’s not being cold when he says it, both things are completely real to him at once. **This is the reverse-trope characterization at full weight: his warmth and his ruthlessness come from the exact same place, and the chapter must never explain that, only show it, letting the reader do the assembling.** Worth being the chapter’s own quiet horror, more unsettling than a villain would be, because nothing he says or does here is dishonest, and none of it required him to know or remember Soren at all. **Consistency note, sets up Book 6’s Ch. 42 beat:** Trav is present for this but doesn’t register or retain who Kest specifically is, consistent with his locked non-reflective cognition, an administrator’s cohort-wide address during the worst week of his life is background noise, not something his RPD-style attention snags on.

- **The Ones Who Knew Him.** Trav, and the others from that first literacy group, sitting with it badly. *Psych: inarticulate grief, physical restlessness, no clean processing, no adult-shaped acceptance.*

- **The Reversal.** **Book 4’s most important character beat.** Reqa uses the joke-ritual on Trav, the first time in four years it runs in reverse. She doesn’t announce it or explain it. This is the origin of everything his humor becomes across Books 5-6. **Never internally explained by Trav.**

- **Back to Class.** The system continues. Training resumes. Nobody stops. The most quietly damning chapter in the book, and nobody says so.

- **Vigilance.** October, Year 4 Bloom fragments land against a cohort that now reads them completely differently.

- **Krethis, Unexpectedly.** Krethis, who dislikes Trav, doesn’t gloat, doesn’t soften either. A small, real, unresolved moment across the cohort divide.

- **Ilyen Notices.** Ilyen watching Trav more closely after the loss. **Third tell, LOCKED, sharper than**** ****“****watching****”****:** she starts a sentence about how he’s handling it and doesn’t finish it, twice, leaves both times before he’d think to ask why. The reader gets the shape of what she almost said. Trav gets a friend who checked in on him and left, nothing stranger than that. Still entirely platonic on the page, the long-fuse thread deepening invisibly.

- **The Team Holds.** Found family under real load, the actual thesis of the academy books tested for the first time.

- **Vrassik, Before.** Vrassik in the run-up to his ritual, ambition undimmed by a death that wasn’t his, honest rather than callous. Different species, different relationship to mortality, worth writing without judgment.

- **Countdown.** Year 4’s exam approaching, everything harder now.

## Act 4 — Grief and triumph in one season (ch. 37-47, November-December)

**Governing psychology:** the deliberate collision. Joy and mourning sharing a calendar, which is simply what happens in real institutions and real lives.

- **Homecoming, Fourth Year.** Diminished, one obvious absence, the team observing traditions that now hurt.

- **The Coliseum, Vrassik’s Year.** The ritual, and Vrassik wins. Full-blooded, festive, genuinely triumphant for him and his whole species’ worth of onlookers. **Placed here on purpose.**

- **The Man.** Vrassik’s confidence surge, “The Man” among his own people, the thing that drives his entire subsequent rise. Lissrak’s reaction is the quiet centerpiece, whether his win costs her something specific or confirms what she already knew, author’s call.

- **The Exam, Day One.** Year 4’s assessment, hardest yet.

- **The Exam, Day Two.**

- **The Exam, Final.** Placements land.

- **What It Cost.** Aftermath, ranks changed, one name absent from the results entirely.

- **Kinship.** December, the team’s first holiday season with a hole in it.

- **The Joke, Again.** Second, smaller instance of the reversed ritual, establishing it as a pattern rather than a one-time event. Escalation begins here, gradual, never a switch flipping.

- **Loose Threads.** **Now Book 4’s second stay-weekend chapter, new, no propaganda-fragment opener** (Qiraki_Process_Notes.md’s weekend-chapter exception). Not a hangout, a grief chapter, unstructured, off the clock, nothing scheduled it, the stronger version of “deliberately incomplete, the year not tied off” rather than a contradiction of it. **Content:** the team together, doing something ordinary, Soren present in the room without anyone narrating a lesson about him, the grief itself doesn’t resolve, what changes is that it’s not being carried solo anymore. **Sits deliberately next to Kinship (Ch.44):** Kinship is the institution’s version of togetherness, a scheduled tradition that now hurts. This is the version nobody scheduled. Putting both in the back half of the book is doing real work, not repeating itself. *Psych: presence, not conversation, is what grief actually responds to at this age, consistent with Rule 4, nobody narrates what the togetherness means.*

- **Bridge.** Closes Year 4 without resolution, opens into Book 5’s institutional anger.
