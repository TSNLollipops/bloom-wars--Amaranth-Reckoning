# ENLIGHTENED: THE ACADEMY ERA, BOOK 1, "THE KING AND THE CLIMB"

## Chapter Four: First Contact

*The Spawn Line exists so that losing here costs you a weapon cooldown, not your life. Remember which one it actually is.*

*Arena safety briefing, cadet-issue*

The yard is already loud with boots on stone when Denic and I come out into it, a few hundred of us at least, all headed the same direction, breath showing in the cold. Fourteen hundred meters, the map said last night. It feels like more than that this morning.

Ilyen's ahead of us already, close to the wall the way she walks everywhere, band on her wrist the normal way, not one thing about her out of place. She doesn't look back.

Denic's collar is turned under itself again, same side it always turns under, and next to her it looks like even more of a mess than it is. I reach over and fix it without asking.

"You'd think after four days you'd learn which side," I say.

"Some people are gifted." He tips his head so I can reach the other side too, not even breaking stride. "Some people have friends for it."

I snort, and that's the whole answer he's getting.

He hasn't noticed the cold, or he's pretending not to, rubbing his hands together more for the sound of it than the warmth.

"You slept," he says. Not a question.

"You didn't?"

"Some. Enough." He grins at me sideways, quick, the grin arriving a half second before whatever he's about to say does. "I timed the band again last night. Works clear to the mess hall now."

"You should sleep."

"I'll sleep after."

The building at the far edge of the yard is low and wide and windowless on this side, and it gets bigger the whole walk instead of arriving all at once. The doors stand open already onto something loud, metal against metal, boots on a hard floor, a few hundred voices layered over each other and none of them clear. A kid ahead of us in the doorway stops dead in it, and the whole line behind him folds sideways to get around, nobody unkind about it, just moving, the way traffic moves around a stalled cart without anyone needing to say anything about it.

Inside, the armory runs the length of the building in racks, and none of it is anything I've seen before. No G-tier casing, no charge lights, no readout strip along a grip telling you what's left in it. Wood and blued steel laid out in long rows under cold light, rifles with worn stocks, revolvers with cylinders that click when you spin them, knives with actual edges instead of a power cell behind the point.

Somebody two racks over doesn't even get both hands around whatever he's reaching for before a flat voice comes from somewhere overhead telling him that one's not his to take yet. He puts it back fast, like it might still be listening, and the rest of us take the hint, standing in front of thousands of weapons the way you stand in front of something you haven't been told you're allowed to want yet. The whole room smells like oil and cold metal and something under that I don't have a name for yet.

Vantree's up on a platform at the front of the room, and her voice isn't raised, but the room drops under it anyway, the way a room does when the person talking isn't planning to repeat herself.

"Pick your weapon and face it."

She's got a knife in one hand already and she shows the line of us the strike without slowing down to make a lesson of it, under the arm, quick, no ceremony to it, and puts it away again before half the room's finished watching. Her hands don't hesitate anywhere in it.

Somebody near the front, a voice I don't know, says, "Face what?"

"You will fight each other."

Nobody answers that one. The room's quiet enough now that I can hear a boot shift on the floor somewhere behind me, and somewhere further back a kid laughing at something that isn't funny, the wrong kind of laugh.

"Four hours," Vantree says. "As many rounds as you can stand. Nobody is grading you on how it feels."

The room breaks all at once, every rack getting hit by a dozen hands at the same time.

Denic's already down the line ahead of me, fast, reading everything with his hands instead of his eyes, palm flat along a stock, thumb testing a hammer. He picks up a revolver, puts it back, picks up a second one, keeps that one, and then goes back for a knife besides, testing the edge of it against his thumbnail the way I've seen Yssa test one at home, which means somebody taught him that and it wasn't the academy.

I go slower. A rifle near the end of the rack has a strap worn soft and a bolt that racks clean under my hand, no catch in it, no grit, and that decides it before I've thought about deciding anything. I take a knife as well, off a bin near the wall, because everyone around me seems to be taking two of something and one of them not being a knife feels like the wrong kind of unusual.

Ilyen passes behind me on her way down the same rack, doesn't say anything, reaches over and resets the strap on my rifle sling where it's already twisted against my shoulder, then keeps moving before I've finished registering that she did it.

"She does that," Denic says, like it explains something. "Doesn't she."

"Does what."

"Nothing." He's grinning again. "Never mind."

The floor under the rows of us goes white at the edges and then isn't the floor anymore.

I'm standing in a street.

Cobblestone under my boots instead of the armory floor, a church spire somewhere off to my left going up higher than anything else around it, shuttered windows three and four stories up on both sides, and it's cold here too, the same cold, like the whole session brought the weather with it. No countdown. No pairing called. One second the armory, the next this, rifle already in my hands and my hands already checking it without asking me first. Somewhere off past the spire, gunfire, more than one gun, more than one direction, the whole city already awake with it before I've taken a single step.

Something moves at the end of the street, past a stalled cart with one wheel gone, and I'm behind the cart's frame before I've decided to move, rifle up, breathing slower than I expect.

Nothing. A shutter banging loose in the wind, same rhythm every time, and under it the city doing what a city with a thousand cadets in it does, gunfire two streets over, something heavier further off that might be a grenade or might just be a door giving up, a scream that cuts off clean partway through and doesn't come back. None of it has anything to do with me.

I go the other way instead, into a narrower street off a square with a dry fountain at the center of it, close to the buildings on the side still in shadow. The rifle's good for this, long enough to reach and steady if I brace it against a doorframe, and the first shot I take is on a shape crossing the far side of the square, Osnian-built even at that distance, wide through the shoulders in a way nothing human reads as, and it goes wide anyway, takes half a shutter off its hinge instead of what it was aimed at.

They don't see where it came from. I do it again before they've worked it out, and this one lands, the size of what's on the other end of it not mattering even a little, and something in my chest unlocks half a degree.

I try it three times more from two different windows before I understand it isn't the window that made it work the second time. It's the wait. Standing still long enough for someone else to move first is the actual trick, not the height, and once I've got that, waiting starts costing me less than it did the first time I tried it.

The bolt sticks the sixth time I try to work it. Not broken. Empty, the small dead click of a chamber with nothing left to feed it, and there's no second magazine coming from anywhere. I drop it against a wall and I've got the knife off my belt before I've finished deciding the rifle's dead weight now.

The knife is worse at range and better at everything else. I stop trying to see how far I can reach and start trying to see how close I can get without being seen first, doorway to doorway, a beat longer than feels comfortable before crossing anything open. It's slower. The next time I check my hands between fights, they've stopped shaking.

The alley behind the church runs long and narrow, a heap of fallen roof tile near the far end of it and someone crouched low behind it, back turned, watching the square instead of the alley. I take the doorway two steps in instead of crossing open ground, close enough that the wall does half the work of hiding me.

I close the rest of the distance before they hear me, and the knife goes in under the arm, exactly the way Vantree showed the line of us, white at the edges and gone before the sound of it can catch up.

Footsteps behind me anyway, coming up the alley from the doorway I just left, close enough by the time I register them that I'm turning with the knife still wet when the second one's already on me, a bayonet coming for my ribs.

We go down together against the alley wall. I lose the knife somewhere in it and end up with a fistful of collar, my forehead doing more of the work than my hands for a second, both of us scrabbling for ground on stone that's slick with something neither of us wants to think about right now. It isn't clean. None of it's clean. It's mostly weight and the wall and getting an elbow free before they do, one bad second where I'm sure the bayonet's found something and it's only the wall behind me it's found. Then the white catches them too, and I'm the one still standing, breathing harder than the fight itself should account for.

Both of their weapons are left behind on the ground, a rifle and the bayonet that almost found me. The rifle's still got a few rounds in it. I take that and the bayonet both, back to two again the way I started, something for range and something for close.

I don't hear the shot that gets me twenty minutes later. I feel it first, high on my side, hard enough to spin me half around before my legs go out from under me, and it hurts, actually hurts, a white streak of it climbing up through my ribs that has no business feeling that real for something that isn't. I'm down on the cobblestones with my hand already pressed to the spot before I've decided to move it there, and the pain's still climbing, not fading, when the world goes white at the edges instead, all at once, no floor, no weight, no cold, half a second of nothing that isn't sleep and isn't waking either, just absence with a shape to it.

Then the Spawn Line has me again, standing in it whole, hands empty, the square gone like it was never there, my rifle gone with it, wherever it actually is right now. The armory floor is under my boots and around me a dozen other cadets are arriving the same way, some of them already turning to go back in before they've finished landing.

Nobody explains it. My hands already know what just happened before the rest of me does, patting down a side that isn't hurt anymore, a chest, a leg that's fine, checking anyway, because thirty seconds ago it wasn't and some part of me still needs the hand there to believe it. I stay there a few seconds longer than the cadets landing around me before I pull a new rifle off the nearest rack, not the same one, a stranger's weight and a stranger's balance.

I go back in, and round a corner straight into someone who wasn't expecting me any more than I was expecting them, both of us half a second from a decision neither of us has made yet. They make theirs first, a knife into their own leg before I've even got anything leveled at them, gone white at the edges before I've finished registering what I just watched.

Their rifle's still on the ground where they dropped it. Empty.

This time I've got company inside of ten minutes, Corw coming around the corner of a bakery with a revolver in each hand and blood on his sleeve that isn't his.

"You're slow," he says, which is how he says hello.

"You're loud."

"I'm efficient." He tips his chin at the street behind him. "Three of them past the bakery, working together. Not friendly."

"How many left in those."

He checks without looking like he's checking. "Enough."

We don't discuss it past that. He takes the left side of the street and I take the right, and it works the way it's supposed to work when two people who don't know each other well enough to argue just fall into the same rhythm without deciding to, cover and move, cover and move, him taking the one that breaks for the open ground and me taking the two that don't, and by the far end of the street there's only one of us with anything left to fire and it isn't a conversation either of us needs to have out loud.

"Told you I was efficient," he says, already crouched over what's left on the ground where the third one stood, pocketing a handful of loose rounds that happen to fit his cylinder, lucky as much as anything.

"You told me that before we knew if it was true."

"I know things before they're true. That's the whole point of being good at this."

He splits off at the next corner without a word, already moving toward something I can't see yet. It doesn't feel like anything ending. It feels like it was always going to be exactly that long.

The next stretch is quieter, a long block of shuttered fronts with nothing moving in any of them, and my legs start telling me something my head hasn't gotten around to yet, that this is closer to hour three than hour one, that the rifle's heavier on my shoulder than it was at the start even though nothing about the rifle's changed.

I misjudge a corner because of it, come around it a half beat too fast, and there's someone already there, close enough that neither of us gets a clean first move. It turns into the kind of fight that isn't really a fight, both of us too tangled up to do anything but shove and grab, and this time I lose the grip first, a blade finding something under my arm that lights up white and wrong a half second before the world does what it did in the square, all at once, gone.

I'm back at the armory rack inside of a breath, whole again, and this one barely has time to register before it's already over, which is its own kind of strange, the hurt gone faster than it arrived. Nobody's grading me on how it feels.

I sit down against the rack for four seconds, longer than I've stopped anywhere all morning, before I make myself stand back up, grab whatever's closest without really looking at it, and go back in.

Denic finds me instead of the other way around, ten minutes later, coming out of a doorway I'd already half decided was empty.

"There's four of them setting up in the square," he says, fast, already checking the angle past my shoulder. "Rooftop, two windows, one on the ground watching the fountain. You were about to walk straight into it."

I hadn't seen any of it. I'd read the fountain as cover and everything past it as nothing, structure first, the way I always do, and it was wrong.

"How do you know it's four."

"I counted."

"You counted from where."

"Does it matter where."

He pulls me back off the corner by the strap of my rifle a half second before something comes through the space I'd been standing in, close enough that I feel the air of it going past, a flat crack off the stone behind me a half beat after it should've already hit something.

"That one," Denic says, breathing hard now, grinning anyway, "was for free. You're welcome."

"You're bleeding."

"I'm always bleeding a little. It's fine." He isn't, really, just a graze along the forearm he hasn't noticed yet.

We take the square from the side instead, the two of us, and it costs both of us something, an ugly ninety seconds where the plan stops being a plan and turns into staying alive next to each other. He takes the ground-level one before I've finished tracking where it went. I take the rooftop shape he flags with two fingers instead of a word, one shot, no second chance needed. The last one comes at both of us at once and it's Denic who closes the distance on it, fast, reckless in a way that would look like carelessness on anyone whose hands weren't doing exactly what they meant to do, and it works, mostly because Denic doesn't hesitate on any of it the way he hesitates on everything back in the classroom.

We're moving away from the fountain afterward, into the street on the far side, still breathing hard, Denic already halfway into a sentence about the rooftop one, when the grenade lands.

I don't see who threw it. Denic's the one who sees it, still turning toward me when it goes.

The Spawn Line takes him before the sound of it finishes.

I'm alone in the street with my ears ringing and a rifle I don't remember raising. Nothing where he was standing except the street being the street again, cobblestone and cold and a smear of smoke already thinning in the wind. I know what that white edge means now, I watched it happen to me an hour ago, and knowing it doesn't change what the next thirty seconds do.

I check the spot where he was standing. I check it twice. There's nothing to check, no mark on the stone, no reason it should look any different than it did thirty seconds ago, and it doesn't stop me looking a third time.

Whoever threw it is still close, has to be, a charge that size doesn't throw far, and I find them before I've finished deciding to look, a shape backed into a doorway two down that hasn't moved fast enough to be anywhere else. I don't wait to see if they clock me coming. The white catches them too, same as it caught Denic, same as it caught me an hour ago, and this time I'm the one who put it there.

Denic's fine. Back at the Spawn Line whole, same as I was an hour ago, and some part of my hands already knows that even if the rest of me hasn't caught up to it yet. I don't get to know it for certain until tonight. Right now all I've got is the street and the ringing and my own hands running an equipment check they don't need, patting the same strap twice, getting the buckle wrong the first try, which it hasn't done all morning.

I hear the three of them before I see them, a knot of cadets coming up the mouth of the street, arguing cheerfully about something that happened a block over, and I'm in the nearest doorway before they're close enough to notice, letting them pass without either side knowing the other one was there. The city doesn't stop being loud around one quiet street. It just isn't loud in this particular one for a minute. I get the buckle right once they're past and step back out, because there isn't a version of staying in that doorway longer that changes anything about the street, and because my legs already know where they're going before I've decided where that is.

I go looking for Ilyen after that, not on purpose exactly, more the way you end up walking toward a specific door in a building without deciding to. Once I notice, I don't stop anyway.

I check the streets nearest the fountain first, then work outward in a pattern that isn't really a pattern, just wherever looks like somewhere she'd choose, close walls, tight corners, nothing open enough to be caught in. A kilometer of city and most of a thousand cadets in it makes not finding her the likely outcome, not the surprising one, and it doesn't stop me checking a fourth street and a fifth. Other fights cross my path on the way, neither of them mine, three cadets breaking off a chase down a side street, a shape sprinting flat out across a rooftop two buildings over with someone else's shadow closing on it, and I let both of them keep happening without me.

Twice I catch a shape at the edge of a street that moves the way she moves, quick and close to the walls instead of the open ground, weight forward the same way, and both times it's someone else's cadet number by the time I'm close enough to tell, a stranger wearing the same caution she'd wear, and both times something in me has to reset before I can move on to whatever's actually in front of me.

I check a sixth street after the fifth turns up empty, and a seventh window in a row of them that all look the same once you've looked at enough of them, and somewhere around the eighth I stop counting the streets and just keep walking them, close to the walls the way she'd walk them, reading doorways the way she'd read them, until checking the next one is just what my legs are doing and not a decision I'm making anymore.

Two more cross my path on the way. One's an ambush that goes exactly to plan for once, clean and quiet and over before they know the street has anyone else in it, my hands finishing it before my head's decided to let them. The other doesn't go to plan at all, a scramble across a collapsed section of wall that ends with a boot finding my throat before I can get free of it, and the world goes white at the edges a third time today, quick now, almost routine, gone and back before I've finished being annoyed about it. Three. New rifle off the nearest rack, same as the last two, and I keep moving.

The announcement comes the way the ceiling voice comes in the dormitory, from nowhere in particular, everywhere at once. Four hours, called. The city doesn't disappear this time, just goes quiet around us, the rifle gone out of my hands in the same half second the voice finishes, no weight there anymore, nothing to drop or set down, just gone the same way it arrived. Cadets start walking out of doorways and alleys in every direction, all of them headed the same way now instead of scattered, hands empty the whole way, a thousand separate mornings turning back into one crowd.

Vantree's at the gate watching us come through, checking each of us for something I can't name, and she looks at my hands for a second longer than she looks at anyone else's, both of them steadier now than they were at the start of the morning, and doesn't say anything about it. She doesn't have to.

Denic's waiting there too, hands already going, talking before I'm even close enough to hear the words.

"You should've seen your face," he says. "When it went off. I didn't see it, obviously, I was already gone, but Toma said you should've seen your face."

"Toma wasn't there."

"Toma heard about it. Word travels." He falls in next to me, matching my pace without seeming to try, the graze on his forearm cleaned up now and already half forgotten. "It doesn't hurt, by the way. Getting pulled. I thought it would and it doesn't, it's just gone and then you're back, like skipping a page."

"It hurt for me."

He looks at me sideways for a second, like he's checking whether I'm joking, decides I'm not, and doesn't have anything to say to that. Neither do I. We just keep walking.

"I got the one who threw it," I say.

That actually stops him for half a step. "You what?"

"Two doors down from where you were standing. Didn't even see me coming."

"You avenged me."

"I avenged your ass. You should've seen your face." He grins, easier this time than it did in the square, nothing behind it working to get there first. "I saved your life, by the way. Formally. For the record."

"You said that already."

"I'll keep saying it. Every day, probably, for the rest of the year."

Corw catches up to us at the gate, both hands still curled loose around a shape that isn't there anymore, an old habit that hasn't caught up to the fact yet.

"You two survive," he says. Not really a question either.

"Some of it," Denic says. "Trav owes me his life. Formally."

"He's told me four times," I say.

"I'll tell him a fifth if it helps it sink in."

Corw looks at me like he's deciding whether that's worth commenting on, decides it isn't, and keeps talking instead. "Ran into this one an hour before I found you," he says, tipping his chin at Denic. "Boarded-up shop on the east side, four of them dug in behind the counter. He went through the front like the door owed him money, I came in through the back and caught the two that broke for it."

"There was no door left to go through," Denic says. "It was already open."

"It was closed enough to make a sound when you hit it. Two each either way, and we're both still walking."

He moves off into the crowd still talking, to somebody else now, one hand cutting through the air to mark out where the four of them had been standing when he and Denic took them. He doesn't look back to see if we're still listening. He doesn't need to.

"It'll be six of them by dinner," Denic says, low, not quite to me.

"For sure."

My own hands are still curled the same way, around the same nothing, and it takes another few steps before they figure that out and let go of it.

Denic's still talking on the walk back to the dormitory, the story getting better with every telling of it, and somewhere in the third version the grenade turns into something he saw coming a full second early instead of something that just happened to him. I let him have it. It's a better story that way, and some of what he's saying actually happened.

Saturday comes in warm instead of cold, a fine summer mist that isn't quite rain and isn't quite not, beading on sleeves and eyelashes like a shower that can't commit to falling. Ground temperature's supposed to be controlled out here same as everywhere else on the grounds, which makes warm mist out of nowhere its own small mystery nobody's bothering to explain, and the yard's quieter for it anyway, less talk, more boots. Denic's not talking either, which is its own kind of loud.

"You good," I say.

"Fine." He shakes the mist off like it's personally offended him, grimacing at the wet more than the cold. "I hate this. Whatever this is."

The armory's not the armory anymore. Same building, same racks, but everything on them's changed overnight, wood and blued steel swapped out for something older, swords with real edges instead of stage dullness, axes, maces, longbows strung and waiting along the back wall with their quivers counted out beside them. No armor anywhere in it, same as yesterday, just weapons.

Vantree doesn't repeat herself here either. "Same rules. Different toys." That's the whole briefing this time.

The room breaks the same way it broke yesterday, every rack getting hit at once. A crossbow near the end catches my eye, heavier than it looks, and I put it back for a straight bow instead, something I can nock faster even if it hits softer.

Denic goes for two swords, one in each hand, testing the balance of the second one before he's decided he needs it at all.

"Whole different century and you still can't pick one weapon," I say.

"Some people like to commit. Not me."

The floor goes white before either of us finishes deciding anything else.

Stone under my boots this time instead of cobblestone road, a walled town climbing a hill instead of spreading flat, streets that switch back on themselves like they were built by someone who wanted an enemy to get lost trying to reach the top. Banners hang dead-still off a few of the higher windows, no wind up here to move them, and somewhere above the rooftops a bell that isn't ringing sits waiting to. Higher still, near the crown of the hill, something's happening at a scale I can't make sense of from down here, a mass of shapes moving against each other around a knot of banners, too far off for any single sound to carry clean, just a low continuous something underneath everything else, metal maybe, or just a great many people breathing hard at once. I like the town before I've decided to, in the part of me that doesn't ask why.

The bow's better than I expect and worse than I hoped, good in the open stretches between buildings, useless the second the streets narrow, and they narrow constantly. I put two shafts into a shape crossing a rooftop before the quiver's down to nothing, faster than the rifle ever ran dry, and there's no reloading a bow with an empty quiver, just a stick with a string on it until I find more arrows or accept it's dead weight.

I don't find more arrows. I switch to the knife I picked up alongside the bow, close work again, doorway to doorway, the same discipline as yesterday wearing a different set of tools. It's quieter than yesterday in a way that doesn't feel safer, no gunfire cracking off stone to tell me where anyone is, just footsteps and breathing and steel finding steel close enough to hear both at once.

The alley here runs uphill instead of flat, which should tip everything in my favor, except the Hiopi coming around the bend below me doesn't seem to need the terrain's help, four legs closing the gap in about half the strides it'd take me to cover the same ground, and gravity ends up mattering a lot less than I was counting on. The spear's built for open ground, reach for reach across a field, but even a street this narrow still gives it more reach than I've got, and I never close the distance far enough to make that matter. The point finds my side before my blade finds anything, and the world goes white before I've finished falling.

Four. New sword off the nearest rack, and back into the city.

Denic catches up with me somewhere past what used to be a market square, sword dulled along one edge in a way that says he's been using it wrong all morning.

"You're going to break that."

"Already broken. Broke it 45 minutes ago. This is just what broken looks like now." He grins, the easy kind, no half-second head start needed to get there. "Also I found a dog."

"You found a dog?"

"It ran off. Didn't want to fight. Smartest thing in this whole city." He falls in step for the length of one street, no further plan to it than that. We clear a doorway together on the way past, quick, wordless, neither of us needing to call it first, and split off again without either of us making it a goodbye.

Corw finds me midway with a mace in one hand and a story already forming behind his eyes.

"Thought you said you were efficient," I say.

"I am efficient. Sword broke two fights back, so I took this off someone who wasn't using it anymore." He rolls the shoulder that used to carry the shield. "The shield's a different story. An Osnian happened to it. Two more that way, holding a chokepoint, one Osnian-built too. Not moving."

"So move them."

"Tried. That's the story I just told you."

We take it together, and the Osnian alone is worth both of us put next to each other, a grip on my sword arm that has the whole limb useless before I've finished registering it's closed, more strength in one hand than I've got in both of mine, and it's only Corw's mace finding a gap in the guard that gets the hand open again. It rounds on him for that, fast for something that size, already swinging before Corw can get the mace back up, and it's my arm that's free now, sword already moving, faster than deciding to move it. The Osnian goes down before the swing finishes. It takes less time than yesterday's version of the same problem after that, both of us reading the other's next move a half beat faster than we did on Friday, which isn't friendship exactly, just repetition doing what repetition does. He's gone again before either of us calls it anything.

I catch a glimpse of Ilyen once, near the base of the hill, gone again before I've finished deciding it was her and not just someone built the same way, and I don't chase it this time. There's only so many hours in a session and I've learned that lesson once already.

Something puts an arrow through my shoulder from a window I never clock, high enough that it just knocks me flat instead of doing what it's built to do, the world going white at the edges before the pain's even finished arriving. Five, if the count still matters by Saturday. New sword off the nearest rack, nothing personal about which one, and back in.

The four hours end the same way they ended yesterday, all at once, everywhere, the sword gone out of my hand in the same instant the announcement lands, cadets pouring back down the hill toward the gate in one long unraveling line, hands empty the whole way down.

Denic's already rewriting his own day out loud on the walk back, the sword he lost somewhere becoming a sword he gave away on purpose by the time we're through the dormitory door, traded off to some kid who needed it more, a whole small act of charity that didn't happen five minutes ago and does now.

I let him have that one too. It's not really about the sword.
