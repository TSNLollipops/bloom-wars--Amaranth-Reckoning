## Vague-truth placeholder trap, standing craft flag

Caught once already (Coherence of Process's second touchpoint, Character Sheets), worth watching for generally, not just there. "She says something true," "he says the right thing," any narrated summary standing in for actual dialogue content, reads fine in a planning doc and becomes an AI-tell the moment it survives unchanged into real drafting, a reader gets told something meaningful happened rather than experiencing it happen. Fine to use as shorthand while planning, since not every beat needs its exact line written a year in advance, but flag it explicitly as a placeholder rather than letting it read as decided content, and always replace it with something specific and concrete before the scene is actually drafted.

## Working relationship, how judgment gets split

Author explicitly wants me acting as a real creative partner here, not just a note-taker executing dictation. Stated division of labor, author's own words: they're strongest on overarching plot and tend to forget the small human details that make a character real day to day. That's my lane to make real judgment calls on without asking permission first, small concrete objects, specific gestures, the texture-level stuff, not just filling in blanks but actually deciding. Overarching plot, character fates, and anything structurally load-bearing stays theirs to call, flag those clearly rather than deciding quietly.

First real example: the socket Yssa gives Trav in Chapter 1 was my invention, not pre-specified, made as a genuine creative choice rather than a placeholder, per this exact permission. Precedent for how to handle "plant a callback seed, detail open at draft time" instructions going forward, decide something specific and real rather than leaving it vague, flag it afterward so it can be swapped if it's wrong.

## Voice and style

- Anthropological-lite, same discipline as The Ninth, but harder to hold here because there's far more to explain: rune-patterning, AI tutors, mech systems, four species, a caste-by-planet political economy. The Ninth's restraint works partly because there's little the reader needs explained. This project will constantly tempt exposition because the systems are inherently interesting. Working rule: show what a system costs or does through action, never through a character explaining how it works. A reader should understand a sync ratio (or whatever it ends up being called, see Technobabble_Glossary) by watching Reqa swear at one, not from a paragraph defining the term.

- Minimize "thing," "stuff," "true." Author-flagged as a personal filler-word weakness, not a style tic worth preserving the way The Ninth's "way X" constructions sometimes are. Just cut these when they appear.

- Hard rule: every character acts on genuine emotion in every action, reaction, and interaction. Build real personalities first, put them in the situation, and let what actually comes out of that be what's written, rather than what the plot needs from them.

## Structure

- One book per school/story year. ~130,000 words per book, ~2,500 word average chapters. Posted weekly, deliberately slower than The Ninth's cadence, chosen for sustainability given the author's real-life time constraints (school and work), not as a fallback.

- No single end-of-year crisis plot per book, deliberately avoiding a Chamber-of-Secrets structure. Each year instead ends on a genuine survival-of-the-fittest exam with real consequences (pairing reshuffles, rank changes), which does the same "school year has a shape" work without needing an external villain-of-the-year.

- Mission-chapter rhythm: minimum 3 mission chapters per major life event or arc beat. After roughly 6-7 running mission chapters, a 3-4 chapter cooldown block. Expect this ratio to drift as the actual material demands it, not a rigid formula.

## World-logic decisions worth remembering

- The Coalition's actual strategic problem is total defensible ground, not manpower. The plague can spawn on any body large enough, including small, non-life-bearing planets (Pluto-scale), and those vastly outnumber life-bearing worlds even accounting for terraforming. This should stay visible in how missions and campaigns get framed, it's a defense-in-depth problem, not a headcount problem.

- Humanity's initial response to the plague was slow and dismissive, "just a curiosity" before it spawned and spread. Escalation should read as compounding institutional failure to take a threat seriously, not instant war footing. A galaxy is large enough that full consumption doesn't happen in 60-odd years even once taken seriously.

- Points-for-gear economy: gear should expand what's possible, skill should still determine whether a pilot reaches that possibility. Avoid writing gear as a substitute for skill, or MC's talent stops mattering next to whoever has better requisition access.

## Quiet structural critique, built by design, protect it

Trav's backstory (father died in the war, mother retired from war- mechanic work to civilian maintenance, household means measurably declined) was locked specifically to build unstated outrage against the Cradle Circle faction. The mechanism: the war killed his father, that death is what pushed the family into the exact subsidized/working-class population the pipeline draws from, meaning the war is quietly refilling its own manpower by funneling families it's already devastated back into producing more soldiers. A closed loop, ugly on its own terms, no commentary needed.

**Discipline: this must never be stated outright by any character or by narration.** Not Trav, not a sympathetic instructor, not anyone. The moment someone says "the system profits from grief" out loud, it becomes thesis and stops being indictment, same rule The Ninth runs on for its own unstated mechanisms (the Ch.3 heritage detail, the ford/bridge echo). Watch Instructor Halvorsen and Director Osei specifically, sympathetic authority figures are the likeliest characters to be tempted into saying the quiet part out loud on Trav's behalf. Let the fact sit there for an attentive reader to assemble, nothing more.

**Parental ignorance, LOCKED, confirmed explicitly rather than left ambiguous:** most parents genuinely don't know what weekly combat class actually involves, full-contact, wound-emulation active, from Year 1 (see Qiraki_Combat_Curriculum_Reference.md). The alternative, a good portion of parents having lived the system themselves and sending their kids anyway, was raised and deliberately declined, since it would soften the critique into a community-wide open secret everyone's quietly accepted rather than a lie the Cradle Circle tells to people who can't check it. Keep the Year 1-2 exams (King of the Hill, Capture the Flag) doing the work of the thing parents and families actually see, genuinely gentle, non-mech, the version the institution shows on purpose. Nobody outside the institution is watching Tuesday, and that's a feature of the propaganda, not an oversight in it.

## Threads to keep consistent across a long project

- **Reqa's name spelling, precise execution rule, see full detail in Qiraki_Character_Sheets.md:** the incorrect spelling "Reaka" is used in prose up through the scene where she corrects Trav for the last time, that scene itself uses "Reaka" once more, then every appearance after is "Reqa" permanently. Not a note-only fact, an actual spelling choice that has to be tracked chapter to chapter.

- MC (Trav) falls for his mech before he falls for Reqa. Romance with Reqa doesn't begin until he's in his main combat unit, well past the academy. She isn't interested early either, it's genuinely all about the mech for her at that stage.

- His bonded AI is also a legitimate love interest, developing later, tied to "what happens when a person lives long enough inside a body that answers them back." Keep this earnest rather than played for novelty.

- Command-arc failures need real cost attached (see Qiraki_Concept.md's RPD research section), not just private self-doubt. A wrong call should have a name attached to what it cost.

- Aulus-style discipline doesn't directly apply here (different project, different flaw), but the underlying lesson does: give any character who's "the reliable one" (Reqa, the Carabil CO) real friction and at least occasional wrongness, not just narrative correctness.

- **Gear reflects character, never the reverse.** The points-shop tiers (Qiraki_Points_Shop_Catalog.md) are a menu, not a rail. Trav's actual weapon path (lance, twin lance, swords, eventual laser-blades) has to read on the page as him discovering what he trusts in a fight, an edge over an impact weapon, movement over raw durability, and the gear purchase follows that discovery rather than causing it. Any scene where a cadet levels up should show the in-fiction reason first, the stat change second, never the other way around.
