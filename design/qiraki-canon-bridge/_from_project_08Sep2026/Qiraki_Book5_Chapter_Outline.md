# THE QIRAKI FILES — Book 5, "The Weight of It," Chapter-by-Chapter Outline

*Part of the Qiraki files. Same template and standing rules as Book 1's outline (instructor ratio, daily-rhythm classification, adolescent-psychology standard), which govern here identically and are not restated in full. Cadets are 16-17 this book.*

**STATUS, READ THIS FIRST. This file is new construction, not a recovered or previously approved document.** Books 1, 2, 3, 4 and 6 each had a chapter-level outline. Book 5 never did. Everything below is assembled from material that was genuinely already locked, but scattered across Qiraki_Book_Expansion_Plan.md (act-level allocation only), Qiraki_Character_Sheets.md (Ilyen's reposition, Kest's third appearance, Vrassik's Book 5 position), the three curriculum reference docs (Year 5 entries in all three), Qiraki_Propaganda_Bank.md (four Year 5 fragment banks plus Tier 3 month themes), and Qiraki_Points_Shop_Catalog.md. Where those docs stated something, it's marked LOCKED and carried verbatim in substance. Where they left a gap, it's marked **PROPOSED** and is a suggestion to accept, replace, or throw out, not a decision made on your behalf. There are five of those, all listed in the open items at the end.

**Locked spine:** combat weight increases hard, Cradle Circle interference turns visibly unjust, Ilyen repositions from Reeps to Munti.

**Curriculum, Year 5, and the three disciplines line up under the locked spine more tightly than any book except 6.** Rune-patterning reaches Circles, real closed feedback loops taught properly for the first time, genuine control theory, a construct that checks its own output and adjusts without being told to run again. Engineering reaches thermodynamics, real heat-engine and energy-conversion fundamentals, how power generation inside a mech produces heat as an unavoidable byproduct and how that heat has to go somewhere or the system fails. Combat class runs Flow Drills, reduced-cueing high-repetition sequences designed to push a skill out of conscious attention entirely, freeing bandwidth for tactical reading layered on top.

**The convergence, and it is the most useful thing this outline can hand the drafting.** "Combat weight increases" has always been the locked beat and has never had a mechanism under it. It has one now, and it's real engineering rather than assertion: heavier, more capable systems draw more power, more power produces more heat, and cooling stops being an afterthought and becomes a constraint a cadet has to design around. That is the actual physical reason this year is heavier, and it's teachable on the page. Meanwhile combat class is doing the exact opposite thing to their bodies, Fitts and Posner's autonomous stage arriving on schedule after four prior years of structured escalating practice, skills dropping out of conscious attention, Trav's locked "fluid and evasive" shift landing precisely where real motor-learning timelines predict it. **The machines get heavier in the same year the pilots get lighter.** Nobody on the page says that. It's structural, not thematic, and the book is better if it stays structural.

**Emotional target, LOCKED:** frown is the dominant register, real earned anger at institutional unfairness, held all year rather than resolved. Balanced by a genuine smile once Ilyen's reposition lands, framed the whole way through as a correction and never as a demotion. Needs an explicit comedy beat somewhere in the middle so the book doesn't run entirely on institutional grimness, the trope bank's "a monthly social event escalating past what anyone intended" is the intended draw and it's placed in Act 1 below.

**Content note, applies to Books 2-6 identically:** cadets are minors throughout the academy years. All relationship material stays friendship, rivalry, and found family. The Trav/Reqa romance is locked to begin years after graduation. **Ilyen's crush, LOCKED craft approach:** stays unnoticed by Trav, his non-reflective POV never editorializes about it, the reader sees it only through her own small, specific, factually-narrated actions. This book carries the **fourth of five tells**, see Ch. 44. Nothing romantic or physical on the page, both are minors throughout.

**Propaganda-fragment allocation, LOCKED.** Every chapter opens with one, drawn against the 176-fragment no-reuse structure. Book 5 uses **Tier 3 month-theme fragments (starkest, propaganda largely worn through, the Books 5-6 era)**, twelve available, one per month. **Year 5 curriculum banks, twelve fragments, unique to this book and never reused**, four banks of three: Bloom, rune-tech, engineering, combat. **Shared flexible pool, 63 fragments**, for the ~23 chapters not tied to a month or a year. Source-type variety is the only constraint still doing real work, never two chapters running the same kind of document back to back.

**One fragment carries this book's whole spine and it is already written.** The Year 5 rune-tech bank contains "The legacy track doesn't get this material until later, if at all. Draw your own conclusions," attributed as overheard, not official, circulated among scholarship-track cadets. That is the institutional-unfairness theme stated by the cadets to each other rather than by narration, in a document the institution never issued, in the exact year the rune-tech reference doc independently predicted it would surface. Place it deliberately, and place it early, Ch. 2 or Ch. 7. Nothing in the chapter that follows should acknowledge it.

**Craft discipline governing draft-time execution.** Style Guide Rule 11 (ordinary resistance under duress), Rule 12 (the tell not the feeling), Rule 10 (isolated single-line paragraphs are a hinge, not a habit), and Qiraki_Emotion_Craft_Reference.md's ban on verification words as a character's private verdict on their own thought. **Rule 4 matters more in this book than in any other**, the ban on paragraph-ending morals and stated thesis. A book whose entire spine is institutional injustice is the single most likely place in the series for a sympathetic character to say the quiet part out loud. Watch Halvorsen and Osei specifically, per the standing note. The moment anyone articulates the critique it becomes thesis and stops being indictment.

## Act 1 — The year gets heavier (ch. 1-12, January-March)

**Governing psychology:** mid-to-late adolescence, abstract reasoning genuinely online for the first time, which is exactly what makes this the year institutional unfairness becomes visible to them. Younger cadets experienced individual unfairness (Book 2's legacy gear, Book 3's bad path choice) as discrete bad events. Sixteen-year-olds can hold a pattern in mind and recognize a system, and that recognition is the actual engine of the year's anger. Real developmental fact, not narrative convenience.

- **Heavier.** Fifth January opens with the combat load visibly up, and this time the reason is mechanical rather than announced. Bigger draw, more heat, and gear that behaves differently late in a session than early in one. Discipline month's Tier 3 fragment ("nobody explains discipline to you by now, you already know, or you didn't make it this far") against a cohort that genuinely doesn't need it explained. *Psych: sixteen-year-olds notice load increases as an insult before they notice them as a curriculum.*

- **Circles.** Year 5 rune-patterning opens, closed self-referencing loops, real control theory, a construct that checks its own output and adjusts. Strong reader-facing curriculum content and the most conceptually satisfying material the cadets have met yet. **Placement note:** this is the natural home for the "legacy track doesn't get this material" fragment, since the chapter's own content is a cadet discovering how capable the material actually is.

- **Heat.** Year 5 engineering opens, thermodynamics, power in means heat out and there is no version of the system that avoids it. Cooling as a real design constraint rather than an afterthought. **This chapter is the mechanism behind Ch. 1** and should follow it closely enough that a reader connects heavier gear to hotter gear without either being told to.

- **Flow Drills.** New combat coursework, reduced cueing, high repetition, sequences drilled until they stop requiring conscious attention. Deliberately unglamorous on the page, this is a montage-shaped chapter that should refuse to be a montage. **Craft note:** the drill works on the whole cohort, not on Trav specifically. Autonomous-stage automaticity is a real, documented outcome of four years of structured practice, and if only the protagonist gets it the science quietly turns back into a talent. **Vantree beat.** Reduced cueing means stepping back and letting them fail unhelped, which is the opposite of everything protective in her. She does it anyway.

- **What They Don't Get.** First hard Cradle Circle beat of the year, resource allocation quietly favoring legacy-track cadets, escalating the seed planted in Book 2's Ch. 7 and paid off in Book 2's Ch. 34. Nobody says the word unfair. **Structural note:** this needs to be a *pattern* rather than an incident, because the whole developmental point of this year is that these kids can now see patterns. One event repeated across three cadets in one chapter does what three separate chapters would.

- **The Event That Got Away From Everyone.** **The book's locked comedy beat, placed here on purpose.** Trope draw: a monthly social event escalating well past what anyone intended. Ambition or Unity month's opening activity is the intended vehicle per the Expansion Plan, though a Discipline-month training-ramp tradition works equally well this early in the calendar. Trav's humor is well developed by sixteen and doing real social work by now, four books past the joke-ritual's origin and one book past its reversal. **Escalation beat, joke-ritual arc.** *Psych: this age group's capacity to turn a sanctioned activity into something else entirely is not rebellion, it's ordinary, and the institution's reaction is more interesting than the escalation.*

- **Study Session I.** Trav and Reqa. **Subject: feedback loops**, the Circles material read against what a thermostat or an autopilot actually does. Genuinely real control theory, no invention required. Reqa's engineering preference over rune-patterning is locked and this is a subject where her side and his side describe the same phenomenon, which is worth letting her enjoy openly.

- **Ilyen, Four Years In.** Her Reeps problem is no longer a struggle, it's a settled fact, and that's worse. Book 3 showed it starting, Book 4 showed it compounding, this shows it as a thing everyone including her has stopped expecting to change. **No softening.** *Psych: at sixteen, a four-year failure has stopped being a setback and started being an identity, which is precisely why the correction later in the book has to be shown as a correction rather than a rescue.*

- **The Pain, Still.** Symbiosis spike carries over from Books 3 and 4, unresolved and still unexamined. **Hard constraint, unchanged: never internally explained, only shown.** **Timing note, added from the points catalog:** Ironmind, the stress-tolerance and pain-management overhaul, is B-tier military-era gear and arrives long after these years rather than fixing them retroactively. The catalog flags keeping that timing honest. There is no version of Book 5 where this gets solved.

- **Vrassik, Elsewhere.** Vrassik rising through his own cohort's version of the year's harder stakes, mostly off Trav's page per his locked arc. Post-ritual confidence from Book 4 compounding. Jifsook, Lissrak, Bruvald, Krethis and Perrin all present. Good chapter for an Osnius fragment against Bruvald and for Lissrak's devotion to keep being legible only in retrospect.

- **Corw, Arriving.** Corw's Tank identity turning the corner it started turning in Book 3, the ego-test arc resolving into something useful. The path itself was always the test, a top-ranked cadet on the one path whose success metric is how well the team did rather than how well he did. Fenn's dry non-reaction still doing quiet work five books on.

- **The Choice.** Weekend-choice chapter. Home is now genuinely strange to kids this far in, and for several of them this is one of the last few. *Psych: at sixteen the loyalty conflict has stopped being painful and started being simply true, which reads sadder than the Book 1 version.* **Yssa beat.** First visit after Soren. He does not tell her. **Not concealment as a decision, concealment as the absence of one**, since he doesn't process backward and there is nothing in him that would produce the sentence. She notices something and doesn't get it out of him.

## Act 2 — What the body learned and what the institution didn't (ch. 13-23, April-July)

**Governing psychology:** the autonomous stage arriving is genuinely pleasurable, competence stops costing attention, and that pleasure sits directly against a year of institutional grinding. Both are real at once and neither resolves the other.

- **Fluid.** **Trav's locked "fluid and evasive" shift lands here.** Not a training breakthrough and not a montage payoff, the textbook description of a real athlete reaching the autonomous stage on exactly the timeline four years of structured escalating practice would predict. He does not notice it happening. Someone else does. **Craft note:** per his hard constraint he cannot register this internally, so the entire beat has to live in what his body does and in another character's reaction to it.

- **Cooling.** Engineering's thermodynamics coursework meets the heavier gear directly, a real design exercise where the heat has nowhere to go. **PROPOSED, fills an open slot:** Qiraki_Engineering_Curriculum_Reference.md carries an empty "Year 5 cooling-system named component" slot. Suggested fill: the **Bleedline**, a passive thermal-shunt assembly, the cheap standard-issue answer that works fine until the draw increases, which is exactly what happened in Ch. 1. Kid-slang naming per the standing convention. Author's call, easy to swap.

- **Study Session II.** Engineering-side. **Subject: thermal management**, why every watt you don't account for becomes a problem you meet later and usually at the worst time. Real, teachable, and directly load-bearing on the year's combat weight. **Heirloom project, fourth beat, and Year 5's curriculum is the wall she hits.** A weapon that creates and manipulates circles in real time draws continuously rather than in bursts, which under this year's thermodynamics coursework means it produces heat continuously and has nowhere to put it. **The problem is not that her design is wrong. The problem is that it works and cooks itself.** That's a genuinely harder and more interesting obstacle than a design flaw, and it comes free from curriculum that was already locked before this thread existed. She doesn't solve it here. *Craft: Trav is present, does not understand the scale of what she's attempting, and helps anyway by being someone to think out loud at. He never asks what it's for. She never says.*

- **The Rule, Enforced.** **Trope draw, LOCKED for this book by the Expansion Plan:** a rule everyone breaks that suddenly gets enforced on one specific cadet. Strong structural fit for exactly this year's kind of unfairness. **In-book resolution obligation, per the two-part trope discipline:** something has to actually happen on the page before this book ends, the cadet eats the consequence, or the unfairness gets exposed, or it doesn't and that itself is the resolution. A downstream echo is a separate, additional obligation and does not substitute. **PROPOSED resolution:** it doesn't get fixed, the cadet absorbs it, and the only thing that changes is what the cohort now knows about how enforcement actually works. That's the version that serves the book's register and the version that hurts.

- **The Legacy Track.** The resource gap made structural rather than anecdotal, and the rune-tech curriculum is the honest mechanism, per the reference doc's own Year 5 note that this is the year cadets start noticing how much more capable their material is than the legacy track's coursework often lets on. **Worth the inversion:** the scholarship track is getting the *better* education here. That's a genuinely more interesting and more damning shape than the legacy kids simply getting more.

- **Talia, Steady.** Talia's grind still working, and this is the year it starts visibly outpacing people who were ahead of her at thirteen. Her friction with Trav has been honest rather than petty for four books and should now be nearly spent, setting up her Book 6 decision to opt out of the transfer race knowing herself well enough to choose the path that suits her.

- **June.** Ensemble day, Curiosity month's Tier 3 fragment ("the questions that matter now don't have answers in any curriculum"). First Year 5 Bloom fragment lands, the Osnian oral-tradition line about it not hating us, hate meaning it noticed us at all, what it does being closer to how grass doesn't hate a field. **Placement note:** this is the single best Bloom fragment in the bank and it should land in the calmest chapter available, not the tensest.

- **Denic, Five Books In.** Denic's Munti work fully load-bearing, the loud kid nobody pegged for it now the reason the team functions. Placed deliberately ahead of Ilyen's reposition so the reader already understands what the Munti role actually is before she chooses it. **Craft note:** his tell is cockpit stillness, hands loud everywhere else and exact in there. Five books of that and no one has ever remarked on it, which is the point.

- **The Suggestion.** Somebody first raises the idea that Ilyen should reposition. **PROPOSED, and this is a real fork:** who says it matters enormously. Petra (her mek, the person she trusts with things she doesn't say elsewhere, seeded in Book 1) makes it an act of care. Her Protocol Guardian makes it institutional and cold and data-driven, which is consistent with the AI's locked dual report-and-help function. An instructor makes it authoritative and slightly humiliating. **Recommendation, not a decision:** Petra, because it protects the reposition from reading as the institution finally getting something right in a book whose entire spine is the institution getting things wrong.

- **Gratitude.** **Retitled, calendar defect I introduced and am correcting.** This was "Ambition," which put August's theme month inside Act 2 (April-July) and duplicated Ch. 24's "Ambition Month." July's actual locked theme is Gratitude. **And the Tier 3 fragment for it is the single best-placed line available anywhere in this book:** "Some of the ones you'd thank aren't here to hear it. Say it to the ones who are." That lands roughly one year after Soren died, in a book where the cohort's anger has nowhere to go, and it is not commented on by anyone. Ensemble chapter otherwise, rivalry energy carrying forward, Vrassik and Trav both climbing and still mostly apart this year.

- **Midpoint.** Act 2 closer, ensemble, every relationship gets a real beat. The team knows Ilyen is considering something and nobody has said the word out loud.

## Act 3 — Interference (ch. 24-36, August-October)

**Governing psychology:** the year's anger reaching the point where it has somewhere to go, and the specific adolescent experience of being certain you're right about an injustice and having no mechanism whatsoever to act on it.

- **Ambition Month.** August, sharpest rivalry energy of the year, and the ordinary year continuing at full intensity right alongside the institutional thread. Tier 3 fragment ("by now, ambition and survival stopped being different words").

- **Interference.** **The year's locked big beat, and it has to be undeniable rather than suspected.** Cradle Circle interference peaks, affecting more than just Ilyen, visible enough that the cohort can name the pattern even if they can't name a person responsible. Per the Expansion Plan this is the escalation of everything seeded since Book 2. *Psych: black-and-white fairness judgment has matured into pattern recognition by this age, and pattern recognition without recourse is the actual emotional content of the chapter.*

- **Kest, The Third Time.** **LOCKED, per Qiraki_Character_Sheets.md, and this outline is where it finally has a home.** Kest is at the academy for a genuine policy review of enrollment fairness across his region, the kind of visit that happens on its own institutional cycle regardless of who's enrolled. Ilyen's case is one example he addresses within that review. **Not personal targeting and not a dedicated trip.** He is warm, certain, sincere, and completely convinced, the same register as Books 1 and 4 and the same register he'll carry in Book 6. He addresses a real unfairness genuinely and the review changes nothing structural, and both of those are true at once without the chapter reconciling them. **Consistency requirement, carried from Book 4's Ch. 27 and setting up Book 6's Ch. 42:** Trav is present and does not register or retain who this is. Third non-registration, same consistent cognitive logic each time, not repetition, an administrator addressing someone else's problem is exactly the kind of thing his RPD-style attention does not snag on. **Stale-doc flag:** Qiraki_Book_Expansion_Plan.md still lists this as an open question ("whether Director Aurelian Kest appears on the page directly here"). The character sheet resolved it. The Expansion Plan hasn't been updated.

- **Study Session III.** Third session, unused subject. **PROPOSED subject:** the Structural Read revisited at Year 5 capability, specifically what changes when a schematic can be read by a construct that adjusts to what it finds rather than by a cadet reading a fixed output. Deliberately reaches back to Book 3's Study Session I so the reader can measure two years of growth against a subject they already met, which is a use the study-session device has never gotten yet.

- **Cross-Cohort.** Trav's team and Vrassik's in shared exercise. Krethis's six-year resentment still unresolved and still unexplained. Jifsook needling her brother. Lissrak steady. Hiopi cultural fragment as the opener rather than anything Coalition-issued.

- **What Precision Is For.** Ilyen's first real exposure to Munti work, and the moment the reader sees why it fits. Everything that made her the cohort's rune-patterning prodigy and a bad Reeps, precision, patience, the unprompted private log of every AI-tutor correction the dorm ever got, is the exact profile the role wants. **This is the chapter that has to earn the reposition**, not the reposition chapter itself.

- **Toma Ruiz, merged here, LOCKED placement, no new chapter, and revised from the earlier version.** He is the one who shows her the work, same cohort, same year, two years into the path she's entering with nothing, a peer rather than a mentor, which keeps the institution out of it. **He is good. That is the revision and it changes the register of the whole scene.** He is not a damaged kid clinging to a fixation, he is a genuinely excellent fifteen-year-old Munti, and the thing he is excellent at is extraction timing specifically. Not repair, not fabrication, not the parts that generate points. Cycle time, drilled past what anyone asked for, taught to her as if it were the only part of the job that counted.

- **The reason is never stated and no character supplies it.** He doesn't mention Soren. Ilyen wasn't on that team and has no reason to connect it. **Trav is POV, was in that arena, and says nothing**, which is exactly what his cognition does with everything. The reader has B4.CH25 and Ch. 30 and can assemble it alone.

- **Why the talented version is better than the washout version this replaces.** A kid who fails and then declines is the expected shape, and it makes his guilt legible *through* his decline, which is a soft way of telling the reader how to feel about him. A kid who fails once and answers it by becoming the best in the cohort at the exact thing that failed is stranger, colder, and refuses to resolve into either tragedy or redemption. It also makes Ilyen's inheritance craft rather than damage, and she is receiving the best instruction available on the subject rather than absorbing somebody's symptom.

- **Toma Ruiz, merged here, LOCKED placement, no new chapter.** He is the one who shows her the work. Same cohort, same year, two years into the path she's entering with nothing, which makes him a peer rather than a mentor and keeps the institution out of it. **The thread his sheet recommends, made concrete:** he is obsessive about extraction timing specifically. Not repair, not fabrication, not the parts of the role that generate points. Cycle time. He drills it past the point anyone asked him to and he teaches it to her that way, as if it were the only part of the job that counted. **Nobody explains why.** He doesn't bring up Soren. Ilyen wasn't on that team and has no particular reason to connect the two. **Trav is POV, was in that arena, and says nothing**, which is exactly what his locked cognition would do with it, no examination, no private naming, attention already moving to whatever is next.

- **Why this merge is better than a Toma chapter would have been.** A dedicated chapter would have to be *about* his guilt, which means either narrating it or having someone say it, and both break Rule 4. Folded into Ilyen's chapter, his guilt is never the subject, it's just the reason a competent fifteen-year-old teaches one specific skill harder than the syllabus asks for, and the reader assembles the rest from a Book 4 chapter they already have. **It also does real work for Ilyen:** the first thing she learns about the Munti role is that the part which matters most is the part nobody scores you on, which is a fair description of her entire six-year characterization and is never stated as one.

- **Unity.** September, cultural exchange, interspecies texture, runway toward Homecoming. Tier 3 fragment ("nobody needs a month to remember who's kept them alive").

- **The Cost of Switching.** **Added from the points catalog, and this is what keeps the reposition from being free.** Repositioning means starting the Munti equipment line near the bottom while her cohort is deep into their own paths. Quickfix kit, physical dock required for every repair, no remote function at all, after four years of accumulated Reeps investment. Real rank cost, real points cost, real visible regression in front of people who watched her struggle for four years already. **One line of Toma here, no more, and revised:** he's already carrying a Lifebox, the cockpit-evacuation tier, bought earlier than most of the cohort managed. Under the revision that reads as a good Munti prioritizing correctly rather than as penance, and it is deliberately impossible to tell which from the outside. She's four tiers below it and the gap is the actual measure of what she's taken on. Neither of them remarks on it. *Psych: at sixteen, voluntarily going backward in front of a peer group is close to the hardest available social act, and the fact that she does it is the actual characterization.*

- **Vigilance.** October, second and third Year 5 Bloom fragments land, including the access-logged internal briefing slide that incursion frequency has not decreased in sixty years and has never once decreased. The official framing sells that as proof the line works. It is also, unstated, proof the thing behind the line has never once needed to try harder. **Never resolve that in narration.**

- **Corw and the Team.** The unit absorbing a member changing roles mid-stream, real friction, real adjustment, not instant support. *Psych: rupture-and-repair, still how trust deepens, even at sixteen.* **Merged here, and Ch. 44's tell does not work without it:** Corw waits on a part. Not a crisis, not a grievance, just a Tank with the highest consumption on the team sitting idle for a day because the thing he needs wasn't stocked ahead. He grumbles, it gets sorted, the chapter moves on. **This is the control case.** Eleven chapters later, Trav's parts are simply always there, and the reader has this to measure that against. Without it the tell has no baseline and reads as generic competence. **Placement is deliberate**, early enough that nobody connects the two, late enough to still be in memory.

- **Vrassik, Sharper.** Rivalry intensifying into exam season. Fourth blood, and notably less damaging than Books 3 and 4, both of them past the associative stage and expressing skill as precision rather than as injury. **Sets up Book 6's Ch. 11 explicitly**, where the clean quality of their sparring is remarked on.

- **The Cohort Holds.** Ensemble chapter, every core relationship gets one more beat before Act 4. **Mek beat. This book: Petra Lindqvist.** She clocked Ilyen's feelings years ago and has never told anyone, out of plain loyalty rather than instruction. **In the book where Ilyen finally gets to be good at something in public, Petra is the only person who knows what that costs her and what it doesn't fix.** She says none of it.

- **Countdown.** Year 5's exam format and stakes laid out. Proportionate, a checkpoint rather than a spectacle, same principle as Book 3's.

## Act 4 — The correction (ch. 37-47, November-December)

**Governing psychology:** the specific relief of a long-running wrongness finally being addressed, sitting next to a much larger wrongness that isn't going to be. Neither cancels the other and the book should end holding both.

- **Homecoming, Fifth Year.** November, and Tier 3's Homecoming fragment is the sharpest tonal marker in the whole bank against Book 1's Tier 1 version. Some of you don't have two homes to choose from anymore. This one still counts. Soren's absence is a year old.

- **The Ritual, Fifth Year.** Hiopi ritual, and the cohort's reactions now sorted by how much each of them has personally bled. Vrassik is a winner watching, which is a different chapter than a competitor watching.

- **The Exam, Day One.** Year 5's assessment, weight and heat both real factors in a way no prior exam has had, per the year's curriculum.

- **The Exam, Day Two.**

- **The Exam, Final.** Placements land, rank changes real.

- **The Reposition.** **The locked beat, and it gets real page time rather than a paragraph.** Ilyen formally moves Reeps to Munti. **Framed the whole way through as a correction and never as a demotion**, per her sheet. She regains rank and gains real recognition once she's in a role where her precision and patience translate into visible success instead of quiet uncredited competence. Two Muntis on one team from here, which is the setup for Book 6's Ch. 20 payoff. *Psych: being good at something in public, for the first time, at sixteen, after four years of not being, is a genuinely large event and should be allowed to be one.*

- **What Didn't Change.** **Immediately after, deliberately.** The Cradle Circle thread does not resolve. The interference from Act 3 stands. One kid got put in the right role by people who cared about her, inside a system that spent the same year quietly allocating resources away from her whole track, and the book puts those two facts next to each other and stops. **Rule 4 applies at maximum force. Nobody comments. Not Trav, not Reqa, not Osei, not narration.**

- **Ilyen, Noticed.** **Fourth of five tells, LOCKED, author's call.** The chain escalates across the books: convenient scheduling (Book 2), watching the door before he arrives (Book 3), an unfinished sentence abandoned twice (Book 4), this, and Book 6's reaction that outlasts the conversation. **The fourth:** with real Munti capability for the first time, she starts keeping the specific parts his build eats, stocked ahead of need, before he's asked for any of them. **Why this is the right one and not a generic gesture.** It requires having tracked a pattern across months, which is the single most locked thing about her, the girl who keeps an unprompted private log of every AI-tutor correction the dorm gets purely because she likes knowing things precisely. It's the same faculty that made her the cohort's rune-patterning prodigy, pointed at one person. And it is **completely deniable**, a good Munti stocking for their team's actual consumption is not a favor, it's the job, and she's newly excellent at the job. **Trav reads it as her being good at her new role. She is.** *Craft note: the reader should be able to work out that nobody else's consumables are stocked this far ahead without a single line saying so, which means at least one other teammate has to visibly wait on a part somewhere in this book.* Entirely platonic on the page, nothing physical, nothing named, and Trav never editorializes, per the locked approach.

- **Kinship.** December, quiet, the team's shared season. Ilyen visibly better. Everything else visibly unchanged.

- **Loose Threads.** Deliberately calm, deliberately incomplete. The anger does not get discharged.

- **Bridge.** Closes Year 5 and opens directly into Book 6, the transfer slot, and the last full year the six of them have together.

## Realism flags surfaced while building this

- **Nothing rises to the level of the two named exceptions.** The year's material is unusually well grounded, all three curricula carry real established science (control theory, thermodynamics, autonomous-stage motor learning), and the institutional-unfairness spine sits inside the already-approved dystopian premise rather than being a new stretch.

- **One worth naming, and it's a craft risk rather than a realism one.** A book built around institutional injustice and a sympathetic authority figure conducting a fairness review is structurally the most dangerous chapter set in the series for the standing "never state the critique" discipline. Kest is written to be sincere, which means the temptation is to have him *almost* see it, or to have a cadet nearly articulate it and stop. Both are softer versions of stating it. The discipline holds only if the reader assembles the contradiction with no character having gestured at the assembly.
