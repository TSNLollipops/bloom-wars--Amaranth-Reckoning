## Honest gap assessment

Each book’s current outline entry (Qiraki_Concept.md’s tightened seven- book section) gives roughly 4-6 concrete, locked beats. That’s nowhere near 47 chapters’ worth of material on its own, and it shouldn’t be, those beats are the spine, not the whole skeleton. What’s missing is connective tissue: slice-of-life, minor conflict, texture, the kind of material that makes a school year feel lived-in rather than a plot outline with dialogue attached.

Good news: this is exactly the genre with the deepest well of tropes to draw from for that connective tissue, academy/school-year fiction has decades of material to pull structure from. The trope bank below is built for that purpose, mixed and matched per book so no two years feel like they’re running the same content.

## Act structure template (applies to every book)

Four acts, 12 / 11 / 13 / 11 chapters, LOCKED to match Qiraki_Chapter_Index.md:

- **Act 1 (ch. 1-12), Arrival/Setup** — reestablish the cast at their new stakes for the year, plant the year’s central complication.

- **Act 2 (ch. 13-23), Rising complication** — the year’s actual conflict develops, mix of plot-forward and texture chapters.

- **Act 3 (ch. 24-36), Escalation** — stakes peak, the year’s locked “big” beat (loss, injury, institutional betrayal, whatever’s specific to that book) lands here.

- **Act 4 (ch. 37-47), Exam/Climax + resolution** — the year-end exam itself, aftermath, and a bridge into next year.

## Trope discipline, LOCKED, applies to every trope used

Two separate obligations, not one, easy to conflate and shouldn’t be.

**In-book resolution, the one that was missing.** A trope pulled from the bank for a given book has to actually resolve within that same book, not just get deployed and left open on the assumption that a downstream callback later covers it. “A rule everyone breaks that suddenly gets enforced on the wrong person,” used in Book 2, needs an actual outcome in Book 2, the wrong person either eats the consequence, or the unfairness gets exposed, or it doesn’t and that itself is the resolution, but something has to happen on the page before that book’s back cover. A callback three books later is not a substitute for this, it’s an entirely separate, additional obligation. Leaving a deployed trope structurally unresolved inside its own book reads as a dropped thread even if it gets a nice echo in Book 5.

**Downstream callback, the original rule, still applies on top of the above.** No trope from the bank gets used once and forgotten across the whole series. Every one that appears should have at least one later echo planted somewhere downstream, doesn’t need to be the very next book, but it needs to exist. A rumor-spiral in Book 2 resolves within Book 2, and also still echoes somewhere by Book 5. A “guess the menu” ritual joke should still be a joke seven years later, changed by what’s happened since. This is what actually makes seven years read as one continuous life rather than seven separate short stories wearing the same character names.

Track both, deployed trope, its in-book resolution, and its planted downstream echo, in the tracker below once drafting is underway.

## Daily-rhythm chapter template, LOCKED

Given the academy’s already-locked three-part day structure, most “day in the life” chapters can follow a repeatable four-beat shape rather than needing improvised pacing each time:

- **Breakfast/socialization** with the dormmate (Denic, then whoever’s relevant later), before class starts.

- **Class period 1**

- **Class period 2**

- **Class period 3**

Plus an **optional evening beat**, only when the plot actually demands one, evenings are established free time, not every chapter needs to use it. This template is also the direct fix for why Ch.1 and Ch.2 both ran short, they each only covered part of a single day. A chapter built on the full four-beat shape has a natural, complete length rather than stopping wherever the scene happened to run out.

Not every chapter needs to be a full daily-rhythm chapter, plenty of Book 1-7 material (study sessions, exams, the class-choice debate, major locked plot beats) will still want their own dedicated shape. This is specifically the template for ordinary days that still need to earn their page time.

## Trope bank (pull freely, mix across books, don’t reuse identically)

**Institutional/school-structure tropes**

- The unfair instructor who turns out to have a real reason for being hard on someone specifically.

- A scholarship/legacy pairing gone wrong, forced group project style.

- Grading scandal or accusation of favoritism, resolved or left deliberately unresolved.

- A rule everyone breaks that suddenly gets enforced on the wrong person.

- Cross-year mentorship, an older cadet taking an interest (genuine or self-serving).

**Social/slice-of-life tropes**

- The four-day-weekend choice (home vs. Cradle event) actually mattering to a specific chapter’s plot, not just background texture.

- A social event gone wrong (the “guess the menu” ritual, a monthly opening/closing activity escalating past what anyone intended).

- A rumor spiraling through the dorm faster than the truth can catch it.

- First real argument between established friends, not romantic, just friction.

- A homesickness chapter, quiet, low-stakes, character-building.

**Competitive/rivalry tropes**

- A rival (Vrassik or a one-book-only rival) winning through means that feel unfair even if they’re technically legal.

- A team-shuffle that breaks up an established pairing right before something important.

- Underdog moment where a background classmate (Ilyen, Denic, Corw) briefly outshines everyone.

- A “prove yourself” test that goes wrong in an interesting way rather than a straightforward pass/fail.

- A cadet whose family pressures them to switch paths mid-year for status reasons, real friction between what they’re good at and what looks good on a family record.

- An instructor pitted against their own former student, now a senior cadet, neither able to fully treat the other as just a teacher and a pupil anymore.

**Danger/tech-cost tropes**

- A training accident that isn’t the year’s “big” locked injury, a smaller one that still costs something.

- A student who can’t handle wound-emulation and has to leave, handled with real weight, not a footnote.

- Equipment failure at the worst possible moment, human error or institutional cost-cutting behind it.

- A visiting veteran or wounded alum whose presence makes the war briefly, uncomfortably real.

- A cadet who pushes symbiosis or gear past what their body’s actually adapted to, chasing status rather than readiness, real consequence, not a cautionary aside.

- A near-miss that doesn’t make the year’s official casualty count but should, per the academy’s own PR discipline, quietly undercutting how “rare” death is actually being counted.

**Mystery/intrigue tropes**

- A minor faction-politics thread a cadet stumbles into without understanding the stakes.

- Something in the maze/test environment that shouldn’t be there.

- A classmate keeping a secret that isn’t sinister, just personal, and the plot briefly (wrongly) suspects it is.

- A points-economy irregularity, someone’s score climbing faster than their visible performance explains, resolved as either quiet cheating or a genuinely unfair advantage nobody’s supposed to have.

- An old rivalry between two instructors surfacing through how they grade or treat rival cadets, never explained outright, background texture a sharp reader assembles.

**Romance-adjacent tropes (non-Trav/Reqa specific, ensemble use)**

- A minor cast romance or crush subplot among secondary characters.

- Someone’s first real heartbreak, used for texture, not main-plot weight.

- A relationship that reads romantic to everyone around it except the two people actually in it, played for gentle comedy rather than secrecy.

## Military-era trope bank, new, for Book 7’s bridge chapters and beyond

The academy bank above assumes a school-year structure, arrival, curriculum, exam, bridge to next year. That structure ends at graduation. Book 7 already runs requisition-politics as a running thread, and everything past it is deployment-shaped, not year-shaped, mission-chapter rhythm (3+ mission chapters per arc beat, cooldown block after 6-7) rather than the four-act academy template. This bank exists so that transition has real material to pull from instead of running dry exactly when the structure changes underneath it.

**Unit/command tropes**

- A new transfer to an established unit who has to earn trust the academy never required of them, reputation means nothing here.

- A commanding officer whose orders are technically correct and get someone killed anyway, no villain, just the actual cost of imperfect information under real stakes.

- A veteran pilot who’s clearly done, past the point of real usefulness, and the unit has to decide what to do about someone they respect.

**Mission-shaped tropes**

- A mission that succeeds by every official metric and still costs something nobody’s willing to say out loud afterward.

- Extraction goes wrong in a way that isn’t anyone’s fault, pure bad timing, and the unit has to sit with that rather than assign blame.

- A cooldown-block chapter where the actual danger is boredom and drift, not combat, worth using deliberately rather than treating downtime as filler.

**Institutional/political tropes**

- A directive from command that conflicts with what the unit on the ground actually knows to be true, real friction between doctrine and lived reality.

- A journalist, propagandist, or Cradle Circle representative embedded with the unit, turning ordinary soldiers into content, resented and occasionally used right back.

- Requisition politics costing the unit something real (better gear denied, delayed, or given to a more photogenic unit instead), direct continuation of the resource-gap seed planted back in the academy books.

## Chapter-by-chapter planning, standing rules, LOCKED

Three rules govern this next level of granularity, below the act-level plan above.

**Scope: Books 1-6 only.** Year 7 (Devereux-Kastel) is deliberately excluded from this pass. It’s a transfer-student year at a wealthy institution, a distinct genre register from the six years before it, and gets its own dedicated research pass rather than being forced into the same template as the others.

**Realism-exception flagging protocol.** The project’s standing rule: everything is as realistic as it can be made, outside of two named, pre-approved exceptions, the invented-science boundary (Sovereign/Heirloom-grade tech, the Bloom’s biology past the “real theory runs out” line) and the alien species themselves. Anything else that reads as a stretch, an institutional behavior, a psychological reaction, a plot convenience, gets flagged explicitly for approval rather than written in quietly. This is distinct from the project’s own deliberate dystopian premise (militarized child soldiers, parental ignorance, wound-emulation training from age twelve), which is already locked, approved, and load-bearing to the story’s critique, not something to re-flag every time it appears, only genuinely new stretches get flagged.

**Adolescent psychological realism, LOCKED as a craft standard.** Every chapter beat involving the cadets should be grounded in how kids this age actually think and feel, not adult psychology in smaller bodies. Concretely: peer approval outweighs adult approval by early adolescence, status and public embarrassment land harder than private failure, black-and-white fairness judgments precede nuanced ones, risk perception is genuinely immature (a real, documented developmental fact, not just narrative convenience, and part of why these kids can undergo real danger without adult-level dread), impulse control is still forming, attachment needs shift from parents toward peers under separation, and conflict between friends needs real rupture-and-repair, not clean resolution. Trav’s own non-reflective RPD-grounded cognition stays intact, he doesn’t introspect, but the psychology underneath should still be accurate, shown through what he does rather than what he thinks about doing it.

## Casualty/survivor roster

Built against the corrected numbers: academy-era death is rare, ~1% or less, restock catches nearly everything else, so this roster is mostly a survivor list with a small number of real exceptions, not a body count. Cumulative military-era attrition (the working ~40%-by-carrier-command average) is a separate, much harsher story that starts once graduation happens, not reflected in these academy-era entries below.

**Book 4’s loss, LOCKED: Soren Adair.** Human, Meeps path, third team (unnamed). Book 1 seed: in Trav’s exact literacy group at arrival, one of the other kids visibly struggling the same way Trav is in Act 1, a specific, small, shared-humiliation-into-camaraderie moment rather than a generic classmate.

**Why him, mechanically, not just narratively:** academy death is rare precisely because it’s almost never the wound itself, restock and extraction catch nearly everything, per the existing battlefield-medicine lock, “just alive” at retrieval is enough to save someone. Death happens specifically when extraction fails or comes too late. Year 4 is mechanically the roughest year for one reason, per the Combat Curriculum Reference, Meeps cadets are stuck in the clumsy, error-prone associative stage of skill acquisition while training scenarios keep escalating around them, the exact, named mechanism already locked behind Trav and Vrassik’s own repeated mutual injury that same year. Soren, sharing Trav’s path and Trav’s year, is standing in the same statistically dangerous position the story’s two leads are already known to occupy. Mikka, a Reeps, built around positioning and setup rather than close-range proximity, was never mechanically exposed to this book’s actual danger the way a fellow Meeps cadet is.

**The scene, LOCKED at the concept level, not yet drafted:** Soren dies during a Live Contact Sparring session (Year 4’s named curriculum exercise, full wound-emulation active, longer duration, harder scenarios than Year 3 asked for). The hit itself is survivable, consistent with the rule that the wound is rarely the real killer. What isn’t survivable is the extraction window, the same chaos and escalated difficulty that’s making the whole year rougher for everyone also means his Munti reaches him late. Worth drafting as genuinely close, not a clean miss, the kind of near-save that makes it worse, not better. Mikka Reyes stays in the cast as a surviving named character, available for later books, her Book 1 seed (the Ilyen friendship, the cross-team social reach) doesn’t go to waste, it’s just not spent here.

**Also logged, general roster texture:** a small number of other named Year-1 cohort members who simply survive the academy years uneventfully and reappear, if at all, as familiar faces once the military-era cumulative attrition starts actually taking people, giving later books real names to spend rather than inventing casualties from nothing when that grimmer math starts applying.
