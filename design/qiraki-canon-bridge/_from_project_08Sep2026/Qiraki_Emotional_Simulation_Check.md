# THE QIRAKI FILES — Emotional Simulation Check, Books 1-6

*Part of the Qiraki files. Runs every named character against five questions and reports which ones are currently living people and which are currently plot functions wearing names. Companion to** **Qiraki_Path_Audit_Books1-6.md**, which covers structure. This one covers whether anyone is actually alive in there.*

**The five questions, applied to every character:**

- **Want.** Do they want something specific, statable, and theirs? Not a role, not a function, a want.

- **Pursuit.** Do they act on it on the page, at cost, rather than having it happen to them?

- **Wrongness.** Are they allowed to be wrong, to refuse, or to cost someone something?

- **Tell.** Do they have a repeatable physical signature, per Style Guide Rule 12?

- **Change.** Are they different at the end of six books than at the start?

A character can miss one and still be alive. Missing three means the reader is watching a device.

## FINDING 1 — Trav has no statable want, and this is the largest problem in the project

Every locked fact about him is **cognitive**: RPD-style split-second reactive genius, poor deliberate planner for mechanical rather than personality reasons, no reflective interiority, moves forward through the next problem rather than examining the last.

Every one of those describes **how he processes**. None of them describes **what he wants**.

His core belief is locked and genuine, a galaxy free of its own war-cycle with AI included as Enlightened, argued through the analogy that his mother made him and that was never grounds to question his right to be free. **That is an endgame position.** It is not available to a twelve-year-old, and nothing in Books 1-6 is a smaller version of it.

**The failure mode this creates.** A protagonist whose defining trait is not examining himself, with no want driving him, becomes a camera. Things happen, he responds brilliantly, the reader watches skill rather than a person. Six books is a very long time to watch skill.

**Why it is not solved by**** ****“****he wants to be a good pilot.****”** Everyone in the building wants that. A want has to be his, and it has to be capable of costing him something.

**The constraint that makes this hard, and also shows the way out.** He cannot state a want, or realise one, or reflect on one. So the want cannot live in interiority. **It has to be visible in what he reaches for when nothing is asking him to reach.** What does he do with an unstructured hour. Who does he go to first when something goes well. What does he keep. What does he refuse without being able to say why.

**Two candidates already present in locked material, offered rather than chosen:**

- **He wants people to stay.** His father died before the story. He is separated from Yssa at twelve. He builds a joke ritual whose entire function is keeping someone from having a bad day, running it for years, one-directionally, at cost to himself, without ever being asked. He later requests Reqa transfer with him. He collects a found family and does not lose one until Book 4. **This is already on the page and has never been named as a want.** It also explains the joke ritual’s origin better than “practicing social skill” does, and it makes Soren’s death land on something rather than beside it.

- **He wants to be worth the cost.** His enrollment is subsidized. His family’s standing collapsed when his father died. Yssa gave up her own work. Under this reading his forward-only cognition is not just how he thinks, it is how he avoids adding anything up. **Riskier, closer to stated thesis, and would need very careful handling.**

**Author call. But something has to fill this slot before drafting scales up**, because a want established in Book 4 retroactively is a different book from a want visible in B1.CH01.

## FINDING 2 — Denic is the most-used character with the least interior life

He is present in all six books, load-bearing by Book 4, and the emotional engine of several ensemble chapters. He has a superb tell, cockpit stillness against noise everywhere else, which does real work every time it appears.

**He wants nothing that anyone has written down.** No sheet entry states a desire. Across 282 chapters he never pursues anything, never fails at anything he cared about, and is never wrong in a way that costs someone.

**What he does have** is a gift he did not ask for and a role he grew into. That is a shape, not a want.

**The cheapest fix is already implied by Doyle’s sheet.** Doyle takes pride in Denic’s cockpit gift specifically because it means the numbers he is careful with actually go somewhere, and Doyle is quietly a real reason Denic never quite washes out of rune-patterning. **Denic is academically mediocre and being carried.** A boy who is brilliant at the one thing that cannot be graded, and mediocre at everything that can, and who knows it, has an obvious want available: **to be good at something that counts on paper.** It costs him nothing to state and would make his Book 2 Munti choice a decision rather than a fit.

## FINDING 3 — Five well-written wants are trapped in the character sheets

Perrin, Fenn, Doyle, Petra and Sana all have specific, non-generic, genuinely good wants in Qiraki_Character_Sheets.md. **None of them reaches a chapter.**

- **Doyle** wants his precision to matter to someone. It does, and he never gets to feel it on the page.

- **Petra** values being trusted with something Ilyen has told nobody else. Six books of holding a secret, and one proposed chapter.

- **Sana** wants hard-won to count for something, and proves it by being consistent rather than by arguing.

- **Fenn** wants nothing from Corw, which is precisely why he is the only person Corw cannot perform at.

- **Perrin** is the one voice with no stake in Vrassik’s win, which is exactly why Vrassik might hear him.

**These are not filler characters.** Each is defined by a specific relationship to someone else’s need, which is the hardest kind of supporting character to write and they are already written. They just are not anywhere.

## FINDING 4 — Soren dies without having wanted anything

His death is the emotional centerpiece of the academy arc. He appears in B1.CH05, vanishes for two books, returns in B4.CH04, and dies in B4.CH25.

**No sheet, no want, no pursuit, no wrongness.** Book 4’s outline correctly insists he must be a real person rather than a plot device, and then never specifies what makes him one.

**A death lands in proportion to what it interrupts.** Right now it interrupts nothing, because he was not going anywhere. He needs one want, ideally small and ordinary and unfinished, and it needs to exist before Book 4.

## FINDING 5 — Reqa’s wrongness gap is closed, and it was the right fix

Qiraki_Process_Notes.md flags her by name as the character most at risk of being the reliable one who is never wrong. **Before the Heirloom thread, she had no failure anywhere in six books.**

She now fails in B4.CH15, at real cost, in a way that is entirely her own fault and entirely invisible to the team. She is furious about it in a way she never is about team work, which is characterization the outlines could not previously reach. **Closed.**

## FINDING 6 — Three characters are pure function

- **Jifsook** exists to needle her brother. That is a behaviour, not a desire. She appears in four books and never wants anything.

- **Bruvald** has a species temperament and no self. He now has a ten-fragment cultural bank written for him and two appearances.

- **Krethis** has a resentment, which is closer to a want than the other two, and it is unexplained across six books and never acted on decisively.

**All three are Vrassik’s team.** That team is structurally the mirror of Trav’s, and Trav’s has five people with wants while Vrassik’s has one.

## FINDING 7 — Vantree is the strongest unused character in the project

She has a want in her sheet and it is the best-articulated want any adult has: **better they bleed here, under her watch, than out there with no one watching.**

She has a tell, the unhidden seam at her wrist. She has a locked six-year tenure. She has an open question with real charge, whether she clocks Trav before he is obviously exceptional. And she has **one scene**.

**Everything needed to make her a living character is already written.** She needs chapters, not development.

## FINDING 8 — Tells are missing for four core characters

Style Guide Rule 12 requires every locked character route emotion through one repeatable physical tell. Trav (forward-set weight), Reqa (mouth-watching), Denic (cockpit stillness), Fenn and Vantree have one.

**Vrassik, Talia, Corw and Ilyen do not**, despite all four being fully alive by every other measure. Ilyen’s five tells are behavioural rather than physical, which is a different device doing a different job and does not satisfy the rule.

Vrassik in particular is the second most-present character in the series and the reader has no physical signature for him.

## Ranked action list

- **Give Trav a want.** Largest problem in the project. The “he wants people to stay” reading is already on the page across six books and has never been named.

- **Give Soren a want**, before Book 4, ideally before Book 2.

- **Give Denic a want.** The material is sitting in Doyle’s sheet.

- **Put Vantree in more than one chapter.** Nothing needs writing except placement.

- **Get the five mek wants onto pages.** Ensemble chapters can carry them at no chapter cost.

- **Assign tells** to Vrassik, Talia, Corw and Ilyen.

- **Give Vrassik’s team what Trav’s team has.** Jifsook and Bruvald need one want each, Krethis needs his resentment to go somewhere.
