# THE QIRAKI FILES — Book 6, "What It Costs to Leave," Chapter-by-Chapter Outline

*Part of the Qiraki files. Same template and standing rules as Book 1's outline. Cadets are 17-18 this book. Final book of this planning tier, Year 7 gets its own dedicated research pass.*

**Locked spine:** the definitive Trav/Vrassik rivalry year, a single Devereux-Kastel transfer slot, Trav wins, Vrassik places second. Equipment-innovation year, lance to improved sword and movement servos.

**Curriculum, Year 6, three disciplines converging on the same beat:** rune-patterning reaches weighted circuits, connections that strengthen with repeated use, the edge of real machine learning. Engineering reaches control systems and servomechanisms, the physical half of the same feedback-loop field rune-patterning introduced a year earlier. Combat class runs Transition Trials, motor schema transfer, structured drills testing whether an existing weapon skill transfers cleanly onto a new weapon shape rather than teaching a new weapon from zero. All three independently explain why Trav's equipment innovation happens in this specific year, which is the actual reason it does.

**Emotional target:** genuinely bittersweet rather than a clean triumph. Trav's win is mechanically Vrassik's loss, no way around it. Real satisfaction around the weapon-shift chapters, an earned discovery rather than a purchase. Needs its own comedy beat so the year doesn't run entirely on rivalry tension, and Trav's now well-developed humor is the obvious vehicle.

## Act 1 — The slot (ch. 1-12, January-March)

**Governing psychology:** near-adult cognition, real future-orientation finally online, and the specific cruelty of a scarce opportunity introduced to a group of friends.

- **One Slot.** The transfer competition formally announced. Stakes affecting the whole cohort, deliberately not framed as Trav-vs-Vrassik yet, a wide field of hopefuls first.

- **Weighted.** Year 6 rune-patterning opens, trainable adaptive constructs, connections strengthening through use. Strong reader-facing content and a quiet thematic mirror of everything the cohort has spent six years doing to themselves.

- **Servos.** Engineering reaches control systems, the physical counterpart to last year's feedback loops. The two curricula visibly converging.

- **Transition Trials.** Combat coursework testing weapon-shape transfer. The formal drill running right alongside Trav's own informal tinkering, two versions of the same real phenomenon happening at once.

- **The Field.** The wider cohort of transfer hopefuls established, including at least one credible contender who isn't Trav or Vrassik, so the eventual narrowing feels earned rather than predetermined.

- **Something Sharper.** Trav starts modifying his own gear, the first year the coursework actually equips a cadet to do it rather than just operate what they're issued.

- **Study Session I.** Trav and Reqa, weighted circuits, the material genuinely exciting to both of them for different reasons.

- **The Comedy Chapter.** Deliberate relief, Trav's humor now fully load-bearing socially, effortless in a way it absolutely was not at twelve. Nobody remarks on the change. **Escalation beat, joke-ritual arc, near its endpoint.**

- **Vrassik, Hungry.** His own campaign for the slot, driven by post-ritual confidence, entirely legitimate, no cheating, no shortcuts. Worth writing him as genuinely deserving it.

- **Talia, Not Competing.** Talia opting out of the transfer race deliberately, knowing herself well enough by eighteen to choose the path that actually suits her. Real growth, quietly stated.

- **Fifth Blood.** Trav and Vrassik spar, and it's notably clean compared to Books 3-4, both of them past the roughest years, skill now expressed as precision instead of damage.

- **The Choice.** **No propaganda-fragment opener** (Qiraki_Process_Notes.md's weekend-chapter exception). Weekend chapter, and for many of them, one of the last, worth letting that be felt without anyone naming it, the reader should feel the countdown before any character could.

## Act 2 — The blade (ch. 13-23, April-July)

**Governing psychology:** identity consolidation, the moment a person's method becomes recognizably theirs rather than something they were taught.

- **Pairblade.** Trav switches from twin lances to a matched set of short swords. **The book's craft centerpiece.** He isn't learning swords from zero, he's transferring six years of motor schema onto a new blade shape, which is exactly why it reads as discovery rather than a new skill. **Hard rule: this is an in-fiction decision he makes because he's found he trusts an edge and his own movement over raw committed impact. Not a purchase that unlocks a sword.**

- **Wrong At First.** The switch costs him. Real regression before real improvement, honest rather than triumphant.

- **Study Session II.** Engineering-side, servomechanisms, feeding his own gear work.

- **The Underdog.** Trope draw, a background classmate briefly outshines everyone, keeping the year from being only about two people.

- **Better.** The swords start working. Movement servos rigged alongside them, his build philosophy visibly shifting toward flexibility over durability, the chassis itself reading leaner across this book.

- **June.** Ensemble day, Year 6 Bloom fragment lands, the research note about a single specimen reading as three species at once, request denied.

- **The Field Narrows.** Other contenders drop away for ordinary reasons, and it becomes a two-person race without anyone announcing it.

- **Denic and Ilyen.** Two Muntis on one team now, the pair of them genuinely excellent, a quiet payoff five books in the making.

- **Corw's Last Year.** Corw fully arrived, the top-ranked cadet from Book 1 having become something more useful than the standout everyone predicted.

- **What Reqa Wants.** Reqa's own stake in the transfer named, she goes with him if he wins, and she has her own reasons for wanting Devereux-Kastel that aren't about him. Important, keeps her agency intact.

- **Midpoint.** Ensemble chapter, the whole team aware that this is the last full year together.

## Act 3 — Two people, one slot (ch. 24-36, August-October)

**Governing psychology:** competing against someone you genuinely like, at an age where friendship and ambition finally have to be weighed against each other for real.

- **Head to Head.** The competition formally narrows to Trav and Vrassik. Both told they have a real shot. Both believe it.

- **Ambition.** August, the year's rivalry at maximum, and notably free of malice, which makes it worse.

- **The Trial.** A specific graded assessment feeding directly into the slot decision.

- **Study Session III.** Third session, unused subject.

- **Cross-Cohort.** Both teams together, Jifsook needling her brother through the highest-stakes stretch of his life, Lissrak steady, Krethis openly rooting against Trav for reasons six books old now.

- **Unity.** September, cultural exchange, and a real conversation between Trav and Vrassik that isn't about the slot.

- **Vigilance.** October, Year 6 Bloom fragments, including the CO's private journal line about no longer writing reports on what it wants.

- **The Second Trial.** Second assessment, the margin genuinely narrow.

- **Doubt.** Trav's non-reflective cognition tested by a situation that actually demands deliberation, direct foreshadowing of his locked command-arc weakness. He handles it badly and doesn't examine why.

- **Vrassik's Case.** A chapter from close enough to Vrassik to see how much he wants it and how legitimately he's earned it. **Critical: the reader should not be certain Trav deserves it more.**

- **The Cohort Holds.** Ensemble beat, the team caught between two people they care about.

- **Reqa, Steady.** Reqa doing for Trav what she's done since Book 4, the reversed ritual now simply part of how they work.

- **Countdown.** The decision imminent.

## Act 4 — Winning and leaving (ch. 37-47, November-December)

**Governing psychology:** the first genuinely irreversible life outcome most of them have experienced, and the strange grief of getting exactly what you wanted.

- **Homecoming, Sixth Year.** The last one at this academy for at least two of them. Heavy without anyone saying so.

- **The Ritual.** Sixth Hiopi ritual, immediately before the decision, Vrassik's own species' stakes running parallel to the human institutional ones.

- **The Exam, Day One.** Year 6's assessment, and the transfer decision rides on it.

- **The Exam, Day Two.**

- **The Exam, Final.**

- **The Slot.** Trav wins. Vrassik places second. **LOCKED, resolved:** he takes it with real honor, not performed grace masking resentment, genuine. He shakes Trav's hand and means it. This isn't the absence of disappointment, he wanted this badly, but losing to someone he respects, fairly, doesn't curdle into bitterness for him, consistent with a character whose whole arc is earning things the harder way and being fine with that. Worth one honest beat of real disappointment before the honor, so it doesn't read as costless, but no bad-loser behavior, no passive edge, no delayed grudge. This is the actual seed of the bro-rival dynamic Books 8+ already lock, not a contradiction of it. **Kest's fourth appearance, LOCKED, brief, bookending his Ch. 3 podium moment six years earlier, reframed for scale:** the single Devereux-Kastel transfer slot is genuinely rare and high-value enough (one per academy, region-spanning prestige) that someone at Kest's level plausibly confirms the results across his whole cluster of academies in one circuit, Trav's academy is one stop among several, not a special trip for him. He's the one who confirms the decision institutionally, a handful of lines, the same register as every prior appearance, warm, certain, sincere. This is the first time it's been about Trav's own outcome specifically, not a background detail (Book 1), not someone else's grief (Book 4), not a teammate's struggle (Book 5), his own future, which is exactly the kind of immediate, personal stake his RPD-style attention actually snags on. That's why this is the one that finally sticks, not coincidence, the same cognitive mechanism that's been the reason for three prior non-registrations. A small, quiet moment of recognition worth landing without comment.

- **What Second Means.** Vrassik's path now runs through the regular ranks and battlefield performance, harder-won, self-made. He doesn't frame it as a consolation, he's already turning it into its own kind of ambition, same hunger from Ch. 9 redirected rather than deflated. He may or may not understand yet that this is the making of him.

- **Telling Them.** Trav telling the team. Six years of built closeness meeting the fact that almost none of them are coming. **Fifth and clearest tell, LOCKED, still never confirmed:** everyone else's reaction is loud, real, immediate. Ilyen's is quiet, and it's the only one Trav notices lasts longer than the conversation, a difference he registers as a fact without ever once wondering why. The book ends without answering the question it just let the reader ask. **The trope loop's biggest payoff so far, and the setup for Book 7's Act 1.**

- **Kinship.** December, the team's last shared season, deliberately warm and deliberately sad.

- **Loose Threads.** **Now Book 6's second stay-weekend chapter, new, no propaganda-fragment opener** (Qiraki_Process_Notes.md's weekend-chapter exception). Calm chapter, everything about to change, nothing changed yet. **The series' found-family throughline pays off here, deliberately.** Whole team together, ensemble rather than one pairing, the last time all of them do something completely ordinary in the same room before Ch.47's transfer splits them. Nobody states that this is what's happening. **Direct callback, new:** this is the concrete, unstated payoff of what Yssa predicted at B1.CH12, that staying would eventually mean something. Nothing in the scene should gesture at that being the point, the reader who's been across all six books gets to feel it arrive on their own. *Psych: the found-family bond doesn't need to be discussed to be the strongest relationship structure in the room by now, that's the entire six-book argument, and it's stronger for staying unstated even here.*

- **Bridge.** Closes Year 6 and the first-academy era entirely. Opens toward Devereux-Kastel, which gets its own dedicated planning pass.
