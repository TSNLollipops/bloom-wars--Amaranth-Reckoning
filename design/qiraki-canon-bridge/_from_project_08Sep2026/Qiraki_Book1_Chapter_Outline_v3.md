# THE QIRAKI FILES — Book 1, "The King and the Climb," Chapter-by-Chapter Outline

*Part of the Qiraki files. Companion to Qiraki_Book_Expansion_Plan.md, one level more granular than that doc's act-level breakdown. This is the calibration pass, Books 2-6 follow the same template once the format's confirmed. Year 7 excluded from this whole planning tier, see the standing rules in Book_Expansion_Plan.md. Cadets are 12-13 in this book, per Trav's locked enrollment age (12y 207d), the youngest and most psychologically distinct year in the series. **Ch. 1-2 status, revised: provisional, not fixed.** Existing prose is "maybe to keep," not locked ground, everything below can build around it, replace it, or reorder it as needed, including the open question of whether the book should open with Trav's pre-academy homeworld life instead of (or before) the transport-arrival scene.*

**Calendar note:** Trav enrolls in January (Discipline month). 47 chapters (see chapter-count note at the end of the outline) map loosely across the year, Act 1 = Jan-Mar, Act 2 = Apr-Jul, Act 3 = Aug-Oct, Act 4 = Nov-Dec.

**Weekly class schedule, LOCKED, established B1.CH02:** Monday: 2 rune-patterning, 1 engineering. Tuesday: 2 engineering, 1 rune-patterning. Wednesday: 2 rune-patterning, 1 engineering. Thursday: 2 engineering, 1 rune-patterning. Friday/Saturday: combat class (pilots) / extended workshop time (meks), per Qiraki_Combat_Curriculum_Reference.md. Sunday: off. Same shape repeats week to week. Confirmed on the page in Ch. 2: each rune-patterning session block runs 2 hours (4 hours total on a 2-rune day), each engineering session runs 2 hours. Rune-patterning is dorm-based (separate room per dorm, same time slot academy-wide, see Qiraki_Concept.md's housing/cohort-scale lock), engineering is matchset-based (cross-dorm pairs share a workshop time). Every weekday Monday-Thursday has at least one engineering slot (Tuesday and Thursday have two), so Trav and Reqa's matchset actually meets every single weekday, not just twice a week, which is why Ch. 2 closes on "See you tomorrow" rather than naming a specific day.

**Staging note, LOCKED, governs every book, not just this one.** Per the already-locked ratio (roughly one real human instructor per subject per 2,000-cadet cohort, most actual instruction and individualized correction coming from each cadet's own Academy-tier AI, "Protocol Guardian"), any trope or beat that needs a personal, individualized adult presence belongs to a cadet's AI, not a human teacher, a human at this ratio simply cannot know one specific kid well enough for that kind of dynamic to be plausible. Human instructors (Halvorsen, Vantree) are mass-lecture and demonstration figures, real, present, occasionally memorable, but not a source of one-on-one personal attention except in genuinely rare, notable cases. This fold was applied once already below (the "unfair instructor" trope moved into the AI-tutor chapter), worth remembering as the default going forward rather than re-solving it each time it comes up.

**Propaganda-fragment frequency, LOCKED, updated rule.** Every chapter now opens with one, not "every several chapters" as originally written. Pull from Qiraki_Propaganda_Bank.md's three relevant sections: the 12 month-theme fragments (use at the start of whichever month a chapter falls in), the Year 1 Bloom curriculum bank (3 fragments, cluster these around Vigilance/October and Curiosity/June per the existing plan), and the general institutional bank (11 fragments, rotate through these for ordinary chapters). Confirmed target is ~34 fragments per book, not 52, reuse within a book is fine as long as the same exact fragment doesn't open two chapters in a row and source type varies chapter to chapter.

**Daily-rhythm template, LOCKED, confirmed still governing.** The four-beat shape (breakfast/social, class 1, class 2, class 3, optional evening) stays the default structure for every chapter that isn't carrying a major locked event, exactly as originally specified in Qiraki_Book_Expansion_Plan.md, "not every chapter needs to be a full daily-rhythm chapter, plenty of material will still want its own dedicated shape… this is specifically the template for ordinary days that still need to earn their page time." The consolidation and padding passes earlier in this file folded extra beats into fuller days, they did not replace the rhythm itself, a padded chapter still moves through the same four beats, it just has more happening inside class 2 or the evening slot than a thinner draft would have. ## Act 1 — Arrival (ch. 1-12, January-March)

**Governing psychology:** separation from primary attachment figures (parents) at the exact developmental point where peer attachment is starting to matter more anyway, real grief compressed by a forward-only cognitive style that doesn't process backward, shown through action, never introspection.

- **Arrival.** *Provisional, "maybe to keep," not fixed.* Existing draft: transport scene, first sight of the academy's real scale, meeting Denic as his assigned bunkmate. **Live option, not yet decided: open the book with Trav's homeworld and pre-academy life instead.** Father dead six years, a soldier, mother doing freelance mek work groundside for food and rent, Trav's own childhood split between shipboard life and the sprawl of urbanization his family calls home since discharge. **Recommendation, not a decision:** this material is genuinely strong and shouldn't be cut, but opening the *book* with it risks delaying the actual hook (arrival, illiteracy, the found-family engine) by a chapter. A likely stronger shape: keep something like the current arrival scene as the true Ch. 1 opener, and thread the homeworld material in as a short Ch. 1 or Ch. 2 flashback beat once Trav's on the transport or just after arrival, grief and context delivered in motion rather than before the story starts moving. Author's call either way, both are real options now that the existing draft is provisional.

- **The First Pattern.** *Provisional, "maybe to keep," not fixed.* Existing draft: first rune-patterning class, illiteracy established as universal, not personal, the peg-game recognition beat.

- **The Standard Message.** Retitled 2026-08-16 from "Dorm Assignment" (DEF-29, closed): the bunkmate-meeting beat that title was built around already landed in Ch.1 as delivered, so this slot needed new primary content, and got it. Runs Tuesday through Thursday. Breakfast-table worldbuilding on the Hiopi games-ritual, status and placement only, the real mating-rights ritual explicitly held off-page for a later, more adult stage of Hiopi life, not this cohort. The band delivers its first message from home and Trav sends the standard reply back, first look at that whole device. Wednesday is four hours of straight rune-patterning, no new pattern, the room's first real wall, paying off Reqa's own Ch.2 line predicting it. An engineering session has Reqa teaching bolt-torque sequence by feel on a real, non-simulated unit for the first time. Thursday evening, a cluster-wide broadcast address from **Director Aurelian Kest, LOCKED here as his first appearance, see the corrected timeline in Qiraki_Character_Sheets.md:** named and quoted on the page rather than podium-anonymous, the scale of it (eleven academies, twenty-two thousand and six cadets) is what actually lands with Trav's table, not Kest himself. Same broadcast carries kit-issue and overflow-rooming announcements and previews Friday's first combat instruction, which the chapter closes on. Ilyen fixes Denic's collar a second time, "That's the last one," a soft deadline for his own competence arc to grow into. *Psych: a big number said aloud by a stranger becomes the fixation object faster than the stranger does, the same way kids latch onto a reported death toll or disaster scale before they ever register who delivered it.*

- **First Contact.** Year 1's first combat class, full-contact, wound-emulation active, real stakes from session one. **Drafted at the concept level, see Qiraki_Combat_Curriculum_Reference.md's First Contact entry:** Vantree's "Pick your weapon and face it" / "You will fight each other" exchange opens the scene, cadets pair off with standard-issue weapons, the arena's automated safety system pulls each cadet clear before the lethal threshold, heals them, and returns them to the Spawn Line for another round with fresh weapons across a full four-hour session, each bout scoring under the points economy. Trav's actual first opponent should stay an unnamed stranger, never returned to. **Staging note:** mass session, hundreds of cadets, Vantree overseeing at scale, individualized correction handled by each cadet's own Academy-tier AI. *Psych: adolescent risk-underestimation lets this land as thrilling-scary rather than adult-scary, and the Spawn Line's respawn structure plausibly deepens that, a real-stakes version of the exact game loop this generation grew up on.*

- **The Cover.** Rune-patterning class, Trav's illiteracy panic starts to show under a cold-call moment, Denic covers for him in front of the room without making it a thing afterward. **Loop-opening beat, Denic.** Also introduces Soren Adair here, briefly, a third kid in the same literacy group visibly struggling the same way Trav is, a small, specific, shared-humiliation-into-camaraderie moment rather than a generic classmate. **Loop-opening beat, Soren Adair (Book 4 payoff).** *Psych: public shame is one of the sharpest fears at this age, a single unthanked rescue creates outsized loyalty fast.*

- **Ilyen.** Same class, days later, Ilyen quietly corrects a rune-pattern mistake of Trav's before the instructor spots it, says nothing about it after. **Loop-opening beat, Ilyen.** *Psych: nonverbal caretaking forms trust fast at this age, well before either kid could name what's happening.*

- **The Ranking.** First real sparring/comparison exercise, Corw wins it, makes sure Trav knows. **Loop-opening beat, Corw.** *Psych: status hierarchies form within days in a new peer group at this age, being bested publicly stings in a way it wouldn't for an older teen with more established self-concept.*

- **The Workshop.** **Merged chapter, per author's explicit preference: Trav and Reqa's day leads, everyone else follows within the same day, not a parallel ensemble scene.** First time the mek/pilot paired workshop space is shown on the page, per the locked detail that pairs share a personalized workshop rather than a shared classroom, real sensory and world-building weight for a whole new part of academy life. Opens and centers on Trav and Reqa specifically, the first "new joke, never repeated" beat lands here, small, unforced, the actual start of their dynamic. Only after that does the same day widen out to the workshop's other pairs: Doyle quietly helps Denic with something technical without being asked, Fenn's flat unimpressed reaction to Corw's bragging plays for a quiet laugh, Petra draws Ilyen out for the first time. **Loop-opening beats, Reqa (primary), Doyle, Fenn, Petra (secondary, same day).**

- **Protocol Guardian.** The "unfair, turns out to have a real reason" trope belongs to the AI, not a human teacher, per the locked instructor ratio (roughly one human per 2,000-cadet cohort). Consistent with the already-drafted Chapter 2 tutor scene's register, not unkind, just accurate. A cadet gets pushed harder than seems fair by their Guardian, revealed by chapter's end to be a specific, data-driven reason. Also carries the dual report-and-help function already established. *Psych: black-and-white "it's just being mean" judgment gets complicated for the first time.*

- **Self-Care Day.** Homesickness chapter, quiet, low-stakes, built around what Trav *does* rather than what he thinks, keeps an object from home, doesn't talk about it, Denic notices and doesn't push. *Psych: regression behaviors are normal and healthy processing at this age, not weakness.*

- **First Blood.** Small-scale Year 1 sparring clash with Vrassik, the precursor Years 3-4's real injury exchange needs. **Loop-opening beat, Vrassik.**

- **The Choice.** Four-day-weekend-choice chapter closes the act, home visit vs. staying for a Cradle-sponsored event, the decision itself revealing where each kid's loyalties currently sit. *Psych: loyalty conflict between family and new peer-world is a normal, real cost of early boarding-away separation.*

## Act 2 — Rising complication (ch. 13-22, April-July)

**Governing psychology:** comparative self-evaluation intensifies as skill differences start actually showing, kids this age define themselves relationally far more than through internal reflection.

- **Who's Good At What.** Rune-literacy visibly advancing at different rates, Ilyen's precision starts standing out, Denic's academic mediocrity starts contrasting against something else nobody's named yet.

- **Study Session I.** First formal study-session chapter, Trav and Reqa, subject: the childhood peg-game parallel deepening into real pattern-reading.

- **Rule-Bending.** Cadets learning to work around Academy-tier AI oversight without breaking rules outright, direct follow-through on Ch. 9's Protocol Guardian thread. *Psych: mild rule-bending as autonomy-seeking is developmentally normal.* **Padded with:** Denic's earlier cover from Ch. 5 gets repaid in miniature within the same chapter.

- **The Rumor.** Rumor-spiral trope chapter. **Padded with:** Ilyen quietly noticing something else about Trav, purely platonic at 11-12, the rumor's chaos giving her a natural, unforced moment to observe. *Psych: reputation anxiety is especially acute in early-adolescent peer groups.*

- **First Real Fight.** First friendship argument, non-romantic, likely Trav and Denic over something small that escalates. **Padded with:** the Corw/Trav comparison recurring, the gap narrowing as Trav's combat-class aptitude shows. *Psych: rupture-and-repair is how trust deepens at this age.*

- **The Accident.** Small-scale training accident, not the year's big one, someone gets hurt, restock, real fear for a friend for the first time. *Psych: witnessing peer vulnerability is a genuine empathy-development milestone.*

- **Study Session II.** Second study-session chapter, engineering-side, an early, Year-1-appropriate simple-machines topic.

- **June.** Ordinary daily-rhythm ensemble chapter. First Bloom curriculum fragment lands here (Curiosity month). **Padded with:** Fenn's calm continuing to quietly check Corw's ego.

- **Second Blood.** Vrassik rivalry deepens. **Padded with:** Denic's cockpit aptitude getting its first real institutional notice.

- **Midpoint.** Act 2 closer, full ensemble chapter, every relationship gets a real beat before Act 3 begins.

## Realism flags surfaced while building this

None rise to the level of the two named exceptions (invented tech, aliens), but two are worth naming explicitly since they're new stretches, not the already-approved dystopian premise:

- **Ch. 18 (the training accident) and the ritual-witnessing chapters (38-39):** real, repeated exposure to genuine danger and real bloodshed at age twelve should plausibly leave some psychological residue even where the physical injury heals clean via restock. **RESOLVED:** approved, and given an actual mechanism rather than staying vague texture. This residue is what the joke-ritual reversal (see Trav and Reqa's sheets in Qiraki_Character_Sheets.md) eventually answers, starting in Book 4, Reqa turning the ritual back on him for the first time. Book 1's own residue (this book, pre-reversal) should stay small and wordless, a flinch, a bad night, nothing named or resolved yet, the mechanism that actually processes it doesn't exist until three books later.

- **Ch. 9, "Protocol Guardian" (formerly the human "unfair instructor" chapter):** superseded, folded into the AI tutor per the corrected instructor-ratio note above, which also fixes the original flag, an AI plausibly *can* know one specific kid's data closely enough to justify being hard on them, a human teacher for 2,000 cadets couldn't. Original concern still worth carrying forward: the reason should explain the behavior without fully excusing it, consistent with never having a sympathetic authority figure (or an AI written with that same sympathetic register) say the quiet part out loud on the institution's behalf.
