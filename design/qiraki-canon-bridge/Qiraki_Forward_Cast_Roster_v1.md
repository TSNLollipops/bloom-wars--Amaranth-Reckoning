# PART ONE --- TWO RULES BEFORE ANYBODY GETS A NAME

## 1.1 The Roll is audible, \[CLAUDE\], surfaced rather than invented

The existing cast already obeys a rule nobody wrote down. Off-Roll
people have working names. Calder, Tremblay, Pral, Green, Castell,
Arnesen, Reyes, Voss, Marsh, Okafor, Lindqvist, Ashe, Renn, Adeyemi-Tal,
Solvig. On-Roll people have names that sound chosen to be inherited.
Aurelian Kest, Devrin Osk, Milo Castellane, Peregrine Thale, Cassia
Vantree.

Stated as a rule so it stops being an accident: an on-Roll given name is
almost always a name somebody in that house already had, which is why
the whole register sounds antique. An off-Roll given name was chosen by
a parent who liked it. The Physical Description Bank's
naming-implies-heritage principle already governs surnames. This governs
the half in front.

What it buys. A reader learns to hear the Roll in a name across a book
or two, with no explanation anywhere, and from then on every
introduction in forty-two books carries a class marker for free. Trav
can hear a name and know the answer to a question he does not know he is
asking.

Never explained by anybody, consistent with POL's absolute restraint on
on-Roll and off-Roll. The moment a character points at it, it stops
working.

## 1.2 Nobody enters this document without passing the project's own test

Qiraki_Emotional_Simulation_Check.docx runs five questions against every
named character: want, pursuit, wrongness, tell, change. Its own
scorecard found nine existing characters failing three or more, which is
its definition of a device wearing a name.

Every figure below is built to pass at least four before drafting
starts, and each entry states its five explicitly. This is the cheapest
possible insurance against a dozen new faction names becoming a dozen
new plot functions, which is the exact failure the forward series is
most exposed to, because political-era characters arrive already
attached to an institutional job.

One deliberate exception, marked where it appears. A character whose
change is none by design, per the Kest precedent on that same scorecard.

# PART TWO --- THE MELTADREAD WARRANT

## Warrant Emeric Anseline, House Anseline

\[CLAUDE, name and house both swappable, character shape follows ERA4's
four design constraints exactly.\]

ERA4 recommends against Hearth Bloc and in favour of a smaller, older,
poorer chartered house with nothing left but the charter, on the grounds
that a Warrant who needs the appointment is a better character than one
who is slumming. Taken.

POL locks that charters were awarded for doing hard things in dangerous
places, and that for a long stretch that was genuinely how they were
earned. House Anseline holds its charter for the Anseline Reach, a gate
route that stopped mattering four centuries ago when a shorter one
opened. The house still holds the charter. It holds nothing else.

**What he is.** He is genuinely excellent at exactly one thing, and it
is rooms. He walks into a Senate delegation and comes out with an
allocation, which is a real service, saves the Guild real resources, and
is worth more to five carriers than most things Trav can do. He is kind,
and the kindness is not a performance. Trav likes him inside a month and
keeps liking him for twenty-five years.

**What he knows.** He knows exactly what he is. This is the addition
that makes him worth the page. A man from a house with nothing but a
charter understands better than anyone alive what a charter is actually
worth, because his is worth nothing except this appointment. He took
MeltaDread because no better house wanted a formation with a joke name
and no better formation wanted a house with no money. Both halves are
true. He says the second half out loud once, lightly, to Trav, in Book
23, as a joke about himself. He never says the first half and nobody
says it for him.

**Tell.** He writes his own correspondence by hand and it is beautiful.
An on-Roll accomplishment that stopped being useful two centuries ago
and that his mother made him learn anyway.

**The free callback.** ERA3 Book 17 puts Warrant investiture language on
the page as a propaganda fragment for the first time in the series,
attached to a different Guild's appointment, and calls it genuinely
beautiful writing about obligation that cannot be set down. That is the
register Emeric was raised inside. The reader has heard his voice five
books before they meet him and will not know it. Nobody remarks on this.

**The five.** Want, the house still existing in two hundred years.
Pursuit, he takes an appointment beneath his standing and works it
seriously for a quarter of a century. Wrongness, he is wrong about the
rebellion. Tell, above. Change, from a man performing an office to a man
who understands the office is the only thing he has, and the story never
narrates the difference.

**Era VI.** ERA6 Book 38 recommends the third of its three shapes, that
he goes home, sincerely and apologetically, because his house is the
only thing he has and the charter is the only thing his house has.
Taken, and one thing added. After Book 38 he writes Trav one letter, by
hand, and it is beautiful, and it is about nothing at all. Trav keeps it
and never answers it, because he does not process backward and would not
know what answering it was for.

**Free and unremarked.** \[CLAUDE\] The Anseline Line, a post-Bloom
battle clan named for the same dead gate route, appears in Part Seven.
The two have nothing to do with each other. Emeric serves alongside a
formation carrying his family's one real achievement as a name and never
mentions it once.

# PART THREE --- THE QUORUM, FOUR SEATS, FOUR NAMES

POL locks the mechanism and asks for exactly one thing from drafting:
the reader should meet the same name attached to the same seat across a
very long story and work it out. So the design instruction here is not
characterisation, it is constancy. These four names do not change
between Book 8 and Book 42, and no book explains why.

**Craft rule, absolute.** Thirty-five books of four unchanging names is
the entire device. Nobody performs the arithmetic on the page.

## 3.1 The Osnius seat --- Halvor Vardagen, House Vardagen

Precedence, and sets the Quorum's agenda. \[CLAUDE\] Osnius-register
name, consistent with Bruvald, Bram, Solvig, Ashe and Thale. What he
wants is continuation, which is the Quorum's locked want and needs no
personal variation. He appears in perhaps four scenes across forty-two
books and is a name on a notice in roughly two hundred.

\[CLAUDE\] Peregrine's family is Osnius elite, holds a private resort
world, and is not the seat. That is its own quiet humiliation and
belongs to his mother rather than to him, and it is available in Book 42
without a word of explanation.

## 3.2 The Terra seat --- Perpetua Ashcroft, House Ashcroft

Ceremonial primacy and almost no practical power, which POL locks as a
real and sore distinction. \[CLAUDE\] The name is the joke and the story
never makes it. She has held a ceremonial seat for eight centuries, her
given name is an inherited one that means what it means, and no
character ever points at it. Terra falls first and most cheaply in Book
40 and she is the only seat-holder who is not surprised.

## 3.3 The Carabil seat --- Continuity of Appointment

\[PROPOSED, and it is the strongest single suggestion in this
document.\]

The military capital's seat, holding appointment power over every
Warrant in the Coalition. Carabil naming convention is locked as
abstract process phrases, identity as function within a system, which is
where Coherence of Process comes from.

So the seat's holder is called Continuity of Appointment. It is a
correct Carabil name under the existing convention. It is also a plain
description of what the seat does and of what the Close is, and nobody
in forty-two books remarks on either. Two Carabil in the story, one
named for how she thinks and one named for what she preserves, and they
are never in a scene together.

**The alternative, offered honestly.** POL says the Carabil seat holds
the appointment power and does not say who holds the seat. A human house
on Carabil is equally available and produces a colder, more conventional
book, in which the species whose world it is has no part in the
instrument that runs its capital. That version is also good and is not
what I would take.

\[OPEN\] Carabil lifespan is not on file anywhere I can find. If it is
short, the seat turning over inside a species that names itself by
function is a different and possibly better problem, and Book 41 is
where it would bite.

## 3.4 The Hiopi seat --- Hesskir Vaun, House Vaun

Finance and trade, holding the gate network and the toll schedule.

\[CLAUDE\] This seat is the one place two locked rules collide, and the
collision is free. Hiopi mating rights are a civic institution held at
city scale under a one-winner rule. A seat is held by a house rather
than a person. Sovereign-grade longevity is gated to ruling families.
Put those three in the same room and the Hiopi seat resolves as a holder
who simply wins, every cycle, for eight hundred years, because a Hiopi
with Sovereign-grade longevity entering against ordinary Hiopi is not
competing.

Nobody designed this. It is what the rules do when you put them
together, which is POL's own method for the seat-and-house arithmetic
and should be handled identically.

**Craft rule,** absolute. Vrassik never learns it. Vrassik gives up a
carrier-scale win in Book 34 for a friend's war and never mentions it
again, and there is one Hiopi alive who has never lost a cycle and never
will, and the two facts are never in the same chapter. The reader can do
the arithmetic. Nobody in the story is positioned to.

# PART FOUR --- THE FIVE FACTIONS

POL owns where each faction sits and what it would trade. This part owns
who is standing there. Wants below are personal and sit underneath the
faction want rather than restating it.

## 4.1 Hearth Bloc

**Milo Castellane.** Exists, needs a full sheet. Senator, Hearth Bloc,
who came to congratulate the year's valedictorian team at the
Devereux-Kastel graduation epilogue and remembered it. Delighted to see
Trav on the other side of a table in Book 30 and the delight is
completely genuine. Not a hypocrite and not stupid. Recurs in Book 40 on
the other side again, still helpful, still Hearth Bloc.

**Selwyn Vellory, House Vellory.** \[CLAUDE\] The Bloc needs somebody
who is not charming, because Milo alone makes the faction likeable and
the faction is not likeable, it is correct. Selwyn runs generation
allocation. He has never been to a frontier world and does not
experience that as a gap.

**The five.** Want, the generators staying in the family, which he
experiences as stewardship rather than as ownership. Pursuit, he
negotiates continuously and extremely well. Wrongness, he is told
directly what the unchanged ration is doing to people in Era V, and he
does not believe it, because he has run the numbers and the numbers are
fine, and the numbers are fine. Tell, he corrects other people's
arithmetic mid-sentence, always accurately, never unkindly. Change,
none, deliberately, per the Kest precedent. The Bloc's entire position
is that they have been right for a thousand years.

## 4.2 The Ledger

**Andrienne Kohl.** \[CLAUDE\] The one who runs the peacetime numbers in
Book 30, correctly bets the arrangement is not sustainable, and conducts
the defection in Book 34 across a table, bloodlessly.

**The five.** Want, margin and stability in that order. She is not
greedy. She finds volatility distasteful the way a good engineer finds a
bad tolerance distasteful, and that is a real difference. Pursuit, she
starts moving money four books before anybody notices. Wrongness, she is
right about the war and wrong about Trav, whom she reads as a stable
asset, and he turns out to be the least stable object in the galaxy.
Tell, she never writes a number anywhere another person can see it.
Change, by Era VII she is running the Open's treasury and is the most
powerful person in it who is not Trav, and nobody voted for that either,
and the story does not comment.

**Bertram Ossley.** \[CLAUDE\] Older, argues against the defection,
loses, and then executes it flawlessly, because the Ledger's culture is
that once the number is settled you stop arguing. He exists so the
faction has an interior and gets perhaps forty lines in the whole
series.

## 4.3 The Frontier Compact

**Governor Nell Aduba.** \[CLAUDE\] Governor of a frontier world four
hundred light-years out. Off-Roll, which matters: the Compact is the
only faction whose leadership is mostly off-Roll, which is exactly why
it loses every argument it makes, and POL's pillar map already implies
this without stating it.

**The five.** Want, a better Draw, and she has wanted it for thirty
years. Pursuit, she has been filing since before Trav enlisted and she
has the filings. Wrongness, she backs him early and hard and is wrong to
trust him. Tell, she quotes filing numbers from memory, always exactly
right, which reads as pedantry until a reader works out it is the only
weapon her faction has ever been issued. Change, from his most useful
early ally to the most credible critic of the Open, and she never
becomes an enemy, which is worse.

**Where she lands.** Book 39's refusal is delivered to her. She is
right, he is right, and ERA6 already rules that he says it badly.

**Governor Tobin Sarr.** \[CLAUDE\] The other governor. He does not back
Trav, stays loyal, and is also correct, because his world eats along a
Coalition supply line and a rebellion would starve it. Two governors,
one faction, opposite calls, both defensible. He exists so that nobody
can read the Compact as a bloc with one opinion.

## 4.4 The Cradle Circle

**Director Aurelian Kest.** Exists and is fully built. Regional
oversight director. Not corrupt, not self-serving, driven by one fear,
and ERA7 Book 43 proposes his fate. Nothing added here.

**Assessor Ven Halloway.** \[CLAUDE\] The assessor who does the actual
selection at academy level and has personally signed off on roughly two
hundred thousand twelve-year-olds. The Circle owns selection criteria
rather than money, per POL, and she is what that looks like at the desk.

**The five.** Want, to be accurate. Pursuit, relentlessly, and she is.
Wrongness, she flagged Trav at eleven and the flag is on file and the
note attached to it is true and unkind. Tell, she remembers cadets'
names decades later and it unnerves people who assumed they were a
number. Change, none.

**Craft rule,** absolute. Neither Trav nor the reader ever sees what the
note says. It is referred to twice in forty-two books, both times as a
file rather than as a sentence, and it is never opened. Entered in PAY's
permanently-unspendable register.

## 4.5 The Osnian Honor Guard

**First-Blooded Ragnvald Hask.** \[CLAUDE\] Commander of the single
formation in the Coalition with no Warrant. Stands aside in Book 35.
Fights in Book 42.

**The five.** Want, the exemption unquestioned, which POL locks as the
species' proudest institutional possession. Pursuit, he refuses to be
asked, twice, in opposite directions. Wrongness, standing aside in Book
35 is arguably a betrayal of the Quorum and fighting in Book 42 is
arguably a betrayal of the same logic, and he experiences neither as
inconsistent, because the exemption was never about sides. Tell, he
gives the same one-sentence answer to every request regardless of who is
asking and regardless of what they want. Change, none, deliberately. He
is the only major figure in the political era who ends exactly where he
started, and against six eras of everybody else changing, that reads.

**Vaskr Hallen.** \[CLAUDE\] Younger, thinks the exemption has quietly
become a way of never deciding anything, says so, and is not promoted.
Four scenes in the whole series. He exists so the Guard is not a
monolith and so somebody inside it has noticed.

# PART FIVE --- THE PILLARS THEMSELVES

## 5.1 The Staff

**Hand-General Ostrava Kell.** \[CLAUDE, and she is the most important
new character in this document.\] Off-Roll, Staff-trained, excellent.
She offers Trav the Guildmaster track in Book 21 and the Legion in Book
28, and ERA3 and ERA4 already establish that both offers are
institutional rather than personal. She is the person who has been
watching him for four books.

**The five.** Want, to win the war. She is the only figure in this
entire document whose want is simply the stated one, and against a cast
of people optimising for something adjacent, that is characterisation.
Pursuit, forty years of it. Wrongness, she promotes him in Book 28 for
sound institutional reasons and hands the rebellion its leader, and she
works that out afterward and never says it to anybody. Tell, she reads
casualty lists in full, aloud, alone, and somebody catches her at it
exactly once. Change, she holds for the Quorum in Book 38, fights him
for four books, and is not a villain for a second of it.

**Why she is needed.** The rebellion currently has no opponent who is as
good at this as Trav is. POL's whole argument is that the Staff is
genuinely excellent and structurally forbidden from converting
competence into a claim. Kell is what that sentence looks like as a
person, and she is the reason Era VI is a war rather than a collapse.

**Hand Bev Sorokin.** \[CLAUDE\] Hand of a sport-named Guild who defects
in an afternoon in Book 38, is not brave, and does not pretend to be.
She exists so the clean defections have a face and so the reader can see
that the easy ones were easy.

**Allocation Clerk Emmet Roos.** \[CLAUDE\] The allocation clerk who

different desk, still correct, still not a villain, while the world

**Assessor Juno Achterberg.** \[CLAUDE\] The revenue assessor who

\[CLAUDE\] Signs the telemetry review on Zeteii in Book 20 and the
throttling action in Book 33. He is never in a room. He is a signature

# PART SIX --- THE BATTLE CLANS

Open since Bible §11 and Concept's battle-clans section, which asks for
distinct class identity and personality per clan and flags that some of
the original teams' culture and temperament should plausibly have
survived the transition into military-adjacent factions.

## 6.1 The two rules that make the roster generate itself

**Rule one, \[CLAUDE\]. A clan's temperament is its old tactical
identity, still running, with nobody left who knows it was once a
tactic.** MeltaDread is locked as one of the original hundred companies
that ran gladiator fights in the pre-war era, saboteurs and tricksters,
new unhinged tactics every cycle, great spectacle, sometimes it worked
and sometimes it did not. That is not backstory. That is a description
of how the Guild fights in Book 8 and of why its recovery doctrine is
unorthodox enough that other Guilds request briefings on it in Era IV.

**Rule two, \[CLAUDE\]. A clan's name tells you which century it is
from.** The original hundred carry single compound names in the kinetic
register, because that is what a pre-war spectacle company was sold
under. MeltaDread, Ironhowl, Sunkfist. Clans formed after the Bloom
arrived are named for what they did or where they formed, and they sound
like it. The Fallow Companies, Second Landing, Coldwater.

A reader can date any formation in the Coalition by its name,
permanently, after meeting four of them. Nobody explains it, exactly as
nobody explains the Roll.

## 6.2 The original hundred, the ones the story needs

**MeltaDread.** Locked. Saboteurs and tricksters. Improvisation as
doctrine, four hundred years after the improvisation was for an
audience. Sport-named, therefore Warrant-vacant for a century, therefore
genuinely unified, therefore able to defect cleanly. Trav's.

**Ironhowl.** \[CLAUDE\] The oldest surviving clan. Attrition and
holding. Has not meaningfully changed a formation in four hundred years
and it still works. Warrant-held, loyalist, and the hardest fighting in
Book 41 is against them.

**Sunkfist.** \[CLAUDE\] Heavy assault. Won more pre-war titles than
anybody and has been living on it for a century. Sport-named, defects
cleanly in Book 38, and is not as good as its reputation, and everybody
including Sunkfist knows.

**Quickmarrow.** \[CLAUDE\] Speed and extraction. Their pre-war
specialty was getting a downed fighter off the sand before the crowd
stopped watching, and four hundred years later the Coalition's entire
restock and recovery doctrine descends from a crowd-pleasing trick.
Nobody connects those two facts, including them.

**Glasswake.** \[CLAUDE\] Precision and extreme range. Naomi Castell's
whole doctrinal lineage without her ever having served in it. Defects.

**Hollowking.** \[CLAUDE, and it is the best one.\] The clan that threw
fights for money in the pre-war era, got caught publicly, and has spent
four hundred years being the most rigidly honest formation in the
service. Nobody remembers why. Loyalist, and refuses to defect on
grounds that no member can articulate, and the grounds are a scandal
every living person has forgotten.

**Redgrist, Palefang, Tallgallows.** \[CLAUDE\] Named, unbuilt,
deliberately available. Do not build them until a book needs one.

## 6.3 The post-Bloom clans

**The Anseline Line.** \[CLAUDE\] Formed on the gate route House
Anseline was chartered for, centuries after the charter stopped meaning
anything. The house and the clan have no relationship whatsoever. See
Part Two.

**Second Landing.** \[CLAUDE\] Formed from the survivors of a world that
was lost and retaken. Their entire culture is that they do not abandon
ground, which is tactically wrong roughly a third of the time, and they
know, and they do it anyway.

**Coldwater.** \[CLAUDE, and it is Reqa's if she ever wants one.\]
Formed from demobilised meks rather than pilots, which makes it the only
clan whose culture is built around the people who fix things. Quietly
the best-supplied formation in the war and has never been fashionable
for one day of its existence.

**Openhand.** \[CLAUDE\] Formed post-Bloom by off-Roll officers and
named as a deliberate provocation aimed at the Warrant and the Hand
both. The Quorum permitted the name, because refusing it would have
meant admitting the provocation landed.

**The Fallow Companies.** \[CLAUDE\] Formed from farm-world survivors.
Small, poor, and the only formation in the Coalition that grows its own
food. They appear in Era VI, forty books after Trav walks through a
field in Book 18, and nobody in the story connects them to anything.

## 6.4 What the five-hundred-year vow actually is

\[CLAUDE, and this is the largest single thing in this document.\]

Locked: the battle clans gather resources and vow to return and settle
their own rivalries in the Milky Way in exactly five hundred years.

The rivalries are sport rivalries. They are four hundred years
fossilised, they belong to companies that ran gladiator spectacles for
an audience, and the clans have been carrying them the entire time
without anybody asking what they were. The vow in Book 44 is a rematch.

That is funny and it is devastating and it is entirely derived from
locked material. Concept locks that the pilot sport was directly
inspired by witnessing a Hiopi city-fight and was built to import
genuine stakes into a species that had made its own combat sport
consequence-free. Krethis says that to Trav in a corridor in Book 9,
correctly, and he has no answer, and she dies in Book 32 without either
of them mentioning it again.

And then in Book 44 the descendants of those companies swear to hold a
final season across intergalactic distance in five hundred years, having
by then acquired more genuine stakes than any species has ever carried.

**Craft rule,** absolute. Nobody says the word rematch. No character
notices. No chapter comments. Entered in PAY's permanently-unspendable
register, and the ceremony is written entirely straight.
