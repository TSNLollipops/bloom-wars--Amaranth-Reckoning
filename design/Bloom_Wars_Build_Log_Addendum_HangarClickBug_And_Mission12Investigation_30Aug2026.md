# Build Log Addendum — Hangar Deck click bug (fixed) + Mission 12 investigation (30 Aug 2026)

## Part 1 — "still cant interact with the hangar window": FIXED

**Root cause, confirmed against Phaser's own source (`node_modules/phaser/src/input/InputPlugin.js`), not guessed.** Hub.ts has one scene-wide click listener (`this.input.on("pointerdown", ...)`, added in `create()`) that decides what a plain click on the room means — door, deploy bay, or now the Hangar Shop, falling back to Talk. Phaser fires every interactive GameObject's *own* pointerdown listener first, then *still* fires this scene-wide listener afterward, for the same click. Every other overlay (History, Highlights, Poker, Darts) only ever opens from clicking a specific NPC, and that click already sets a `npcClickConsumed` flag this listener checks and bails out on — so they never hit this. The Hangar Shop is the one overlay opened by *proximity* instead (standing at the terminal, not clicking an NPC), so nothing was telling this listener to stand down while it was open.

The result: every click anywhere inside the open Hangar Shop — Buy, Upgrade, Recruit, even the panel's own [close] button — reached its own button handler correctly, and then this same click *also* fell through to the scene-wide listener, which saw the player still standing at the terminal and called `openHangarShop()` again. That function does an unconditional `hangarShop.render()` every time (by design, so the panel always reflects the latest state) — so every click's own effect landed, then got its visible result immediately overwritten by a same-click re-render. Nothing looked like it worked; the previous click's actual effect only became visible after the *next* click, one step behind. That's exactly "can't interact with it."

**The fix:** the scene-wide listener now checks Phaser's own hit-test list (`currentlyOver`, passed into the pointerdown event) and bails out if the click landed on any interactive element at all — which covers the Hangar Shop's full-panel interactive backdrop, its buttons, and its close button all at once, without needing a new state flag. (A simpler fix — checking the `hangarShopOpen` boolean instead — was tried first and rejected once traced through: the close button's own listener runs *before* this one and already flips that flag to false, so that version would have let a close-button click fall straight through and immediately reopen the panel it had just closed. Caught before shipping, not after.)

**Verification:** `npm run typecheck` and `npm run lint` clean; `npm test` still 1140/1140. Hub.ts has no unit test suite of its own (the standing gap this file's progress log already tracks) — this is logic-traced against Phaser's actual source and the surrounding code, not click-tested live, same caveat as every other Hub.ts change this session.

## Part 2 — "i still havent found out whi I keep losing mission 12 tho": investigated, real mechanism found, decision needed

Ran the actual sim rather than guessing. **Mission 12 (The Fallow Line, the Act I finale) wins 12% of the time (7/60)** — by a wide margin the hardest mission in Act I, and one of only two missions across the whole campaign this session has found sitting far outside the 50-100% band its own comments implied (Mission 10 is the other, ~10-20%, reported separately). Worth flagging on its own: **this is the only mission in the entire 36-mission file whose own comments never record a tested win rate** — every other mission's own code says something like "tested at X%." That's a real signal this one may simply never have been sim-verified after it was built, unlike everything else.

**The actual failure mechanism, reproduced identically in 3 out of 3 losing runs pulled for inspection:**

1. Turns 1-3 go fine — the squad clears the opening Crawlmass/Splitfang wave together, clustered near the hold zone.
2. Turn 4, hostile phase: **The Choir** (the mission's returning mid-boss, 3 units, built specifically with a shared "pack" targeting rule — its whole design point, per its own Mission 8 debut comment, is coordinating fire on one target) arrives and, together with a surviving Splitfang, concentrates every attack on a single squad member in that one hostile phase.
3. In every loss pulled, that single member was **2nd Lt. Dessa Rourke — "Lark," the player's own commander.** Two or three 29-damage Choir hits plus a 34-damage Splitfang hit in one phase is enough to drop her outright from full health.
4. Every *other* unit going down in this game is recoverable — "downed, standard restock, mission continues" (the same permadeath-gated system every other mission uses). The commander is the one exception: her going down is instant, unrecoverable mission failure, full stop, no matter how healthy the rest of the squad is.

So the 12% isn't really "the numbers are too hard" in general — the squad handles the Crawlmass/Splitfang opener and the later Sirenmaw wave fine in the runs that survive past turn 4. It's a single-point-of-failure: one coordinated alpha strike, on one specific unit, at one specific turn, where that unit's death alone ends the mission regardless of everyone else's health.

**A real, already-built counter-tool that may not be getting used.** `abil_taunt` was unlocked starting at Mission 8 — the Choir's own debut — and its own code comment says exactly why: it "wins the choice among whichever hostiles already have eyes on it... ahead of a Splitfang/Choir pack's shared... pick." That is *the* designed answer to this exact scenario — Taunt-ing with a different unit right around turn 4 should pull the Choir's pack fire off Rourke entirely. Worth checking directly: is Taunt actually getting used on that turn in your own runs? (The automated test-bot I used to find this mechanism doesn't use Taunt at all — it isn't wired into that harness — so the 12% figure almost certainly *understates* what's achievable with it. It isn't proof the mission is fine as shipped, but it's the most direct thing to try before anything else changes.)

**Three ways to go from here, genuinely your call rather than mine to make unilaterally** — this is the campaign's Act I finale, and how hard it's supposed to be is a real design decision, not a bug with one obvious right answer:

1. **Leave the numbers as they are.** If Taunt (or just keeping Rourke inside the main cluster instead of forward, since she ends up somewhat isolated by turn 4 in these runs) reliably fixes it, this was working as intended and just needed the right play, not a rebalance.
2. **Soften it slightly** — a small, sim-verified trim to the Choir/Splitfang turn-4 timing or count, the same small-steps-and-retest way every other mission in this file got tuned. I can do this the same careful way as the Tier 6 edits if you want it.
3. **Give the commander some structural protection** — a bigger, more invasive change (e.g., some innate resilience, or changing what "commander down" actually costs). Worth naming as an option since it's on the table, but this reaches past a balance tweak into changing a core rule, so flagging it rather than just doing it.

Let me know which direction, or just tell me what you saw when you tried Taunt on that turn — either way I'll keep digging with you rather than guess at the "right" difficulty for your own finale.
