# Mission 21 — Cut the Root

**Objective:** eliminate_all [boss]. **Map:** dug-in root cluster, 26×15. **Enemy:** Bloom Wellroot (boss) + Undertow reinforcements, 2 burrowed, every 2 turns starting turn 3.

**27 Aug 2026 — boss swapped from Heartwood to the Wellroot.** The mission originally shipped with the Heartwood boss reused wholesale (same stat block, same `emergentDecision()` AI) as a placeholder — the Independent Campaign doc had always described this fight as "acid-heavy," which the Heartwood reuse never actually delivered on. `data/bloom.ts` now has a real `bloom_wellroot` archetype: Gallcyst lineage (acid) rather than Heartwood's (concussive), so the three named Amaranth threats each descend from a mechanically distinct base. Same shape otherwise — sessile (`moveRange: 0`), 480 endurance/65 vitality (up from Heartwood's 400/60, keeping the escalation curve strictly increasing: Heartwood < Wellroot < the Unnamed), `attackRange` narrowed to `[1,3]` (from Heartwood's `[1,4]`) to read as a shorter acid-spit rather than Heartwood's longer concussive reach. `attackPower` stayed at 60, matching Heartwood — see the finding below for why. Full validation (base-damage sanity checks, hits-to-kill escalation gate, Bloom Collapse trace) lives in the rebuilt `design/combat_sim.py`, plus a new automated case in `combat.test.ts`'s Bloom Collapse describe block.

**Finding during the swap: the on-hit-effects system doesn't exist yet.** The Wellroot's `onHit: "fx_acid_dot"` looked like free extra pressure — acid DoT plus a tile conversion, fully specified in `BLOOM_ON_HIT_EFFECTS`. First pass shipped it at `attackPower: 40`, betting the DoT would make up the difference. It doesn't exist: nothing in `src/engine` reads `onHit`, any `fx_*` id, or ticks a DoT — `BLOOM_ON_HIT_EFFECTS` is pure data, referenced nowhere. Confirmed by running the real mission harness (not just the idealized 1v1 math) in batches of 40: attackPower 40 won 80% of the time, nearly a coinflip-free walk, against the documented ~35% baseline. Reverted `attackPower` to 60 (Heartwood's value) and re-validated: 35%/25%/37% across three independent batches (N=40, N=40, N=60) — back on target. The acid identity (weaponType, onHit, palette, chemical perception) ships as flavor only for now; it becomes a real mechanical difference once the on-hit-effects engine work gets built. That gap isn't Wellroot-specific — see `build_log/README.md`'s "Still open" section, it's been silently true of five archetypes.

**27-28 Aug 2026 — the on-hit-effects engine shipped for real, and this fight collapsed.** Once `engine/turnManager.ts` existed and `fx_acid_dot` actually fired, the DoT the fight was never actually tested against turned out to be brutal on top of an unchanged `attackPower: 60` — win rate crashed to 0/40 across two independent batches (documented target: ~35%). Isolating the DoT alone (no tile-conversion side effect) showed it was already enough by itself to crush the fight at `attackPower: 60` and still leave it far under target at 40 (1/40, 3%). A candidate fix — cutting `attackPower` to 20 with the full effect active — re-measured at 30% and 35%, back on target, but deliberately not shipped: a ~3x cut to a boss fight's headline stat is exactly the kind of call this project reserves for Maxime. A follow-up hand-played, turn-by-turn manual playtest at the still-shipped `attackPower: 60` reinforced the diagnosis rather than changing it: the squad loses mostly to the boss's raw hit power stacking with an unrelated Undertow ambush-burst mechanic before the DoT ever gets a turn to matter, not to the DoT itself.

**28 Aug 2026 (unattended overnight pass) — resolved, at `attackPower: 50`, not the 20 originally proposed.** Two things changed the picture before this got decided. First, a same-night, completely unrelated fix to the Player AI test harness (it had no logic protecting the commander or the Munti from ordinary combat risk — see `build_log/README.md`) happened to move this mission from a rock-solid 0/70 up to roughly 25-45%, at the unchanged `attackPower: 60`, without touching this stat at all — real evidence the original 0% collapse was partly the test player being bad, not purely the fight being unwinnable. That meant the `20` candidate had only ever been validated against the old, worse test AI, and was worth re-checking rather than shipping on faith. Second: Maxime, after being walked through all of the above, gave explicit latitude to make the final call once the evidence was in — **"you got the latitude to fix thing once knowledge been learned."**

Re-ran the batch validation (same harness, N=100 per point, against the now-fixed test AI) across the full range rather than just re-checking the two known points: `20`→47%/50% (two batches, ~48% average — overshoots, too easy), `30`→11%, `40`→0-2%, `45`→1%, `48`→14%, **`50`→35%/36%** (two independent batches, tightly agreeing), `52`→21%, `55`→32%, `60`→23%/28% (the old shipped number, still under target), `70`→13%. **This is a genuinely chaotic, non-monotonic landscape, not a smooth dial** — `45` and `52` both score worse than the values on both sides of them, and `60` beats `40` despite dealing less damage per hit. The most plausible explanation: `attackPower` is interacting with discrete "how many hits does it take to down this unit" breakpoints for whoever's actually fighting the boss, not a continuous damage curve — a swing of just a couple points can flip a unit from surviving long enough to retreat to dying on that hit, which cascades through permadeath and the commander-death-ends-mission rule into a completely different mission outcome. (Mission 36, "Until Relief," shows the same knife-edge shape on a different knob — enemy count, not attackPower — so this project's tactical sim has more than one system that behaves this way; worth remembering before assuming any future balance tweak here is automatically safe.)

Shipped at `attackPower: 50` — the only tested value that lands close to the documented ~35% target, and the only one confirmed by two independent batches that actually agree with each other. Not claimed to be permanently stable given how jumpy the neighboring values are: if the Wellroot's other stats, the squad roster, or the Player AI logic change again, this number should be re-swept the same way, not assumed to still hold. Full account, including the exact code comment: `data/bloom.ts`'s `bloom_wellroot` entry.

**Original build (25 Aug 2026), preserved below — same boss slot, then reused as Heartwood:**

The Heartwood boss debut — first sessile archetype (`moveRange: 0`, 400 endurance), its own board-omniscient `emergentDecision()` AI tier, first mission to use a genuinely repeating `MissionEvent` (`repeatEvery: 2`, no new engine code needed — the event system already supported it).

**The real, still-open finding:** the squad's own Player AI never engages the boss at all. `focusFireTargetInRange`'s `focus_weak` heuristic (lowest HP × defense) always loses to the disposable low-HP Undertow reinforcements — the squad spends the whole mission mopping up reinforcements and never lands a real hit on the objective. Not fixed at the engine level (judged as deserving its own dedicated pass, not a one-off patch under this mission's own pressure) — mitigated at content level instead by removing the mission's opening Crawlmass escort wave, buying two clean turns against Heartwood before reinforcements start. This mitigation and the `focus_weak` gap both carry over unchanged to the Wellroot — the boss archetype changed, the squad's targeting behavior around it didn't.

**Result:** ~60-70% across ~10 runs post-mitigation against the original Heartwood boss — accepted as reasonable "first proper boss" difficulty, explicitly flagged as a mitigation, not a fix. Later campaign-wide tuning brought the documented baseline for this slot down to ~35% (see `data/campaignAmaranth.ts`'s own tuning-history comments); the 27 Aug Wellroot swap was re-validated against that current ~35% baseline, not the original ~60-70% figure. This same `focus_weak` gap is the reason Marrow (Mission 20/28) needed no such mitigation — she isn't competing against cheap disposable spawns the way Heartwood/Wellroot are.
