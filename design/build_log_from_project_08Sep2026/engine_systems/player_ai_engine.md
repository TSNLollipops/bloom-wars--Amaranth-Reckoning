# The Player AI Engine (`src/sim/playerAi/`)

Maxime: "make it a separate engine we can plug into our future games... something we can reuse like the characterisation formula." Replaced the old flat `src/sim/testPlayerAi.ts`. Structure: `types.ts` (shared shapes), `combat.ts` (kill/focus-fire/retreat/kiting/cohesion/terrain/commander protection), `support.ts` (repair decisions), `index.ts` (priority chain: kill > critical repair > retreat/hold > routine repair > focus fire > advance > seek fight, plus objective branches — see below).

## What it does today

- **Combat basics** — kill-if-lethal, focus fire on a squad-shared weakest target (not per-attacker), retreat/kiting at low HP, repair (heal-in-place, two HP thresholds, range-aware since 28 Aug 2026 — see the dedicated section below).
- **Squad cohesion** — `cohesiveMoveToward` caps how far a unit advances ahead of its nearest living ally; a wounded, unspotted unit with nothing to kill/heal regroups toward the squad instead of chasing alone. Fixed a real oscillation bug (retreat/seek_fight ping-ponging) and a real infinite-regroup stall along the way.
- **Terrain/cover** — reachable-tile scoring folds in `defenceStars` as a tiebreaker (tuned down 5-10x from its first cut, which was strong enough to fight the cohesion fix).
- **Objective awareness** — reads a narrow `PlayerAiMissionContext` (objective, extractUnitId, bonusObjective kind, holdZone, exitTiles) and branches: beeline for an exit while carrying a rescue; pick up an adjacent uncarried rescuable NPC; clear bloom for a Munti on a clear-bloom objective/bonus (with a first `use_screen` heuristic layered on top, though it turned out to rarely actually fire in practice — see the Screen note below); path to the exit for the named `extract_unit` target; converge on the nearest hold-zone tile for `hold_zone`; escort convergence on the exit once nothing better is left to do (`escort_to_exit`, fixes a real deadlock where idle escorts froze and boxed in the extract target).
- **Commander/Munti protection (28 Aug 2026, test-only — new).** Found via a 5-archetype batch validation of the Bloom on-hit-effects engine: Missions 8 and 12 batch-tested at a flat 0% win rate, in every condition tried, effects on or off — not a regression from that pass, a pre-existing gap here. Root cause, confirmed against verbose single-mission traces, not guessed: nothing in this decision tree treated Rourke (the commander — `engine/mission.ts`'s `handleDowned()` ends the mission attempt outright the instant she's downed, no restock, no permadeath roll) or the squad's Munti (the sole source of repair; losing them turns every later downing into a permanent loss via the live permadeath rule) as needing more caution than an ordinary pilot. Two real mechanisms fixed:
  1. **`reachableIntoRangePreferringSafety` and `cohesiveMoveToward` both let a protected unit become the single most-exposed unit in the formation.** The real hostile AI's own targeting (`bestAttackTargetInRange`, GDD §5.3 — "attack whichever in-range target takes the most damage," genuinely shipped, not touched by this fix) means a fragile Meeps or Munti standing anywhere near a "pack"-intelligence swarm (e.g. `bloom_choir`, `swarmSize: [3, 4]`) gets focus-fired regardless of exact tile — the fix that actually helped wasn't a distance-from-allies leash (traced and found NOT to be the operative constraint — the whole squad was already advancing together, cohesion-legal) but a front-line cap: `commanderSafePathPrefix` (`combat.ts`) truncates a protected unit's candidate path to the longest prefix that never puts them closer to the nearest living enemy than their own most-forward living ally currently is. She still advances, just never past whoever's already most exposed — graceful, not a hard freeze, and the cap loosens naturally as allies push forward each turn.
  2. **Her own retreat/regroup threshold was identical to every other unit's (`RETREAT_HP_FRACTION = 0.3`).** `COMMANDER_RETREAT_HP_FRACTION = 0.5` (a deliberately generous, safe-side placeholder, not sim-tuned) applies to protected units only, via a `retreatThreshold` local that's exactly `RETREAT_HP_FRACTION` for everyone else.
  - Gated by `needsFrontLineProtection(unit)` = `isCommanderUnit(unit) || unit.path === "munti"`, where `isCommanderUnit` reads the same data-driven `PilotRecord.exemptFromPermadeath` flag `mission.ts`'s own `handleDowned()` checks — not a hardcoded pilot id.
  - **Switched off entirely on `extract_unit` missions** — found via a real regression during verification, not guessed. Mission 11 ("The Long Walk Back") dropped from a 97% baseline to as low as 20% before this exemption: its own turn limit is razor-thin (win completes turn 18, loss hits the limit turn 19), and extra caution from anyone — the named extract target detouring toward a stray reachable enemy, or an escort stopping to regroup/retreat sooner than before — cost just enough wasted movement to blow it. A narrower first attempt (exempt only the named extract target) wasn't sufficient; the interaction runs through the whole squad's pacing. The fix is switched off for the whole objective type rather than chased tile-by-tile further.
  - **Verified against a full-campaign diagnostic sweep** (`npm run sim`-style batch runs, N=20/mission, unseeded — not a rigorous, `combat_sim.py`-level balance pass, just a regression/sanity check for this change): most combat missions improved, several substantially (Mission 1 5%→40%, Mission 9 90%→100%, Mission 14 37%→100%, Mission 30 43%→80%). Missions 8 and 12 remain very low (0%/5%) even with this fix — see "Known, un-fixed limitations" below for why. All three `extract_unit` missions (5, 11, 31) confirmed unchanged from their own baselines after the exemption. Full vitest suite (996/996) unaffected — this module is never imported by `mission.ts`/`Battle.ts`, so nothing here touches shipped game balance.
  - **A real, campaign-wide side effect worth flagging plainly: this changes batch win-rate numbers on most missions, including some already-documented ones.** Most strikingly, Mission 21 (the Wellroot fight, `attackPower: 60`, on-hit effects live — see the Master Index's own "Bloom on-hit effects engine" section) moved from a rock-solid 0/70 across three independent baseline batches to roughly 25-45% across two post-fix batches (N=20 and N=40), *without touching `attackPower` at all*. This doesn't resolve the still-open question of whether to ship the validated `attackPower: 20` candidate — if anything it's new evidence worth weighing before that call, not a reason to make it here — but it does mean most of this campaign's previously-recorded batch numbers are measuring a less-competent test AI than now exists, and are stale until re-validated. That full re-validation (all 36 missions, larger N, ideally seeded for reproducibility) is a real follow-up task, not done tonight.

- **Repair-range dead code, found and fixed (28 Aug 2026, same overnight pass, test-only).** Maxime: "give more range to munty heal," narrowed via clarifying questions to **"Base range, everyone (1→3)"** — raise every Munti's baseline, no-purchase-required repair range from 1 tile to 3, campaign-wide (`DEFAULT_REPAIR_RANGE`, `data/weaponBranches.ts`). Implementing the constant bump alone would have been a silent no-op for both a real player and this test AI: `engine/mission.ts`'s `getRepairableFrom()` (the function `Battle.ts`'s UI actually highlights repair targets from) was hardcoded to `chebyshevDistance === 1` regardless of the constant, and this module's own `support.ts` had the identical hardcoded-adjacency limit in its repair-target search — the second one documented in its own header as a *deliberate* design match to the first, which made it a real bug rather than an oversight once the first one changed. Both fixed in the same pass, so a batch validation of the range change would actually exercise it rather than silently never looking past 1 tile. `RAPID_RESPONSE_REPAIR_RANGE` (the Munti's Weapon Branch purchase, previously a flat `2`) now derives as `DEFAULT_REPAIR_RANGE + 1` (→ `4`) instead of a second independent literal, so the branch keeps a real edge over the new baseline instead of becoming pointless, and can't silently go stale the same way again.

  Still heal-in-place only — no pathing toward an ally out of range, unchanged from the existing design (see `support.ts`'s own header). Verified: `tsc`/`eslint` clean, full vitest suite green (1004/1004, including 2 renamed + 4 new regression tests for the range fix itself, `repair.test.ts` and a new `support.test.ts`), `npm run build` clean. **Diagnostic re-sweep** (`npm run sim`-style batch runs, N=20/mission, unseeded — informational, not `combat_sim.py`-grade) against the full 36-mission campaign, for comparison against this same night's post-commander-protection-fix numbers above: broadly consistent — Missions 8/12/3 (the diagnosed pack-swarm alpha-strike ceiling and the pre-existing bot-only 0%s) stayed at 0%/0%/0%, unmoved, exactly as expected since more repair range doesn't help against damage that lands faster than a heal can follow it; Mission 21 (Wellroot, freshly shipped at `attackPower: 50` — see the Master Index) landed at 8/20 (40%), in line with that decision's own 35-36% N=100 figure; most other missions held steady or moved within ordinary N=20 sampling noise of their prior numbers. **One real standout worth flagging, not glossing over: Mission 36 ("Until Relief," the campaign finale) came back 19/20 (95%)** — well above the 29-73% run-to-run spread that mission's own file (`act3/mission36_until_relief.md`) already had on record as unexplained and unstable before tonight. Could be that same pre-existing instability landing at the high end of an even wider true range, or a real effect of more available healing in a `survive_n_turns` attrition fight — this single N=20 batch can't tell those apart, and it isn't guessed at here. Flagged for a dedicated larger-N re-check, not treated as resolved.

  **Resolved for real, same night, via a dedicated seeded harness — see `act3/mission36_until_relief.md` for the full account.** Confirmed by grep, not assumed: the Meeps dodge roll is the sole randomness source anywhere in a Mission battle sim, so seeding `Math.random` (mulberry32) makes an entire run fully reproducible. A determinism check (5 seeds, 3 repeats each) matched perfectly every time — directly disproving this file's own "tile-scoring ties broken by iteration order" guess above. A 100-distinct-seed win-rate sweep landed 99/100 (99%); the sole loss (seed 92) traced turn by turn to the exact same known, already-documented gap directly below in "Known, un-fixed limitations" — no predictive threat avoidance against a same-turn alpha-strike on the commander — not a new failure mode. Still can't cleanly separate whether the repair-range bump itself, the two dead-code bugs fixed in the same pass, or some mix of the three caused the *original* 29-73% spread — all three shipped together — but the current, post-fix state is conclusively no longer unstable or mysterious.

- **Civilian awareness** — none; civilians run their own separate `decideCivilianAction` (see `civilian_extraction_system.md`), not this engine.

## Known, un-fixed limitations (flagged repeatedly, not silently)

- **No ability usage.** Ambush/Interdict/Screen/Sensor Sweep/Taunt are all real, shipped verbs the bot never uses (Screen got one narrow, rarely-firing heuristic — see Mission 3's file). A wrong heuristic for a charge-limited ability was judged worse than an honest zero.
- **`focus_weak` has no notion of "this is the boss."** Surfaced hard on Mission 21 (Heartwood) — the bot always deprioritizes a tough, low-priority-by-the-formula boss in favor of cheap reinforcement kills. Mitigated at the mission-content level each time it recurs, never fixed at the engine level.
- **No real detour-seeking.** `cohesiveMoveToward`'s distance-field search is terrain-only and greedy — several idle units standing shoulder-to-shoulder can still wall off a narrow approach even with occupied-exit-tile awareness (Mission 23; recurred at squad-scale on Mission 26).
- **The sim is not a difficulty oracle.** Multiple missions (3, 5) went 0% under the bot and were cleared easily by Maxime playing for real — the bot's flat, reactive heuristics have a real tactical ceiling below "protect the fragile unit before it's critical," not a balance problem with the mission. Missions 3 and 5 stayed at 0% even after the commander/Munti protection fix above (28 Aug 2026) — confirmed, not assumed, against the same baseline the fix was validated against — consistent with this being the same pre-existing ceiling, not a new gap.
- **No genuinely predictive threat avoidance — the actual ceiling behind Missions 8/12's remaining near-0% win rate, and Mission 36's own sole seeded loss (see above).** `bloom_choir`'s swarm (Mission 8) and Mission 12's own hostile composition can concentrate 60+ damage from 2-4 simultaneous "pack"-intelligence attackers onto a single target in one hostile phase, with `moveRange`+`attackRange` threat bubbles large enough that threshold-based *reactive* retreat (fall back once already hurt) can't reliably outrun them — the softest unit on the field dies to alpha-strike burst before a same-turn or next-turn retreat can matter, and once that's the Munti, the rest of the squad grinds down unhealed regardless of anyone's positioning. The commander/Munti front-line cap (above) measurably helps — Mission 8 traces now run to turn 8 instead of turn 4 before the commander goes down — but doesn't fully close this, because the real lever (predicting the swarm's threat range before committing to a position, or deliberately baiting/screening with a tankier unit first) is exactly the kind of forward-looking tactical judgment this file's own header has flagged as out of scope since the 25 Aug restructure ("Bait is NOT this pass... deliberately exposing a unit and predicting how the hostile AI will respond... worth doing right, not worth guessing at"). Missions 8 and 12 still don't have a trustworthy batch-validated win rate; unlike before, that's now a diagnosed and documented gap rather than an unexplained one. Mission 36's own dedicated seeded-harness investigation traced its sole loss (out of 100 seeds) to this identical ceiling — the first confirmed case of it surfacing outside Missions 8/12.

  **A direct seeded re-check of Missions 8 and 12 themselves, same night, same technique (mulberry32, 100 distinct seeds each) — the "full re-validation, larger N, ideally seeded" follow-up flagged repeatedly above, run for these two specifically.** The two missions turn out NOT to be the same shape of problem. **Mission 8 is an absolute wall: 0/100, every single seed ending in `commander_down`**, turns 5 through 100 (the long tail is a genuine losing stalemate that drags on, not a fast wipe every time) — no seed in this sample beats it, so this isn't sampling noise landing on a bad run, it's a hard ceiling under the current test AI. **Mission 12 is NOT absolute the same way: 10/100 (10%)**, with 87 `commander_down` and 3 outright `loss` outcomes making up the other 90 — a real, if low, win rate, not just an unexplained low number. Both missions now have a trustworthy, reproducible figure for the first time since this ceiling was first flagged; neither number is a balance target to hit on its own — closing the ceiling for real still needs the same predictive-threat-avoidance engine work described above, not a stat tweak on either mission.
