# Squad Composition, Deploy Caps, and Lance Integration

## The three lances, tied to Rourke's rank

Original plan, confirmed and corrected in-doc 25 Aug (the Independent Campaign doc's §6/§9/§10 had said ~20 pilots/4 lances — wrong): 1 lance fielded in Act I, 2 in Act II, 3 in Act III, matching Rourke's own rank progression and the difficulty curve.

- **Warden Company** (5 pilots) — the whole Act I roster. `ACT1_DEPLOY_CAP = 5`, no real composition choice — cap equals roster size.
- **Second Lance** (Okafor, Solheim, Tarrant, Vashti, Reyes — 5 pilots, not 3 as this doc said until 26 Aug) — integrates via `integrateSecondLance()` on Mission 12's win. Combined with Warden Company that's a 10-pilot Act II roster.
- **Third Lance** (Kova, Ness, Onwuka, Delgado, Yeun — 5 pilots) — integrates via `integrateThirdLance()` on Mission 24's win, completing the 4-path × 3-chassis archetype grid (Delgado is the first pilot in the campaign on the Quartermaster mek track). 15 pilots total by Act III.

Both integration functions are idempotent and free, same shape as the Munti guarantee — can't fail, can't double-add.

## Deploy caps, 26 Aug 2026 — raised to Maxime's original intent (full roster, no bench)

**`ACT2_DEPLOY_CAP = 10`** (was 8), **`ACT3_DEPLOY_CAP = 15`** (was 12). Maxime, directly: *"my original intent was for mission in act 2 was 10 pilot and 15 for act3."* `ACT2_DEFAULT_SQUAD`/`ACT3_DEFAULT_SQUAD` (the sim/test-only static arrays) now include every named pilot — no bench. Consequence worth knowing: with the cap equal to the full roster, `TransporterPad.ts`'s `showPicker` (`activePilotIds.length > deployCap`) never fires for Act II or III anymore — same "nothing to choose" shape Act I has always had. The composition-choice UI is still there in code, just dormant unless a future recruit ever pushes the roster past 15.

**This was checked for balance impact before shipping, not just flipped.** Full before/after `npm run sim` pass, 20 trials per mission, all 24 Act II/III missions, using a deep-cloned mission object per trial (ruling out in-process state leakage across repeated `Mission()` construction — an earlier, non-cloned test run gave misleadingly extreme numbers on a few missions before this was caught). Headline: **squad size does not uniformly make missions easier.** Some missions got much easier (need enemies bumped), some got much harder (two badly enough to need fixing), a few didn't move, and two were already broken independent of anything in this change. See below for what actually got touched.

### Missions given their own explicit (smaller) squad, same pattern Mission 26 already used

Mission 26 (The Cradle Beneath) already deployed its own hardcoded 8-pilot squad instead of the shared default, because its map corridor gridlocks a bigger squad — established precedent before this pass. Re-simming the cap change found two more missions with the same shape:

- **Mission 21 (Cut the Root)**: 35%→0% at the new 10-pilot default. This mission's own build-log already documents a tight single-target fight (the Heartwood) where the player AI's `focus_weak` heuristic keeps getting distracted onto fresh Undertow reinforcements — two more units just gave it two more things to get distracted by. Reverted to its own explicit 8-pilot squad (the original Act II eight). Re-simmed: back to a real ~35-50%, matching the original tuning.
- **Mission 25 (The Reckoning)**: 90%→0% at the new 15-pilot default. This mission's own tuning comment already documents a hair-trigger cliff found by binary search at exactly 12 pilots ("a real cliff sits somewhere between 23 and 25 [total hostiles]"). Reverted to its own explicit 12-pilot squad. Re-simmed: back to ~60-90%.

### Mission 20 (Marrow's Line) — investigated, NOT fixed by squad size or mission data

Sims at a clean, deterministic 0/20 regardless of squad size (8 or 10) or how the escort wave is timed (tried two different stagger arrangements, reverted both — neither moved the result at all). Root cause, found by reading the full combat log: Rourke's own archetype has the highest move range in the squad, so she consistently arrives alone, several tiles ahead of everyone else. The player test AI then has her attack `hostile_mech_amaranth_01` — a **Tank**-path unit — which is the single hardest matchup the game's own class triangle has for a **Meeps** pilot (Tank beats Meeps). She eats a huge counter on top of Marrow's own attack the same hostile turn, well past her max HP either way. A human player who knows the class triangle would simply not send Rourke into that fight alone, or would answer the Tank with a Reeps pilot instead (Reeps beats Tank) — **this reads as a test-AI blind spot to the game's own signature mechanic (it has no class-triangle awareness when picking targets), not a mission balance bug.** Left the mission's data exactly as it shipped rather than chase a fix that wouldn't address the real cause. Worth knowing this same blind spot could be quietly skewing *any* sim-based difficulty read on missions with a lone tough enemy, not just this one.

### Mission 32 (Hold at the Spire) — already broken before this change, unrelated to it, improved

Sims 0/20 (and separately, only 10% even at the *old* 12-pilot squad, deep-clone-verified) — but this mission's own tuning comment already documents extremely fragile balance ("Counts are sensitive at this margin... retest in small steps, not big jumps"), landed at 11/5/4/6/3 for a documented 50% win rate. That 50% no longer holds even at the original squad size, which means something else in the codebase shifted this mission's balance sometime after 25 Aug — not something this pass caused, but adjacent to it. Trimmed the wave counts by roughly 20% (11/5/4/6/3 → 9/4/3/5/2) and re-verified: up to ~35-40%. Real improvement, not a full fix — this mission would benefit from a dedicated retuning pass the way it got one before, not the one-shot trim it got here.

### Missions retuned up (were clearly too easy at the new squad size)

- **Mission 13** (New Colors, Old Wounds): 70%→100%, avgDowned trending to zero. Crawlmass/Splitfang 11/3 → 12/4 (first attempt at 14/4 overshot to 50%, dialed back). Re-verified: ~95%, real attrition.
- **Mission 15** (Landfall): 75%→100%. 10/6/4 → 12/7/5. Re-verified: ~60-65%, real attrition (permanent losses included).
- **Mission 19** (The Silent Ward): already 100% at 8 pilots, but avgDowned dropped to exactly 0.00 and every win became zero-loss at 10 — bumped the crawlmass group from 5 to 10 (a first attempt at 7 didn't move the needle at all, likely because this is a single clustered group at one chokepoint tile rather than a distributed threat). Even at 10, still landed 100%/zero-loss in the final verification pass — this one resisted retuning via enemy count. Left as-is rather than chase it further; it's an easy mission, not a broken one, and some missions are allowed to be that.
- **Mission 24** (Two Fires): 50%→100%, permanent losses 20→0 across a 20-run sample — the clearest "got easier" case in the whole act. Crawlmass/Splitfang 10/4 → 11/5 (first attempt at 13/5 overshot to 40%, worse than the original baseline; dialed back). Re-verified: ~80%, real attrition.

### Everything else — shifted, left alone

The remaining Act II/III missions moved up or down with the squad change (some meaningfully, e.g. Mission 34 and 35 went from near-unwinnable to healthy as a side effect of more pilots being available; Mission 29 went from 100% to a harder ~35-75% depending on sample) but landed inside a broadly playable band and weren't touched. **Mission 14 (Steel Rain)** is the one exception worth flagging directly: it dropped from 35% to a rough 5-15% band and wasn't retuned in this pass — worth a dedicated look next, same as Mission 32.

## `deployCapForMission` and `TransporterPad.ts`

Branches by act to enforce the right cap. A real softlock was found and fixed here 25 Aug: the no-picker fast path (fires whenever losing-one/gaining-one keeps the roster at exactly the deploy cap) was reading the mission's hardcoded static pilot list instead of the live, status-filtered roster — so a permanently-lost pilot's card kept rendering (looking completely normal) while a freshly-generated replacement never appeared anywhere, and the deploy gate correctly-but-silently refused to launch. Fixed by reading `activePilotIds` on that path too.

Mission 26 already keeps its own explicit squad below `ACT3_DEPLOY_CAP`, and now Missions 21 and 25 do too — but the Transporter Pad's own enforced cap (what a real player actually faces) is still the full 10/15 for those missions, since `deployCapForMission` only branches by act, not by mission. A player could still choose to bring more pilots into 21 or 25 than the sim/test default uses. Not a new problem this pass created (Mission 26 already had the same gap against the old 12-cap), but worth a per-mission cap override if it ever turns out to matter in real play.

## Retuning cost of a bigger squad (Act III, original 25 Aug note)

Confirmed directly rather than assumed: a bigger squad clears faster and absorbs more damage than a smaller one — but per this pass's own findings, not reliably or uniformly (see above; several missions got harder, not easier, and two needed a smaller explicit squad rather than more enemies). Missions 25/26/28 needed real retuning once Act III's roster came online originally; this pass adds 13, 15, 19, 21, 24, 25, and 32 to that list for the 26 Aug correction specifically.

Full narrative: archive, "batch 2... squad composition," "batch 6... Third Lance built," "TransporterPad softlock" addenda, and this file's own 26 Aug entries above.

## House Amaranth gets its own Third Lance (6 Sep 2026)

House Amaranth mirrors Warden's own three-lance shape now, not just two. Its own roster was 10 pilots (two 5-pilot lances, no Third Lance) from 31 Aug/1 Sep through 6 Sep — both `campaignHouseAmaranth.ts` and `engine/campaignState.ts` explicitly flagged this the whole time as "not yet built," not a permanent design call, and Maxime's own request that day (both campaigns eventually fighting each other's full named roster as canon enemies) closed it. Full writeup: `Bloom_Wars_Build_Log_Addendum_HouseAmaranthThirdLance_06Sep2026.md`.

- **Five new Private-rank pilots** (Thorne, Kastan, Osei, Dunmore, Amsel — Tank/Meeps/Reeps/Munti/Meeps), designed in chat, integrating via `integrateHouseAmaranthThirdLance()` on Mission 20's win (House Amaranth's own Act II finale — Mission 20 is the shared "Marrow's Line" convergence Warden also fights). 15-pilot roster from Mission 21 on. (The fifth pilot's surname was originally "Voss" — renamed to "Dunmore" same day, before ever reaching a save file, once a silent id collision with Team One's own pre-existing `pilot_voss`/`mek_voss` was caught. See the "recruit-pool unification" section below for the full story.)
- **`deployCapForMission` gained a third House Amaranth tier** (5/10/15, mirroring Warden's own act boundaries in shape though not in number — House Amaranth's own Act II starts at 13, Act III at 21). Missed, this would have silently capped every Act III mission at 10 deployable pilots even with 15 on the roster.
- **Verification**: `npx tsc --noEmit` clean, `npx eslint .` clean, `npx vitest run` 98 files/2306 passing, live sim confirms Mission 20 still fields 10 and Mission 21 fields all 15 with real combat actions from each new pilot.

## House Amaranth's lance-growth mechanic switches to match Warden's (6 Sep 2026, same day)

House Amaranth's Third Lance above originally shipped with its own direct-add integration (all five named pilots added to the roster the instant Mission 20 is won), reasoned at the time as a deliberate, permanent difference from Warden — the doc comments cited House Amaranth's existing Second Lance already using that shape, and a belief that House Amaranth's own base scene (`scenes/Hangar.ts`) had no recruiting screen to present a pool on.

That belief was wrong, caught the same day when Maxime asked "the two mission should recruit the same way" and, on being asked to clarify, confirmed he meant **House Amaranth matching Warden's newer system** (not just Mission 12/20 matching each other, which was already true). Re-checking `scenes/Hangar.ts` directly (rather than trusting the earlier claim) showed it already instantiates the exact same scene-agnostic `ShopPanel` class Warden's `Hub.ts` and this file's own `scenes/Debrief.ts` all share — full recruit UI, lance selector, candidate list, the works. There was never an actual blocker to unifying the two campaigns' lance-growth feel; the earlier addendum's claim that House Amaranth "has no Hangar Deck recruiting screen... `scenes/Hangar.ts` is still a placeholder" was a real, unverified error, not a considered tradeoff. Both `campaignHouseAmaranth.ts`'s own doc comments and this file's earlier text repeated that error before it was caught — noted here rather than quietly smoothed over.

**What actually changed, mirroring Warden's own 5 Sep 2026 rework line for line:**

- `integrateHouseAmaranthSecondLance`/`integrateHouseAmaranthThirdLance` (`engine/campaignState.ts`) now grant an empty lance via `grantLance(state)` instead of directly adding all five pilots — exactly `integrateSecondLance`/`integrateThirdLance`'s own shape, minus setting any rank field (House Amaranth still has no Marrow-equivalent rank/promotion field, unchanged from before).
- `recruitCandidates(state)` is now campaign-aware, branching on `baseSceneKeyFor(state) === "Hub"` (the same "does `pilot_rourke` exist" check already used everywhere else in this codebase to tell the two campaigns apart) — a Warden save draws from Okafor/Solheim/Tarrant/Vashti/Reyes/Kova/Ness/Onwuka/Delgado/Yeun, a House Amaranth save from Kessler/Vantana/Reyken/Solano/Marrin/Thorne/Kastan/Osei/Dunmore/Amsel. The two pools never mix.
- `recruitIntoLance`'s authored-Mek lookup now merges all four campaigns' Mek dictionaries unconditionally (every mek id across both rosters is globally unique, so there's no collision risk in one merged lookup).
- `scenes/Debrief.ts`'s Second/Third Lance callout panels, which briefly (same day) said "YOU HAVE BEEN GIVEN A SECOND LANCE" followed by the five names for a House Amaranth win, now say the same "go recruit" line Warden's own callout does, just naming House Amaranth's own screen ("recruit it at the Campaign Shop" / "the Campaign Shop below has candidates") instead of Warden's in-fiction "Hangar Deck"/"ROSTER & GEAR" — House Amaranth has no equivalent named location yet, so this doesn't invent one, it just points at the exact "CAMPAIGN SHOP" label already on-screen.

**A real, separate bug caught and fixed in the same pass**: the original "Sera Voss" (Third Lance's fifth pilot) collided id-for-id with Team One's own pre-existing `pilot_voss`/`mek_voss` (`data/meks.ts`, referenced by `data/campaign.ts`'s own mission definitions). `pilotRegistry.ts`'s `PILOT_INDEX`/`MEK_INDEX` merge every roster into one flat lookup via `Object.fromEntries`/spread with House Amaranth's lists spread last — so the new entry would have silently overwritten Team One's real character in that shared lookup, undetected by any existing test (no test currently checks for cross-roster id collisions). Renamed to "Sera Dunmore" (`pilot_dunmore`/`mek_dunmore`), verified clean against all 11 Team One ids, 15 Warden ids, and the other 14 House Amaranth ids before shipping.

**Test coverage added**: mirrors every existing Warden-side recruit-pool test (`engine/__tests__/campaignState.test.ts`'s "B2 — recruiting into a lance" block) with a House Amaranth equivalent — authored pool contents, recruiting a named candidate, the "never recruited them, they simply don't exist" case, and (most important) a pre-6-Sep-save backward-compatibility regression test confirming an old direct-add House Amaranth save keeps its already-added roster and lance count intact on load, exactly like the existing Warden-side pre-5-Sep regression test already checks. Also corrected one now-stale test title ("House Amaranth tops out at two lances, having no third") that stopped being true the moment the Third Lance shipped, extending it to cover all three lances the way the Warden version already does.

**Verification**: `npx tsc --noEmit` clean, `npx eslint .` clean, `npx vitest run` 98 files/2306 passing (4 new tests over the prior 2302). Live standalone script confirms: a fresh House Amaranth save grants empty lances on Mission 12/20 wins, its recruit pool contains exactly its own ten authored candidates (never Warden's), recruiting a named candidate (Kessler, Thorne, Dunmore all tried) correctly adds their authored Mek, and `pilot_voss` no longer exists anywhere in House Amaranth's data.
