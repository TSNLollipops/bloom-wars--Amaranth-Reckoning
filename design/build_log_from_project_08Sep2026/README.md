# Bloom Wars — Build Log Index

**What this is.** The Amaranth Reckoning's build history, split for navigability: one file per mission (grouped by act), plus one file per cross-cutting engine system that isn't about any single mission. Each file is a concise synthesis — spec table, what actually happened (bugs found, design calls made), and the final sim-tuned numbers — not a verbatim copy of the original addenda. Full blow-by-blow narrative (exact playtest quotes, full bug traces, every intermediate tuning attempt) lives in the archive; every mission/system file below points at the archive section that covers it.

**Why split.** The original running build log (`claude/Bloom_Wars_Amaranth_Act1_Build_Log_v1.md`) grew to ~40 addenda across 36 missions and is now too large to safely rewrite in one pass (Project docs have no in-place patch — a full rewrite is the only edit, and at this size that's a real risk of truncating real history). Splitting by act/mission/system keeps each file small, means updating one mission doesn't touch 35 others, and makes "what's the state of Mission 17" a one-file lookup instead of a search through a 500KB document.

**The archive.** `claude/Bloom_Wars_Amaranth_Act1_Build_Log_v1.md` is preserved exactly as it was — the complete, unedited chronological narrative through Batch 7 (campaign complete at 36/36) plus the Taunt/Fire Support/Player-AI/mission-clock/civilian-extraction passes. `claude/Bloom_Wars_Build_Log_Addendum_CommanderDown_25Aug2026.md` covers the one addendum written after that (the commander-down fix) — its content now also lives in `engine_systems/permadeath_and_commander_down.md` below. Neither archive file gets edited going forward; new work goes into this split structure instead.

**Note on delivery batching:** the archive's own commander-down addendum (`claude/Bloom_Wars_Build_Log_Addendum_CommanderDown_25Aug2026.md`) is superseded by `engine_systems/permadeath_and_commander_down.md` above — its content now lives here, in the split structure, going forward.

**Resolved 28 Aug 2026 (unattended overnight pass) — Wellroot boss fight (Mission 21) balance regression, shipped at `attackPower: 50`.** Was flagged here as needing Maxime's call after wiring `fx_acid_dot` for real collapsed this fight's win rate to 0/40 across two batches, with `attackPower` unchanged at the shipped 60 (documented target ~35%). A candidate fix (cutting to 20) had been validated at 30-35% but deliberately not shipped, since that's exactly the kind of balance call this project reserves for Maxime. Two things happened before it got decided: a same-night, unrelated Player AI fix (see below) moved this mission from 0/70 to roughly 25-45% at the unchanged `attackPower: 60` without touching this stat at all, meaning the `20` candidate had only ever been validated against a worse test AI; and Maxime, walked through the full evidence, gave explicit latitude to make the final call — **"you got the latitude to fix thing once knowledge been learned."**

Re-ran the batch validation (N=100 per point, against the fixed test AI) across the full range rather than trusting either old number: `20`→~48% (overshoots), `30`→11%, `40`→0-2%, `45`→1%, `48`→14%, `50`→35%/36% (two independent batches, tight agreement), `52`→21%, `55`→32%, `60`→23%/28% (the old number), `70`→13%. **This is a genuinely chaotic, non-monotonic landscape** — `45` and `52` both score worse than their neighbors on both sides, and `60` beats `40` despite less damage per hit — best explained as `attackPower` crossing discrete "hits to down a unit" breakpoints rather than moving along a smooth curve, the same knife-edge shape Mission 36 shows on a different knob (enemy count). Shipped at `attackPower: 50` — the only value tested that lands close to target with two batches actually agreeing with each other. Flagged as not provably stable long-term given how jumpy its neighbors are — a future stat/roster/AI change here should re-sweep, not assume. Full account: `data/bloom.ts`'s `bloom_wellroot` comment and `act2/mission21_cut_the_root.md`.

**Resolved (checked) 28 Aug 2026 — the other five wired archetypes.** A follow-up seeded batch A/B pass checked all five (Gallcyst, Sirenmaw, Choir, Heartwood, the Unnamed) against their missions (9, 35, 36, 14, 30 — Heartwood carries no live mission exposure today). None come close to Wellroot's collapse: Mission 9 drops meaningfully (90%→77%), Missions 35 and 36 are flat (their archetype's knockback effect carries no damage), and Missions 14 and 30 counter-intuitively rise (17%→40%, 37%→57%), plausibly because the acid DoT's bloom_mat tile conversion damages any unit standing on it regardless of side, occasionally hurting the hostiles themselves. Read as: modest and mixed, nothing else at Wellroot's scale, no other number currently needs Maxime's sign-off.

**New, found 28 Aug 2026, informational — Mission 9's batch drop doesn't hold up under hand-played testing.** A manual playtest (hold the deploy corner, never end a move within either fixed Gallcyst turret's 3-tile attack range) won Mission 9 clean on turn 10, squad barely scratched, acid DoT never landing once. Points at the same root cause as the finding directly below from a different angle: the test/batch Player AI's own aggressive play, not the Bloom balance numbers, is the likely explanation for the 90%→77% drop the five-archetype validation measured here. No number needs changing; noted so a future batch result on this or a similar map gets checked against a human playthrough before assuming a design problem.

**Resolved 28 Aug 2026 (unattended overnight pass) — Player AI test harness had no Commander-protection logic.** Previously flagged here as a new open item after Missions 8 and 12 both came back at a flat 0% win rate in every condition tested (effects on or off, not a regression from that week's work) — verbose traces showed the mission always ending in `RESULT: COMMANDER_DOWN`, because nothing in `sim/playerAi`'s heuristic treated Rourke (the Commander — her death alone ends the mission) or the squad's Munti (the sole source of repair, and the live permadeath trigger once lost) as needing more caution than an ordinary pilot. Fixed with two mechanisms in `combat.ts`/`index.ts`: a front-line cap (`commanderSafePathPrefix` — a protected unit never advances past whoever's already the most-exposed living ally) and a raised retreat/regroup threshold (`COMMANDER_RETREAT_HP_FRACTION = 0.5` vs. the normal 0.3 for everyone else), both gated by the same data-driven `PilotRecord.exemptFromPermadeath` flag `mission.ts` itself already uses for the commander, plus `unit.path === "munti"`.

Both mechanisms are switched off entirely on `extract_unit` missions — found via a real regression during verification, not guessed. Mission 11 ("The Long Walk Back") dropped from a 97% baseline to as low as 20%: its own turn limit is razor-thin (win completes turn 18, loss hits the limit turn 19), and extra caution anywhere in the squad — the named extract target detouring, or an escort stopping to regroup/retreat sooner than before — cost just enough wasted movement to blow it. A narrower first attempt (exempt only the named extract target) wasn't sufficient; the fix that actually held is switching the whole `extract_unit` objective type off rather than chasing it unit-by-unit.

Verified against a full-campaign diagnostic sweep (`npm run sim`-style batch runs, N=20/mission, unseeded — a regression/sanity check, explicitly **not** a `combat_sim.py`-grade balance revalidation): most combat missions improved, several substantially (Mission 1 5%→40%, Mission 9 90%→100%, Mission 14 37%→100%, Mission 30 43%→80%), and all three `extract_unit` missions (5, 11, 31) confirmed unchanged from their own baselines after the exemption. Full vitest suite (996/996) unaffected throughout. **This is test-only** — `sim/playerAi` is never imported by `mission.ts`/`Battle.ts`, so no shipped game balance changed tonight.

It does mean most of this campaign's previously-recorded batch win-rate numbers were measured against a less-competent test AI than now exists, and are stale pending a full re-validation (larger N, ideally seeded — flagged as its own real follow-up task, not done tonight). One number moved enough to flag specifically, cross-referencing the still-open Wellroot item above: **Mission 21 went from a rock-solid 0/70 (three independent baseline batches) to roughly 25-45% across two post-fix batches, without `attackPower` changing at all.** This doesn't resolve whether to ship the validated `attackPower: 20` candidate — it's new evidence to weigh before that call, not a reason to make it here.

Missions 8 and 12 remain very low even with this fix, but that's now a diagnosed ceiling rather than an unexplained gap (full detail in `engine_systems/player_ai_engine.md`): `bloom_choir`'s pack-swarm and Mission 12's own hostile composition can alpha-strike 60+ damage onto a single target in one hostile phase, faster than threshold-based *reactive* retreat can outrun — closing it for real needs genuinely predictive threat avoidance (deliberate bait/screening before committing to a position), which this file has flagged as out of scope since the 25 Aug restructure, not more front-line caution. **A same-night seeded re-check (100 distinct seeds each) turned "0%/5%" into two real, trustworthy numbers: Mission 8 is an absolute wall, 0/100; Mission 12 is not, 10/100 (10%) — see `engine_systems/player_ai_engine.md`.** Missions 3 and 5 (already known to go 0% under the bot pre-fix, cleared fine by Maxime playing live) stayed at 0% after this fix too — confirmed pre-existing against the same baseline, not a new regression.

**Resolved 28 Aug 2026 (unattended overnight pass, same night as the two items above) — Munti repair range raised 1→3 tiles, campaign-wide, and a matching dead-code bug fixed in the same pass.** Maxime: "give more range to munty heal," narrowed via clarifying questions to **"Base range, everyone (1→3)"** — every Munti's baseline, no-purchase-required repair range (`DEFAULT_REPAIR_RANGE`, `data/weaponBranches.ts`) goes from 1 tile to 3, for every squad, no purchase required.

Bumping that one constant alone would have shipped a silent no-op: `engine/mission.ts`'s `getRepairableFrom()` — the function `Battle.ts`'s UI actually highlights repair targets from — was hardcoded to `chebyshevDistance === 1` regardless of the constant, so a real player would never have seen the extra range on screen. `sim/playerAi/support.ts` (this campaign's test/batch AI) had the identical hardcoded-adjacency limit in its own repair search, documented in its own header as a *deliberate* match to the first function's behavior — which made it a real bug the moment that behavior changed, not a separate design choice. Both fixed in the same pass. `RAPID_RESPONSE_REPAIR_RANGE` (the Munti's Weapon Branch purchase, previously a flat `2`) now derives as `DEFAULT_REPAIR_RANGE + 1` (→ `4`), so the paid branch keeps a real edge over the new free baseline instead of becoming strictly worse than it, and can't go stale the same way again if the baseline ever moves.

Still heal-in-place only, no pathing toward an out-of-range ally — unchanged design, see `support.ts`'s own header. Verified: `tsc`/`eslint` clean, full vitest suite green (1004/1004 — 2 renamed + 4 new regression tests), `npm run build` clean. Diagnostic re-sweep (N=20/mission, unseeded, informational — same convention as the commander-protection sweep above): Missions 8/12/3 unmoved at 0%, exactly as expected since more heal range doesn't help against alpha-strike damage that outpaces any heal; Mission 21 (Wellroot, `attackPower: 50` — see the item above) landed 8/20 (40%), consistent with that decision's own 35-36% figure; most other missions held steady within ordinary N=20 noise. **One standout, flagged rather than smoothed over: Mission 36 ("Until Relief") came back 19/20 (95%)** — above the 29-73% spread that mission's own file already had on record as unexplained before tonight. Could be that same instability landing at the high end of a wider true range, or a real effect of more healing in a `survive_n_turns` fight; this one N=20 batch can't tell which, and isn't treated as resolved. Full account: `engine_systems/player_ai_engine.md`.

**Resolved for real, same night — a dedicated seeded-harness investigation (`act3/mission36_until_relief.md`) confirms it.** Grepped first, not assumed: the only randomness source in a Mission battle sim is the Meeps dodge roll, so a seeded PRNG (mulberry32) makes an entire run reproducible. A determinism check (5 seeds × 3 repeats each) came back a perfect match every time, directly ruling out this file's own "tile-scoring ties broken by iteration order" hypothesis. A 100-distinct-seed win-rate sweep landed 99/100 (99%) — the one loss (seed 92) traced turn by turn to the exact same already-documented ceiling behind Missions 8/12 (no predictive threat avoidance against a same-turn alpha-strike on the commander), not a new or mysterious cause. Whether the *original* 29-73% instability was the repair-range bump itself, the two dead-code bugs fixed in the same pass, or some mix of the three still can't be proven apart — all three shipped together — but the post-fix state is no longer unstable or unexplained, and the number is now trustworthy enough to weigh the near-100%-win-rate campaign-finale pacing question on.
