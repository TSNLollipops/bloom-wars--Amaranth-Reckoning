## Method

No Python balance/map source files exist anywhere in the staged repo tree (`combat_sim.py`/`maps.py`, the project's nominal source of truth) — consistent with every prior session's own build-log record, this pass worked by hand-editing `campaignAmaranth.ts` directly and verifying via `npx tsx src/sim/runBatch.ts`. Each mission was isolated, sim-tested at increasing sample sizes as the composition converged (starting ~60-150, confirmed at 200-500 for anything landing close to the 15% line), and cross-checked with `npx tsx src/sim/run.ts <id>`'s verbose single-run log whenever an aggregate number alone didn't explain what was actually happening on the board.

## First batch: Missions 1, 2, 7, 9, 11

**Mission 1, "Muster" (`eliminate_all`).** Was a single wave of 11 Crawlmass. **Discovered and confirmed a spawn-tile-overflow cliff independent of this mission's own tuning:** `map_amaranth_muster` has exactly 3 "enemy_deploy" tiles; `engine/mission.ts`'s `spawnWavesForTurn()` cycles `i % spots.length` across them, so a wave count that divides evenly (12 = 4 per tile) places cleanly, while one unit over (13) pushes the last unit through `findFreeAdjacent()`'s BFS fan-out onto a materially worse tile — confirmed directly: 12 = 95% win, 13 = 0% win, same composition otherwise. Retuned to a 3-wave escalation, each wave sized to stay on the safe side of that cliff: 12 (turn 1), 11 (turn 2), 7 (turn 4). **Result: 12.9% pooled (168/1300 across two batches), all COMMANDER_DOWN, zero real LOSS.**

**Mission 2, "Wire and Mud" (`hold_zone`).** A true single-doorway sealed room — verbose traces confirmed only ONE melee attacker can ever engage per turn through the doorway regardless of total hostile headcount (tested up to 20 Splitfang + 8 Sporethrower via "enemy_deploy" with zero measurable effect on win rate). Pure count/timing tuning cannot touch this mission at all. **Fixed with a positional exploit instead of a count change:** kept the original Splitfang waves (6 at turn 1, 6 at turn 3) untouched, and replaced the existing, ineffective 2-count Sporethrower wave with explicit `spawnAt` coordinates on the WEST side of the sealed wall — 3 Sporethrower at turn 1, 1 more at turn 2. Sporethrower's `attackRange: [2,3]` (it cannot hit adjacent targets) means it never needs to enter or path around the chokepoint at all; staged just outside the wall, it pressures the doorway from a position door-plugging can't block. **Result: 10% (52/500), 447 COMMANDER_DOWN, 1 real LOSS.**

**Mission 7, "Sporewatch Ridge" (`hold_zone`, open ridge terrain — no true chokepoint anywhere on the map).** Moved a dead-weight turn-6 Splitfang wave (count 2) that resolved AFTER the mission's own win-check timing (hold_zone resolves at the start of the `holdUntilTurn` player phase, before that turn's hostile phase runs — so a wave arriving exactly at `holdUntilTurn` never mattered, confirmed from the original design's own history) forward to turn 4 at an increased count of 7, and bumped the existing turn-3 Undertow wave from 2 to 4. **Result: 30% (148/500) — flagged honestly as NOT fully compliant with the 15% ceiling.** Exhaustively tested further escalation past this point (single-lever isolation, then paired combinations, including splitting pressure across two flanks): every attempt either crashed outright to a 0% rout with heavy real-squad-wipe LOSS, or reached 12-13% compliance at the cost of a 32-39% real-LOSS rate — a worse mission than the original 95%-win baseline, not a better one. Judged this mission to have a genuine structural ~30% floor given its open terrain and the archetypes available, and made the deliberate call to leave it honestly non-compliant rather than force compliance at the cost of an unacceptable rout — the same judgment call precedent House Amaranth's own Mission 20 addendum already established ("comfortably under the ceiling without tipping into a rout the other way").

**Mission 9, "Cut Off" (`survive_n_turns`).** Crawlmass 10→13 (turn 1), Undertow 2→4 (turn 5), plus one new single-unit Crawlmass reinforcement at turn 3. **Result: 5% (26/500), all COMMANDER_DOWN, zero real LOSS** — a clean profile, and a reminder that on this objective type (no single point of failure, no chokepoint to defend) even a single added unit can move the number substantially.

**Mission 11, "The Long Walk Back" (`extract_unit`, single named target `pilot_lask`).** This objective type has a now-repeatedly-confirmed campaign-wide fragility pattern: single-named-target loss conditions punish added damage-per-turn far harder than `eliminate_all`'s margin implies, and ranged/sniper additions in particular tend to introduce a catastrophic new real-squad-wipe LOSS mode rather than a smooth gradient (already documented in this mission's own history, and independently rediscovered on Missions 17 and 26). Deliberately avoided Sporethrower this time — kept the turn-3 "pursuit" Crawlmass wave at its original count of 3 (tested up to 8; too volatile, too LOSS-heavy) and instead increased the turn-1 "bridge" Crawlmass wave from 2 to 5, a melee unit at a literal physical bridge chokepoint the whole squad must cross. **Result: pooled ~13-14% win across multiple batches (individual reads 12-18%), with a real LOSS component (~13%) alongside a COMMANDER_DOWN majority** — a genuine gradient (80%→36%→13% across the tested counts), not another cliff. Worth carrying forward: a melee lever at a real physical bottleneck behaves far more predictably on this objective type than a ranged lever does.

### Two new tuning techniques found this batch, worth carrying forward

**The "crowding paradox."** On certain chokepoint/narrow-corridor maps (confirmed on Missions 29 and 30, partially on 25 and 27), adding MORE hostiles at the same spawn points — or spreading the same total hostile count across MORE spawn points — can make a mission EASIER, not harder. The working theory is self-crowding/pathing congestion reducing the attackers' own effective throughput at the chokepoint. This directly contradicts the "more spawn tiles reduces crowding, therefore harder" assumption used in earlier sessions' work — the correct answer is mission-specific and needs re-verifying per map, never assumed from precedent.

**Burrowed-ambush-on-the-defended-tile.** For chokepoint maps where corridor headcount/timing changes are non-monotonic or simply ineffective (Missions 29, partially 30), placing burrowed ambush units (`bloom_undertow`, `attackRange [1,1]`) directly on or adjacent to the actual defended/held tiles bypasses the pathing/chokepoint problem entirely — they don't need to travel, they just surface and strike once a player unit is adjacent. This works because `engine/ai.ts`'s `isVisibleTo` vision/attack-range check is pure Chebyshev distance with no line-of-sight blocking (confirmed by reading the function directly) — walls only block movement, never vision or attack range, so a burrowed unit staged on the objective tile is a legal, undetectable threat until it strikes.

**Front-loading / wave-timing, confirmed again on Mission 30.** For `eliminate_all` missions (turnLimit is display-only, no timeout-loss branch, confirmed via a 25-turn verbose trace), a fight that naturally runs long past the nominal turn limit is decided by who wins the OPENING clash, not by late reinforcements arriving after the squad has already won the attrition war. Moving staggered later waves to all arrive at turn 1 together was the deciding lever on Mission 30 — the same wave-timing-over-headcount lesson House Amaranth's own build log already drew from its own Missions 22/26/27/28/29/30, now confirmed independently on Warden's side too.

**Gradual small-step tuning on known-sensitive missions (32, 34, 36).** All three carried their own pre-existing comments warning of extreme sensitivity to big jumps ("retest in small steps, not big jumps"). Stepping +1 unit or a small percentage at a time across every wave, rather than bisecting on big deltas, found real usable gradients on all three without triggering the catastrophic cliffs their own history warned about — and on Mission 36 specifically, tolerated a total headcount well past the old documented cliff point without breaking, confirming that cliff was specific to an exact composition split rather than a fixed total-headcount threshold.

**Baseline drift, confirmed again.** Missions 32, 34, and 36 all showed current baselines (97-100% win) sharply higher than their own historically-documented baselines (50-80%) — the same drift phenomenon already flagged for Mission 14 (below) and independently re-discovered on Mission 34's own new comment, attributed to accumulated AI/engine changes since those older figures were recorded.
