## 1. What's actually built (ground truth from the code, 29 Aug 2026)

This is far more than "Phase 1, a walkable Rec Room." As of the last commit to `Hub.ts`:

- **8 walkable rooms across 3 decks**: Rec Room, Hangar Deck, Workshop, Vault, Berths, CIC/Bridge, the Grotto (alone on the middle deck), and a new Spar Room — room-to-room doors, stairs, and a per-deck grid. Per Antfarm Grid v1 (now confirmed to exist, see §0): 3 decks is fixed by design, intra-deck movement is open-floor with only stairs/bay as real portals, and the Grotto shipped an "egg-hull" pass (a real bigger ellipse floor) the same day the other two decks got enlarged outer bounds.
- **The full verb framework** from the original plan's §3 is built: Talk (sound-range broadcast), Ask Out (`romance.ts`, with romanceable-species gating), Share a Drink (drunk debuff, now with a real expiry/persistence), and all **three** Rec Room minigames — The Peg Board, Poker (real Texas Hold'em, `engine/holdem.ts` on top of a generic card-table engine), and Fletchers/darts — plus Anger Blowup and Breakdown as their own verb-driven crisis events.

## 2. What "ready for early access" means for this pass

Maxime's call: finish the real remaining Hub-plan pieces, not just harden what exists. Given §1's actual state, that means this plan has three kinds of work, in order of how much they block a release versus how much they grow it:

- **§3 — Won't ship honestly without this.** Cleanup of dev-only artifacts players would currently see, the one known layout bug, and — the load-bearing one — actually verifying the whole assembled system together, since evidence suggests no one has done that since the Mek NPC pass landed.
- **§4 — The calendar economy.** A locked design decision that never got built. Flagged on its own because it's a real feature gap, not a bug, and touches every verb.
- **§5 — The remaining room mechanics.** Vault dedication and CIC (Spar Room moved out — it's already built, see §0/§1's correction). Recommending a cut line here (below) rather than all-or-nothing.

## 3. Hardening — do this regardless of what else gets greenlit

1. **Strip player-visible dev labels.** The room title reads `THE ANTFARM — REC ROOM (PROTOTYPE)`; the on-screen control legend literally says `M = muster call (debug), R = test rumor (debug)`; the Favorability readout appends `(demo)` to every number. None of this should reach an early-access player. Proposed: drop "(PROTOTYPE)" and "(demo)" outright (the feature set in §1 is well past prototype); keep the M/R debug keys for continued dev use but gate them out of the production build (`import.meta.env.DEV` or similar) and strip them from the shown control legend rather than deleting the keys themselves.
2. **Verify (and if needed fix) the Iyari seat/bay-path collision.** Flagged 26 Aug and never fixed: her default seat sits almost on the straight-line path from spawn to the bay door, blocking a new player's first instinct with no visible reason why. Low-risk, needs a current look since rooms/seats have moved since (Mek NPCs, the Spar Room, the deck reorg).
3. **A real, whole-system verification pass — now with four more unverified changes stacked on top.** Every addendum so far verified its own slice (the minigames individually, roaming individually, Mek NPCs individually) — and two of those slices were built three times because a sandbox result never reached the device. Nothing on record has walked the entire assembled Hub in one pass since the last several features landed. As of this correction, that list now also includes the concurrent session's Tier 0–2 work (§1): Player AI target selection, all four muster/door/spawn fixes, and minigame room gating — all logic-traced and gate-clean (typecheck/test/lint) by their own addenda's honest account, **none click-tested in a live browser**. The full list this pass needs to actually walk: all 8 rooms, all 8 verbs, roaming/cliques, rumor propagation across rooms, Mek NPCs, muster from every deck (confirming Meks/CO don't answer it), door-clustering at a busy doorway, player spawn never landing on a parked NPC, each room-gated verb refusing a target standing just across a zone seam, save → reload → state-intact, and Hub → bay → MapSelect → back. Stage the full repo into a sandbox, run `typecheck`/`lint`/`test`/`build`, then a headless Playwright walkthrough of that full list, and commit back through the device bridge carefully (the exact failure mode that cost two rebuilds).
4. **Zero automated test coverage on `Hub.ts` itself.** Every other system in this codebase (combat, AI, campaign state, even the small `chatIntent.ts`) has a `__tests__` file; the 5,676-line scene that now carries most of the social-sim logic's orchestration has none — only manual/Playwright checks, addendum by addendum. Not proposing to unit-test Phaser rendering, but the pure logic currently living inline in the scene (propagation/distortion routing, roaming destination selection, breakdown/anger-blowup eligibility *as wired into the scene*, not just the already-tested `data/` modules) has no regression net. Worth at least flagging as tech debt even if it doesn't block this pass.
5. **HOW_TO_PLAY.html has zero mentions of the Hub.** It's all tactical-battle instructions. An early-access player reaching the Hub for the first time has no document and no in-scene tutorial beyond one line of on-screen hint text. Needs a section.

## 4. The calendar / cost economy — a locked decision that was never built

§4 of the original Hub plan locked this shape on 25 Aug: verbs cost in-fiction calendar time, itemized per verb (Talk near-free, Rec Room more, Berths romance the most), advanced only by verb-cost and mission completion — never real elapsed play time. It was never implemented; every verb today is free and repeatable forever, gated only by cooldowns. Two honest options, not resolved here:

- **Build it now**, as its own numbered pass (pick real per-verb day-costs, wire a calendar-day counter into `CampaignState`, gate verb availability/cost display in `Hub.ts`) — the real remaining-pieces work Maxime asked for.
- **Explicitly defer it past this EA pass** and say so in the build log, rather than let it keep quietly not existing. An early-access build that never implements a locked design decision isn't wrong, but it should be a decision, not an oversight that's now three sessions old.

Recommend deciding this one directly rather than me picking — it changes the shape of every verb's UI (a cost to show) and touches save data (a new persisted counter), which is more of a product call than the rest of this plan.

## 5. The remaining room mechanics — proposed cut line

Given the choice to finish remaining pieces rather than just harden, here's a concrete ranking, cheapest/highest-value first:

1. **The Vault's Heirloom dedication scene — now also the acquisition gate for Heirloom Pilots (§9).** Smallest footprint (one room, one hand-authored template per the Antfarm doc's own note — "whoever gets picked still gets a scene that reads like it was written for them," not fully procedural), and it's the one piece flagged as load-bearing rather than ambient. Highest narrative payoff for the cost. (Spar Room dropped off this list — it's already built, per §0's correction.)

Recommend scoping this as its own follow-up plan rather than folding it into this one, same discipline this project already uses for Carrier Defense Ops and the Major.

**Not recommending for this pass:** Carrier Defense Ops, the Major, in-mission comms, formal Insult/talk-down — all explicitly Phase 4+ since the original plan, genuinely new systems (a persistent antagonist, a combat-layer feature) rather than hub polish, and building them now would be the scope-creep this project's own docs have flagged against repeatedly.

## 8. New scope, added by Maxime 30 Aug — Meks as fully realized NPCs, not bond-stubs

Maxime's ask, verbatim: Meks should become "fully realised ant[s]" — NPCs with their own personality, real names, and backstory, the same depth the named pilot crew already has. Maxime's own later clarification, verbatim, and worth stating up front because it reframes the whole section: **"mek are exactly the same kind of npc as pilot. but they are the scientist and engeneer of the fleet."** Not a separate, lesser NPC category needing its own bespoke treatment — the same *kind* of character as a named pilot, same data shape, same depth; the only real difference is the in-fiction job (fleet scientist/engineer, not a combat pilot).

Checked directly against `types.ts` rather than assumed, and this clarification lines up exactly with a real gap in the actual code: **`PilotRecord`** (id, displayName, archetypeId, mekId, tier, plus the permadeath/weapon-branch fields) is the real shape a named pilot is built from. **`MekArchetype`** — confusingly the *old* meaning of "Mek," now the Loadout system — is a completely different shape with no personality fields at all (primary/secondary track, spareParts). The walkable Mek-as-person NPCs shipped 29 Aug don't have a `PilotRecord`-equivalent of their own; they're a `HubNpc` built on top of the Loadout's own `displayName` ("Rourke's Mek"), with a bond/catalyst bolted on separately. So Maxime's clarification isn't just framing — it names the actual fix: **Meks need their own small `PilotRecord`-shaped record (name, archetype/species, a role field distinguishing engineer/scientist from pilot, catalyst, tier-or-equivalent), decoupled from the Loadout's `MekArchetype`**, which stays exactly as it is, untouched. This also simplifies the Character Editor question from just above: since a Mek is pilot-shaped, not a new category, the editor's existing Layer 1/Layer 2 fields (chassis/species, name, portrait, animal-label) already cover a Mek natively once the editor itself exists — no separate Mek design needed there, just the same fields with "engineer/scientist" as the role instead of a combat path.

This is a real, currently-open gap, confirmed against the actual data files rather than assumed:

- **`src/data/meks.ts` is a different namespace entirely** — it's the Loadout system (the old "Mek" meaning, renamed to "Loadout" in UI per the Mek NPC Introduction Plan), pure stat objects (`primary`/`secondary` track, `spareParts`). It has no bearing on the walkable Mek NPCs and isn't where this work would land.
- **The walkable Mek NPCs have no individual name today.** Every one reads as `"<Pilot>'s Mek"` — a possessive label tied to the pilot they serve, not a character name of their own.
- **No backstory exists for any Mek**, named or not.
- **"Personality" is currently a mechanical stub, not authored content.** The 5 Act I Meks (Rourke's, Bosk's, Iyari's, Anand's, Lask's) have a seeded Matchset bond and 18 hot-topic lines — real, but thin. Every other Mek (recruits, Second/Third Lance, the roster bench) gets `catalystForPilot(mekId)` and a flat `{favorability: 0, stress: 10, morale: 70}` as of today's Tier 3 pass (confirmed live, see below) — a catalyst assignment exists purely so Grief Catalyst mourning has someone to attach a line to, not because any Mek-specific ambient/hot-topic content has been written for that catalyst yet.
- **Ground-truth check, just run**: a third concurrent-session addendum landed on the device while writing this (`Bloom_Wars_Build_Log_Addendum_Tier3RosterDrivenHubPopulation_30Aug2026.md`) — Tier 3 of the Consolidated Build Plan, roster-driven Hub population, is now also code-complete (Playwright-unverified, same caveat as Tiers 0–2). It's directly relevant here: it's what makes every active pilot's Mek walkable at all now, not just the original 5 — so this new scope item's real surface is now the full active roster, not a fixed five.

**What "fully realized" would concretely need**, scoped against the existing engine rather than invented fresh:
1. **Real individual names.** A proper character name per Mek, independent of their pilot — `meks.ts`'s own header already points at the source to draw from (`Qiraki_Character_Sheets_v5.md` and the other Qiraki canon files the 5 named pilots were transcribed from), so this is plausibly a lookup/transcription pass, not invention from scratch, for the Act I five at least.
2. **A short hand-authored backstory per named Mek** — same "whoever gets picked still gets a scene that reads like it was written for them" standard the Vault dedication (§5) is held to, not a procedural generator.
3. **Real personality content on top of the catalyst they already mechanically have** — ambient lines, hot topics, and relationship-stage content written for Meks specifically, the same treatment the named pilot crew gets, so the catalyst value means something a player can actually observe rather than just feeding Grief Catalyst's lookup.
4. **The "want/goal" piece, clarified by Maxime 30 Aug: it's Gate 1 of his own Reaction Formula** (`Reaction_Formula_Applied_Model_v3.docx`, part of his "formula for a perfect story" — a separate, pre-existing narrative framework for the Qiraki books, not something invented for this plan). That formula's core is `(A + B) + (a · b⁴(c)) = D + E`, worked per-archetype inside a catalyst (`c`); gate 1, literally named "A to D" in the doc, is the cheapest and most-used check in the whole system: **does this character react at all — yes or no, checked first.** "No" is a valid, common, intentionally cheap answer; everything richer (the four-echo pass, the animal, self-vs-self/self-vs-other) only runs when gate 1 says yes. Two things worth flagging directly: (a) this isn't a new mechanic to design — it's an existing authorial framework Maxime wants Meks' reactions held to the same standard as everything else in the fiction; (b) it lines up with what's already shipped, not against it — the game's own `catalyst` field (wolf/raven/crow/bear/fox, etc., `ambientLines.ts`) is literally the formula's `c`, and `Echo` (the four-emotion type already imported by `needsCounter.ts`) is the formula's Love/Fear/Anger/Sadness echo-pick in step 3. So "give Meks a want" doesn't mean new state or a new system — it means: before any Mek ambient line or hot-topic content fires, gate it the same cheap yes/no way gate 1 gates a character's reaction in a chapter, rather than always firing once a catalyst happens to be assigned. The full richer formula (three-animal internal conflict, the self-vs-other loop) is explicitly reserved in the source doc for "the beat that's actually about the relationship" — not every Mek, every time — which maps onto the existing "most reactions are a single-animal pass, the loop is the heaviest configuration, save it" framing the doc itself uses. **Explicitly decided by Maxime, 30 Aug: the simple yes/no gate-1 check is what ships for this EA pass. A more complex need/want system — richer than a binary gate — is on the roadmap, but deliberately scoped for *after* early access, not this one.** Worth remembering as its own line item once EA ships, the same discipline the calendar economy and CIC already got (§4, §5) — flagged here so it doesn't quietly get folded back into scope later the way the calendar economy almost did.

5. **Authorship tool, resolved by Maxime 30 Aug: the Character Editor writes every NPC, not just player-made recruits.** Checked against the actual doc (`Bloom_Wars_Character_Editor_v1.md`, 25 Aug) rather than assumed — real, useful context and a real gap both surfaced:
   - **It's currently 100% unbuilt.** "Design pass only, zero code, per Maxime's own explicit call... nothing here gets built before the hard tactical loop is proven." The 36-mission campaign is now complete, so that original gating condition may already be satisfied — worth confirming directly with Maxime rather than assuming, since the doc itself never revisits that call.
   - **It already has the right two-layer shape for this.** Layer 1 (chassis/species/class, name, portrait, tier) is the mechanical floor, already partly shipped via the emergency-replacement recruit path. Layer 2 is a 9-label animal/catalyst picker (Wolf/Dog/Cat/Crow/Raven/Bear/Fox/Rabbit/Shark) for personality — small to build, the vocabulary already exists as the same `catalyst` field Meks already mechanically carry.
   - **One real gap: it was scoped for player-created pilots only, and §7 explicitly left "do named pilots get animal labels too, or something more bespoke" open.** Maxime's instruction today resolves that: yes — every NPC, named crew and Meks included, should go through the same tool, not stay on a separate hand-assigned-only path.
   - **A second real gap: it has no backstory field at all.** Layer 1/Layer 2 cover chassis/species/class/name/portrait/animal-label — never prose. §8's own ask (a hand-authored backstory per Mek) needs a new field this doc never designed, not just wider use of what's there.
   - **The real prerequisite, flagged by the doc itself and still true: the JSON/runtime-data migration (§6).** Every pilot/Mek/unit definition today is a TypeScript module that needs a recompile to change. An editor that writes "all NPC characters" at runtime is incompatible with that on its face — this is probably the single biggest piece of engineering hiding inside "add a character editor," bigger than the UI or the backstory field.

**Open decisions, not made here:**
- Scope: the 5 Act I Meks first (same discipline as every other named-content pass this project has used), or design it to scale across the full active roster from the start, given Tier 3 just made every Mek walkable?
- Whether the Character Editor's original build-gate ("not before the hard tactical loop is proven") is now lifted, now that the campaign is done — this decides whether the editor itself is in scope for this pass at all, or whether Mek names/backstory still need a manual stopgap (Maxime or me, with a check-in) until the editor exists for real.
- Sequencing: build the editor's runtime-data migration + backstory field first and author Meks through it, or hand-author the first Mek pass now (same way the named pilot cast always has been) and let the editor retrofit onto existing content later.
- ~~Whether to build the "personal want" mechanic (item 4 above) now alongside the content pass, or treat it as its own later system~~ — **resolved, see item 4: gate-1 yes/no ships now, the richer need/want system is explicitly post-EA roadmap.**

Not yet ranked against §3–§5's existing items — worth a direct call on where this sits once the scope questions above are answered; it reads as closer in kind to the Vault dedication (hand-authored, narrative, load-bearing for "alive" NPCs) than to the hardening/engine items.

## 9. New scope, added by Maxime 30 Aug — Heirloom Pilots and Meks: Vault-gated specialists

Maxime's ask, verbatim: "heirloom pilot and mek are specialist, can only get heirloom pilot when buying a vault. can only field 1 heirloom pilot in a mission, heirloom [sinker?] and mek are th[eir] own npc with their own lines and need, want."

What this adds, distinct from §8 (which is about *all* Meks): a new specialist subcategory — **Heirloom Pilots and Heirloom Meks** — layered on top of the regular roster, with real mechanical constraints:

- **Acquisition is gated by the Vault.** "Buying a vault" unlocks getting a Heirloom Pilot at all — this directly reshapes the Vault (§1, §5) from a pure narrative dedication scene into also a gameplay acquisition gate. The two jobs need designing together, not sequenced as if the Vault were only ever the one thing.
- **A deploy cap: at most 1 Heirloom Pilot per mission**, regardless of how many are owned — a new roster constraint, same shape as the existing Second/Third Lance deploy caps already documented in `engine_systems/squad_and_deploy_structure.md`, just a new named cap rather than reuse of an existing one.
- **Heirloom Pilots and Heirloom Meks get the full NPC treatment** — their own lines (ambient/hot-topic content) and their own need/want (§8 item 4's gate-1 check) — the same standard §8 sets for ordinary Meks, extended to this specialist tier too.
